# The 2026-09-07 design — what moved, and what it costs to build

**Status: plan notes — nothing of it is built; §5's three rows were RULED by the owner on 2026-09-07 (D414) — the feed is the only door, the sub-scales stay on the device for now, and the facet items are written next.** Every item is measured
against the tree with the constraint that shapes it and its backend half
named (D167); §6 is the build order as worklist lines and §7 the gate per
step. The source is the owner's `InSight_10.html` upload of 2026-09-07 —
tenth in the numbered series, one day after `InSight_9` — extracted to
[`design/standalone-2026-09-07/`](../design/standalone-2026-09-07/). That
directory's README is the inventory (every changed file, the patches, the
unchanged list); this file is the plan. `docs/VISUAL-VISION.md` names this
design as the current vision (D361's standing rule; the arrival record is
D413).

**What the owner asked for with the upload, in their words:** *"politic
test and big 5 have gotten subcategories as well so we need to make a plan
for testing them as well."* So §2 is not a port note — it is the plan for
how thirty facets and eighteen positions get MEASURED: which items, asked
where, scored how, stored where, proved by what. The design draws the
readings; for the compass it also draws the questions; for the Big Five it
draws the readings alone, and the items are this plan's to name.

## What was measured

**Everything below was checked against the tree, not assumed.** Against
the latest recorded design state (v18 + the v28 patches + the eight later
extractions + the two READMEs' recorded hunks), **99 of 117 modules are
byte-identical** and eight more differ only by hunks the record already
carries in prose. What actually moved is **three new modules and seven
that moved — and no stylesheet at all**. They read as three additions and
one polish pass:

1. **The two instruments most people know get a second level.** The Big
   Five's five domains unfold into the thirty IPIP-NEO facets, six per
   domain, each on a 4–20 track, with IPIP-NEO's own report text behind
   every one; the compass's six axes unfold into eighteen positions,
   three per axis, on a 0–100 track with a hollow *most people* ring.
   Both live on the profile's Big 5 and Politics tabs, under the result
   card, as rows that unfold in place — one open at a time.
2. **The compass carries its own way in; the Big Five does not.** An
   axis you have not gone deep on shows three empty positions and *Six
   questions place them →*: two questions per position on the five-step
   agreement scale, one at a time, after which the axis's compass score
   becomes the mean of its positions — *"so level one and level two never
   disagree."* The Big Five module has no questions in it at all: thirty
   demo scores and no way to earn one.
3. **Circle and 1v1 get a first day.** Not a form: one day of the game
   drawn with nothing invented — today's World question over the hairline
   ballot with the seal on its seam, the reveal clock, tomorrow's reveal
   as a real one is drawn — then *Start one* and the other door in one
   line. An invitation waiting and a tapped link get hero beats; an
   account with no name gets the field inside *Start one*; circles that
   already exist get a *Start another* row at the end of the stack.
4. **The person overlay's three record sections go boxless**: the
   receipts lead with a sentence and the exception side, *Play together*
   becomes doors, the read-each-other card becomes a two-column hit-rate
   table under a sentence. Plus one line on the feed's two-option ballot.

None of it needs a rules change or a new collection. Two things need
more than a port: **the facets and positions need items, a fold and a
place to live** (§2 — the bank grows by 156 questions, the scoring
metadata has to ride the bank rather than the eager graph, and the
politics half is inside D331's consent gate), and **the compass's
in-place questions are a sit-down flow, which D121 retired on the owner's
own ask** (§5.1 — an owner row, not a routine's call). One thing is
free: the receipts section the design redraws was never ported (§4), so
the redraw is a first port rather than a change.

## 1 · The instruments in depth — what the design draws, and what the tree has

### 1.1 · The Big Five's thirty facets

`big5-deep.jsx` (→ a lazy panel under the Big 5 tab): five domain rows
in IPIP-NEO's order — name, band, a one-line trait, and the six facet
scores as dots on one 4–20 track with a grey tick at the midpoint 12 —
one open at a time. The open domain shows its definition in the serif,
a *you* paragraph, then the facets as rows: a label column (name over its
`lo`/`hi` reading, whichever side the score falls), the score's track
and dot, the figure; tap a facet to read IPIP-NEO's paragraph on it; a
`4 · midpoint · 20` foot. Six facets per domain, thirty in all, the
IPIP-NEO names verbatim (*Anxiety · Anger · Depression ·
Self-Consciousness · Immoderation · Vulnerability* under Neuroticism,
and so on), the report text kept verbatim.

Measured against the tree: **nothing behind it exists.** The Big Five
in the tree is five axes and 25 items (`content/tests.json`, five per
domain, two of them reverse-keyed — D85), scored passively from feed
answers by `data/passiveProfile.ts` once every axis has two answers
(D121), drawn by `spec/result-card.jsx`. No item carries a facet, no
fold computes one, no document stores one. The design's scores are
Mira's, and its module says so in its first line. The 4–20 scale is
IPIP-NEO-120's: four items per facet, each 1–5, summed — which is also
the arithmetic the plan adopts (§2.3), because the design's tracks,
midpoint and report text all assume it.

Two labels the port must reconcile: the tree's fifth domain is
**Sensitivity** (`IS_TESTS.big5.dims`, the result card, the type names)
and the design's module says **Neuroticism** (IPIP-NEO's word, which its
report text uses throughout). The domain row takes the card's label —
one instrument, one vocabulary on one screen — and the definition line
under it says what the instrument calls it. That is a build note, not a
decision; §5.2 carries the one facet name that is.

### 1.2 · The compass's eighteen positions

`politics-deep.jsx` (→ a lazy panel under the Politics tab): six axis
rows — name, side (*left / centre / right* by `sideOf`, < 45 / > 55),
the axis blurb, and a 0–100 track with a hollow *most people* ring and
your positions as dots (or your single axis score until you go deep) —
one open at a time. The open axis shows its definition in the serif and
its three positions as rows: name over reading, the track with the ring
and your dot, the figure; tap one to read it; the poles and *most people*
as the foot; *Answer again* under placed positions. An axis with no
positions yet shows them empty (*not placed yet*, a dash for the figure)
and one primary button: **Six questions place them →**. The questions
run in place — a progress strip, the position's name as kicker, *N of
6*, the prompt, the five-step scale with its filled/hollow marks
(*Strongly disagree … Strongly agree*), *← Back* / *Not now* — and on
the sixth answer the positions are scored (a position is the mean of its
two items, an unanswered item counting as the middle) and the axis's
compass score becomes their mean.

Measured against the tree: the compass is six axes and 30 items (five
per axis, D85), scored by the same passive fold, **computed and
published only with the political consent (D330/D331)** — the fold's
`continue` in `syncPassiveResults` (`data/live.ts`), the ask on D151's
screen (`ui/LiveProfileSetup.tsx`: *"Say yes and your answers to those
cards build a six-axis compass on your profile, which anyone signed in
can read"*), the control in the account panel. The design's 36 prompts
are new content — none of them is in the bank. Its `avg` per position
(*most people*) is authored, which D157 allows in the demo build and
nowhere else. Its persistence (`insight.politicsDeep.v1`) is a device
key holding raw answers that the profile's coordinate is then derived
from — in the tree the raw answers are ANSWERS, one document per
question (§2.2), and a second store of them would be the thing D312's
pending mirror exists to prevent.

### 1.3 · Where they mount, and the shape of the port

The design mounts both under the result card on their tabs
(`profile-overlay.jsx.patch`); the tree's sites are
`spec/profile-overlay.jsx:344` (Big 5) and `:345` (Politics), where
`ResultProfileCard` and `TestProgress` render inline. The panels are
built in this repo as **typed, lazy** components beside
`ui/LiveRolesPanel.tsx` — `React.lazy` behind `<React.Suspense
fallback={null}>`, the shape the Roles tab already uses on that very
screen — reading the fold of §2.3 through `LIVE`, never a window global
(`check:globals` rule 4 must not move; the design's `window.BIG5_DEEP`
and `window.POLITICS_DEEP` publications do not port). The profile
overlay itself is past first paint (`loadOverlays()`, D38), so the
panels cost bytes in that chunk only. The design's inline 10.5 kickers
and 11 readings port at the 12px floor (D391's rule); its 10px facet
rows (padding 10, content ~22) grow to the 44 px `check:tap-targets`
wants; every unfolding row already carries `aria-expanded`.

Both panels draw the demo persona's scores in a demo build (Mira's
facets and three placed axes — the persona IS the content, D167) and
your own in a live build; a live account with no facets measured sees
the domain rows with empty tracks and the count that fills them
(§2.3's thresholds) — never Mira's dots.

## 2 · Measuring the facets and the positions — the plan for testing them

### 2.1 · The item banks

**Politics: the design's 36 items, verbatim.** Two per position, one
reverse-keyed, on the five-step agreement scale — the same `type:
"scale"` shape the 30 compass items have. They go into
`content/tests.json` under `political` with two new fields, `facet`
(the position id: `redis · reg · pub · speech · police · private ·
borders · aid · trade · climate · land · growth · ai · bio · data · inst
· experts · direct`) and `invert`, keeping `d` (the axis). The bank's
`test: "political"` marker follows automatically, which is what
`POLITICAL_QIDS` derives from (documentation since D98, not a fold
rule) and what the consent gate keys on.

**Big Five: IPIP-NEO-120's items, verbatim — the recommendation, and
§5.3's owner row.** Four items per facet, 120 in all, public domain
(the IPIP's standing terms; Johnson's 120-item form, 2014), on the same
five-step scale, summed to the 4–20 score the design draws, with the
report text the design already carries being IPIP-NEO's own. Keyed
`facet` (thirty ids) + `d` (the domain, so every facet item is also a
domain item) + `invert` per IPIP-NEO's key. The alternative — sixty
items in the app's voice, two per facet, the compass design's pattern —
is the owner's to choose, because the bank's copy is *"the canonical
product voice"* (`content/README.md`) and IPIP-NEO's items are not in
it; §5.3 states the trade.

**Both banks feed the seed.** `content/tests.json` → `npm run
build:content` → `functions/src/v2content.ts` → `seedContentV2`, which
appends new ids and refuses to re-key existing ones (D52), so the 110
core items keep their documents and their aggregates. `check:content`
holds the generated file equal to the source; `content-parity.test.jsx`
holds `IS_TESTS` equal to the bank item for item and asserts **exactly
K = 5 items per dimension** — a constant that becomes per-instrument
and per-level (5 domain items + 24 facet items per Big Five domain; 5 +
6 per axis), or the parity test would refuse the bank on day one. The
`invert` flag lives only in `IS_TESTS` today (*"the join matches on the
PROMPT TEXT rather than trusting an id convention"*, `similarity.ts`);
§2.5 moves it.

**What the new items are not.** They are not core (`core` is a feed
field; the corpus rule is D161's), so they enter neither the Patterns
Map nor the online engine — `PATTERNS_QIDS` admits two-option daily and
core-feed items only. Whether they join D395's candidate corpus is that
corpus's own rule, unchanged by this plan; the 110 core items went in
under it and the 156 follow or not by the same clause. They are not
sponsored, not learn, not pulse: ordinary public answers whose option
counts publish exactly (D98).

### 2.2 · How the items are answered — the feed, and the flow the design draws

**The feed, as today (D121).** A `surface: "test"` item is an ordinary
feed card, interleaved one every four (`TEST_EVERY = 4`,
`data/feed-interleave.ts`), deferrable with *later* (`data/deferQueue.ts`)
and coming back until answered. The arithmetic is the reason this
section has a second half: 156 new items at one in four is **~624 feed
cards** to fill both instruments passively, on top of the 110 the feed
serves today. A person who answers twenty cards a day meets their last
facet item in a month. Passive filling stays the writer of record —
every facet and position fills from the feed with no act by the user —
but it cannot be the only door if a domain is to be readable the day it
is asked for.

**The flow the design draws — for the compass only, and it is D121's
retired shape.** *Six questions place them →* is an in-place sit-down:
six items, one at a time, then a result. D121 retired exactly that on
the owner's 2026-08-12 ask (*"tests should appear only passively in the
feed with no active test-taking"*) and the design of 2026-09-07 draws
it back, on one instrument. Both are the owner's words, a routine
decides between them for nobody, and §5.1 is the row. What this plan
fixes is the SHAPE the flow would have if built, so that a tick builds
one thing and not two:

- **An in-place answer IS the feed answer.** Each of the six items is a
  bank question; the tap writes it through `LIVE.vote` — the same
  document `v2_users/{uid}/answers/{qid}` the feed would write, with the
  same anchors snapshot (D8), the same D86 edit shape, D312's pending
  mirror. The feed then finds those cards answered and never re-asks;
  the position and the axis fold from the same answers either way.
  There is no second store of raw answers — the design's
  `insight.politicsDeep.v1` key does not port, and `check:purge`'s
  subject set does not grow.
- **The Big Five gets the same door, per domain**, if the row ticks —
  twenty-four items for an open domain (the design draws none; the
  compass's flow is the drawn grammar, one domain over). Six positions
  at two items is two minutes; a domain at twenty-four is six, which is
  the price of a 4–20 facet and the reason §5.3 lists the sixty-item
  alternative.
- **An unanswered item scores as the middle** in the design
  (`scorePositions`: `v == null ? 2`). That ports for the flow's own
  progress strip and nowhere else: the fold (§2.3) counts what was
  answered and says how many, because a position drawn at 50 from one
  answer is the invented-number failure D157 is about.

**Ruled 2026-09-07 (D414): the feed is the only door.** The owner's
answer to §5.1 — *"no they should only be in the feed"* — so the flow
above does not build: the design's `Questions` component and its *Six
questions place them →* button do not port, an axis with no positions
shows them empty with their counts, and the deep panels read what the
feed has filled. The three bullets stand as the record of what a tick
would have built; the arithmetic stands as the cost the owner accepted
with open eyes.

### 2.3 · Scoring — the facet fold, the level-one rule, the thresholds, the bands, the norms

`data/passiveProfile.ts` grows one level: beside `PassiveTest.dims` a
`facets` array — per facet its id, its domain, `n` answered of `k`, and
its score. **The arithmetic is the design's:** the Big Five facet is the
sum of its four 1–5 answers (4–20; the option index 0..4 plus one,
reverse-keyed items flipped), the position is the mean of its two
answers on 0–100 (the same `mean / 4 × 100` the axes already use). The
join is `testItemMeta`'s, extended by `facet` (§2.5).

**The threshold is "all of them".** A facet draws its dot when every
one of its items is answered — four for a facet, two for a position.
D121 set two answers per axis as the floor for a result on a five-item
axis and gave the reason (*"one 5-point answer maps to one of {0, 25,
50, 75, 100} and lands the axis on an extreme more often than not"*);
a facet has fewer items and a finer claim, and a 4–20 sum of three
answers is not a number on that scale. Below the floor the facet row
draws its count (*2 of 4*) in the figure column, no dot, no reading —
D190's rule that an empty row says why it is empty. A domain row's
six-dot track draws the dots that exist.

**The level-one rule is the design's, stated once and pinned.** *"Once
an axis has positions its compass score becomes their mean, so level
one and level two never disagree."* In the tree: when every facet of a
domain (or every position of an axis) is measured, the axis value is
the mean of the facet values (each on 0–100; a 4–20 facet maps as
`(s − 4) / 16`); until then it is what it is today, the mean over the
axis's answered items — which after the seed includes the facet items,
because they carry `d`. So level one moves toward level two as facets
fill and equals it when they are full; there is no moment it
contradicts it. `MIN_AXIS_ITEMS = 2` is untouched — the result card's
type still needs two answers per axis and no facet.

**Bands are about the scale, not the crowd.** The design's *low / high*
is the side of the midpoint (12 on 4–20; `sideOf`'s < 45 / > 55 /
centre on 0–100). That is a claim about where a score sits on its own
scale, needs no norm, and is what ports. IPIP-NEO's own bands are
percentiles against norms; the tree's norms are measured or absent
(D157), so a percentile band waits for the norm below.

**The hollow *most people* ring is D157's.** `data/testNorms.ts` folds
the crowd's published counts on an axis's items into its average, at
`NORM_MIN_ANSWERS = 30` across `NORM_MIN_ITEMS = 2` of them, and hands
back the authored constants in the demo build only. The same fold one
level down — over a position's two items, over a facet's four — gives
the ring the design draws on the position tracks, with its basis
stateable (*over N answers to these two questions*). The design's
authored `avg` values (`46 · 52 · 52 …`) go where `IS_TEST_AVG` went:
the demo build. A live position with no norm draws no ring. The Big
Five facet track carries only the midpoint tick in the design; a norm
ring there is free once the fold exists and is the build's call.

**The narratives.** The design ships one *you* paragraph per domain
(Mira's band). IPIP-NEO's report has three per domain (low · average ·
high) and one per facet; the port carries all fifteen domain paragraphs
and the thirty facet paragraphs as copy in the lazy chunk (~14 KB,
past first paint), choosing the domain paragraph by band. The compass
positions' `text` (eighteen paragraphs) is the design's own copy and
ports as written. All of it is UI copy, not question content, so
`check:eager-content` is not in its way — as long as it never reaches
`test-definitions.js`.

### 2.4 · What is stored and what is published

**v1: on the device, from your own answers — nothing new stored, nothing
new published.** The fold runs where `passiveProfile` runs, over the
answers cache; the panels read it through `LIVE`. `testResults.big5` and
`.political` on the world-readable profile keep their shape (`dims`,
`passive`, `answered`, `total`) — the level-one rule changes the VALUES
in `dims` once facets fill, which is a fold change, not a schema one.
The Compare lens, Kindred, the similarity field and the pair card read
`dims` and are unaffected. No rules change, no collection, no read.

**Publishing facets and positions is §5.2's owner row.** A cross-user
surface that would draw someone else's facets (the person overlay is
the natural one; the design does not draw it) needs them on the profile:
a `facets` map on `testResults.big5` (30 numbers, ~200 bytes) and on
`.political` (18). Two constraints stand on that write and both are
already on the owner's list: `testResults` is bounded in key count
(`firestore.rules`, `keys().size() <= 8`) and not in bytes — the open
row *"one profile field has no size limit"* — and the political half is
inside D331's gate, where positions ride the same `continue` as the six
axes and are deleted with them when consent ends. Publishing positions
also changes what the consent WORDS promise (*"a six-axis compass"* on
D151's screen and *"a six-axis political profile"* in
`web/privacy.html`): the page moves first (D183, `check:policy-claims`)
and `POLITICAL_CONSENT_VERSION` bumps so everyone who agreed to six axes
is asked again about eighteen positions under them. None of that is
needed while facets stay device-side, which is the reason v1 does.

### 2.5 · First paint, and the join

**The eager graph is 602 KB against a 607 ceiling** (the shipping build,
measured 2026-09-07: `2183 KB total / 602 KB eager (max 2440 / 607)`),
and `src/v2/spec/test-definitions.js` — the 110 prompts with their
`d`/`invert` — is on `check:eager-content`'s allowlist as named debt
(*"the list may only shrink"*). 156 more prompts there would be ~12 KB
of first paint the gate exists to refuse, and the five kilobytes of
headroom would not hold them anyway.

So **the scoring metadata rides the bank document**: `axis` already
does (the seed transports it, `QuestionDoc.axis`); `facet` and `invert`
join it, and `testItemMeta` joins by **id** where a doc carries them,
falling back to the prompt join for the 110 items until they carry them
too. `IS_TESTS` then stops being the only source of `invert`, the
content-parity suite pins the bank against the definitions on the
fields both carry, and `test-definitions.js` gains the facet TABLE only
if the panels need it eagerly — they do not; it lives in the panels'
lazy chunk. The eager delta is quoted in the PR body and should be ~0.

### 2.6 · What proves it — the suites and the gates

The owner's ask was a plan for *testing* the subcategories; this is the
half of it that is the repo's five runners, per step.

- **The banks** (`npm run test:unit`, `check:content`, `check:quality`,
  `check:answer-shape`, `test:scripts`): `content-parity.test.jsx`
  asserts, per instrument and level, exactly K items per facet (4 · 2)
  and per axis (5 domain items); at least one reverse-keyed item per
  position (the design has exactly one) and IPIP-NEO's keying per facet;
  every `facet` id belongs to the `d` it sits under; the 110 old ids and
  prompts unchanged (D52's immutability, pinned so a seed cannot
  re-key). `functions/` `seed.test.ts` drives the seed with the new
  items and proves append-only.
- **The fold** (`test:unit`): `passiveProfile.test.ts` — a facet with 3
  of 4 is `n: 3` and no score; with 4 of 4 the sum; a reverse-keyed item
  flipped; a domain with all six facets measured reads their mean and
  with five reads its item mean; a position of two; the political fold
  refuses to run without consent and the facets ride the same refusal
  (`politicalConsent.test.ts`'s matrix, one case more); `testNorms.test.ts`
  — a position norm at 30 answers over 2 items, none below, authored
  in demo only; `similarity.test.ts` — the id join beats the prompt join
  where a doc carries `facet`, and an item whose prompt no longer
  matches is dropped, not mis-scored.
- **The panels** (`test:unit` mount suites, `check:tap-targets`,
  `check:a11y`, `check:labels`, `check:public-copy`, `check:bundle`,
  `check:globals`): the Big 5 and Politics tabs mount the panels through
  `mount-app.jsx` and assert on the `ErrorBoundary`, not a throw (the
  harness's rule); one domain open at a time; a facet row under its
  floor shows the count and no dot; the domain row's label is the card's
  (*Sensitivity*); every unfolding row has `aria-expanded` and 44 px;
  rule 4's baseline unmoved; the lazy chunk's bytes quoted and the eager
  delta ~0.
- **The flow, if ticked** (`test:unit`, `check:answer-shape`,
  `check:purge`): a tap writes through `LIVE.vote` with anchors (the
  gate reads the call site); the sixth answer leaves no device key; the
  feed's copy of the item is answered afterwards; *Not now* mid-run
  keeps the answered items and loses nothing.
- **The e2e** (`test:e2e:all`): unchanged — a facet item is a test item,
  and the loop already votes on one and reads its aggregate. The
  political-items slicing pin (`functions/`, derived from the bank in
  both directions) picks the 36 up by their marker without an edit.

## 3 · The first day of Circle and 1v1

### 3.1 · What it draws

`first-day.jsx`: the empty Circle or 1v1 tab as **one day of the game,
drawn with nothing invented, then the doors.** *Today · sealed*: today's
World question in the serif over the two-column hairline ballot, both
halves blank in the quiet ink, the seal (a solid disc with one inner
ring, `aria-label="sealed until tomorrow"`) on the seam, and one line —
*Today's World question stands in — a circle draws its own each day.*
*Tonight*: the reveal clock. *Tomorrow · revealed*: two reveal rows, your
mark in the first seat and open seats as dashed rings (*revealed with
names* / *revealed if you both play*), and the second tap's empty run of
seven dots (*Your call on where the room lands* / *Your guess at their
answer*). Then *Start a circle* / *Start a 1v1*: the group name and, if
the account has none, *Your name* as hairline fields; *Who's coming* —
