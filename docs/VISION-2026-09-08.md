# The 2026-09-08 design — what moved, and what it costs to build

**Status: mixed — three of the five moves are BUILT (D430, the owner's
2026-09-08 ruling): the paid family leaves with the buyer's room, the
person's page is four tabs on the demo page, and the polish pass; one
move (§2) is withdrawn to an owner question, because a group is not a
circle; the live person's page (§4.6), the demo twins under rounds (§1)
and the calendar words (§1.4) stay open.** Every item is measured
against the tree with the constraint that shapes it and its backend half
named (D167); §8 is the build order as worklist lines and §9 the gate per
step. The source is the owner's `InSight_11.html` upload of 2026-09-08 —
eleventh in the numbered series, one day after `InSight_10` and the same
day as the rounds canvas — extracted to
[`design/standalone-2026-09-08/`](../design/standalone-2026-09-08/). That
directory's README is the inventory (every changed file, the patches, the
unchanged list, and the recipe that reads a **minified** bundle exactly);
this file is the plan. `docs/VISUAL-VISION.md` names this design as the
current vision (D361's standing rule; the arrival record is D429).

**What the owner asked for with the upload, in their words:** *"some new
visuals — plan the implementation of those."* No item in it came with a
sentence of its own, so nothing below leans on one; where the design
makes a claim the tree cannot make true, the claim is an owner row (§7)
rather than a reading of intent.

## What was measured

**Everything below was checked against the tree, not assumed.** Against
the latest recorded design state (v18 + the v28 patches + the nine later
extractions + the two READMEs' recorded hunks), minified with the
recipe the README gives, **89 of 110 modules are byte-identical**. What
moved is **21 modules, 4 stylesheets, and 7 modules and one sheet
deleted**. They read as five moves:

1. **Rounds reach the prototype.** The demo engine and both daily bodies
   take the card the owner drew the same day for request 12
   (`design/rounds-card-2026-09-08/`) — the five-round lead, the
   deadline that starts on a round's first answer, the late answer, World
   questions on every even round with the partner's public answer as the
   no-guess signal, the nine states, the SAID · CALLED table, the
   three-column World reveal, the sealed list, *answer ahead*, the run of
   rounds. The reveal clock module is deleted; the first run loses its
   *Tonight* beat and its *Start another* row; *days* become *rounds*;
   twelve duel prompts lose their calendar words.
2. **The ruler's second stop says Groups · 1v1s** — what the stop draws
   is named, in the shell and the daily both; the first run says *group*.
   The app's label on the same stop is *Circle*, and a group is not a
   circle (§2, corrected at D430).
3. **The paid family leaves the prototype**: the suggestion board, the
   buyer's room, the catalog sheet, the paid report, the feed's and the
   daily's *Suggest a question* doors, the header's ask button, the place
   stops' *Asked for Oslo*, the scorecard's *Author a metric*, the
   profile's *You asked* shelf.
4. **The person's page becomes four tabs** — Match · Answers · Together ·
   Map — with a horizontal swipe between them, which the profile overlay's
   sub-tabs get too. Match is an accordion of the instruments, Answers a
   seven-wide grid of topic-hued dots, Together is tiles with a figure and
   the pair's type over two reading rings, Map is the person's own map with
   a *friends see everything* line.
5. **A polish-and-optimise pass**: 44 px hit slack by pseudo-element, a
   12 px floor on avatar initials, one reduced-motion switch, `Lazy`'s
   timers collapsed into one, the feed's head-hide off React state, some
   two hundred dead CSS lines swept, a dozen D182 copy cuts.

Most of (1) and all of (3) is the prototype catching up with the tree:
the live 1v1 and group card already draws the rounds card from the
canvas (D426's second amendment), and the paid door left the app at D368.
What is new to the tree is the person's page (4), the polish (5), the
DEMO half of (1) — the bodies a build with `LIVE` off still draws — and
the question (2) raises about the stop's label. Nothing in it needs a
rules change or a new collection. One claim in it the tree cannot make
true on a live page (§4.5) went to the owner; one deletion the tree had
made the opposite call on (§3) went to the owner and was ruled the
design's way the same day (D430).

## 1 · Rounds reach the prototype

### 1.1 · The demo twins take the rounds card

`group-daily.jsx` and `duo-daily.jsx` (carried whole) draw, in the demo
bodies, exactly the card the canvas drew and `ui/LiveDuelPanel.tsx`
built: `RoundKicker` (*Round 9 · World · revealed · closed at the
deadline*), the serif `Prompt`, `OptBtn`, the `Band` (*3 rounds waiting
for you*), `GroupRevealBlock` / `DuoRevealBlock` (the split with faces or
the SAID · CALLED table; `WorldCols` for a World round; open seats;
*The room landed on Food · ✓ you called it*; *Answered after the reveal.
It shows, and scores nothing.*), then the state block — the call, the
wait (*Reveals when everyone has played · 1d 16h at the latest* /
*waiting on Ada*), the `SealedList` at the lead with `AheadBtn`, or the
ask — and `RoundRun` at the foot (six kinds, two runs on a 1v1). The
primitives are published once from the group body and read by the 1v1
body, the shape the two demo bodies already share for `GDAv` (D418
converted that pair to imports).

Measured against the tree: **the live card is built** (D426's second
amendment, request 12 crossed out) and **the demo twins are not**.
`spec/group-daily.jsx` still imports `RevealClock`, says *Reveals
tomorrow · swipe down for Leo* and *done for today*, draws the day dots,
and `spec/duo-daily.jsx` prints the `N-day run` and `theyDone ? <RevealClock/>
: "still answering…"`. `daily-split.jsx` lazy-mounts these bodies only
when `LIVE.enabled` is false (its own comment: *"it is the DEMO Circle
body"*), so a live build never shows them — but five mount suites do:
the smoke suites walk both modes, and `daily-modes.test.jsx` goes past
first paint into Circle and 1v1 on the demo engine. So the port is
demo-only and test-visible, which is the right shape: the demo is the
device the owner and every screenshot lane look at, and today it shows a
game the app no longer plays.

**What the port is.** `spec/group-daily.jsx` and `spec/duo-daily.jsx`
take the two whole files as adaptations (the repo's copies are the
converted-import ones, D418): the primitives become named exports of
`group-daily.jsx` imported by `duo-daily.jsx`, never `window.RoundRun`
(rule 4 must not move; the design publishes fourteen names onto
`window`, and every one of them ports as an export). `RevealClock` leaves
both files; `spec/reveal-clock.js` then has **no consumer** (the live
panel counts to a deadline through its own `until`), so it is deleted
with its `spec-index.js` line — the design deleted it too, and a module
kept for a game nobody plays is D39's number going the wrong way.
`FirstDayCompact` was never in the tree, and the design deletes it.

### 1.2 · The demo engine

`duels-data.js` (carried whole) is the tree's own model, mirrored:
`LEAD = 5`, a 48-hour `DEADLINE` from a round's first answer, `late`
flagged on an answer after the deadline and scored nothing, even rounds
drawn from the World pool (`DAILYQ.questions`, two-to-four-option, not a
scale) with `publicAnswer` as the no-guess signal, a seeded closed room
(`The Cousins`, deadline already past — state 6), a room three rounds
ahead (`Book Club` — the band), partners with their own `THEIR_AHEAD`
and `LAG` (a partner who answers 14 s after you, one who never does —
the sealed list), and two view objects, `groupView` / `duoView`, that
give a card everything in one call. Measured: `spec/duels-data.js` keys
by `DAYS = ['Today', 'Yesterday', …]` throughout; the typed layer has the
live model (`data/duelRuns.ts`, `groupPortrait.ts`, `roles.ts` and the
group document). The port replaces the demo engine's day arithmetic with
the round arithmetic, keeps every export the Mirror's Groups stop and the
person's page read (`groupPortrait`, `groupAlignment`, `partners`,
`impressions`, `domainRows`, `weakDomain` — the design keeps them, with
`rounds` beside `days` in the portrait so a consumer can move at its own
pace), and moves the storage key to `insight.duels.v2` so a device
carrying `v1` demo state starts clean. `check:purge` lists the demo keys
the purge sweeps; the new key joins it and the old one stays until the
next sweep of dead keys.

The twelve reworded prompts are §1.4's, not this step's: the demo engine
keeps its own copy of the duel bank for the demo build and the tree's is
`content/duel-questions.json` (D52).

### 1.3 · Days → rounds, in the copy that says a unit

`role-data.js`, `roles-panel.jsx` and `map-people.jsx` change the word:
*same answer on N of M rounds*, *N of 6 rounds* on the Roles panel's thin
row, *N rounds* on the Map's people card (which now reads `played`, not
`streak`). Measured: the tree's typed `data/roles.ts` counts *days* by
name in eleven comments and two doc-strings (*"Days behind this reading,
in the unit the floor counts — both guessed for a 1v1, played for a
group"*), and `ui/LiveRolesPanel.tsx` prints the unit. Under rounds the
unit is a round: the fold counts reveals, and a reveal is a round. The
port is the word at the printing sites plus the doc-strings; the
arithmetic does not move (a reveal was a day, now it is a round, and the
floors — 4 both guessed, 6, 3 — count reveals either way). The demo
`spec/role-data.js` and `spec/roles-panel.jsx` take the design's lines as
written; `spec/map-people.jsx` takes `played` for `streak`, which the
demo engine of §1.2 provides.

### 1.4 · The duel bank's calendar words

The design rewrites twelve prompts in its demo bank so that no question
names a day of the week, a week, a weekend or a night: *A free afternoon,
zero plans* (was *A free Saturday*), *Our default plan for a free
evening?* (*a free Friday*), *A cabin with no wifi* (*A winter cabin*),
*a truth that would sting for a while* (*hurt for a week*), *A stretch
alone in a cabin* (*A week alone*), *A long stretch together, no phones*
(*A whole weekend*), *After a brutal stretch* (*a brutal week*), *They
had a hard time* (*a hard day*), *Book a trip at once* (*that night*), *a
proper sleep* (*a proper night of sleep*), *Ideal holiday?* (*holiday
day*), *Nothing planned at all* (*A day with nothing in it*), *Who talks
to a coconut first?* (*by day three*). This is D419 §3 applied to
content: under rounds a 1v1 can play five of these in an hour, and a
question that assumes a week between answers reads wrong.

Measured: **eleven of the twelve are in `content/duel-questions.json`
verbatim** (the coconut line is only in the demo engine). The bank is
seeded (D52: `seedContentV2` appends new ids and never re-keys), and an
answer is an option index (D86), so a prompt reworded with its options
untouched orphans nothing — the aggregates, the reveals and the roles
fold all key on the id and the index. What holds a rewrite is the
farm's own rules (`check:quality`, `check:content`) and the parity pins
that name the 110 core prompts as immutable — the duel bank is not in
that pin. So the step is a content-lane edit of twelve `prompt` fields,
a `build:content`, and a seed run that writes the twelve fields to
existing documents — the one field the seed may change on an existing
question, and the reason it may is that the id and the options are the
answer's key and the prompt is not. `functions/` `seed.test.ts` proves
the write is a prompt-only update on an existing id and a refusal on
anything else.

played, or at the deadline*), the reveal clock is gone from it, the seal
is *sealed until the reveal*, *circle* is *group*, the marks are 26 and

first-run section, request 12 state 9 — *A World question stands in — a

§3's step 5 is **superseded by what is built**, and its worklist line is
closed as such rather than ticked as done.

## 2 · The daily's second stop says Groups — and a group is not a circle

**Corrected 2026-09-08 (D430).** This section first read the design's
ruler — **World · Groups · 1v1s** (`app.jsx.patch`,
`daily-split.jsx.patch`) against the app's World · Circle · 1v1
(`spec/app-shell.jsx:230–231`, `spec/daily-split.jsx:420`) — as a
RENAME: one stop, a new word for it, and a vocabulary sweep to follow,
reaching `web/privacy.html`'s two *circle's id* sentences. The owner's
first words on the plan were *"there is a misunderstanding — group and
circle is not the same"*, and that is the fact this section now states
instead of the sweep it planned.

Two things, not one word: a **group** is a named room — The Crew, Book
Club — that plays rounds (D426), drawn by the daily's second stop in
both the app and the design, in `GroupDailyBody` and `LiveDuelPanel`
alike; a **circle** is your friends — the follow graph (D101), the
population the Mirror's Circle stop draws, the word the person's page
uses in *Remove from your circle?*. The design's ruler says what its
second stop draws: groups. The app's ruler says *Circle* over the same
stop, which is the word for the other thing. The design's copy follows
its own word (*Start a group*, *The Crew · Mira's group*, the tweak's
*Group / 1v1 start*, the `data-screen-label` *Group — first run*), and
the Mirror's Circle stop is untouched in it (`mirror-tab.jsx` and
`mirror-field-pops.jsx` are byte-identical).

**What the plan does with it: nothing on its own.** Which word the app's
stop wears is a decision about the product's vocabulary — the label a
person reads every day, and the one the walkthrough, the store text and
the privacy page's two D98 sentences would follow — and the plan has no
standing to make it. It is the owner's, and it is on `OWNER-LIST.md`
as a question: should the daily's second stop say *Groups*, as the
design does, given that a group is not a circle? Nothing in the tree
moved on it, and no worklist step sweeps it. The measurement that
would serve the answer is kept: the label is at two sites, four suites
pin it by name (`test/daily-modes.test.jsx`, `test/smoke-nav.test.jsx`,
`test/smoke-live.test.jsx`, `ui/LiveWalkthrough.test.tsx`), and
`web/privacy.html` says *that circle's id* twice under
`check:policy-claims`' D98 patterns, where the page would move first if
the word did (D183). `1v1s` is a plural on the third stop's label,
which the same answer decides.

## 4 · The person's page as four tabs

### 4.1 · What it draws

`person-overlay.jsx` (carried whole) redraws the page a person opens on
someone else. The hero shrinks — a 116 px affinity ring around an 86 px
avatar, the name at 24 — and gains the receipts' lead sentence under the
role line (*Same answer 34 times out of 35. One split.*). Under it a
`subnav` of up to four tabs in the person's colour, and a horizontal
swipe between them:

- **Match** — *What makes the number*: `CompareList`, the instruments as
  an accordion sorted by alignment (Big Five · Politics · Values · the
  interests ladder), one open at a time, each row *title · the alignment
  figure · the align glyph · a chevron*, the body the carousel's slide
  was, the you / them key under the list. `CompareCarousel` stays as a
  component; this page stops using it.
- **Answers** (present at four shared rows) — a `role="listbox"` grid of
  26 px dots, seven across, one per question you both answered, sorted
  by topic hue then splits first: **filled in the topic's hue = same
  answer, a 3 px ring = you differ**; the first split is selected on
  open, and the selected question is read in a card — the topic as
  kicker, the prompt in the serif, *both X* or *you X · Ada Y*. A key
  under the grid (*same answer · you differ*). Every dot is a 44 px
  button whose label is the prompt and the verdict.
- **Together** (present when a 1v1, a friendship or a shared group
  exists) — *Play together* as tiles: a wide 1v1 tile carrying the two
  marks as a cluster, a 26 px figure over *ROUNDS*, *1v1 · The Twin*
  (the type in the person's colour), and on the ⓘ the type's line; or
  *1v1 · new · the first round is open*, *1v1 · invited · waiting on
  Ada* with *Cancel*, *Start a 1v1 · answer, then guess theirs —
  revealed when you both have played* with *Start*, or the quiet *1v1 ·
  for friends — add Ada first*. Group tiles two across: the members as
  a cluster with Ada ringed, *N people*, the name, the type; *invited ·
  waiting on Ada* where pending. *Add Ada to a group* as chips with
  three-mark clusters, or *No groups together yet*. Then **How well you
  read each other**: the lead sentence (*You read Ada better than Ada
  reads you, except on self-image.*), two `MatchRing`s (*you read Ada*
  in ink, *Ada reads you* in the colour) with `n/total` inside, one row
  per domain with both runs clustered to fourteen dots (*self-image* for
  the mirror domain), and a *called it · missed* key.
- **Map** — *Ada's map*: the still at 320 tall (was 158) over a 440
  canvas, `own: true` (hidden nodes dropped, no same / differ colouring
  — it is her map, not a comparison), the full-screen map behind it with
  a key that says *with the crowd · a rarer take*. And two sentences:
  *some of it stays private until you are friends* beside the kicker,
  *friends see everything* in the full map's key, both only when you are
  not friends.

`useSubSwipe` (in `shared-primitives.jsx`) is the swipe: a horizontal
drag of 66 px on the overlay's body steps the tab, the panel follows the
finger at 0.7 and springs back below the throw, a horizontal wheel steps
it too; SVGs, canvases, the map's own drag, the compare rails, the subnav
and fields keep their gestures. The profile overlay takes the same hook
over its own sub-tabs (`profile-overlay.jsx.patch`).

### 4.5 · The friends-only sentence — an owner row, not a build

*Some of it stays private until you are friends* and *friends see
everything* say that a person's map shows a stranger less than it shows
a friend. **The tree cannot make that true and does not want to by
default.** Answers are public (D98): any signed-in user may read any
other user's answers and profile, there is no floor and no carve-out,
and *showing how one person's answers link to everyone else's IS the
product*. A sentence that promises a restriction the rules do not enforce
is the failure D1 forbids (COPY.md §3: a claim is not a word count), and
a restriction added to make the sentence true is a privacy constraint on
a link — which D334 makes an ASK, both ways: *shipping past a constraint
silently is the same failure pointed the other way*. The design proposes
it; the routine brings the arithmetic (§7.1); the owner rules. Until
then a LIVE map draws whole for everyone, as it does today, and the two
sentences are not written on it. The key's other change — *with the
crowd · a rarer take* for *same answer · you differ*, which says what
the full map actually encodes — ports.

**As built (D430), one nuance.** The demo page, which is the only page
there is, already said *N details hidden · friends see everything*
about its own sample map before this build: the demo's `locked` answers
are invented data the map hides from a non-follower, and the still's
door said *partial*. On that page the design's two sentences describe
the demo's own behaviour, so they are written there; the refusal above
is about the live page, where D98 applies, and it stands.

## 5 · The polish-and-optimise pass

### 5.1 · 44 px hit slack, by pseudo-element

`lens.css`'s two new blocks grow a hit box on every small round control
without moving what is drawn: `.icon-btn`, `.avatar-btn` and
`.mmt-card-x` by `::after { inset: -6px }`; `.tab-btn` and `.pt-pop` by a
44 px band centred on the control (`.pt-pops` trades 7 px of padding for
the same negative margin, so the band fits inside its clip box); the
map's zoom buttons at `inset: -7px`; `.pt-info` at `-8px`. Measured: the
tree has the same device — `.tap44` (`styles.css` §12, *"grows the hit
box with a pseudo-element and changes nothing you can see"*) — applied
per control, and `check:tap-targets` reads inline sizes on `<button>`s
with an empty exemption list (*"every small control in the tree carries
`.tap44`"*). The gate cannot see a class-sized control, and says so: the
class-sized cases are *"handled in the stylesheet with a comment rather
than pretended about here"*. So the port is the design's rules into
`styles.css` §12 beside `.tap44`, each with its comment naming the
control and its drawn size, and the gate's own header gains the four
class names it now trusts by comment. Nothing on the page moves.

### 5.2 · One reduced-motion switch

`@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) { #root *, #root *::before,
#root *::after { animation-duration: .01ms !important;
animation-iteration-count: 1 !important; transition-duration: .01ms
!important; scroll-behavior: auto !important } }`. Measured: the tree
carries 27 targeted rules (23 in `styles.css`, 4 in `patterns.css`), each
naming what it turns off — and inline `animation:` values (`popIn` on
the daily cards, the pulse tints, the ink-in delays) that no targeted
rule reaches. The switch reaches them all. `.01ms` rather than `none` is
the design's choice and the right one: `animationend` still fires, so a
sheet whose dismissal waits on its slide (`sheet-drag.js`, the takes
sheet) still unmounts. The port adds the switch at the end of
`styles.css` and keeps the 27 rules — they are not wrong, and a later
sweep can retire the ones the switch makes redundant with a measurement
rather than a guess. `check:a11y` has no motion rule; the pin is a mount
test under a `matchMedia` stub asserting the switch's rule is in the
sheet.

### 5.3 · `Lazy`'s one timer

`spec/primitives.jsx:86`: every `Lazy` card owns a 400 ms `setInterval`
until it shows. Eleven files render `Lazy`, the feed dozens of times per
page, so a long feed runs dozens of timers. The design's `lazyWatch` is
one shared interval that ticks every pending card's check and stops when
none is pending, with the scroll listener throttled to one
`requestAnimationFrame` and a `dead` flag so a queued tick after cleanup
does nothing. A pure port, its own unit test (N cards, one interval; the
last cleanup clears it).

### 5.4 · The feed's head-hide, off React state

`spec/world-feed.jsx` flips `state.headHide` on every scroll direction
change and re-renders the whole feed for a transform on one sticky
element (`:379`, `:629`, `:4498`, `:4506`). The design writes the
transform, opacity and pointer-events to the element through a ref
(`setHeadHidden`) and keeps `_headHidden` on the instance; the topics
chip calls it before its `setState`. Measured: the D392 `headHold` guard
sits in the same handler and is untouched by the change. A port with the
guard kept, pinned by the existing feed suites plus one assertion: a
scroll flip does not call `setState`.

### 5.5 · The initials floor

Every avatar mark in the daily bodies and the first run draws its
initials at `Math.max(12, size * 0.4)` and the marks grow (22 → 26, 26 →
30); the group mark's radius follows its size. D391's 12 px floor was
for microtype; a 20 px mark drew 8 px initials, which is the same
failure in a circle. Ports with §1.1.

### 5.6 · `.pt-info`

The Patterns explainer button: 28 px, transparent, an ink hairline at
38 %, the serif *i* at 15 px, with the 44 px slack of §5.1. The Together
tab reuses it. `ui/patterns.css` holds it today at 32 px on
`--surface-2`; the restyle ports with the slack, and `PatternsTab`'s
mount test pins the class, not the size.

## 7 · The owner items this design moves

### 7.1 · May a person's map show a stranger less than it shows a friend?

The design writes *some of it stays private until you are friends* on the
Map tab and *friends see everything* in the full map's key. The tree
draws every answer for every signed-in reader (D98). What to bring
(D334's list): **what would be exposed** — nothing new; the question is
what would be *hidden*: the map's leaf nodes, or the answers the person
has hidden from the map, from readers who are not friends. **To whom** —
any signed-in non-friend, which today is everyone but your circle.
**Which of the four it touches** — the privacy page: `web/privacy.html`
promises answers are public with no carve-out, so a friends-only cut is a
new promise and the page moves first (D183, `check:policy-claims`); not a
deny, not a store form, not a consent in law. **The smallest shapes** —
*consent*: a per-person switch, *hide the leaves of my map from people
outside my circle*, off by default (a field on the profile, a rule on the
map's read — but the map reads answers, which are public, so the rule
would have to move answers, which is D98 whole); *coarsen*: strangers see
the still (branch names only, which the still already draws for
everyone) and friends the full map — a UI cut with no rules change and a
sentence that is TRUE; *aggregate*: none applies; *defer*: draw the map
whole and write no sentence, which is the tree today. **What each
costs** — the consent shape reopens D98 for one surface and is the
model D98 retired; the coarsen shape costs one `isFriend` check on the
door to the full map and the sentence becomes honest. The recommendation
is the coarsen shape if the owner wants the sentence, and defer if not;
the routine builds neither on its own.

### 7.2 · Does the buyer's room leave the app too?

The prototype deleted `asked-by-you.jsx` with the rest of the paid family
(§3). D368 kept `ui/AskedByYouOverlay.tsx` deliberately and said why: the
door leaves, the results room stays, a buyer reads their report in the
app. The row: **keep the room as D368 ruled, or take it out as the
prototype did** — in which case the report's public results page
(`web/`) is the buyer's only reading, `profile-general.jsx`'s door and
the `app-shell.jsx` mount go, and `CurSwitch.tsx` loses one of its two
consumers. Nothing else rides on it. Until it ticks, D368 stands.
