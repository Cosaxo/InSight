# The 2026-09-08 design — the group as a cast, measured against the tree

**Status: plan notes — step 1 is BUILT (2026-09-08, D429): the content
model, the rotation, the card and the fold; steps 2–4 propose, and two
items are the owner's.** The owner's `InSight_12` upload of 2026-09-08,
extracted to [`design/standalone-2026-09-08/`](../design/standalone-2026-09-08/README.md)
(the readable half: what each module draws and where it lands). It
arrived with a ruling, not a brief: having met the world round the tree
had built that afternoon, the owner said *"yeah this is functonality that
should not have been added they should be more like this"* — *this* being
the upload's 1v1 and group — and *"its worst for group as that should
mostly be about what role you have in the group."* D426's third amendment
took the world rounds out; this plan is what the upload puts in their
place. Every item is measured against the tree with its backend half
named (D167) and the constraint that shapes it; §6 is the build order and
§7 the gate per step.

**The finding that makes step 1 small: the tree already had the
mechanism and lacked the content.** A role vote is the group bank's
`pick` kind — the members as options, D224's snapshot of who an index
meant, the rules' fall-through to the member count — which shipped at
D40 and has eight questions. The design adds the *pack* around it (a
scenario, a role, a hue) and one new kind, the rating, whose answer is
still an option index. So the write path, the rules, the trigger and the
reveal are unchanged; what changed is which questions a group is asked,
and what the card and the fold make of them.

## 1 · What the design draws

### 1.1 · A role vote

`duels-data.js`: five `SCENARIOS` (Bank Heist · Desert Island · The
Sitcom · Zombie Plan · Road Trip), each a hue and four roles, each role a
prompt — *Who plans the whole thing?* → *the mastermind*; twenty in all.
`groupQ` deals them in order as `kind: "vote"` with the active members
and *You* as `options` and their ids as `targets`. The card
(`group-daily.jsx`): `RoundKicker` with the pack as a `tag` in
`scenInk(scen)` — `oklch(0.605 0.118 hue)` — the prompt in the serif, an
`OptBtn` per member with their mark as `lead`, and **no second tap**: the
vote seals on the option. The reveal (`VoteReveal`): the named, most
named first, the crown's row bordered in the pack's ink at 60 %, a
*contested* runner-up at 40 % (`contested`: within one vote of the crown
with two or more behind it), each row's count and the marks of who cast
it; the verdict *Leo is the mastermind* or *Leo and Ada share the
mastermind · contested*. The run's mark for a vote: filled in the pack's
ink where the room named you, a ring where it named someone else.

### 1.2 · A rating

`RATE_QS`: ten dims — *This group's energy? Calm ↔ Chaos*, *When we
disagree, we… Talk it out ↔ Loudest wins*, timekeeping, a free evening,
a new person, what goes unsaid, honesty, the group chat, a group
holiday, ten years out. `isRatingRound = (r + phase) % 4 === 0`; the
options are `stepLabel(poles, i)`: *Calm · mostly Calm · in between ·
mostly Chaos · Chaos*. The ballot (`PoleBallot`): the poles as words,
five equal buttons whose dot grows toward either end and hollows at the
middle, sealing on one tap. The reveal (`RateReveal`): the members' marks
stacked on the five stops, the group's dot at the mean on a track, the
leaning pole in bold; the verdict *The group lands on mostly Chaos · 72*
(`score = mean / 4 × 100`). The run's mark: a square.

### 1.3 · The rest of the card, and the 1v1

Everything else on the group card is the rounds card the tree built the
same afternoon (request 12): the lead of five, *N rounds waiting on The
Crew*, the sealed list (now *Bank Heist · you named Leo*), the deadline,
the late answer (*Answered after the reveal. It shows, and counts for
nothing*), the *New group* sheet (*a role a round, revealed with
names*). The 1v1 (`duo-daily.jsx`) is the tree's: the domain-tagged
pools, the SAID · CALLED table, the two runs, the *Question set* toggle,
*End this 1v1? Your history stays on your map* — and world rounds on even
numbers with `WorldCols`, which the owner retired that evening (D426's
third amendment). The design records them; the tree does not build them.

### 1.4 · The Mirror's Groups stop, and the instrument

`group-mirror.jsx`: the identity ring is the share of votes that named
you; an *Overview* draws the cast (`group-role-map.jsx`: the members on a
ring, each earned role a satellite on its holder, a contested role dashed
between two, a tap opening the vote or the person's sheet — *how The Crew
cast you*); lenses *Votes* (*Who the room named*, grouped by pack, each
row opening the vote's counts and who cast them), *People* (*Who's who*,
the likeness placement with twin and contrarian), *Scores* (*How the
group rates itself*: each dim's marks, the group's mean dot, yours, the
leaning pole), *Compare* (the crowns as chips — *hollow = contested* —
and *the room named you N of M votes*). `role-data.js`: the group's four
dims `own` · `pull` · `cast` · `settle` (Independence · Centrality ·
Standing · Steadiness), `cast` = crowned in N of M scenario votes scaled
against `1/targets`, `MIN_GROUP = 2`, and the nine group types the tree
dropped to six for want of `cast` (D204).

## 2 · Measured against the tree — step 1, built

### 2.1 · The role vote is the pick, plus a pack

The tree's `pick` (D40): `options: []` in the bank, the members filled in
by `duelQFor`, `optionIdx` bounded by `duelIndexSpace()`'s fall-through
to `memberUids.size()`, `pickUid` snapshotted by `voteDuel` (D224) and
carried into the reveal. All of that is a role vote already. What the
design adds is **decoration and grouping**: `scen` and `role` on the
question. So a role vote is seeded as `topic: "pick"` with two new
fields, the packs written once in the source (`duel-questions.json`
`scenarios`, resolved by `gen-v2content` so the document carries the
pack whole), and the card reads `q.scen` / `q.role` off the same
`todayQ` / `roundQ` / `bankQ` door as before. No rules change, no
trigger change, no new write shape. Built: twenty questions `gr0`–`gr19`,
`scen`/`role` in `V2SeedQuestion`, the payload, `SEEDED_FIELDS` and
`storedForm` (`check:seed-fields` holds the four equal), `QuestionDoc`
and `DuelRoundQ`.

### 2.2 · The rating is a five-option question with ends

A rating's answer is a step, 0–4. Seeded as `topic: "rate"` with the two
`poles` and `options` = the five step labels, derived at seed time from
the poles (and by the demo layer the same way), it is an ordinary
option-index question to the rules (`options.size()` = 5), to the trigger
and to the reveal; `check:content` holds the shape — exactly two poles,
exactly five steps, poles on nothing else. Built: ten questions
`gs0`–`gs9`. **It takes no call**, as designed: the group about itself is
not a reading of the room, and `LdPoleBallot` seals on the tap
(`voteDuel(gid, idx)` with no `guessIdx`, which the rules already allow).

### 2.3 · The rotation

`duelQFor` (`data/deck.ts`) for a group now walks two pools: on a rating
round (`isRatingRound(r) = r % 4 === 0`) the `rate` pool, at
`round / 4`, so the ten dims come round in turn; otherwise the `pick`
pool, at `round − ⌊round / 4⌋`, so consecutive votes are consecutive
questions. The older `us` / `classic` questions leave the rotation and
stay in the bank (the reveals that name them draw their prompt through
`bankQ`). Two fallbacks, both the D70 drift every bank change has had: a
bank with no ratings yet plays every round as a vote; a bank with no
picks at all plays the whole surface. Pinned in `deck.test.ts`. The 1v1
is untouched.

### 2.4 · The card

`ui/LiveDuelPanel.tsx`: `tagOf(q)` puts the pack on the kicker in
`scenInk`, or *Rate the group*; a pick's options lead with the member's
mark (`optionLead`); `LdPoleBallot` is the design's ballot; on the reveal
a role vote's `LdRevealBars` order by count with the crown's border and a
contested rival, each row led by its member, and the verdict is the cast
— *Ada is the mastermind*, *Ada and Bo share the mastermind ·
contested*, *You are the mastermind* — with the crown read off D224's
snapshots when they agree and the roster's index only when a reveal
predates them; `LdRateReveal` is the design's track with the group's dot
and *The group lands on mostly Chaos · 75*. The run's kinds gain `r` (a
square) and a role vote's `1`/`0` mean *the room named you* / *named
someone else* in the pack's ink. The sealed list's sub says the pack; the
wait block says *you named*. Pinned in `LiveDuelPanel.test.tsx`.

### 2.5 · The fold

`data/roles.ts`: a rating round leaves the group instrument entirely
(`isRatingReveal`, shared with the Mirror's portrait so the stop's
numbers agree with the role's), and **Standing** arrives as an aside —
*the room named you in N of M role votes*, scored against luck like every
other rate (a room of *k* names you by chance one time in *k*), read off
`majorityPickUid`, counted only on a role vote (a pick whose bank entry
casts a role) that two or more answered. An aside rather than a dim
because the tables do not carry it (D386's rule); the registry cases hold
the six types to three dims until step 2.

## 3 · What is measured and proposed

### 3.1 · Step 2 — the tables: Standing as a dim, nine types

`archetype-data.js`'s six group types become the design's nine with
`cast` as a fourth signature dim (the prototype's table is in
`role-data.js`, and ROLES-PLAN §3.5's redesigned table — `own · room ·
named · present`, eight types — is the alternative the owner has not yet
ruled on). What it costs: the type table and its shares, `IS_TEST_AVG`,
`IS_RULE_WORD` / `IS_RULE_ADJ` / `IS_DIM_WORD` for `cast`, a hue, the
matcher's eligibility rule (a type defined by `cast` is out of the
running before three role votes with snapshots — ROLES-PLAN §3.6), and
the registry cases. Zero reads. **Gate**: `test:unit` (the matcher's
three assumptions), `check:public-copy`.

### 3.2 · Step 3 — the Mirror's cast and scores (request 6, designed)

`LiveGroupsMirrorBody` gains the design's *Votes* and *Scores* lenses and
the cast drawn on the People tab, from folds over the stop's own reveals:
`roleVotes` (per role vote: the crown, the runner-up, contested, who cast
whom — `majorityPickUid` and the per-vote snapshots), `groupScores` (per
rating dim: the marks, the mean, the score). The identity ring becomes
the share of votes that named you. `group-role-map.jsx` is an SVG canvas
and goes behind `React.lazy` as `LgField` does. Zero reads beyond the
stop's own. **Gate**: `test:unit` (the folds pinned like `groupPortrait`),
`check:bundle`, `check:a11y`, `check:tap-targets`.

### 3.3 · Step 4 — the lane writes packs and ratings

`QUESTION-FARM.md` § The duel lane says the group array is *interleaved
us/pick/classic*; it is now packs and ratings, and a run that appended
twelve more *us* questions would be writing into a pool nothing serves.
The section is updated in this PR (the kinds, the ids `gr`/`gs`, the
packs table, the rule that a new pack is four roles and a hue); the
Routine's prompt names the section and so needs no edit, but the account
that runs it is not this one — an owner row to confirm the lane's next
run reads the new contract before it fires. **The bundle cap** (§4.3) was
the harder half, and is built (D430).

## 4 · The owner's calls and the recorded limits

### 4.1 · The room's call on a role vote

The design asks no guess on any group round. D386 built *And the room
lands on…?* on the owner's *"yes build the first two steps"*, and
ROLES-PLAN §3.5 makes it the Bellwether's measurement — a Contrarian who
calls the winner and votes against it is a different person from an
Outlier who cannot call it. **As built, the call stays on a role vote and
is absent on a rating.** One tap a round is the cost; the design's
silence on it is the reason to ask. Recorded on `OWNER-LIST.md`.

### 4.2 · The older group questions

Twelve *us* and six untagged group questions predate the cast. Five were
re-asked by the design word for word as ratings (`gu3` → `gs1`, `gu7` →
`gs9`, `gu8` → `gs7`, `gu11` → `gs4`, `gd7` → `gs8`) and are retired in
the source (`active: false`, D52's shape; the dedup gates skip retired
entries, as they do for the feed). The other thirteen stay in the bank
and out of the rotation. Their `active` flag on the live documents is the
operator's — the seed never rewrites it — so retiring them for real, or
folding the eight plain picks into packs, is an owner row.

### 4.3 · The bank's bundle cap

`content/duel-questions.json` was compiled into the demo build whole, and
`check:content` capped it at 24 KiB with the instruction that crossing it
means giving the bank learn's treatment (D284: a generated sample for the
demo, a live read for the app) rather than raising the number. The thirty
questions took it to 25.2; writing the packs once and deriving the step
labels brought it to 22.8. **The daily burst (ROUNDS-PLAN §6.1, 25 a run)
would have crossed it on its first run.** So the learn treatment was not
optional and not step 1's PR — and it is **built (D430, 2026-09-09)**:
`content/duel-sample.json` is what the demo imports now, generated by
`scripts/gen-duel-sample.mjs` as the first few served questions of each
group kind and each 1v1 domain plus the packs those votes name, so an
append never moves it; `check:duel-sample` holds it to its source, and
`BUNDLED_CONTENT` caps the sample instead of the bank. The live read
needed no building — a live build never loaded `duels-data.js`, and
`duelQFor` has read the seeded bank since the rounds landed.
