## 1 · The name

### 1.1 What the design does

`app.jsx` carries three names — `doxa` (*Do·x·a*, `serif: true`),
`endoxa` (*En·doxa*), `insight` (*in·Sight*) — behind a Tweaks radio
labelled *Brand*, remembered in `insight.brand.v2`, default **Doxa**. The
wordmark takes `wm-serif` for the serif name: DM Serif Display at 25 px,
weight 400, the *x* as the `<em>` that carries the tab accent the way
*Sight* did. The boot splash draws *Doxa* in the same face before React
runs, with a four-line script that swaps it if the device remembers
another name. `document.title` and `window.BRAND_NAME` follow the pick,
and `demographics.js` reads it for *Who's on Doxa in Oslo*. Everything
else in the bundle still says *inSight* only where it is invisible
(console prefixes, storage keys).

Two of the names are Greek. δόξα, *doxa*, is opinion — what people
believe, as against what is known — and ἔνδοξα, *endoxa*, is Aristotle's
word for the reputable opinions, what most people or the wise hold. For
an app whose product is what a population's answers say about it, both
are apt; which is the owner's, and so is the search that a name needs
before it goes on a store (Doxa is a common word and an existing brand in
at least one other category; the plan does not check that and says so).

### 1.2 Where the name lives today

Counted 2026-09-12, files carrying *InSight* or *inSight*:

| Where | Files | Reaches a user? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `src/` | 64 | the wordmark in `spec/app-shell.jsx` and the walkthrough copy do; the rest are comments, console prefixes and `insight.*` storage keys, which do not change |
| `web/` | 16 | yes — the marketing home, terms, privacy, the join page |
| `design/store/listing.json` | 1 | yes — the App Store name and subtitle, the Play title, every description |
| `ios/App/App/Info.plist`, `android/…/strings.xml`, `capacitor.config.ts` | 4 | yes — the name under the icon |
| `index.html` | 1 | the tab title |
| `functions/src/` | 8 | push copy where it names the app |
| `docs/` | 45 | no |

What does **not** move with a name: the bundle id `com.cosaxo.insight`
(an identifier, never renamed), the Firebase project, the hosting
domain, the `insight.*` device keys (renaming them is the purge's
problem, D51, for no gain), and the iris mark itself — D302's canvas
specifies the mark and its lockups with the wordmark, so a new name is a
new lockup, not a new mark.

## 2 · Friends

### 2.1 What the design does

`follows.js` is now `insight.friends.v2`: beside `friends` and `invited`
it keeps `requests` (people who asked you) and `dismissed` (suggestions
you waved away). `status(id)` answers `friends · invited · requested ·
none`; `invite` on someone who already asked you is an `accept`;
`ignore` drops a request; `unfriend` drops the person from every list.
The person page's button reads *Add friend* → *Invited · waiting* (with
*they'll see it soon · tap to cancel* under it) → *Friends ✓*, or *Accept
request* when they asked first — and the demo still accepts on your
behalf after a believable delay, which is the prototype's stand-in for
the other person.

### 2.2 What the tree has, and why the handshake is D101 read twice

The tree's people graph is **D101**: `v2_users/{uid}/following/{target}`,
one row that exists or does not, written by its owner, world-readable,
no request, no acceptance, no notification. D101's reasoning is that a
follow is a bookmark — every answer is already public (D98), so
following conveys no access and consent is owed for none — and that a
mutual follow is *a reading, not a state*. That reasoning stands, and it
is exactly what makes the design cheap: every one of its four states is
the two rows read both ways.

| The design's state | On D101's rows |
| --- | --- |
| `none` | neither row |
| `invited` — you asked | your row → them exists; theirs → you does not |
| `requested` — they asked | their row → you exists; yours does not |
| `friends` | both rows |
| `ignored`, `dismissed` | a device mirror (`insight.friendsSet.v1`), swept by the purge (D51, `check:purge`); you cannot delete their row and should not |

So: **no new collection, no rules change, no new consent** — an
*accept* is the follow the tree already writes, and *unfriend* is the
delete it already allows. What the design adds over D101 is three
things, each priced:

- **The other side learns.** *Invited · waiting* and *they'll see it
  soon* are claims, and today a follow tells nobody. A request is
  delivered the way an invitation is (D236: *the pick is the delivery*)
  — one Firestore trigger on `following/{target}` create, sending
  through `sendPushToUids` with *{name} wants to compare answers with
  you*, and on the second row *{name} said yes — you're comparing
  answers now*. Follows are capped at 50 an account (`FOLLOW_CAP`), so
  the trigger's cost is nil; it needs `check:appcheck`'s exemption only
  if it becomes a callable, which it need not.
- **Requests are readable.** *Who follows me* is the collection group
  filtered on `to == me` that `circle.ts`'s `fetchFollowersOf` already
  runs with the `where` the rules demand (D65's lesson). Requests =
  followers − following, one query, session-cached.
- **Unfriend ends the 1v1.** The remove sheet says *any 1v1 you have
  together ends* — on live a 1v1 is a room of two, and `leaveGroup` is
  the call; the sheet makes the claim true by making it. *X isn't told*
  is true today (no push on an unfollow) and stays so.

One thing the tree has to stop doing: `spec/follows.js` on a live build
is an **empty demo store**, and the person page's *Add friend* writes
to it — a friendship that exists on one device and nowhere else. The
Mirror's people rows write the real row (`LIVE.isFollowing`,
`LivePeopleSearch.tsx`, `LiveSimilarityField.tsx`). Step 1 is one
`FRIENDS` surface over D101's rows for both builds, the demo's store
keeping the design's v2 shape so the demo plays what live plays.

### 2.3 The Friends overlay

*Your friends* — *Your circle in the app — the people you compare
answers with.* Four groups and a sheet:

- **Requests** (count) — face, name, the reason line, *Accept*
  (primary, in the person's hue) and a round ✕ (*Ignore request*).
- **Suggested** — up to four, *Show N more* — face, name, reason, *Add*
  and a round ✕ (*Dismiss suggestion*). Sorted: people reached through
  a friend first, then by affinity.
- **Invited** (count) — *waiting for them to accept*, *Cancel*.
- **Friends** (count) — relation · last seen, a ⋯ that opens the remove
  sheet: *Remove Henrik?* · *You'll stop comparing answers, and any 1v1
  you have together ends. Henrik isn't told. You can add them again
  later.* · *Keep* / *Remove*. Empty: *No friends yet — add someone from
  the suggestions above or from their page.*

It mounts in the overlay slot (`LIVE_OVERLAYS` gains `friends`), opens
from four doors (§2.5), and hands a tap on a row to the person page.

### 2.4 The suggestions' basis on live — and the one departure

The demo reasons from two things the live build cannot copy verbatim:

- ***through Henrik*** — the demo matches a person's note against your
  friends' first names. On live the reason is real: D101's graph is
  world-readable, so *the people your friends follow* is one
  `fetchFollowing` per friend (≤ 50, bounded, behind the overlay's own
  open, session-cached) — the join the app exists to draw, on data that
  already publishes. Affinity is the kindred score the City stop already
  folds (`similarity.ts`) where both have test scores; absent, the row
  says nothing rather than a number (D1).
- ***a few streets away*** — **not drawable, and this is the
  departure.** The presence cell is one of the three denies that
  survived D98 (physical safety — the app publishes what people
  answered, not where their phone stands), and Near is presence-only
  since D111. A suggestion from the Near stop's list therefore reads
  *in Oslo now*, the fact Near already shows, never a distance. Not an
  ask under D334: it is one of the four things the ask does not reach.

### 2.5 The doors

- The New-1v1 sheet: *Everyone in your circle already has a 1v1 with
  you.* now ends in *Find more friends →*, and the same link sits under
  the invite list — `ui/LiveDuelPanel.tsx`.
- Search: the *Friends* group header gains *Manage →* —
  `spec/search-overlay.jsx`.
- The profile header: a friends glyph in the right slot, a red dot when
  a request waits, `aria-label` *N friends, M pending* —
  `spec/profile-overlay.jsx`.
- The person page: *Accept request* as the primary state — the button
  the tree already draws, one more branch.

### 2.6 What the design draws that live must not

*some of it stays private until you are friends*, beside *Henrik's
map*, and the map's *N details hidden · friends see everything*. Both
are in the record since 09-09 and the second is in the tree — and both
describe the **demo's** invented locked nodes (`person-mindmap.jsx`
locks 42 % of the non-real nodes by a hash). On live every node is real
and nothing is locked: answers are public (D98) and a follow grants no
access (D101). The tree does not draw the first line and should not
port it; the second draws nothing on live because `lockedN` is zero.
Under D334 the choice is the owner's to make, not a routine's to
default: §6 Q3 asks it once, with the recommendation that the line ships
nowhere.

## 3 · The Map's trail and Find

The Mirror's You stop opens the Map — every answer you have given,
orbiting a ring of anchors (`spec/map-tab.jsx`, 64 KB, lazy since D207).
The tree has fit, replay, recenter and the time scrub; the design adds a
way to find your way, and every piece of it reads data the map already
holds. Nothing new is fetched.

- **The trail** — the chip row's *All* becomes a pill that says where
  you are: *You*, or *‹ You › Everyday* inside a group, the first crumb
  the way back; the active crumb wears the group's hue; the row scrolls
  the active chip to centre (`map-chips.jsx` → `spec/map-chiprow.jsx`).
- **Find** — a 36 px glass at the row's end turns the rail into a
  field: *Find an answer…*, ✕ to clear, and two toggles, **This week**
  (`age ≤ 7`) and **Rare takes** (the minority answers the map already
  marks hollow). Matches light on the map (`is-hit`: a double ring in
  the hue) and the card lists them (`map-find.jsx`'s `MTFindCard`: *12
  matches lit on the map*, prompt · when · your answer as a chip, the
  first forty, *Showing the first 40.*; empty, *Nothing on the map
  matches that yet.*; idle, *Type a word from a question or an answer,
  or filter to this week's and your rarer takes. 214 answers on the
  map.*). A tap on a row selects that answer.
- **The card you resize** — `MTCardGrab`: a grab bar, three modes
  (peek 96 px · half · full 86 %), a drag of 28 px steps between them,
  a tap toggles half and full.
- **Prev / next** — `MTCardNav`: *‹ 3 of 7 · Everyday ›* through the
  siblings of the selected answer, disabled at the ends; a horizontal
  swipe on the card does the same.
- **Two coach hints**, once each — *Pinch to zoom · tap a branch to open
  it* on first arrival, *‹ You in the trail steps back out* the first
  time a group opens; nine seconds or a tap, remembered in
  `insight.mapHints.v1` (a device key, so `check:purge`'s listener rule
  applies).
- **The legend** on the root card — *near the centre — with the crowd ·
  far out — a rarer take · halo — answered this week · hollow — a
  minority answer* — four dots drawn as the map draws them.
- **Hub badges** — a small count on a branch hub when the layout gives
  it one (`c.badge`), and `is-wait` on a hub with an unanswered
  question.
- **The exit** — `MapTab` takes `onExit`, and the Mirror hands it
  `goNav(backTo)` so the Map's back lands where you came from.

Where it lands: the trail, Find and the results card in
`spec/map-chiprow.jsx` and a new `spec/map-find.jsx`; the grab, the nav,
the legend and the hint keys in `spec/map-tab.jsx` and
`spec/map-bottom-card.jsx`; the 36 classes into `styles.css`'s map
section. Costs: none on the wire; the Map chunk grows by a few KB
(`check:bundle` counts the eager graph, and the Map is not in it); the
field is a real `<input type=search>` (`check:touch-zoom`'s 16 px floor
applies to it); the grab is `role=button` with a label
(`check:a11y`); every new control is 36 px with a pseudo-element to 44
(`check:tap-targets`). The measured-body mount suite (`CLAUDE.md`'s
list) is where a crash would show.
