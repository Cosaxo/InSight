# The 2026-09-02 design — what moved, and what it costs to build

**Status: built, except the half that waits on an owner decision.**
§1–§4.1 shipped 2026-09-02 in six commits, each section carrying its
as-built note — including what the build proved this file had wrong.
§4.2 (the catalog window) still waits on VISION-2026-08-26 §2.2, which
is the owner's sentence, and nothing of it is in the tree. Every item
was checked against the files it landed in, with the constraint that
shapes it and its backend half named (D167); §7 is the order it was
built in and §8 the gate each step passed. The two owner decisions
VISION-2026-08-26 left open (§1 anonymous answers, §2.2 the seat split)
are still open and this design touched neither.

The source is the owner's `InSight_7.html` upload of 2026-09-02 —
seventh in the numbered series after `InSight_4` (2026-08-26; five and
six were never uploaded here) — extracted to
[`design/standalone-2026-09-02/`](../design/standalone-2026-09-02/).
That directory's README is the inventory (every changed file, the
patches, the unchanged list, and the one thing this upload does
differently: it ships compiled JSX, not source); this file is the plan.
`docs/VISUAL-VISION.md` names this design as the current vision.

## What was measured

**Everything below was checked against the tree, not assumed.** Against
the latest recorded design state (v18 + v28 patches + the six later
extractions + the two READMEs' recorded hunks), **94 of 114 modules are
byte-identical** and ten more differ only by hunks the record already
carries in prose. What actually moved is **ten modules, three
stylesheets, one new stylesheet and one new font**, and they read as
four moves:

1. **The three Patterns lenses become one instrument** — a round dusk
   field in a light card, a title and one plain sentence above it, a
   legend in words below (`lens.css`, new). The Map is redrawn as a
   **ring** (questions on a rim by topic, ties as chords), the Oracle
   moves into the field (the two options are its two halves), the
   People lens colours every dot by **agreement** in three steps. The
   shell gains a meta line, a topic select, and a swipe axis that runs
   off the far end into the daily.
2. **The questions get a voice**: Spectral, a serif, for every prompt
   a person answers — the daily's world card, the feed's snap and focus
   prompts, the Oracle's question, the Map's tie sentence, the
   Crossroads ending — while UI, figures and captions stay sans.
3. **The ballot splits**: a two-option vote is one block divided by a
   hairline seam, on the daily and on every feed vote card; the seam
   moves to the crowd's split when you vote.
4. **Smaller passes**: topic-hued answer rows in the Mirror's Answers
   lens, a quieter Crossroads tree, the paid door's rate rows redrawn
   with the pricing law as scannable tokens, the catalog window's
   polish, and the tab bar's buttons losing weight.

None of it needs a new read, a rules change, a new collection or a
schema. Three things need care: the **Map ring says less than the plane
it replaces** (position stops meaning similarity — §1.2), a **second
font family** is first-paint bytes the JS ceiling does not see (§2.1),
and **`VISUAL-REQUESTS.md` request 1** was written against the plane the
ring retires (§1.2).

## 1 · The Patterns tab: one instrument for three lenses

### 1.1 · The field, and the accent question it settles

`lens.css` is the whole idea in 60 lines: `.ln-card` (light), `.ln-head`
with `.ln-title` (15/800) and `.ln-sub` (12/500, one sentence),
`.ln-field` — `aspect-ratio: 1`, a radial dusk gradient at hue 282 —
and a token set every lens draws from (`--ln-ink`, `--ln-sub`,
`--ln-line`, `--ln-hub`, `--ln-ring`, `--ln-halo`, `--ln-beacon`,
`--ln-me`), then `.ln-key` (swatches + words), `.ln-hint` (one accent
line: *Tap a dot to light up only its links*) and the `.ln-rail` /
`.ln-chip` chip rail. `.app.lens-paper` swaps the field to paper; **no
control in the upload sets that class** — it is a hook, and the port
carries the tokens without adding a setting (a setting is a control, and
nobody has asked for it).

Measured against the tree: `ui/patterns.css` (258 lines) is the 08-20
sheet ported verbatim plus the D310 additions, and its header keeps
eleven selectors that style nothing *"so the ports that will fill the
lenses out can diff against it"*. This is that port. The lens rules land
in `patterns.css` (or a sibling `ui/lens.css` imported by
`PatternsTab.tsx` — either way the lazy patterns chunk, never the
first-paint sheet), and the pass that lands them **finishes the
verbatim question the header leaves open**: delete the eleven dead
selectors and the paragraph that annotates them, since the three lenses
now draw the new grammar.

**The accent.** D310 §4.5 deleted the Patterns tab's dusk override so the
tab wore the app's sienna; this upload's `page.css` carries the override
again. Nothing needs to move: the field's palette is written in absolute
hue-282 tokens, so it is dusk whatever the tab accent is, and the
`--accent`-reading chrome around it (the ruler, the meta line, the
`.ln-hint`) stays sienna as the tree has it. Judge at a screen; if the
sienna hint over a dusk field reads wrong, the fix is the hint's colour
token, not the tab override.

### 1.2 · The Map as a ring — and what the ring stops saying

The redrawn `question-map.jsx`: every question is a dot on a rim of
radius 131 in a 352 box, grouped by topic in `WORLD_TOPICS` order with
a 2.6-step gap between groups; a 3px arc outside the rim names each
topic in its hue, labelled along the arc when the group is long enough
to carry the word; a tie is a **chord** bundled 20% toward the hub
(`Q` curve), dashed when the answers go opposite ways, thicker when
stronger; at rest the strongest ten speak (`FIG_N`) and the rest
whisper at 0.13 opacity; the strongest link is written **on the field**
as a pill at the chord's 0.26 point (*pick ↔ then · 78%*); the hub
prints *N of M · ANSWERED*; the *Answer next →* beacon pulses on the
rim with its label inside it. Tap a dot: the field dims to its three
ties (`nearOf`) and the card below says each out loud. The idle card
becomes one sentence — *People who pick **X** on "prompt" mostly go on
to pick **Y** on "prompt"* — with the percentage as a 26px figure and
*counted over everyone who answered both · tap to open* under it.

Measured against `ui/PatternsMap.tsx` (335 lines) and
`data/patternsMap.ts`: the drawing is a pure re-layout. `edgesOf(U, 3)`
gives the chords, `nearOf` the tapped web, `PATTERNS.say` the sentences
— all unchanged, all bounded the same way (fetched for the links on
screen, rows shared per session). The ring layout depends only on the
pool's identity and topic order (memoised on `Q.length` and `Q[0]`), so
it never re-runs on a vote. **`planeOf` loses its only consumer**
(`PatternsMap.tsx:70`); keep it exported for `patternsMap.test.ts` or
delete both — decide in the PR, and say which in the commit.

**What the ring stops saying, and the sentences that have to change.**
On the plane, position was a claim: *close together = answers that
predict each other*. On the ring, position is topic membership and
nothing else; every claim about two questions is a chord. That is the
owner's trade — a legible figure over a hairball — and it is theirs to
make (D352: the vision is what the tree is built toward). What the port
owes is honesty about it:

- The D310 basis line *"Close together = answers that predict each
  other · drawn from the crowd's latest answers"* is **false on a ring**
  and goes; the design's `.ln-sub` replaces it — *"Every question is a
  dot. A line joins two questions when how people answer one predicts
  how they answer the other. Thicker line = stronger link."* — and the
  tie card keeps *"counted over everyone who answered both"*, which was
  always the true sentence.
- The card's live copy must keep D310's bounded-sample wording where the
  prototype says "everyone": the live 2×2 is over *the people in both
  samples*, not everyone who answered both. Port the sentence with the
  live basis, as D310 did.
- **`VISUAL-REQUESTS.md` request 1** — trait-axis directions *"drawn in
  that plane"* — was written against the plane. On a ring an axis is a
  band on the rim or a family of chords, not a direction. The request
  goes back to `requested` with a note (this PR adds it) and gets its
  plan against the ring, not the plane.

The hub is not a tap target in the design and gains none in the port;
the *N from feed votes* sub-row count is still D287's refusal.

### 1.3 · The Oracle in the field

`oracle.jsx` keeps its engine and moves its instrument. The 280 box has
a dashed seam down the middle; the two options are the two halves
(`or2-half`, labelled in small caps with *tap to pick* under them); the
sealed guess is a disc on the seam whose radius is confidence (15–26)
and whose ink is evidence (`discOp = 0.35 + 0.65 × sol`), under *SEALED
GUESS* and one word — `orSure`: *sure / fairly sure / leaning /
guessing*, or *nothing to go on* when the mass is under 0.15. On your
tap the disc springs to the called half (`.or2-disc`, one transform)
and that half **fills from the bottom to its confidence**
(`orFillPath`, a half-disc clipped at the water line); on landing the
disc is solid (it had you) or a broken ring (you broke it), and the
verdict is **said in words** under the field: *It called X, fairly sure.
You said Y. **You broke it.** A broken ring means you surprised it.* A
*you* tag sits on your half. *Why? →* opens the working exactly as
D310 built it; *Next →* is a pill in the card head beside a *Question
N* kicker.

Measured against `ui/PatternsOracle.tsx` (433 lines): the seal, the
grade and `PATTERNS.working` are the same calls in the same order, so
**the pin in `data/patterns.test.ts` must not move** — if a diff touches
it, the diff is wrong (the same rule VISION-2026-08-26 §3 wrote). Three
deletions: the one-time hints (`OR_HINT`, `insight.oracle.hints.v1` and
its read/write) — the card's standing sentence replaces what they
taught; the `.or-cap` key under the ledger — the record's kicker now
says *up = you broke it, tick = it had you*; and the *Start over*
footer, which the tree never ported. The hint key leaves the purge
sweep (`check:purge`) with its reader. The prototype has no fallback for a question with more than two options
and needs none: `pool()` skips anything that is not two-option
(`patterns.ts`, `q.options.length !== 2`), because the engine encodes an
answer as ±1 and the guess as P(option 0) — a third option has no
representation anywhere in it. **As built:** a fallback screen was
written, found unreachable, and deleted; the two halves read
`options.slice(0, 2)` with that reasoning at the draw site, so a reader
who ever loosens that filter lands on it. The two copy corrections D310 forced stay forced: the
no-evidence line is *"the call is the crowd's own lean, and the faint
ink says so"* (the design says *faint disc*, which is now true), and
thin evidence keeps its own state.

**Tap targets.** The halves are half-discs (fine); *Next →* is a
6px-padded pill and the ledger marks are small — `check:tap-targets`
wants 44px or `.tap44` on every button drawn smaller. The existing lens
already names its ledger marks; do the same for the pill.

### 1.4 · The People lens: colour says one thing

`people-lens.jsx` puts the crowd in the same disc. Every dot is coloured
in **three steps of agreement** over the answers you share — mostly
agrees (> 0.6, hue 282), split, mostly disagrees (< 0.4, hue 20) — and
sized in **two steps** (bigger = more answers in common; under 4
shared, not drawn — the standing floor). The plane is framed by the
farthest person into a 150 radius, overlaps eased over 60 passes,
everyone clamped inside the rim; the nearest five to you are named
(least-crowded of four spots, scored rather than first-fit) and repeated
under the field as a **"Most like you"** chip rail — avatar initial,
name, *agrees 9 of 12*; a tap on a chip selects the person. *you* is a
white dot in a beacon ring. The legend says the three colours and the
size rule in words; the card's foot states the floor: *everyone who
answered at least 4 of your N questions. More appear as the crowd
answers.*

Measured against `ui/PatternsPeople.tsx` (304 lines) and
`data/peopleMap.ts`: `agree` and `shared` per placed person are the
figures the lens already states on its card and aria-labels, so the
colour step is a fold with no new data, and the rail is the five
nearest placed people by the solve's own distance — the same ordering
the prototype uses. D310's rescope stands: the sentence is *"closer
means answering more alike"* (the ridge solve's truth), never the
prototype's agreement-layout claim; the design's own `.ln-sub` — *"The
closer two dots, the more alike their answers"* — is the same sentence
and ships as written. Nameless accounts stay *Someone* (the live lens's
rule) rather than the prototype's invented `NAMES`. Populations (D216)
are untouched: the chips row stays the People sub-row.

### 1.5 · The shell: the meta line, the topic select, the swipe axis

Three moves in `patterns-tab.jsx`, all on `ui/PatternsTab.tsx` (224
lines):

1. **The map's sub-row becomes a meta line** (`.pt-meta`): *● N
   answered · ○ N open · N ties* on the left (the pool, your answers
   in it, and `edgesOf`'s count — figures the tab already holds) and
   one **topic control**
   on the right — a native `<select>` dressed as a pill (`.pt-topic`,
   30px tall, the whole label a `<label>`), *All topics* by default. It
   replaces the scrolling topic chips. A native select is the right
   control on a phone and the wrong height for `check:tap-targets`;
   grow its box to 44 or wrap it in `.tap44`.
2. **The lens body drags on the horizontal axis**: touch-drag (9px
   dead zone, 1.4:1 horizontal test, 56px commit) or a horizontal wheel
   (24px, 620ms lock) slides the stack 34px toward the next ruler stop
   and swaps the lens; **past the far end the axis continues into the
   daily** (`goNav('track:world')`); the first stop springs back; a
   drag that starts in an SVG, a canvas, a scroller, a rail or a field
   belongs to that surface — *except the Oracle's*, whose disc and
   ledger are tap-only and hand the drag to the axis (`.or-lens`).
   Measured against the tree: `NAV.goNav(key)` returns whether it
   navigated and `spec/daily-split.jsx` already springs back on a
   refusal — this is D166's one licensed joint, used from the other
   side. Port the exit through `NAV`, never through a window global
   (`check:globals` rule 4 must not move), and pin both ends: the
   far-end swipe calls `goNav` once, the first-stop swipe calls nothing.
3. **The ruler's labels lose their size jump** — 13.5 always, 700 on,
   500 off (the daily's ruler gets the same in `daily-split.jsx.patch`).
   Reduced-motion collapses the slide as D310's `pt-slide-*` already
   does.

### 1.6 · What the port must prove — and what it proved

**As built.** All four suites exist and hold; `data/patterns.test.ts` is
byte-identical, so the seal's pin never moved. Five things the build
settled that this plan had left open or had wrong:

1. **`planeOf` is deleted**, with `MapPoint`, `PATTERNS_MIN_GAP` and its
   two tests — §1.2 left the choice to the PR. A 110-line layout engine
   kept alive only by its own tests is residue, and git history has it.
2. **The Oracle's option labels are uppercased by CSS, not by
   `String.toUpperCase`** — the design does the latter, which mangles the
   accessible name and some locales' casing. Same picture, real words
   underneath.
3. **The People rail's chips are buttons wrapped in `listitem` spans.**
   The prototype puts `role="listitem"` on the button itself, which is an
   interactive element assigned a non-interactive role — `check:a11y`
   catches it, and it is a real reading bug rather than a lint opinion.
4. **The rail lists only NAMED people** (the five the field labels). The
   design chips every one of the five nearest with an initial avatar; live
   that would dress a nameless account in an invented identity, which
   D167 refuses and the labels already refused.
5. **The Oracle now writes no device state at all** — the retired hints
   took `insight.oracle.hints.v1` with them, so `check:purge`'s derived
   subject set simply stops naming this file. Pinned, because a key
   coming back silently is exactly what that gate cannot see.

The original list, unchanged:


- `PatternsMap.test.tsx`: every pool question gets a rim point; topic

  for an `edgesOf` edge; the callout names the strongest tie under the

- `PatternsOracle.test.tsx`: the seal pin unchanged (`patterns.test.ts`
  untouched); the sealed disc is identical whichever half is called

  verdict sentence matches the grade; three options render buttons.
- `PatternsPeople.test.tsx`: the colour step follows `agree / shared` at
  0.4 / 0.6; the rail is the five nearest placed; a nameless person is

- `PatternsTab.test.tsx`: the meta line's three counts; the far-end

- The smoke suites that walk the patterns tab still pass with the

## 2 · The prompt voice and the split ballot

### 2.1 · Spectral — a second face, for the questions only

**As built, and the plan understated the constraint.** §0 said
"check:bundle counts JS only, so measure the woff2 cost by hand" — the
gate has had a FONT ceiling since D223 (96 KB, against 64 in the tree),
so the measurement was a gate rather than a note. Two weights × two
subsets would have been ~87 KB of new bytes and failed it. **One face
ships: Spectral 500, latin** (86 KB total measured). What that costs is
in `styles.css` beside the face: a Central-European letter in a prompt
falls back to Georgia for that glyph, and the one place the design asks
for 600 (the Crossroads ending) is drawn at 500 rather than letting the
browser synthesise a bold serif. The `.sg-rise`/`.sg-tick` keyframes
this section grouped here moved to §4.1, which lands their consumer:
CSS nothing draws is what §1.1 had just finished paying off.


`tokens.css` adds `--serif: 'Spectral', Georgia, 'Times New Roman',
serif` with the rule in the line: *"the prompt voice — the one place
the app speaks in a second face. Every question people answer is set in
it; UI, figures and captions stay sans."* The upload embeds Spectral
400 / 500 / 600 in five subsets (15 faces) and uses **500** almost
everywhere: the daily's world prompt (31/27 · 500, was the display sans
at 37/31 · 800), the feed's snap and focus prompts (500, balance-wrapped;
the row prompt stays sans at 15/600), the Oracle's question (21/500),
the Map's tie card prompt (21/500) and the quoted prompts inside its
sentence, and the Crossroads ending name (24/**600**).

Measured against the tree: `src/v2/styles.css` declares Hanken Grotesk
from four files under `public/fonts/` and has no `--serif`. The JS
ceilings in `check:bundle` do not see a font, so the cost is first
paint, measured by hand: ship **500 and 600, latin + latin-ext** (the
bank is English with Norwegian names — æøå live in latin-ext), four
files, `font-display: swap` so the prompt paints in the fallback and
swaps, ~80 KB by the upload's own file sizes. Do not ship 400, cyrillic
or vietnamese — nothing in the bank draws them. Add the token to
`:root`, the faces beside the Hanken ones, and set each of the five
sites from the token, never from the family name. The fallback stack
matters on a device before the swap: Georgia is on iOS; Android's serif
is what `serif` resolves to.

### 2.2 · The split ballot

The two-option vote becomes **one block divided by a hairline seam**
(`display: grid; grid-template-columns: 1fr 1fr; gap: 2; background:
var(--rule)`): two sides, each a `press` button on `--surface-2` with a
dot in the topic hue and its label (column layout, left-aligned; the
daily's 88 tall, the feed's 84/68); three or more options **stack as
rows in the same block**. On vote the same block draws the result:
two sides whose **width is share** (the seam moves to the crowd's
split; 128 tall on the daily, 112/92 on the feed), or the stacked tiles
whose height is share as before. *· you* becomes an uppercase **you**
pill (10.5/800, ink on paper). The feed's kicker also gains the card's
form in words when no sponsor mark occupies it (*this or that · rank ·
rate · dial · place it · learn · pick one · predict · read the room*),
in small caps.

Measured against the tree: both surfaces are spec files —
`spec/daily-split.jsx` (`castVote`'s option row at line 982 wears
`press sd-opt`; the reveal block; the ruler) and `spec/world-feed.jsx`
(`renderVote` at 1933, `renderVoteTiles` at 1964, the kicker, the
prompt block). Both repo copies are converted-import files, so the port
is an adaptation of the patch, not a paste, and the live cards use the
same renderers (`q.live` changes what is drawn *around* the vote, not
the vote). `.sd-opt` leaves the daily's buttons, so the `.sd-opt:hover`
rule in `styles.css:652` styles nothing afterwards — delete it (the
first-paint sheet deletes residue; only the lazy sheet annotates it).
D86's edit path (long-press your own bar to change a vote) is inside the
reveal block and must survive the re-layout; the mount suites walk both
tabs and both daily modes, so a ballot that crashes shows up as the
`ErrorBoundary`.

### 2.3 · The tab bar, and two keyframes

`.tab-btn` goes 13px / **500**, active **700**, and the active pill
loses its inset ring (the tree has 600 / 750 + the ring at
`styles.css:496–516`). `.sg-rise` (entrance rise, 0.44s) and `.sg-tick`
(the day-strip tick pop, 0.5s spring) join the first-paint sheet with
their `prefers-reduced-motion` guard, because the door that uses them
(§4.1) is on the first-paint path. The `--ink-3` line in the same hunk
is the 08-24 record and is already in the tree.

## 4 · The paid family: the door's rate rows, and the window that still waits
