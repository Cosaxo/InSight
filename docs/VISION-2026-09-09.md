## 1 · The brief, and what each principle costs

### 1.1 Everything is played in rounds

*"Either side may run ahead (`LEAD`); the card says what is waiting,
never what is forbidden."* Built: D426's rounds, `ROUND_LEAD = 5` in
`functions/src/pure.ts` and the rules, the sealed list, the *answer
ahead* button (request 12). No change.

### 1.2 Nothing in a group is predicted or called

*"The room casts roles and rates itself; that's all."* The tree keeps
one call — D386's guess on a role vote — because the owner approved it
and it was an owner row. The brief is the answer to that row (§6 Q3);
the plan reads it as *drop* and asks once.

### 1.3 Titles are for result cards only

*"In play, every prompt and answer is a plain sentence a person would
say."* The design keeps the pack label on the kicker (*Bank Heist*) and
the role in the verdict (*Leo is the mastermind*); what it forbids is a
seat's label in play — `SEATS[i].line` (*the one who gets things
going*), never `label` (*the Engine*), which appears on the result card
alone. The voice rule that follows — *votes, rounds, said; never days,
majority, crowned, cast as a badge, in charge* — is what `COPY.md` §3
would call claims, and it is checkable: `check:public-copy` is a
closed-vocabulary gate over user-facing files, and a second word list
scoped to the duel surfaces is its shape exactly. What the sweep finds
today: *days* throughout `ui/LiveRolesPanel.tsx` and
`ui/LiveGroupsMirrorBody.tsx` (*3 of 3 days both guessed*, *shared
days*, *aligned with you · N of M days*), *You keep the days you played*
on the leave sheet, and the `crown` vocabulary in code comments (not
copy). One thing the design itself trips: the Zombie Plan's leader is
asked *Who ends up in charge?* — §6 Q8.

### 1.4 Stored answers carry the question they answered

*"`dState`/`gState` return null when the question moved, so the round
re-opens."* The tree's answers carry `qid` (D426), and the reveal names
the question by plurality (`revealQid`). The demo can re-open a round
because its state is a local map; a live answer is a Firestore document
that is create-only (D86 — duels are frozen), so re-opening would mean
a second answer to the same round, which the rules refuse by id
(`g_{gid}_r{n}`). The tree's shape keeps the principle's point — nobody
is counted against a question they did not see — by counting only the
answers whose `qid` is the plurality's. Recorded as a departure, and
confirmed by Q7.

## 2 · The 1v1

### 2.1 Round kinds

`duoKind(r)`: `r % 4 === 0` → cast, `r % 2 === 0` → World, else own.
Own rounds walk the pool by depth as the tree does (`duelQFor`'s duo
branch). **The World round is the conflict**: the tree built it 09-08
afternoon (D426's second amendment) and took it out the same evening on
the owner's word — *"this can go that is stufff you already find on the
world feed so is totaly pointless"* — with the rules' second arm, the
pool, the no-guess signal and the third column. The design carries it,
in a 1v1 only and with the no-guess rule (`publicAnswer`), and the
brief's first bullet lists it as current. §6 Q1. If it returns, the
build is the reverse of commit 335871e's 1v1 half, with one difference:
the design's `worldQ` draws from the DAILY pool, the tree's version drew
from the feed's core — the plan would draw from both, as ROUNDS-PLAN
§6.2 priced.

### 2.2 The cast round

Every fourth round: *Most days, {name} is…* with four answers from
`CAST_SETS[mode]`, each `{ id, dim, label, them }`. You answer about
them; then you guess **what they said about you**, over the *them*
forms (*the one Liv tells first*). The reveal's lead line: *You are the
one Liv tells first. Liv is the one you ask what to do.* `castOf(pid)`
folds every cast round: `youAre` (the answer they gave most often, the
latest on a tie), `theyAre`, `sawIt` (your guesses at their cast that
landed), and the tallies the instrument reads.

**Where the question lives on live.** A duel answer must reference an
active question on its own surface (`firestore.rules`, `isDuelAnswer`),
so the cast is a bank entry, not a client-side template: one `kind:
"cast"` entry in `oneVsOne` and one in `romantic`, `prompt` carrying the
`{name}` placeholder, `options` the four labels, and two new transported
fields — `them` (the four *them* forms) and `dims` (the four axes) —
through `gen-v2content.mjs`, the payload whitelist, `SEEDED_FIELDS` and
the seed test (`check:seed-fields`). `duelQFor` deals it on every fourth
round the way it deals a rating, and the option arithmetic is the
bank's, so the rules need no change. The prompt's placeholder is
substituted where the card and the person page render it; the two
places that print a duo prompt cold — the Mirror's answer rows and the
sealed list — substitute too, or the bank's prompt reads as written.

### 2.3 Reveal, runs, doors

The 1v1 reveals the moment both are in (built). *"No clock"* is the
brief's word, and the tree has one: the owner's 2026-09-08 rule made a
1v1 close at the deadline for one player too, because a partner who
stopped playing used to seal the other's answer forever. §6 Q2 asks
which stands. The two runs at the foot (how well you read them, how
well they read you) are the tree's `LdRun` rows; a cast round marks
`sawIt` on yours and `byRight` on theirs, and a no-guess World round
marks `n`. *End this 1v1? Your history stays on your map.* replaces
*You keep the days you played* (§1.3).

## 3 · The group

### 3.1 Two kinds of round, no call

Three role votes then a rating — built (D432). The call goes (§1.2,
Q3): the guess step on the group card, the `room` aside in
`data/roles.ts`, the Bellwether's argument in ROLES-PLAN §3.5, and the
`guessIdx` the group surface's answers carry today (the rules may keep
admitting the field — refusing it is one clause, and the plan prefers
the clause, so *no call* is a rule and not a client's manners).

### 3.2 Seats

`SEATS`: engine (*the one who gets things going*) · hands (*the one who
gets it done*) · heart (*the one who holds the room together*) · wild
(*the one the story happens to*). Every role carries one, and each pack
has one role per seat — which is why two roles changed since 09-08:
Zombie Plan's *the supply hoarder* (`gr13`) became *the medic — Who
patches everyone up?* (heart), and Road Trip's *the snack captain*
(`gr19`) became *the hourly stop — Who needs to pull over every hour?*
(wild). Answers key on (qid, optionIdx) forever (D30, D52), so a role
change is a retirement and a new entry, never an edit: `gr13` and `gr19`
retired, `gr20` and `gr21` seeded, `role.seat` on all twenty, the seed
transporting it, `check:content` holding every pack to exactly one role
per seat, and the duel lane's contract saying a pack is four roles, one
a seat, and a hue. The cast has not reached the live bank yet (the
reseed is an owner click), so nothing live is remapped. §6 Q4 asks
whether the two swaps are wanted or only the seats.

`archetypeOf(gid, id)`: the votes a member received, summed by the seat
of the role they were cast in; the seat with the most, ties broken by
roles won; `pct` per seat. In play the seat is its line; on the roles
panel it is a type.

### 3.3 The ballot, the rotation, the phase

The ballot is a 2-across grid of faces — `q.targets` ∥ `q.options`, You
last — replacing D432's rows with a lead; `PoleBallot` for a rating is
the tree's `LdPoleBallot`. `isRatingRound(gid, r) = (r + phase) % 4 ===
0` with a phase per group, so the rating rounds of two groups you are
in do not fall on the same numbers; the tree's `isRatingRound(round)`
has no phase, and the plan gives it one from the group id's hash —
cheap, and free today because no live group has played a cast round.

### 3.4 The run as a record

One mark per round: a dot in the pack's ink for a vote, a square for a
rating, dotted (`l`) where you were late, sealed and open as today.
**Not a personal score** — D432's filled-or-ring encoding (named you /
named someone else) goes — and a tap on a mark prints its caption:
*Round 7 · Bank Heist · Leo is the mastermind*, *Round 8 · Rate the
group · mostly Chaos · 72*, *· you were late*. `LdRun` already takes
`onPick`; the marks and the caption are the change.

### 3.5 The reveal and the deadline

*Reveals when everyone has played · 1d 6h at the latest*; the reveal
block says *· closed at the deadline* when seats were open, and a late
answer reads *Answered after the reveal. It shows, and counts for
nothing.* — D426's late arm, in the design's words. The design's
`DEADLINE` is 48 hours; the tree's `ROUND_DEADLINE_MS` is 24, from the
open round's first answer. §6 Q2.

### 3.6 The first run

The group's preview draws a role vote: the pack kicker, a 2×2 grid of
You and open seats, *A role a round. The ballot is whoever is in; the
room names one of you.*, and *Who the room names, round by round* over
the empty run. The tree's `LdFirstRun` still draws *A World question
stands in — a group draws its own* — a D426 leftover, and a build item
whatever the answers.

### 3.7 The older questions

The design's bank is packs and ratings alone (`GROUP_QS` is in the
brief's removed list). The tree keeps thirteen older group questions in
the bank out of the rotation and eight plain picks in it (`OWNER-LIST`'s
row); this plan adds the design's answer to that row — retire all
twenty-one — and does not act on it.

## 5 · The instrument — one idea, two settings

### 5.1 The dims

A 1v1's four dims are the share of cast rounds in which they named you
each axis — Trust (*told first*), Spark (*starts it*), Judgement
(*consulted*), Constancy (*always there*) — from **one cast**
(`MIN_DUO = 1`). A group's four are the share of your received votes per
seat — Engine · Hands · Heart · Wildcard — from **two votes**
(`MIN_GROUP = 2`). Baseline 25 a dim (`AVG`), and a share is not scored
against luck: four options at 25 each is the chance line already.

### 5.2 The types

Ten 1v1 types (The Confidant, The Instigator, The Compass, The Constant;
The Ride-or-Die, The Co-conspirator, The Sounding Board, The Rock, The
Ringleader; The Everything) and eleven group types (The Engine, The
Hands, The Heart, The Wildcard; The Doer, The Organiser, The Gamble, The
Host, The Plot Twist, The Scout; The Ensemble), pure seats at 76/8/8/8
and blends at 42/42 — written in `archetype-data.js`'s shape, shares
summing to 100, every type extreme on a dim, so `roles.test.ts`'s
registry cases hold as they are.

### 5.3 What it replaces, and what moves

D204's two instruments and D386's scoring go, with the `project`,
`mirror`, `mirrorBy`, `room` and `cast` asides and the six group types;
ROLES-PLAN §3.4–§3.5's proposed tables are answered by these rather
than adopted, and its §3.6 name rule (a runner-up when the gap is
small, hysteresis) still applies over the new dims. The read/seen
accuracy is not lost: it moves to the person page (§5.4). The floors
are the plan's one real question about the instrument — §6 Q5: one
cast makes a person a pure type at 100 on one axis, and a claim said
to a face from a single round is what `COPY.md` §3 and D1 call a
promise the data does not keep.

### 5.4 The person page's Together tab

Match · Answers · Together · Map. Together: *What you are to each
other* (`castOf` — the lead line, *Liv said so in 2 of 3 rounds asked ·
you guessed it 2 of 3*), *How well you read each other* (two rings,
the reader line — *You read Liv better than Liv reads you, except on
under pressure* — the per-domain rows), the groups you share with each
person's seat, and the doors. The Answers panel beside it is new since
the 09-07 record too: the questions you both answered as a 7-across
grid of dots, filled where you agree, one selected opening the pair.
The roles panel's explain sheet gets the new copy (`explain-sheet.jsx`).
