**Status: partly built.** The visual scope shipped at **D273** — the
Patterns polish (§5), the small pass (§6) and the "so what" line (§4);
each section carries its as-built state, including what the build proved
this file had wrong. The rest keeps the
[`VISION-V28.md`](VISION-V28.md) convention: an item picked up graduates
to a real record in [`DECISIONS.md`](DECISIONS.md), and until then a
*build* verdict here is this document's recommendation, not approval.
Two items still need an owner decision before any code; they are named
in §0 and nowhere assumed.

The source is the owner's `InSight_3.html` upload of 2026-08-24 — third
in the numbered series after `InSight_1` (2026-08-20, the Patterns
redesign) and `InSight_2` (2026-08-22, the paid report) — extracted to
[`design/standalone-2026-08-24/`](../design/standalone-2026-08-24/).
That directory's README is the inventory (every changed file, the
micro-hunks verbatim, the unchanged list); this file is the plan.

## What was measured

**Everything below was checked against the tree, not assumed** — the
same exercise VISION-V28 §"What was measured" did, with the same result:
the diff is much louder than the delta. Against the latest recorded
design state (v18 + v28 patches + the v24/v25/08-20/08-22 extractions),
43 of 59 components and 44 of 53 data modules are **byte-identical**.
The shell's bottom bar, the five-stop Mirror, the tweak teardown, pulse
trends, the deviation view — everything that looks new in a cold read of
the bundle — is v28's, already recorded, already planned or shipped. And
two of the changes that remain are the design catching up to the app:
the Roles profile subtab (shipped at D204) and the sponsored disclosure
copy (`ui/SponsorMark.tsx` dropped "never names" first; its test pins
the absence).

What actually moved is one product decision wearing several files, one
room, one scorecard split, one sentence under the Mirror's fields, a
polish pass on Patterns, and nine one-hunk corrections.

## 1 · The paid door — "Suggest a question" becomes "Ask a question"

The board flips its thesis. v24's design (ported, D138 behind it) is a
community board with a paid door beside it — propose free, upvote, the
top become Dailies, and money can skip the queue. The 2026-08-24 design
deletes the community half outright: **no upvotes, no board of other
people's proposals** — every question is bought for a place and a
window, reviewed, and always marked PAID. What stands on the page is the
rate card (per-cohort per-answer line, demand ticks, next open day), the
composer as the paid flow (question → shape hints → scope ruler →
audience dims → window → what-everyone-sees preview → contract sheet),
and your own submissions with the decline-that-makes-an-offer flow.

**What the tree already has, verified:**

- The submission path is real (D138): `suggestQuestionV2` (App Check,
  3/day), `v2_suggestions` owner-read, operator review callables,
  promotion by human PR. The door's your-submissions half rides it
  unchanged — statuses, declines, the resend-with-narrower-audience
  offer are all fields that store already carries.
- D138's function **already declines place-scoped civic asks at the
  door with "that subject is the paid research path"** — the inventory
  row says so. The new design is that sentence grown into the whole
  page.
- The pricing model the door draws is D164's, to the letter: scheduled
  slots not impressions, billed per answer against a cap, one paid slot
  a day per place. The audience dims are D228's one-to-three, each
  printed on the band. The disclosure copy ("the same public numbers you
  do — no private cut") is what `SponsorMark.tsx` already says.
- The community half — other people's suggestions, the upvote budget,
  the Top/New/Picked lenses — is demo-only today (`SEED` data behind a
  Preview tag). VISION-V28 §13 named it a real backlog item belonging
  to the suggestions work. **The new design closes that item by
  deletion.** No backend was ever built for it, so the deletion costs
  rules nothing.

**The decision the owner must make before any code:** retire the
community board as a product, or keep both doors. The design says
retire; this file recommends taking it — it deletes the one remaining
Preview surface instead of building a backend for it, and the review
path (human, 3/day, PR promotion) survives identically — but a designed
feature dies, and that is not an engineering call. Record it in
DECISIONS.md either way; the D138 machinery, budget and rules are
untouched in both branches.

### 1.1 · What ships with the door, mock-free

The composer, the scope ruler with the **committed rate card's**
prices, the audience chips, the preview, the contract sheet — all real
on arrival, because the contract path is the human one the app already
discloses ("the paid path is a human contract today"; the design keeps
the sentence: *"Arranged directly for now — no self-serve yet. We reply
within a day…"*). The rate card figures come from the committed
`content/pricing.json` PAID-PLAN §6 describes; until that file exists,
the door can print the hand-set prices D251's report contracts already
use — a price a buyer can see, which is the §6 rule.

The shell wiring rides along: the header's compose "+" beside search
(`app.jsx` in the extraction), `openAskedByYou`, `openPaidReport`, and
the `paidQ` overlay slot. New overlays arrive as typed ESM or spec
files per the standing conventions; `check:globals` rule 4 only moves
down.

One immediate truth fix is independent of the whole decision and should
not wait for it: `spec/suggestions.jsx:334` still promises the buyer
*"counts and cuts — never names"*, which D98 made false the day voter
lists went public and `SponsorMark` already stopped saying. One line;
§6 carries it.

### 1.2 · What does NOT ship yet: the demand board

`SG_DEMAND`'s 14-day ticks and `MARKET`'s demand index are mocked, and
their comments say what the real source is: the booking ledger. PAID-PLAN
§9.5 gates pricing mechanism on demand evidence ("the day a buyer is
turned away from a full window is the day pricing needs a mechanism"),
and D167 forbids shipping the mock meanwhile. So the door ships v1
**without** the booked/open ticks and the ×-index line — the rate card
prints the committed prices, the window section states the next open day
from the (initially near-empty) booking records of §2, and the demand
language arrives when there are bookings to fold. This is the same
"narrow the trial, never fabricate" rule Patterns shipped under.

## 2 · "Asked by you" — the buyer's room

PAID-PLAN §7 sketched it; §9.3 sequenced it first among in-app builds;
the 2026-08-22 extraction recorded `PaidMineCard` as "designed and
deliberately not built — it graduates with 'Asked by you'". The
2026-08-24 design is that graduation: the room (overlay off the account
sheet), every purchase as a card — PAID band, running/closed chip, live
public split, budget meter (answers against the cap — the billing unit,
D164), window hairline, report shelf — plus `PaidMineCard` joining the
profile's General tab under "You asked".

**Backend half, per PAID-PLAN §7:** `v2_purchases/{buyerUid}/…`,
server-written at contract time, owner-read. Rules + rules tests +
`docs/data-inventory.md` row (`check:data-inventory`, D130). The public
numbers on the card are the same aggregates everyone reads — no new
read path, which is the room's whole honesty story, stated in its foot.
The report shelf points at D251's builder artifacts; "reports are picked
up here (no bells, no email — by design)" spares a notification path.

**What does not ship:** the subscriptions section (`SUBS` is
illustrative demo data for PAID-PLAN §5 subscriptions, which do not
exist yet — the section arrives with §5, wearing the pulse grammar's
honest series exactly as drawn), and the HTML/CSV chips for reports not
yet built by contract. A room with one real purchase renders one card;
a room with none says so. The `CurSwitch` (€/kr/$, one persisted
preference, every price reads it) ships with the room and the door
together — it is the same three-line store.

Every piece gets its `smoke-live` case in the standing shape: mount
live, assert the real thing renders and the demo cast does not.

## 3 · The place scorecards hold two crowds

City and country scorecards draw locals (filled dot) and visitors
(ring) on one 0–10 axis per category, the span bar between them as the
story, split headline numbers, and an "I live here / I'm visiting"
switch that dims the other crowd and decides where your own marks land.
Your score becomes a tick instead of a third dot. The world keeps one
crowd — it has no visitors.

**The honesty problem, named before it ships wrong:** the app can know
a rater's own place (the anchors an answer carries) and therefore split
*residents of the rated place* from *everyone else*. It cannot know who
**visited** — a person in Bergen rating Oslo may never have been there.
Two honest shapes, pick one and record it:

1. **Derive from anchors, relabel the crowds** — "live there" vs
   "from elsewhere" (or the design's exact words only if the owner
   accepts the looser claim). No new field, no D86 widening, no rules
   change; the switch becomes a *lens* over which crowd is fore, not a
   claim about you, because your crowd is your anchor's fact.
2. **Ask the rater** — a role captured with the rate answer. A new
   field on the answer create shape, a rules change, an inventory note,
   and a question the card must ask before it can draw. Costs real
   surface for a nicety the copy can carry.

This file recommends (1). **Before building, measure the fold**: the
split needs per-place cells for rate-question aggregates (the rater's
place × the rated place). Whether `agg.by`'s existing cuts carry that
for `rates:` questions is a fact to check in `data/` against the
Scores-lens fold, not to assume from this document — if the cell does
not publish today, the fold change is the item's real cost and goes to
`docs/COSTS.md` first. The spec store's baked crowd averages
(`place-stats.js`, "keep the cards full from day one") stay demo-only
furniture either way; live cards draw the cells that exist, per the
standing honest-absence rules.

## 4 · The "so what" line under the Mirror fields — BUILT (D273)

One plain-language sentence under the Circle/Groups/City/Country/World
fields: *"**Jonas** mirrors you closest; **Astrid** least · most in
step on **food**, least on **politics**."* Folded entirely from what
the field already computed (the drawn nodes' match figures) plus the
per-topic agreement the feed's own record carries. Client-only; no new
reads.

The live port keeps two disciplines the prototype line does not need:
the counted-basis clause where a figure could be mistaken for a census
(D146's "of the N in both samples"), and the thin-guard — a field too
sparse to draw does not get a sentence pretending otherwise (the
prototype already gates on two placeable nodes; the live gate is
whatever the field's own sparse state is). Lands in
`spec/mirror-field-pops.jsx` beside the field bodies it reads.

**As built (D273):** the demo field carries the design's fold verbatim
at its two reachable mounts (Circle, Groups — the design's other
branches were unreachable at both mount points and are not carried);
the live line ships on `LiveCircleBody`, names only, quiet below two
placeable named members, printing no number the People rows beneath
don't already state with their shared-answer basis. The live groups
half waits on a per-scene likeness fold that does not exist — the
demo's `g.match` is authored data.

## 5 · The Patterns polish pass — BUILT (D273)

Five moves, one commit, all in `src/v2/ui/` — supersedes the three
2026-08-20 files re-extracted here; that directory stays the D214–D216
port record.

1. **Lens labels**: Oracle · **Question map** · **People map**
   (`PatternsTab.tsx` — the ruler's own comment gains the third clause:
   "the question map is the whole pool, the people map is the whole
   crowd").
2. **The explainer notes retire** — `PT_NOTES`, the `pt-note` div and
   the `insight.patterns.used.v1` key are deleted; each lens teaches
   its own marks (the map's footer legend, the oracle's one-time
   hints). The key is swept by prefix on purge, so its deletion needs
   no inventory change — but delete the read/write code whole, not the
   render alone.
3. **The resting constellation** (`PatternsMap.tsx`): the ten strongest
   ties draw at full voice before any tap — accent-leaning stroke,
   member dots fully inked — so the map shows a figure at rest; the
   next-up beacon moves to its own top SVG layer so no neighbour buries
   the ring or the "answer next" label.
4. **The Oracle's sealed state** (`PatternsOracle.tsx` +
   `ui/patterns.css`): a dusk aura around the sealed disc, a breath of
   shade at each tile's foot, a dashed landing seat — identical both
   sides, so nothing leaks the call (the seal is pinned by test; the
   styling must not give the pin a reason to move) — and at reveal the
   un-picked tile wears the topic's muted hue.
5. **`.qm-nextlab` 10 → 10.5px**, the tab's own "no type below 10.5px"
   rule applied to its one violation. The design also moves the oracle
   sub-row's feed-votes count from a `title` tooltip to an inline "N
   from feed votes" — **not ported (D273)**: the app's port never had
   the tooltip, and the live store records seals and grades, not vote
   provenance, so `answered − graded` would miscount the Question map's
   own votes as feed votes. An honest count needs a provenance datum
   nobody has proposed keeping.
