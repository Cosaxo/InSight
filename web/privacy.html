InSight · privacy

Privacy policy

last updated 2026-08-08

InSight asks you one question a day, shows you how everyone else answered, and lets you play the same question against people you know. This page is the long version of what that means for your data — what is stored, who can read it, and how to get rid of all of it.

The short version: your answers are readable by you and nobody else, ever. Not by us, not by your friends, not by other users. What other people see is a count, and only once enough people have answered that a count cannot point at anyone.

You do not need an account

The app works the moment you open it. It signs you in anonymously — a random identifier, no email, no name, no phone number. You can use InSight indefinitely without ever telling us who you are.

Linking a Google account is optional, and it is an upgrade: it keeps the same identifier and the same history, so your answers survive losing your phone. We receive your Google account identifier and nothing else from it.

What is stored

  • Your answers. Which option you picked, for each question, and when. Stored under your account and readable only by you. They cannot be edited or deleted individually — that is what makes the counts honest — but deleting your account removes all of them.
  • Your duel answers and guesses. Same rule, with one addition: the day after a group or 1v1 round, a server process publishes that day's picks to the people in that group. Before then nobody can read them, including us. After then, the members of that group can see what you picked for that day.
  • A display name, if you set one. Shown to people in your groups when a day is revealed. It is whatever you type; it does not have to be your real name.
  • Optional details about you, if you fill them in. An age band (never your exact birthday — the band is worked out on your device and the date never leaves it), gender, city, country, education and relationship status. Every one is optional and skippable, and the app works fully without any of them.
    These are the only things that are deliberately used to build something public: they are what let a question show how each kind of person answered. A copy is taken at the moment you answer, so changing your profile later never moves a past answer into a different group. The same minimum applies as to every other count — a group smaller than the threshold is not published at all, and if hiding one group would let you work it out by subtracting the rest, a second one is hidden too.
    Your city is either chosen from a fixed list of places, or — only if you tap “use my location” and allow it — worked out from a single approximate location reading. That reading is matched to the nearest city on your own device, using a list of cities that ships inside the app. Your coordinates are not sent to us, not stored, and not written down anywhere. What is saved is the city name, exactly as if you had picked it from the list yourself.
    Location is never requested unless you tap that button, is never read in the background or continuously, and declining changes nothing except that you pick your city by hand. We do not work out your location from your internet connection.
    We do not ask for political views, religion, health, ethnicity, or anything else the law treats as a special category, and none of it is used in these groupings.
  • Group membership and invite codes. Which groups you are in, and who else is in them.
  • A notification token, on phones. Only if you allow notifications, and only used for the one notification this app sends: your group's day has been revealed.
  • Data on your device. A cache of the questions and your own answers, so the app opens fast and works offline, plus small preferences. This stays on your device. On Android, system backup is switched off for this app so it is not copied to your Google Drive.

What we do not collect

No coordinates. The app can ask your device for an approximate location if — and only if — you tap “use my location”, but that reading is turned into a city name on your device and then discarded. No latitude or longitude is ever transmitted to us or stored, so the most precise thing we could ever hold about where you are is the name of a city. No location history, no background location, and no working out where you are from your IP address.

No contacts. No photos. No microphone or camera. No advertising identifiers. No third-party analytics or tracking of any kind — there is no product analytics SDK in this app at all. We do not sell or share data with advertisers, because we do not have the kind of data an advertiser would want and do not intend to.

How the public counts work

When you answer, a server process adds one to a tally for that question. The tally is the only thing anyone else can see, and it is published under two rules:

  • A minimum. Below a threshold of respondents, the public record says only "not enough answers yet" and carries no numbers at all. Not a hidden number — no number.
  • No timing. The public tally carries no per-answer timestamps, so it cannot be used to work out who answered when, and therefore who is behind a change in the count.

The exact, pre-threshold counts live in a separate place that no app or user can read — only the server process that writes them.

Who can see what

  • Your answers: you.
  • Your duel picks for a revealed day: the members of that group.
  • Aggregate counts: anyone using the app, once the minimum is met.
  • Everything else: nobody.

These are enforced by server-side access rules, not by the app's interface. A modified app cannot read what the rules do not allow.

Third parties

Firebase (Google Cloud)

Hosting, sign-in, database and notifications. Your data is stored on Google Cloud infrastructure under Google's terms as our processor.

Sentry — crash reports, on by default

Crash reporting is on by default, and you can switch it off at any time in the app's privacy panel. While it is on, an error report carries your anonymous account identifier and technical details of the failure — never your email, your name, or a recording of your screen. Note that deleting your account does not remove reports already sent to Sentry; those age out on Sentry's retention schedule.

Deleting everything

The privacy panel in the app has a delete button. It removes your profile, every answer you have ever given, your group memberships, your picks and name inside past group reveals, your notification tokens, and your sign-in itself. It also clears the app's data on the device you ran it from. There is no undo and no grace period.

Two things intentionally survive, and neither can identify you: the anonymous aggregate counts your answers contributed to (there is no per-person attribution in them to unwind), and — unless you had switched crash reporting off — any error reports already sent, until they age out.

Children

InSight is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect data from them. There is little to collect: no account is required and no personal details are requested.

Security

Access rules are enforced on the server and covered by an automated test suite that runs on every change — including tests that deliberately attempt to read other people's data and must fail. If you find something that should not be possible, please tell us: see SECURITY.md in the project repository.

Changes

If this policy changes in a way that affects what is collected or who can read it, the date at the top changes and the app will say so.

Contact

Questions, deletion requests and security disclosures — contact us at the support address listed on the terms of service page.

If you are in the EEA

InSight is operated from Norway, so the GDPR applies in full. If you are in the EEA and believe your data is being handled in a way the GDPR does not allow, you have the right to lodge a complaint with your local data protection supervisory authority. In Norway that is Datatilsynet (datatilsynet.no). You can do that whether or not you have raised it with us first.
