Deleting your account

last updated 2026-09-01

This page is how you get rid of everything. It exists so you can request deletion without needing the app installed — there are two routes below, and both end in the same wipe.

In the app — immediate

Open InSight, go to the account panel, and use the delete button. Nothing else is needed: no email, no waiting, no ticket. The wipe runs while you wait and the account is gone when it returns. There is no undo and no grace period.

Without the app — by email

If you have uninstalled InSight, or cannot reach the button, email olaftaule01@gmail.com with the subject Delete my account. We run exactly the same deletion on your behalf.

We have to be able to tell that the account is yours before deleting it, because deleting the wrong one is not reversible either. Include whichever of these applies:

  • the Google address you signed in with, sent from that address; or
  • your handle, if you claimed one — it is unique to your account.

We answer within 30 days, and in practice much sooner. If we cannot identify the account from what you send, we will say so rather than guess.

The one case this route cannot serve

InSight gives every install an account straight away, without asking you to sign up. If you never linked Google and never claimed a handle, that account has nothing on it that can be matched to you — which is a privacy property rather than an oversight, and it means we genuinely cannot find it from an email. The delete button on the device is the only route to such an account. If the device is gone, the account is unreachable to you and to us; it holds no name, no address and no identifier of yours, and its answers are not attributable to anyone.

What gets deleted

All of it, in one pass, whichever route you use:

  • your profile, and the anchors on it — age band, gender, city, country, education, relationship status, profession
  • every answer you have ever given, and the record of when you gave it
  • every take you have written
  • your follows, including the ones other people hold of you
  • your group memberships, and your picks and name inside past group reveals
  • your duel history
  • your handle, released back to the registry
  • your test results, including the political compass if you turned it on
  • your presence square
  • your profile photo, if you added one
  • your notification tokens, so nothing can be sent to you again
  • your sign-in itself

The app also clears its own data on the device you ran the delete from.

What is kept, and why

Two things survive, and neither can identify you.

  • The aggregate counts your answers contributed to. These are tallies — how many people picked each option — with no per-person provenance in them. The one record that could attribute a tally back to you is deleted first, before the counts are touched, so by the time the deletion returns there is nothing left to unwind. Removing your vote from a published total would change a number other people are reading without making you any more anonymous than you already are.
  • Error reports already sent. If the app crashed and reported it, that report carries the account identifier and ages out on the crash reporter’s own retention schedule. We do not hold it after that.

A vote still in flight when the deletion runs can land its record moments afterwards. It expires on its own schedule and is not readable by anyone in the meantime.

The rest of the policy

What is stored in the first place, and who can read it, is on the privacy policy page — including the part worth knowing before you decide: your answers are public while the account exists. The terms of service page carries the operator and contact details.
