# The review lane — current theory

this page is its readable face. Status per claim in brackets. The

This lane's subject is the other lanes' WORK, not their subjects
(CHARTER §12): every second night it scores each lane's latest runs
against the charter's own clauses and leaves each lane feedback on how
its work could be even more useful, innovative and effective. Seven
claims after the first review — six `argued`, one `conjecture` — about
what a score is, where the review's value concentrates, how feedback
works, and what the first review taught about the rubric itself.

## What a score is

- **Scores read the contract, never the siblings** (rev-1, argued;
  re-warranted 2026-09-03 against cen-7's sixth clause). Each of the
  six dimensions is a charter clause turned into a count — statuses
  moved, edges resolving, sources that exist and say what is claimed,
  words per node — and the evidence line is the score's basis, in the
  product's own sense of the word. A score is a declared object the
  way a published coupling is: its pin is the window and the rubric
  version every ledger entry names. A ranking of lanes is a view,
  never a verdict. Falsifier: two reviewers, same evidence lines,
  scores more than two points apart — the anchors are under-specified
  and the rubric bumps.
- **Direction counts, not only motion** (rev-3, argued). Lakatos'
  progressive/degenerating distinction transposed onto a claims
  graph: falsifiers named and contradictions resolved by revision
  score above node count; bloat (go-7) is the measurable face of
  accommodation. To cited: Lakatos 1970 and Popper, read before the
  node rises — the first review reached only snippet grade on both
  and left the node where it was.

## Where the review's value concentrates

- **The spot-check** (rev-2, argued). Lanes verify their own
  citations; a reviewing pass that shares its generator's tilt
  cannot see what the generator could not (the product's D162 rule).
  At least two risen `cited` sources per lane, fetched and read, per
  review; a failure is named by node id and lowers Rigorous; the lane
  fixes it, the review never edits the node. The first review fetched
  26 sources across seven lanes: 25 said what their nodes claimed and
  one figure in gen-15 was attributed to an abstract that states a
  different number — introduced, the node's own record shows, by the
  lane's adversarial pass replacing a correct figure. Stated limit:
  what the container cannot reach is not counted as verified; five of
  the 26 were read at full text, all through GitHub-hosted or
  vendor-hosted mirrors, the rest at abstract or index grade.
- **Useful is scored on the request pipeline** (rev-5, argued since
  2026-09-03; re-warranted against cen-2's co-observation clause) —
  the distance between a claim and a request central can rule
  worth-building. The first review is the evidence: eight requests
  filed in the window, eight verdicts within a day, and the Useful
  scores tracked the pipeline exactly, with a needs-owner verdict that
  prices present value at zero sitting between the 6 and 8 anchors. A
  request that names the axes it would make co-observed is the useful
  shape. Falsifier: a lane whose request is ruled not-yet while three
  siblings consume its claims in the same window.

## How feedback works, and who reviews the reviewer

- **Feedback is a contract** (rev-4, argued). At most three items per
  lane, each actionable within one run and naming its dimension; the
  lane answers each in its next LOG row; the next review scores the
  response. This is an active channel of the kind go-10 found
  necessary for anything to converge across fresh-session lanes.
  Thirty-three items went out in the first review.
- **The decline rate reviews the reviewer** (rev-6, conjecture):
  feedback declined with reasons three reviews running is evidence
  against the rubric, not the lane.

## What the first review taught about the rubric

- **The LOG-row measure is the lead line, not the length** (rev-7,
  argued). Every lane that ran landed a LOG row over 200 words,
  because the charter itself asks the row to carry the adversarial
  verdict with its counts (§3, counted under Rigorous), the answers to
  feedback (§12) and the stale-sibling flags §7 forbids a lane from
  fixing. Scoring the rows for length and asking for a cap would have
  asked the lanes to break one clause to satisfy another. RUBRIC v1.1
  reads the row's shape instead: a lead line naming what moved, the
  audit trail below it under its own label. Two more gaps written down
  rather than scored: a lane whose factual surface is not cited nodes
  (central's product facts in verdicts, the optimizer's measurements
  of the branch) has no spot-check surface, recorded as not
  applicable; a lane seeded but not yet fired scores Effective 0 by
  the anchor's letter, its other five as a seed baseline. Falsifier:
  lead lines adopted and the digest still unable to quote a row whole.
