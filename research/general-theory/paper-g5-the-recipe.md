# The recipe

*General paper G5: what the language-model recipe — a corpus, a tokenization, one predictive objective, scale, and then reading the representation — transfers to a population measured by many sources, what it changes about how the data should be structured, and the four places it does not transfer at all.*

**Status: theory research.** Nothing here describes any product; `research/README.md` says what a general paper is and what holds it to its setting. G1 supplies the co-observation graph and the two evaluation streams; G2 the ledger the sequence view is a view over, and the version row a tokenization must have; G3 the classical floor, the distilled layer, faithfulness as a log-ratio, and the admission test; G4 the value ledger and the caution that a better mechanism can move the class it is measured against.

**Perfect-form test.** If the app did not exist, this would still be worth saying because the most productive pattern-finding recipe of the last decade has one shape — take everything as a sequence, choose how to cut it into tokens, train one model to predict the next token, scale it, and then work out what it learned — and a population measured by many sources under a budget is a corpus of sequences that recipe can be run on, so that the questions worth asking are which parts of the recipe carry over, which part of it is the data-structure decision in disguise, and where a population of people differs from a pile of text in ways that would make the recipe learn the wrong thing while its loss went down.

**Setting.** A population of units measured by K sources under an attention budget, as in G1: each source yields a response through a measurement model from a latent state, with a bias structure, a cost, a custody class and a grain, and the object of discovery is the coupling structure among sources across units, within units over periods, and on relations. Add a learner class: models trained by a predictive objective over sequences, whose internal state is a representation that can be read only by further work, and whose capacity and training data can both be scaled. Add that the sequences are not found but collected, under a policy that chooses what to observe next and that may itself consult the learner. Nothing here depends on what the units, the sources or the learner's architecture are.

**Abstract.** A language model is a recipe rather than an architecture: assemble a corpus, decide how to cut it into tokens, train a single model to predict each token from the ones before it, scale the model and the corpus together, and then read what the representation learned with tools built for that purpose. This paper asks what of that transfers to a population measured by many sources, and its first answer is that the recipe's least glamorous step is the one this setting most needs. A person's record is a sequence of observations from many sources at many grains, and how it is cut into tokens — whether each source has its own vocabulary or all share one, whether a continuous reading is a bin or a coordinate, whether time is a position or a token, and whether the policy's own serving decision is a token the model sees — is the data-structure decision, made where G2's ledger becomes a view a learner can read; every tokenization is a claim about what may be compared with what, and it is versioned like a model because it is one. The second answer is that the objective must match the missingness. Next-observation prediction is honest because the past is what it is; masked prediction is honest only if the mask is drawn from the serving distribution or weighted to the evaluation stream, because a mask drawn uniformly over a tensor the policy filled non-uniformly teaches the model the policy and calls it the person. Attention is then a learned, context-dependent co-observation graph — which past observations matter for this prediction, per unit, per moment — and it is what a static graph is not, while being unable to do the one thing the static graph decides: attention over a pair no unit ever supplied together is attention over nothing, so identification is untouched by any learner. Placing a new unit from their first observations is in-context learning, and the placement curve is its efficiency. Scaling has four axes here — units, observations per unit, sources, and span — and the paper's hypothesis is that the loss floor for a unit's own future is set by within-unit noise, so beyond a point more units do not help predict a person and only more of that person does, which is G1's two regimes returned as a scaling law. Reading the representation is where the recipe supplies what G3 called open: probes tested against latents the floor already measures, sparse features under the admission test, and interventions that ask whether a feature causes a prediction rather than accompanies it. Four things do not transfer, and each is a condition: the corpus is chosen by a policy the learner feeds, so the loop is closed where a text corpus is open; a model pretrained on text about people arrives with a prior about what kinds of people say what, which is a stereotype imported wholesale and must be ablated; the honesty structure — the two streams, the floor, the admission test — is scaffolding the learner must not be allowed to learn away; and prediction is not identification, so a lower loss never settles a structural claim.

**1 · The recipe, and why its dull step matters here**

The recipe has five steps and they are usually described with the emphasis on the middle. Assemble a corpus. Cut it into tokens. Train one model to predict each token from its predecessors. Scale model and corpus together, and watch the loss fall on a predictable curve. Then, since the model cannot say what it learned, build tools to read its representation.

In the setting that produced the recipe the corpus is found and the tokenization is a nuisance. Here both are reversed. The corpus is collected under a budget by a policy that decides what to observe, so the corpus is a design object and the learner is one of its consumers. And the tokenization is not a nuisance but the decision the setting has been circling since G2 asked how the data should be structured: a token vocabulary is a statement of what may be compared with what, at what grain, and every choice in it either preserves a coupling for the learner to find or erases it before the learner starts. The paper takes the steps in order, and it spends the most time on the two the recipe's own practitioners spend the least.

**2 · The record as a sequence, and tokenization as the data-structure decision**

*The view.* G2 holds the data as a ledger of observation attempts with three grains and a tensor as a view. The recipe adds a fourth view: each unit's attempts in order as one sequence, and the population as a corpus of such sequences. It is a view, not a store; nothing is copied out of the ledger, and a sequence is regenerated from it under a stated tokenization, which is versioned with a row as any model is, because a change in how the record is cut is a change in what every downstream number means.

*Five decisions make a tokenization, and each is a claim.*

The vocabulary. Each source may carry its own token vocabulary with a source tag, or all sources may share one embedding space into which each response is projected. Separate vocabularies preserve the sources as distinct instruments and let the model learn their couplings; a shared space asserts that a response on one source and a response on another are the same kind of thing, which is a claim the measurement models of G1 may not support, and which erases the distinction G3's bias terms need. The honest default is separate vocabularies with a shared position, and a shared space only where a measurement model says two sources read one latent.

The value. A continuous reading — a metabolite level, a latency, a score — is either binned into a discrete token or kept as a coordinate the model reads through a learned projection. Binning is what text does and what a learner is built for, and it throws away resolution at a boundary the designer chose; a coordinate keeps the resolution and asks the model to learn a scale. The bin edges, where bins are used, are the calibration decision of G3 in another form, and they are recorded.

Time. A period can be a position in the sequence, so that the model reads order and learns gaps; or a token in its own right, so that the interval between two observations is a thing the model attends to. For an irregular schedule — which every budgeted population has — the interval carries information, since a gap is itself a fact about the person and about the policy, and a tokenization that flattens irregular time to a position has hidden that fact from the learner.

The policy. The serving decision — why this observation was taken, at what realised probability — is in the ledger. Whether it is a token the model sees is a choice with a consequence stated in section 3: a model that sees the policy can learn the person net of the policy, and a model that does not will learn the policy and report it as the person. So the propensity is a token, and the model is asked to predict the response given that the observation was chosen, which is the conditional the log makes estimable.

The event. Reveals, ties forming, an intervention the system made, an event the world supplied: each is a token with its class from G3's causal taxonomy carried as a tag, because a learner that cannot tell a randomized event from an observed one will learn what follows both and let a reader mistake the second for the first.

*What a tokenization cannot fix.* A coupling between two sources that no unit supplied together is absent from every sequence, and no vocabulary puts it there. Tokenization decides how the data is cut; the co-observation graph decides what the data contains, and G1's identification result stands before any learner runs.

**3 · The objective, and why it must match the missingness**

*Next-observation prediction is honest by construction.* A model that predicts each observation from the ones before it conditions on the past as it happened, including the policy's decisions if they are tokens, so the thing it learns is a conditional distribution over what comes next given a record that was collected as it was. That is a well-defined object on any sequence, however the sequence was assembled.

*Masked prediction is honest only under a matched mask.* The alternative objective hides some observations and asks the model to recover them from the rest, which reads as the natural objective for a tensor that is mostly holes. It is not, unless the holes the training hides are distributed as the holes the policy left. A mask drawn uniformly over the tensor asks the model to recover observations the policy would never have withheld, in contexts the policy never produced, and the model learns a recovery that is right on the mask's distribution and wrong on the ledger's; a mask drawn from the serving distribution, or reweighted toward the evaluation stream, asks the model to recover exactly what the policy did not take, which is the missingness the data actually has. The rule is the one G1 states for every estimator: selection is a known function of recorded variables, and an objective that ignores the record of selection learns the selection.

*The two streams are the only honest loss.* On the adaptive stream the policy chose what to observe using a model, and if the learner is that model or fed by it, its loss on the adaptive stream is a loss on data it selected — the number improves while the model does not. The loss that means anything is on the two never-selected-on streams G1 defines, the anchor-drawn one for the anchors and the eligible-drawn one for any other source, and the recipe's scaling curve is drawn on those or it is not drawn.

**4 · Attention as learned co-observation, and what it cannot do**

*What it is.* A learner of this kind computes, for each prediction, a weighting over the past — which earlier observations of this unit matter for what comes next, in this context. Aggregated over units and contexts, that weighting is a map of which sources' observations inform which, and it is the transfer map the learned-representation paper describes, seen from inside the model.

*What it adds to the static graph.* G1's co-observation graph says which pairs of sources were jointly observed and how often; it is a property of the corpus and it is the same for every unit. The learned weighting is per unit and per moment: it can say that for this person, a body reading matters for an answer on the morning it was taken and not a week later, that a tie forming changes how every subsequent answer should be read, that an event of one class matters and an event of another does not. That is structure a covariance model does not express and a static graph does not hold, and it is the recipe's genuine contribution to pattern-finding in this setting.

*What it cannot do.* Attention is computed over the past that exists. A pair of sources no unit ever supplied together is a pair with no joint token in any sequence, and a weighting over the absent is a weighting over nothing. So the learner's map is a map of the couplings the corpus contains, sharpened per unit, and it cannot create the co-observation that G1 says is the whole answer for a structural zero. The loop of section 6 closes here: the learner can tell the policy which pairs it wishes it had seen together, and only the policy can arrange to see them.

**5 · Placement as in-context learning, and scaling with four axes**

*A new unit is a context.* The recipe's model learns a new task from a few examples in its input without changing its weights; a new unit's first observations are exactly such an input, and the model's prediction for their next observation, given only those, is placement without a fit. The placement curve — how the model's loss on a unit's future falls with the number of that unit's observations in context — is the efficiency of in-context learning for persons, and it is the currency of the foundation papers made into a curve the recipe can bend. What it depends on is stated: the model must have seen enough units that the population prior in its weights is sharp, and each unit's first observations must be from sources the prior can read, which is the anchor set's job.

*Four axes of scale, and a hypothesis about which dominates.* In the recipe's home the scaling axes are model size and corpus size, and the loss falls smoothly with both. Here the corpus has four axes: the number of units, observations per unit, the number of sources, and the span in periods. The hypothesis this paper states, to be tested rather than assumed, is that they do not trade evenly, and the reason is G1's two regimes returned as a scaling law. Prediction of a population coupling improves with units, and keeps improving. Prediction of a unit's own future has a floor set by that unit's within-unit noise — the part of tomorrow's answer that nothing about the person predicts — and beyond a point more units do not lower it; only more observations of that unit do, up to the unit's own floor. So the loss curve on the eligible-drawn stream should bend at a population size that depends on how much of each unit's variance is idiosyncratic, and the bend is a measurement of the population's within-unit noise, which is a quantity no other design reads. A third axis, sources, behaves differently again: a new source lowers the loss on every other source only through the couplings the corpus contains, per section 4, so adding a source is worth what its co-observation buys, which is the value ledger of G4 read as a scaling exponent.

**6 · Reading the representation: the psychometrics of a learned geometry**

G3 left open what it called a psychometrics of learned representations, and this is where the recipe's tooling supplies one, under conditions.

*Probes with a known answer.* A probe asks whether a quantity is linearly readable from the representation. Run blind it answers only whether something correlated with the quantity is there. Run against latents the classical floor already estimates — a unit's position on a dimension the covariance model places them on — it has a known answer and becomes a test: if the probe recovers the floor's placement, the representation encodes it, and the residual between the probe and the floor is the part of the representation that is not the floor's latent. That residual is the candidate structure, and it goes to the admission test like any other.

*Sparse features under the admission test.* Decomposing the representation into a sparse dictionary of features yields directions that may each correspond to one thing, and may not. A feature is a proposed structure and nothing more until it passes G3's five clauses: found on one set of units and tested on another, surviving on the evaluation stream, predicting a source other than the one it was found in, surviving the propensity by weighting or matching, and not absorbed by a widened bias model. A feature that fails is a feature of the policy or of the missingness, and a dictionary that is not put to the test is a list of names for artefacts.

*Interventions, which is what the distilled layer never had.* A probe says a feature is present; only an intervention says it matters. Change the feature's value in the representation and read the change in the prediction: a feature whose alteration moves the prediction in the direction its name implies is a feature the model uses, and one whose alteration moves nothing is a passenger. This is the reading G3's faithfulness number could not give — two models can agree on every prediction and disagree about why, and an intervention is how the why is asked — and it is how a learned geometry becomes something a reader can make a claim about, with the claim scoped to the model, not to the population, until the admission test passes.

*The distilled layer, placed.* G3's factor layer fitted to the representation is one probe among these, the one that asks for a named low-dimensional summary, and its faithfulness is the licence it always was: substitution for prediction on a named distribution, never a structural reading. The intervention tools are what let a substitution be checked, by asking whether the layer's factors move the teacher's predictions the way they move the layer's.

**7 · What does not transfer, and each is a condition**

*The corpus is chosen by the learner it feeds.* A text corpus is found; this corpus is collected by a policy that consults a model, and if the learner is that model then the observations it learns from next were chosen by it. The recipe has no step for this because it never needed one. Here it is the loop the foundation papers name: the propensity is a token, the loss is on the never-selected-on streams, and every structure the learner proposes for the policy to pursue is a candidate under the admission test — because a learner that chooses its data and then finds structure in it has found its choices. And a further consequence the learned-representation paper states: once the learner's outputs drive pairings and displays, they move the phenomena the learner predicts, which no selection correction touches and only randomized withholding identifies.

*A model pretrained on text arrives with a prior about people.* The most tempting shortcut is to render a unit's record as text and hand it to a model already trained on a great deal of text, so that it brings what it knows about sleep and risk and reasoning to the task. What it also brings is what text says about what kinds of people answer how, which is a prior over categories — the stereotype door of the appearance paper, imported at the scale of everything ever written. A prediction such a model makes leans on that prior in a way no ablation of the record's own category tokens can reach, because the prior is in the weights. So a pretrained model is compared against a model trained from nothing on the same tokens, the gap is the text prior's contribution, and it is published as the category share the learned-representation paper demands — and where the prior's contribution is large, the prediction is a reading of the text corpus about people like this one, not of this one.

*The honesty structure is scaffolding the learner must not learn away.* The two evaluation streams, the classical floor, the propensity as a token, the admission test: each is a constraint on what the learner may be credited with, and a learner optimized hard enough will find the version of its task in which the constraint costs least — a model that learns to predict the anchor-drawn stream because that is where it is judged, and lets the eligible-drawn stream go. So the constraints are enforced as design and audited as measurement, never entrusted to the objective: the floor is refit and compared per estimand, the streams are separated in the ledger and not in the model, and a learner that outperforms the floor on one stream and not the other has been read for what it did.

*Prediction is not identification.* The recipe's whole currency is loss, and G3 is explicit that loss settles predictive claims and is nearly flat in the parameters where structural and causal claims live. A learner with lower loss has not identified a coupling, has not made an interval a point, and has not turned an observed event into an assigned one. What it has done is predict better, and the reading of why belongs to section 6's tools under the admission test, and the reading of whether a coupling exists at all belongs to G1's graph, which no loss curve touches.

**8 · What would have to hold**

*The sequence is a view over the ledger, regenerated under a versioned tokenization*, with each tokenization decision — vocabulary, value, time, policy, event — recorded as the claim it is.

*Sources keep separate vocabularies unless a measurement model says two read one latent*, and continuous values keep their resolution or record where it was cut.

*Time is tokenized so an irregular interval is visible to the learner*, since the gap is a fact.

*The propensity is a token and the event class is a tag*, so the learner conditions on the selection and cannot mistake an observed event for an assigned one.

*A masked objective draws its mask from the serving distribution or weights toward the evaluation stream*; a uniform mask over the tensor is a model of the policy.

*Loss is read only on the two never-selected-on streams*, and the scaling curve is drawn there.

*A learner proposes co-observation and cannot supply it*; every pair it wishes it had seen is a request to the policy and a structural zero until the policy acts.

*The placement curve is measured on held-out units with a sharp population prior and anchor-set first observations.*

*The four-axis scaling hypothesis is tested, not assumed*, with the bend in the units axis read as a measurement of within-unit noise.

*Probes are run against latents with a known answer*, sparse features pass the five clauses or are artefacts, and a feature is credited with a role only under intervention.

*A pretrained text model is ablated against a from-scratch model on the same tokens*, and the gap is published as the text prior's category share.

*The honesty structure is enforced as design and audited as measurement*, never entrusted to the objective.

*A lower loss settles a predictive claim and no other*, and every structural reading routes through G1's graph and G3's test.

**9 · The potential, at full scale**

At full scale — a population large enough that the learner's loss on the eligible-drawn stream has found its bend, with every source tokenized under a versioned cut and the propensity in the sequence — the recipe gives the general setting three things it did not have. A per-unit, per-moment map of which observations inform which, which is a coupling structure conditional on history that no covariance model expresses and no static graph holds. A placement curve for a new unit that is the efficiency of learning a person from their first days, bent by the whole population's prior. And a scaling law with a bend that measures the population's own within-unit noise, which is a quantity about people that only this design reads. What it gives the reading of a representation is the tooling G3 lacked: a probe with a known answer, a feature under a test, and an intervention that asks why. And what it costs is the four conditions of section 7, each of which the recipe's home setting never needed and each of which, ignored, produces a learner whose loss falls while it learns the policy, the text corpus's prior about people, or its own choices.

**10 · What remains open**

Whether the four-axis scaling hypothesis holds — whether the units axis bends where within-unit noise says it should, or whether more people keep helping predict a person in ways the two-regime argument does not anticipate. Whether a shared embedding space across sources is ever licensed by a measurement model, or whether separate vocabularies are always the honest cut. How much of a text-pretrained model's advantage survives its ablation, and whether any of it is knowledge rather than prior. Whether a matched mask can be drawn at all when the serving distribution is itself the learner's output. Whether intervention on a learned feature can be made to mean something about the population rather than about the model, which is the step from the psychometrics of a representation to a psychometrics of persons and is not taken here. And whether a learner can be kept from learning its scaffolding away over years of retraining, which is the loop's long-run question and the one that decides whether this recipe finds patterns or manufactures them.
