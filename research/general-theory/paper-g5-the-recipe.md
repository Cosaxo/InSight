# The recipe

*General paper G5: what the sequence-learning recipe — a corpus, a cut into tokens, one predictive objective, scale, and then reading the representation — supplies to a population measured by many sources, what the objective must be factored into before any of it is honest, where the sequence may be assembled at all, and what the recipe does not supply and acquires here.*

**Status: theory research.** Nothing here describes any product; `research/README.md` says what a general paper is and what holds it to its setting. G1 supplies the co-observation graph, the probability floor, the replication that identifies within-unit noise, the two evaluation streams and the exploration share; G2 the ledger, the fold version over a cut that a tokenization is, the custody rule for a join, and the release a model version is; G3 the classical floor, amortized inference with its two errors, faithfulness with counterfactual inputs, level separation and the five-clause admission test; G4 the value ledger, the objective check, and the caution that a mechanism can move the class it is measured against.

**Perfect-form test.** If the app did not exist, this would still be worth saying because one recipe for finding structure — treat everything as a sequence, cut it into tokens, train a single learner to predict the next token, scale, then work out what was learned — can be run on a population measured by many sources under a budget, and the questions worth asking are which of its steps carry over, which step is a decision already made in the ledger under another name, what its objective must be factored into before its loss means anything about the units rather than about the policy that observed them, and where the recipe's home setting never had to ask what this setting cannot avoid.

**Setting.** A population of units measured by K sources under an attention budget of B units per unit per period, as in G1: each source yields a response through a measurement model from a latent state, with a bias structure, a cost, a custody class and a grain, and the object of discovery is the coupling structure among sources across units, within units over periods, and on relations. Add a learner class: models trained by a predictive objective over sequences, whose internal state is a representation readable only by further work, and whose capacity and training corpus can both be scaled. The class is defined by its objective and not by its routing mechanism, so nothing below depends on how a learner attends to its past. Add that the sequences are not found but collected, under a policy that chooses what to observe next and that may consult the learner. Nothing here depends on what the units or the sources are.

**Abstract.** A sequence-learning recipe has five steps: assemble a corpus, cut it into tokens, train one learner to predict each token from its predecessors, scale learner and corpus together, and read the representation with tools built for that. This paper asks what of that a measured population inherits, and its answers are ordered by what the recipe's home setting never had to face. The corpus here is collected by a policy, so a unit's sequence is a fold version over a ledger cut in G2's sense and not a new structure, and each decision in that cut — vocabulary, value, time's representation and resolution, refusals and expiries, the fold window of a stream, the placement of fixed attributes, the order of a period's observations, the context a learner can hold, and the relational grain a per-unit sequence cannot hold at all — is a claim about what may be compared with what, versioned as any fold is. The objective is honest only once it is factored: on a policy-generated sequence, next-token loss is a product of predicting which observation the policy served and what value it took, and only the second is scored, because a learner lowers the first by learning the policy. The propensity does not enter as a token — a predictor has no estimand for a unit-versus-policy decomposition to be about — but as import weights toward a target distribution stated first, with the residual bounded and the variance priced against G1's floor; a masked objective is the reweighting to that target and nothing else. A unit's sequence across sources is the join G2 says runs only where a custodian holds every column, so the sequence is assembled there or from summaries, and every learner version is a release with a row and a differencing budget. What the recipe supplies that no covariance model does is a per-unit, per-context map of which observations inform which, defined as an ablation quantity on the eligible-drawn stream and never as a routing weight; what it cannot do is create co-observation, so it narrows a coupling only as far as G1's third-source conditioning licenses. Placement of a new unit is amortized posterior inference with its two errors, and the learner's edge is smallest exactly there. Scaling is a constrained frontier under the budget with a loss form stated and an asymptote that is noise plus response error plus class error, read as a consistency check against the replication that identifies noise, and with the coupling target priced in posterior information because loss is flat where it lives. Reading the representation adds representation-level intervention to G3's input-level counterfactuals, and closes nothing G3 left open about shift. And the recipe's non-transfer is one — the loop, in which the learner chooses its own corpus — beside three things it acquires here: the honesty scaffolding with its channel named, an interval on the prior a text-pretrained shortcut imports, and the limit that a loss settles no structural claim, which transfers intact and is stated as a limit.

**1 · The recipe, and the steps that carry the weight here**

The recipe has five steps. Assemble a corpus. Cut it into tokens. Train one learner to predict each token from its predecessors. Scale learner and corpus together. Then, since the learner cannot say what it learned, build tools to read its representation.

Where the recipe comes from, the corpus is found and the cut is a preprocessing choice. Here both are reversed. The corpus is collected under a budget by a policy that decides what to observe, so it is a design object with a log, and the learner is one of the log's readers. And the cut is not a preprocessing choice but a fold version over a ledger cut, which is what G2 calls any derived view with a stated window and a stated version — so the step the recipe treats as a nuisance is the one this setting has already committed to versioning, and every decision in it is a claim the learner inherits. The paper takes the steps in order and spends the most time on the two that carry the weight: the cut, and what the objective must be factored into before its loss is about the units.

**2 · The sequence as a fold version, and the decisions in the cut**

*The view.* G2 holds the data as a ledger of observation attempts — each with its decision, its outcome, its observed state and a reference to the estimate the policy used — in three grains with the tensor as a view. A sequence per unit is one more view: the unit's attempts in order, regenerated from the ledger under a stated cut. It is a fold version in G2's sense, with a row, because a change in how the record is cut changes what every downstream number means, and it is not a new structure.

*The decisions in the cut, each a claim.* An earlier draft named five and called them the set. There are more, and one of the missing ones carries the paper's honesty argument.

The vocabulary. Each source may carry its own token vocabulary with a source tag, or all sources may share one embedding into which each response is projected. Separate vocabularies keep the sources as distinct instruments; a shared space asserts that a response on one source and on another are one kind of thing, which is a coupling claim. The rule is not "share only where a measurement model says two sources read one latent," since that coupling is identified only from co-observed units and the rule would wait on the discovery it was meant to serve. The rule is an invariance condition: two sources may share an embedding where a stated test — the same latent recovered from each on the units observed on both, at a stated tolerance — has been run and passed, and the observations that buy that test are priced. Until it passes, separate vocabularies.

The value. A continuous response is a bin or a coordinate. A bin throws away resolution at an edge the designer chose, and the edges are a calibration decision recorded as one; a coordinate keeps the resolution and asks the learner to learn a scale. Which is used, per source, is in the cut's row.

Time. The decision is not whether time is a token or a position — a learner with a continuous positional encoding sees a gap without a token, and one with an index sees it in neither form. The decision is whether elapsed time is represented at all, at what resolution, and in what units, because on an irregular schedule the gap is a fact about the unit and about the policy. That second half is a warning: a time representation carries the policy's rhythm, and a learner that reads it has been handed part of the serving decision.

Refusals and expiries. Whether an attempt the unit declined, or one that expired unanswered, is a token. G2 is explicit that a ledger which omits them records propensities only where the unit complied, and ignorability is lost exactly where it matters. So a refusal is a token with its own class, or the honesty argument of section 3 has no record to stand on. This is the decision an earlier draft did not make.

The fold window. A source that is a stream or a vector enters the sequence only as a fold — a window and a fold version, which G2 makes the atom for such sources — and the window is a resolution decision of the same kind as a bin edge.

Fixed and slow attributes. A unit's fixed columns and slow-changing ones do not occur at a period; they enter as a prefix to the sequence, as a context the learner conditions on, or as tokens repeated at a stated cadence, and each placement is a claim about how the learner may use them, with the category share of section 9 read differently under each.

Ordering within a period. G2's ledger is partially ordered; observations sharing a period have no order unless the cut assigns one, and an assigned order is a claim the learner will read as sequence.

Context. A learner holds a bounded window of the past, and for a span of years the window decides which couplings are visible at all: a coupling across a gap longer than the window is not in any input. The window length, and what is summarized beyond it, is a cut decision that sets an identification limit before any training.

The event. Each event carries its class from G3's causal taxonomy as a tag, so a learner cannot mistake an observed event for an assigned one.

The propensity. Whether the serving decision is a token is deferred to section 3, which says it is not.

*The relational grain, which a per-unit sequence cannot hold.* The Setting names couplings on relations, and G2 says an observation on a relation belongs to neither member. A per-unit sequence has no place for it. Either a relation is its own sequence, with a stated rule for how its observations appear in each member's sequence without being counted twice, or the recipe is scoped to the unit grain with relations entering as context, and the paper states which: this paper takes the first, prices the double-counting rule as a cut decision, and notes that the relational level is identifiable only from both directions and repeated pairs, per G1, which a sequence per relation must carry.

*What a cut cannot do.* A coupling between two sources no unit supplied together is absent from every sequence, and no vocabulary puts it there. The cut decides how the record is cut; the co-observation graph decides what it contains.

**3 · The objective, factored, and the honest loss**

*The loss is a product, and only one factor is scored.* On a sequence the policy generated, each token carries a source tag and a value, and predicting the next token is predicting two things: which observation the policy served next, and what value it took. The first is the policy; a learner lowers it by learning the serving rule, and an earlier draft reasoned about the undecomposed total, which is the failure the recipe must not have. So the objective is factored explicitly into a serving factor and a response factor, the serving factor is either fixed from the log or excluded from the score, and every claim below about loss is a claim about the response factor alone.

*Next-observation prediction is well defined on any sequence, and a population quantity only under weighting.* An earlier draft called it honest by construction, on three justifications that did not agree. The precise statement is this: a learner fitted to predict the response factor conditions on the past as it happened, which is a well-defined conditional on the training distribution, as any fitted conditional is. It is a quantity about the population only when the training distribution is weighted toward a target distribution stated first — the eligible-drawn evaluation stream of G1, or the anchor-drawn one for the anchors — by import weights computed from the log, with the residual mismatch stated as a bound. The propensity does not enter as a token or a feature. A predictor has no estimand for a unit-versus-policy decomposition to be about, and a propensity supplied as an input is used exactly insofar as it predicts, including as a proxy for the state that drove the selection; G3's admission test forbids the same move for a proposed structure, requiring the propensity to be met by weighting or matching and never entered as a term, and the objective is held to the same rule.

*A masked objective is the reweighting to the target, and its variance is priced against the floor.* The alternative objective hides observations and asks the learner to recover them. The question is not whether the mask is honest but what distribution it is drawn from, and the answer follows from the target: the mask is the reweighting of the tensor's holes toward the stated evaluation stream, which is one thing, and not "the serving distribution" — an earlier draft offered both as alternatives, and they point in opposite directions, since a serving-matched mask trains recovery of exactly what the policy withheld in contexts the policy produced, a mismatch against the eligible-drawn target. A uniform mask over the tensor is then not dishonest but expensive: G1's probability floor guarantees positivity, so every context has support, and the cost of a uniform mask is the variance of import weights up to one over the floor, which is priced and not forbidden.

*The loss that means anything is on the two streams.* On the adaptive stream the policy chose what to observe using a model, and if the learner is that model or feeds it, its loss there is a loss on data it selected. The scored loss is the response factor on the two never-selected-on streams, anchor-drawn for the anchors and eligible-drawn for any other source, reported per stream and per source apart — which is also the measurement that section 9 needs.

**4 · Custody: where a sequence may be assembled**

A unit's sequence across every source is a cross-source join, and G2 is explicit that a join is computed by the custodians of the columns it touches and never by moving them. An earlier draft said the sequence is regenerated from the ledger and did not say where. So: where one custodian holds every column a unit's sequence touches, the sequence is assembled there, and what leaves is what the learner's objective needs — a clipped gradient at the current parameter, or a summary the model admits — under G2's aggregation with its trust conditions stated. Where no single custodian holds every column, the sequence is not assembled anywhere; the learner is trained on summaries the custodians emit, and the couplings across custody classes are reachable only to the extent those summaries carry them, which is a limit on the recipe this setting imposes and the home setting never met.

Two consequences follow from G2 and are conditions here. Every learner version is a release: it carries its training data in recoverable form to a degree measured as a lower bound against a named adversary, it has a row in the publication ledger, and the sequence of versions over a growing population differences like any release sequence and spends against the output-privacy budget as one. And erasure reaches the learner: a unit whose data is withdrawn leaves a fitted representation only at the next version, and the version row records the cut of the ledger each version was fitted on.

**5 · The per-unit map, defined without the architecture**

*What the recipe supplies.* G1's co-observation graph says which sources were jointly observed and how often, and it is the same for every unit. A learner of this class can supply what that graph does not: for this unit, at this moment, which earlier observations inform the prediction of the next — a coupling structure conditional on the unit's own history, which is exactly the structure a covariance model does not express.

*How the map is defined, and how it is not.* An earlier draft defined it as the learner's attention weighting, which fails twice. Attention is one routing mechanism, and the Setting admits learners that have none; and a forward-pass weight has no interval, no identification basis and no type in G2's sense, so it is not a measured quantity. The map is defined as an ablation: for a unit and a context, the code length saved on the next observation of one source by including the past of another, measured on the eligible-drawn stream, with its error stated. That is architecture-free, it has an interval, and it is what a routing weight may be a diagnostic for under conditions a reader would have to state.

*What the map cannot do.* It is computed over the past that exists. A pair of sources no unit supplied together has no joint token in any sequence, so the learner cannot create the co-observation that G1 says is the whole answer for a structural zero. What the learner can do to a coupling's interval is what G1's first proposition already licenses — narrow it by conditioning on a third source jointly observed with each — and no further; a learner that narrows a coupling has done that conditioning, in its own class, and a reader should know that is all it did.

**6 · Placement as amortized inference, with its errors**

A new unit's first observations, read by a learner whose weights carry the population, yield a prediction for the unit's next observation without a per-unit fit. An earlier draft called this in-context learning, which is task inference from examples of a task and coincides with placing a unit only if the training corpus is a mixture over units and the "task" is the unit's own latent — a claim about the corpus, not a property of the learner. The precise statement is G3's: placement is amortized posterior inference, a single pass rather than a fit, and it carries the two errors G3 measures — the amortization gap between the learner's output and the posterior a per-unit fit would give, and the family's own gap where the learner's class is misspecified — with every interval naming the posterior that produced it.

The placement curve — the response-factor loss on a unit's future as a function of that unit's observations in the input, on held-out units with a population prior sharp in G3's stated sense — is then read with its direction stated correctly. The learner's edge over the classical floor is in structure conditional on history, which a unit with three observations barely has, so the edge is smallest where placement happens and opens as the unit's own record grows. The curve is the efficiency of amortized inference for a unit's first days, and it should be expected to show the floor and the learner close together there.

**7 · Scaling as a constrained frontier**

*The axes are not four dials.* Under B attention units per unit per period, the number of units, the observations per unit and the number of sources are one budget: adding a source to a schedule displaces observations of the others, and a larger population at fixed B is more units at the same depth. Span is calendar time and no budget buys it. So the scaling surface is a constrained frontier, and a curve along one axis is drawn at stated values of the others.

*The loss form, stated so it can be tested.* On the eligible-drawn stream, for the response factor, the loss along an axis is an irreducible term plus a decaying term with an exponent per axis, and the exponent is the hypothesis. The irreducible term at fixed capacity is three things: within-unit noise, the part of a unit's next response that nothing about the unit predicts; response error, which G1 and G3 separate from noise only by replication at a lag short enough that true change is negligible; and class error, the misspecification a learner of fixed capacity cannot remove. An earlier draft read the asymptote of the units axis as within-unit noise alone, which its own paragraph contradicted and which conflates all three.

*The hypothesis, and what the bend is.* G1's two regimes predict that the units axis and the observations-per-unit axis decay differently: a unit's own future has a floor no number of other units lowers, and the coupling structure keeps improving with units. So the units-axis curve for unit-future prediction should bend, and the bend is not a measurement of noise — the replication of G1's fifth section identifies noise directly and separates it from response error, which no curve does — but a consistency check: the asymptote at fixed capacity should equal the replication-identified noise plus response error plus a class term estimable from the floor's gap, and a bend that does not match has found capacity or misspecification, not the population.

*The coupling target is not on this curve.* Prediction of a population coupling is a structural claim, and G3 states, and G4 and this paper's section 9 repeat, that code length is nearly flat in the parameters structural claims live in. So the units axis is drawn for unit placement and unit-future prediction in code length, and for the coupling target in posterior information about the coupling under the prior-free information set, which is a different currency on a different curve, and the two are not commensurable.

*The instrument decays along the sources axis.* The curve along sources is read on the eligible-drawn stream, whose per-source share falls as the number of sources rises, per G1, so the resolution of the measurement decays along the axis being measured. The stream's required share is derived from the smallest loss difference the curve must resolve: on the order of the response variance over the square of that difference, in never-selected-on observations, per source — which at a dozen sources and a difference of a few hundredths of a nat is a multi-percent standing charge on every unit's daily budget, stated as such.

**8 · Reading the representation**

G3 fits a distilled layer to the representation and reports its faithfulness as a log-ratio over inputs named in advance, shifted and counterfactual included. An earlier draft claimed the recipe's tools supply what G3 lacked, and misattributed twice: G3's counterfactual inputs are already interventions, at the input; and G3's open item is faithfulness with known behaviour under shift, which nothing here closes. What the recipe adds is stated exactly.

*Probes, with their conditions.* A linear probe asks whether a quantity is linearly readable from the representation, and run against a placement the classical floor already makes it has a known answer — under the floor's rotation convention, recorded with its version, since agreement is evidence that the representation encodes something in that convention's span and not that it encodes the quantity as the floor means it. The residual between probe and floor is computed net of the floor's own posterior error, as G3 computes every level's discrepancy net of that level's error, or it is inflated by an error that is not the representation's. And the probe's class is stated: a linear probe answers a linear question, and a nonlinear one answers a different question with its own capacity to find things that are not there.

*Sparse features under the admission test.* A decomposition of the representation into a sparse dictionary yields directions that may each correspond to one thing and may not, and a feature is a proposed structure until it survives G3's five clauses — found on one set of units and tested on another, surviving on the evaluation stream, predicting a source other than the one it was found in, surviving the propensity by weighting or matching, and not absorbed by a widened bias model. A dictionary not put to the test is a list of names for artefacts of the serving.

*Intervention at the representation, which is the addition.* G3 intervenes on inputs. The recipe permits intervening on the representation itself: alter a feature's value and read the change in the response-factor prediction. That answers a question input intervention cannot, whether a feature the learner holds is one it uses, and it carries a caution the earlier draft omitted: an altered feature produces an activation the learner never saw in training, so the prediction under intervention is off the training distribution, and the reading is scoped to the model and licensed to say nothing about the population until the feature has passed the five clauses.

*What none of this closes.* Faithfulness with known behaviour under shift is G3's open item and remains open; the paper lists it in section 12 rather than claiming it.

**9 · What the recipe does not supply, and what it acquires here**

An earlier draft listed four non-transfers. One is a non-transfer; the others are things the setting adds, and the sort matters because it says which is a limit of the recipe and which is a cost of the setting.

*The non-transfer: the loop.* The recipe's corpus is found; this corpus is collected by a policy that consults a learner, so the observations the learner trains on next were chosen by it. The recipe has no step for this. Here the propensity is met by import weighting, the loss is on the never-selected-on streams, every structure the learner proposes for the policy is a candidate under the admission test, and G1's exploration share is what funds observations the learner's own value estimate would never choose — the corrective for a learner-driven policy confirming itself, without which the loop is closed in the sense that matters. And once the learner's outputs drive what the units are shown or paired with, they move the phenomena the learner predicts, which no selection correction touches and only randomized withholding of learner-driven action identifies.

*An acquisition: the honesty scaffolding, with its channel named.* The two streams, the floor, the admission test, the objective check: none is part of the recipe, each is a constraint on what the learner may be credited with, and each has a channel through which a learner optimized hard enough would cost it least. The channel is the training distribution: the anchors are the most-served observations, so capacity and fit allocate to them, and a learner can be excellent on the anchor-drawn stream and poor on the eligible-drawn one without ever seeing which stream grades it. The measurement is the per-stream, per-source loss of section 3 reported apart, which makes that allocation visible. And the programme-level exposure — that the objective the learner minimizes is a proxy for what is worth knowing — is G4's, with G4's remedy: a periodic check of the objective against something it does not weight.

*An acquisition: an interval on the prior a text-pretrained shortcut imports.* The tempting shortcut renders a unit's record as text and uses a learner already trained on a great deal of text about people. What such a learner brings includes a prior over what kinds of units respond how, learned from text about kinds of people, which is a category prior in the weights. The gap between it and a learner trained from nothing on the same tokens is not that prior's contribution — the gap confounds the prior with initialization, inductive bias, sample efficiency and a corpus orders of magnitude larger — and an earlier draft published it as one. It is published as an interval: the from-scratch gap as an upper bound on the prior's contribution, a learner given only the reconstructed category tokens as a second arm, and where available a pretrained control whose corpus excluded person-descriptive text. The claim is a claim about people and not about units in general, and it is stated as the one transfer paragraph in this paper that is.

*A limit that transfers intact: a loss settles no structural claim.* This is not a non-transfer; it is true in the recipe's home setting too, which simply makes no structural claims. Here the setting makes them, so the limit binds: a lower response-factor loss has predicted better and has not identified a coupling, made an interval a point, or turned an observed event into an assigned one. Those readings route through G1's graph and G3's test, and no loss curve touches them.

**10 · What would have to hold**

*The sequence is a fold version over a ledger cut, with each decision in the cut recorded as the claim it is* — vocabulary under an invariance test, value, time's representation and resolution, refusals and expiries as tokens with a class, the fold window, the placement of fixed attributes, within-period order, context length, the event class — and the relational grain as its own sequence with a double-counting rule.

*The objective is factored into a serving factor and a response factor, and only the response factor is scored.*

*The propensity enters by import weighting toward a target stated first, never as a token or feature*, with the residual bounded and the variance priced against G1's floor; a masked objective is that reweighting and nothing else.

*Loss is reported per stream and per source apart, on the two never-selected-on streams only.*

*A unit's sequence is assembled only where one custodian holds every column it touches, or the learner trains on emitted summaries*, with G2's aggregation conditions stated; every learner version is a release with a row and a differencing budget, its leakage a lower bound against a named adversary, and erasure reaches it at the next version.

*The per-unit map is an ablation quantity on the eligible-drawn stream with an interval*, and a routing weight is at most a diagnostic; the learner creates no co-observation and narrows a coupling only by G1's third-source conditioning.

*Placement is amortized posterior inference with its two errors stated*, and the placement curve is read with the learner's edge smallest at small n.

*Scaling is a constrained frontier under B with span unpurchasable*, the loss form has an irreducible term of noise, response error and class error at fixed capacity, the bend is a consistency check against replication-identified noise, the coupling target is priced in posterior information on its own curve, and the eligible-drawn share is derived from the smallest difference to be resolved.

*Probes are linear unless stated, run under the floor's rotation convention with the residual net of the floor's posterior error; sparse features pass the five clauses or are artefacts; a representation-level intervention is scoped to the model.*

*The exploration share, the objective check and the per-stream loss are in force as the loop's correctives*, and learner-driven action is randomly withheld for identification.

*A text-pretrained shortcut's prior is published as an interval with its arms, and the claim is scoped to people.*

*A lower loss settles a predictive claim and no other.*

**11 · The potential, at full scale**

At full scale — a population whose eligible-drawn stream is wide enough to resolve the curve, cut under a versioned fold, with the objective factored and the loop's correctives in force — the recipe gives this setting three things it did not have. A per-unit, per-context map of which observations inform which, as an ablation quantity with an interval, which is coupling structure conditional on history and outside any covariance class. A placement curve that is the measured efficiency of amortized inference for a unit's first observations, with the learner's edge honestly small there and opening as the record grows. And a scaling frontier whose asymptote, checked against the noise that replication identifies, says how much of a unit's future is unpredictable at any scale — which is not a quantity only this design reads, but is one this design checks. What it gives the reading of a representation is a probe with a known answer under a stated convention, a feature under a test, and an intervention at the representation that asks whether a feature is used. And what it costs is what the home setting never paid: a factored objective, a custodian for the sequence, a released model version, and the exploration share that keeps a learner-driven policy from confirming itself.

**12 · What remains open**

Whether the invariance test that licenses a shared embedding across sources ever passes at a tolerance worth having, or whether separate vocabularies are always the honest cut. Whether the units-axis asymptote matches the replication-identified noise plus response error plus the floor's gap, or whether the mismatch is large and says more about capacity than about the population. How much of a text-pretrained learner's edge survives its interval, and whether any of it is knowledge rather than prior. Whether import weights toward the eligible-drawn target can be computed at all when the serving distribution is itself the learner's output, which is the loop's identification question. Whether a representation-level intervention can be given a population reading, which is the step from a psychometrics of a representation to a psychometrics of units and is not taken here. Faithfulness with known behaviour under shift, which G3 left open and this paper leaves open. And whether a learner can be kept from allocating to the anchors over years of retraining while its scored loss falls, which is the scaffolding's long-run question and the one that decides whether the recipe finds structure here or reproduces the serving.
