# Two levers

*General paper G4: a frame in which a research programme over a measured population has two levers on what it knows — what the observations carry, and what the mechanism extracts — the three things that frame cannot see, and the conditions under which a mature programme pulls the first lever more than the second.*

**Status: theory research.** Nothing here describes any product; `research/README.md` says what a general paper is and what holds it to its setting. G1 supplies the value of a source on the evaluation stream, the two streams, the honesty constraints and the identification result this paper must respect; G2 the ledgers the levers are recorded in; G3 the mechanisms, the classical floor, the admission test and the claim types code length does not settle.

**Perfect-form test.** If the app did not exist, this would still be worth saying because a programme that organizes its decisions as two levers — observe more or better, extract more from what is observed — and measures where it can rather than argues where it cannot, has not removed theory but relocated it to a small number of places where it becomes a written, priced object; and the value of that relocation, and its limits, can be stated without naming any field, which is what makes the disagreements a programme lives on partly resolvable by a number and honest about the part that is not.

**Setting.** A population of units measured by K sources under a budget, as in G1: each source yields a response through a measurement model from a latent state, with a bias structure, a cost in attention units, a custody class and a grain. The object of discovery is the coupling structure among the sources' latent quantities — across units, within units over periods, and on relations — and G1 establishes that some of that structure is a matter of identification, not estimation: a coupling on a pair of sources never jointly observed is an interval no estimator narrows. Add a *programme*: an ongoing enterprise that decides, over time, what to observe and how to analyse it, run by people who begin with beliefs about what matters. This paper adopts a frame in which those decisions are organized as two levers, and is honest that the frame is a choice rather than a theorem. Nothing here depends on what the units, the sources, or the beliefs are.

**Abstract.** What a programme knows about a population's coupling structure moves with two levers: what the observations carry about that structure, which a programme raises by observing more or better, and what the mechanism recovers of what they carry, which a programme raises by analysing better. This paper adopts that frame, states plainly that it is a frame and not a decomposition proved complete — a programme is *defined* as making decisions of those two kinds, so "there is no third move" is a stipulation, and the paper says which real decisions the stipulation strains — and develops a bounded thesis: a programme that measures where it can and writes down what it cannot measure shifts its arguments from the day-to-day choice of what to study to three places where theory relocates and becomes legible. The measurable half is real. A source's value in G1's sense — the code length it saves on the evaluation stream, marginal to the portfolio — is a number a belief cannot fake, so a disagreement about what matters, *given a fixed target*, becomes a disagreement about which source to fund and is settled after the fact. The unmeasurable half is named rather than hidden: the target itself is a theory, so the value is belief-relative through the target; the candidate set is a belief, and its omissions are exactly as invisible as a theory-first programme's; identification is a state of a coupling the value ledger does not see, so the single most valuable observation G1 admits can register near zero in it; and the method's own failure mode, optimizing a proxy target until the number rises while the thing it stood for does not, is one the evaluation stream does not catch, because the evaluation stream defends against a mechanism grading its own data and not against a target that was the wrong thing to know. The asymmetry that would make a mature programme source-driven is stated with its premises exposed: it holds for a fixed target and a fixed model class, and a class-expanding mechanism or a fixed budget that a new source must be carved out of each breaks it, so "source-driven in the long run" is a claim under conditions and not a law. What the frame makes decidable, the constraints that keep it from fooling itself, and the transfer to three kinds of programme follow, with the two-programme test that would put the thesis at risk stated so that one of its outcomes refutes rather than reduces it.

**1 · The frame, and the honesty about it being one**

Fix a population, a budget and a moment. Organize what a programme could do to know more into two levers. It can raise what the observations carry about the coupling structure — add a source, tighten a source's measurement model, lower its cost, sharpen its grain, or allocate the budget so the same sources carry more. Or it can raise what the mechanism recovers of what they carry — a model that expresses structure the last one could not, estimates it with less error, or judges it more honestly.

That is a frame, and its completeness is a stipulation, not a result. The Setting *defines* a programme as making decisions of those two kinds, so a claim that there is no third kind is true by that definition and cannot be evidence for anything. The frame earns its place only if the decisions a real programme faces fall into the two levers without being forced, and this paper is explicit about three that strain the fit. Changing the budget, recruiting units, or extending the periods raises what the observations carry without touching any source, and is folded into the first lever only by treating allocation as part of "observe better" — a reduction the paper states and defends in section 5 rather than assuming. Changing the target — what counts as a coupling worth knowing — changes the metric the levers are read in rather than either lever, so it is not a move within the frame but a re-specification of the frame, and section 3 places it accordingly. And improving the honesty apparatus — a larger evaluation stream, a stronger floor — consumes budget and changes what the programme can *see* about its own levers without directly raising either, which the frame treats as a governance act and not a lever. A frame that hid these would be worse than the arguments it replaces; naming them is the price of using it.

There is no product-form identity here, and an earlier draft asserted one. "Knowledge is the observations times the extraction" is either contentless — if extraction is defined as knowledge over observations, the equation is a tautology — or it presumes an information measure the frame has not fixed, and G1 is explicit that the candidate measures order sources differently and agree only in a limit. So the paper keeps the two levers as two levers, names the measure it uses when it needs one (code length on the evaluation stream, per G1), and makes no claim that knowledge factors.

**2 · Theory-first and measure-where-you-can, as two programmes**

A theory-first programme begins with a belief about the structure, derives a claim, and observes what tests it. Its reach is bounded by the claims its participants can derive, and its next decision is chosen by which claim is most interesting, which is a judgement about beliefs held where they cannot be read.

A measure-where-you-can programme begins with a portfolio and a mechanism, measures the value of each source in G1's sense on the stream it did not select on, and chooses among the decisions it *can* price by the price. Its reach is bounded by the structure the sources carry and the mechanism can express — a bound the paper counts rather than hides — and its next *priced* decision is chosen by a number.

Neither is pure, and the second is not theory-free; section 3 says where its theory lives and section 4 says why writing it there is an improvement over holding it in the head. The paper does not claim the second programme knows more. It claims the second programme's beliefs are legible and its priced decisions are settled by measurement, and it is honest that "priced decisions" is a proper subset of "decisions."

**3 · Where theory relocates — four places, not three**

The method does not remove theory. It moves it, and a programme that believes it has become theory-free has stopped looking at these.

*The candidate set.* A source's value can be measured only once someone has proposed it, so the set of candidates is a belief about what could matter, and its omissions are invisible — exactly as invisible as what a theory-first programme's participants cannot derive. The difference is not that this belief is measurable; it is not. The difference is that a candidate set is a list that can be published, extended by anyone, and annotated with each entry's reason, where a theory-first programme's beliefs are a habit of mind. Legible is not the same as measured, and the paper does not conflate them.

*The objective, or target.* The mechanism optimizes a scalarization — bits about population couplings, about unit placement, about within-unit dynamics — and every weight in it is a claim about what is worth knowing. A source's measured value is a value *for that target*, so the number is belief-relative through the target even though it is not fakeable given the target. A programme that calls itself data-driven while leaving its target unstated has hidden its largest theory in the place hardest to find.

*The units and grains.* What is a unit, what is a period, whether a relation is an object: theories of what exists, prior to every source, and a programme cannot price a grain it did not define.

*The model class.* An earlier draft omitted this and section 5 silently depended on it. The class the mechanism can express is a theory of what shape the structure has, and G3 makes the classical-versus-learned question an assumption settled by a race rather than known in advance. The class matters here specifically because the asymmetry of section 5 is stated against a fixed class, and a mechanism move that expands the class is a move that changes what the bound even means.

So theory is confined to four written objects, and everything outside them that can be priced is chosen by price. That is a smaller and more honest claim than the slogan, and it is the one that survives the rest of the paper.

**4 · What the measurable half buys, and the two things it does not**

*A measured value cannot be argued with, only re-measured — given the target.* G1 defines a source's value as its code-length saving on the evaluation stream, marginal to the portfolio. That number is computed after the source is in place, so hope cannot inflate it; it is relative to the portfolio, so a duplicate is worth what its duplication leaves; and it is measured on the stream the policy never selected on, so a mechanism cannot credit itself with its own data selection. Two people who disagree about which of two funded sources mattered are settled by the ledger. The qualifier is load-bearing and the earlier draft dropped it: the value is a value for the target, and if the two disagree about the target they disagree in relocation two, which no ledger settles.

*The ledger settles the funded, not the choice to fund.* A source's value exists only once it is funded, so a disagreement about *which* candidate to fund is not settled by measurement — it is settled by whoever wins the funding argument, and the unfunded candidate's value is a cell never collected, a structural zero in G1's exact sense. This is the pilot problem, and it belongs here as a limit on the thesis rather than in the open questions as an afterthought. How long even the funded comparison takes is set by the stream's resolution: G1 shows that separating two code lengths that differ by a small amount takes observations on the order of the variance over the square of that difference, so the closer the contest the longer the wait, which is exactly the contested case.

*The direction is a gradient only within a lever, and only after the horizon is priced.* Ranking sources against sources is G1's price list; ranking mechanism improvements against each other is a code-length comparison on the evaluation stream. Ranking a source against a mechanism improvement is not a gradient at all, because the two are priced in different units — a source costs attention units, a mechanism improvement costs development effort, and the frame has no exchange rate between them. So the across-lever choice is a judgement the paper does not dress as a measurement. And within the source lever, the ledger entry must be the value of the *bundle* a source belongs to, not its standalone marginal, because G1 shows a source whose whole worth is to unlock a later pairing prices at zero under a greedy per-cost rule and is never taken; the value ledger inherits G1's horizon and bundle terms or it systematically underprices the sources that matter most.

*Failure is visible for one failure and invisible for the worst one.* A theory-first programme learns it looked in the wrong place when a claim fails, which can take years. A measure-where-you-can programme learns that a *priced* avenue is exhausted when the ledger stops moving. But the failure the method uniquely invites is the opposite of a stalled ledger: a target that is a poor proxy for what is worth knowing produces a ledger that keeps rising while the thing the target stood for does not move, and the evaluation stream does not catch it, because the code length is computed against that same target. This is the method's Goodhart exposure and it is real; section 6 makes a fourth constraint out of it. The honest summary is that the method replaces one unmeasurable bias — beliefs in the head, expressed as which claims get tested — with a smaller unmeasurable bias in the objective's proxy quality plus the candidate set's omissions, and a measurable core in between. That the replaced bias is smaller is the claim; that it is zero is not.

**5 · The asymmetry, stated with its premises exposed**

An earlier draft argued that extraction has a ceiling of one so mechanism value vanishes while source value does not, and that a mature programme is therefore source-driven. The argument is not a law; it holds under conditions, and the conditions are where the content is.

*It is stated against a fixed target and a fixed model class.* "How much of what the observations carry the mechanism recovers" is defined only once the target and the class are fixed, and G3's central mechanism — a learned representation that expresses structure the classical class cannot — is precisely a move that expands the class. Against a fixed class the crossover is a recovered fraction approaching a ceiling; against a moving class the ceiling moves with the mechanism, and the asymmetry does not hold. So the claim is: for a fixed target and a fixed class, mechanism improvements have diminishing returns as recovery approaches what that class can express, while a new source can raise what the observations carry about that target. Change the class and the second clause is what a mechanism move does.

*A new source raises what the observations carry only at a fixed or larger budget.* Under G1's per-unit budget, adding a source to a unit's schedule displaces observations of the sources it replaces, thinning their co-observation edges and possibly lowering what the observations carry about the couplings among them. So "a new source raises the bound" holds at a fixed or increased budget and is false in general, which makes budget growth a third lever hiding inside the first and is one reason section 1 flags allocation.

*The two levers are complements as much as substitutes.* If one wrote the two effects additively, a new source's benefit would scale with how much the mechanism can currently extract, and a mechanism improvement's benefit would scale with how much the observations currently carry — so a large, rich portfolio makes mechanism work *more* valuable, not less, which is the opposite of the slogan. Only the fixed-class ceiling pulls the other way. The net direction is an empirical balance between a diminishing recovery fraction and a growing thing to recover from, and it is not settled by the frame.

*So "source-driven in the long run" is a conditional.* It holds where the target and class are stable, the budget can grow, and mechanism improvement has in fact run into its class ceiling. Those are two empirical premises — that mechanism improvement is exhaustible and source value is not — and both are stated as assumptions with their failure cases: source value is *not* inexhaustible for a finite-dimensional latent structure, where the sources past its dimension are redundant by construction; and mechanism improvement is retroactive, re-extracting from the whole accumulated archive at once, while a new source starts with no co-observation edges and its couplings are intervals until units accrue, so mechanism value is immediate where source value is delayed by design. The paper claims the conditional, names the premises, and does not claim the law.

*The state is not directly observed, and its proxy is weak.* Whether a programme is near its recovery ceiling is read from the trend in mechanism gains against the floor, and that proxy cannot tell an exhausted class from exhausted imagination, because successive mechanism improvements are proposed by the same people whose beliefs bound the programme. A programme that reads diminishing mechanism returns and stops funding mechanism work guarantees the trend continues — a self-confirming loop of the kind G1 corrects for sources with an exploration share and which this paper must correct for mechanisms the same way. And code length is blind to exactly the mechanism improvements that matter most for the stated object of discovery, because G3 says code length does not settle structural or causal claims — a mechanism that buys a better identification result or a cleaner bias term registers as a flat trend and is misread as evidence the programme is observation-bound. So the state proxy is split by claim type: a code-length trend for predictive claims, a separate identification-and-falsification record for structural ones, and neither alone licenses a move.

**6 · What the frame makes decidable, and the constraints that keep it honest**

*The value ledger.* Each funded source's marginal value in G1's sense, per target, on the evaluation stream, as the bundle it belongs to — renamed here the value ledger to keep it distinct from G1's co-observation graph and G2's record of attempts, three different objects an earlier draft all called "the ledger."

*Identification, tracked separately.* A coupling is in one of two states before it is a number: identified, or an interval no estimator narrows because the pair was never co-observed. The value ledger, being a predictive code-length saving, does not see this — a source whose whole worth is to co-observe a pair and turn an interval into a point can save little predictive code length and register near zero — so identification is tracked as its own state per coupling, and the move that changes it, a co-observation edge, is priced by what it identifies and not only by what it predicts. This is the correction for finding that the frame's headline number is blind to G1's most valuable move.

*The state of the programme, per claim type.* Observation-bound or extraction-bound, from the split proxy of section 5, which decides which lever to pull and admits that the decision is under-determined near the ceiling.

*The order, and what its path dependence is a property of.* A source's value is portfolio-relative, so the order of additions changes each measured value, and the order is recorded. The averaged-over-orders value is a real quantity and it is the one an earlier draft over-promised: it splits credit symmetrically between two redundant sources, so it says keep both where the marginal reading and section 8's retirement rule say drop one, and many of its coalitions are portfolios the mechanism was never fitted on and on which G1 says a rare source's value falls below the stream's resolution. So the averaged number is named, its use is stated — a fair attribution across a fixed portfolio, never a retirement test — and the marginal reading is what retirement takes. And the path dependence it measures is a property of the population, the portfolio, the objective and the mechanism version jointly, published with the last two named, not a property of the population alone.

*The next-source question, priced honestly.* Which candidate to fund is answered by a pilot at a stated cost with an interval, and the paper concedes the pilot problem of section 4: the answer exists only for candidates funded enough to measure.

*The four constraints that are the price of the method.* Three are G1's and G3's, corrected here from an earlier draft's misstatement. The exploration share, which funds sources — and, this paper adds, mechanisms — the ledger's trend would starve, so the self-confirming loop of section 5 is corrected on both levers. The admission test, which stops the loop that chooses its own data from manufacturing a factor out of its own missingness. The two evaluation streams — anchor-drawn and eligible-drawn, kept separate as G1 requires, since pricing a non-anchor source needs the eligible one, and an earlier draft used one stream throughout. And the fourth, which the method's Goodhart exposure requires and no prior paper supplies: a periodic check of the objective against something it does not weight — a held-out target, an external criterion, or a deliberate perturbation of the objective's weights — because the evaluation stream defends against a mechanism grading its own data and not against the target being the wrong thing to know. Coverage, which an earlier draft listed as a constraint "correcting a bias the objective has," is not one: G1 says the objective seeks the uncertain rather than neglecting them, names two other starvation mechanisms with two different correctors, and calls coverage beyond those a stated preference with a policy parameter. So coverage sits in relocation two as a written weight, not among the constraints in force, and the paper says so.

A programme that adopts the two levers without these four is more biased than a theory-first one, because its biases are invisible to it.

**7 · The transfer**

The setting names no field, so the frame applies wherever units are measured by sources under a budget. Three kinds of programme show what the two levers are, stated as conditionals a reader can check rather than as claims about what fields do, and without enumerating instrument kinds, since an instrument list is where a general frame quietly becomes one instance.

*A programme measuring persons.* The sources are whatever instruments read a person, of any grain. The frame predicts: a programme that adds instruments without pricing each one's value marginal to its portfolio will accumulate instruments that duplicate the portfolio, because nothing retires them; and a programme that keeps adding instruments to the same units without the admission test will report structure that is its own missingness pattern, because instruments added to a fixed population co-vary through who answered which. Both are checkable predictions of the frame, and the constraint that binds hardest is the admission test.

*A programme measuring agents and exchanges.* The sources are records of choice and the interventions the programme itself makes, and here the frame inherits G3's causal apparatus: a coupling among records of choice is a marginal regression until a design identifies it, so the value of a record type is entangled with the identification state of section 6, and the programme's own interventions are observations whose propensity must be logged or the programme's actions become the confound it cannot see. The frame predicts that a programme which does not log its own interventions will price its records against a stream its actions contaminated. The constraint that binds hardest is that log.

*A programme developing a learned system.* The sources are the data the system trains and evaluates on; the mechanism is its architecture and objective. If the frame's two-lever stipulation is adopted, "this kind of programme already runs on two levers" is true by that stipulation and confirms nothing, so the frame's content here is not that observation but a prediction: a system that selects or generates the data it trains on has closed the loop the admission test polices, and a programme that evaluates it on data the system influenced has lost the eligible-drawn stream, so its measured gains are its own selection returned to it. The constraint that binds hardest is the separation of the evaluation stream from the data the system touched.

**8 · What would have to hold**

*The four relocations are written objects* — the candidate set with reasons, the objective as one scalarization, the units and grains, and the model class — producible on request, or the programme has theory it is not looking at.

*A source's value is G1's* — marginal to the portfolio, per target, on the eligible-drawn stream, as a bundle — and never a bare correlation, which is what a source's champion offers instead.

*Identification is tracked as a state and priced separately from predictive value*, so the move that turns an interval into a point is not lost in a ledger that only sees prediction.

*The four constraints are in force* — exploration on both levers, the admission test, two separated streams, and the objective check against something it does not weight — as the price of the method, because without them its biases exceed the ones it replaced and are invisible.

*The across-lever choice is named a judgement*, not dressed as a measurement, until an exchange rate between attention units and development effort is stated as a policy parameter.

*The classical floor runs continuously and the state proxy is split by claim type*, so a flat code-length trend is not read as a stalled programme where the live lever is identification.

*The value averaged over orders is used only for attribution*, never for retirement, and published with the mechanism version and objective it was measured under.

*A decision is recorded with its measured value before and after where a value exists*, and recorded as a judgement where one does not, so the programme's history distinguishes the two.

**9 · The potential, at full scale**

At full scale — a programme run long enough on a large population under the four constraints — the frame offers something no theory-first programme has: a standing answer to *what should we do next* that is a measurement wherever a measurement is possible and a named judgement wherever it is not, with the boundary between the two written down rather than blurred. For a programme measuring persons, that is a value per instrument, a retirement rule, and an admission test standing between a new instrument and a manufactured factor. For a programme measuring agents and exchanges, it is a value per record type under the identification the causal apparatus supplies, with the programme's own interventions logged so they do not become its confound. For a programme developing a learned system, it is the name for what the field already does and the one separation it most often loses, between the system's own influence and the stream that is supposed to grade it. None of these is a discovery; each is a way of turning the largest recurring argument in a research programme — where to spend the next unit of budget — into a decision that carries its reasons on its face. The ceiling on all of it is the unmeasurable half of section 4: the frame makes the priced decisions legible and cannot make the target, the candidate set, or the pilot's chicken-and-egg go away, and a programme that forgets this has mistaken a smaller bias for none.

**10 · What would put the thesis at risk**

The thesis is a claim about programmes and is testable on them, and the test must be built so that one of its outcomes refutes rather than reduces it. Two programmes on disjoint unit samples from one population at matched budgets over one span, several teams per arm so the outcome is not a single draw of programme-level noise, and a metric set fixed before either arm runs and chosen by neither — including at least one non-predictive criterion settled by identification or falsification, since a purely predictive metric is one arm's theory of what is worth knowing and G3 says it is nearly flat in the parameters structural claims live in. The refuting outcome is stated in advance: if the measure-where-you-can arm does not exceed the theory-first arm on the pre-registered metric set beyond the between-team interval, the thesis that the method shifts a programme's centre of gravity is not supported, and convergence is that outcome and not a second win. The reduction to section 3 — that the two differ only in where they keep their theory — is the fallback the abstract already concedes, not a result the test returns. The test is expensive and it is the only one, and its known confound, that a well-run theory-first programme measures its claims and a well-run measure-where-you-can programme proposes from beliefs, is why the metric set and the team replication are what the comparison rests on rather than a single pair of programmes.

**11 · What remains open**

Whether the state of a programme can be read from its split proxy with enough precision to choose a lever near the ceiling, or whether the proxy is too weak exactly where the choice matters. How to price a candidate whose value exists only once it is funded, which is the pilot problem and has no free solution. Whether the averaged-over-orders value can be estimated at all on a portfolio of many sources, where the coalitions the average needs are portfolios the stream cannot resolve. Whether the objective check of section 6 can find a Goodharted target before the programme has spent years optimizing it, or whether a proxy that is wrong is only ever caught from outside. Whether the two-programme test of section 9 can be run on a population that is not itself a programme's own subject. And the one the frame cannot answer about itself: whether four relocation sites are all of them, or whether a programme run long enough finds a fifth — which the frame would have to record as a re-specification of itself, not as a move, because a move is one of two things and a new place for theory is neither.
