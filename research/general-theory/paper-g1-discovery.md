# The discovery problem

*General theory, paper G1: how to find the connections between measurement sources on the same units as efficiently as possible, when observation is budgeted.*

**Status: theory research.** General track: this paper is about any population measured by many sources, and it names no particular product or source. `research/README.md` says what a paper is and how the tracks relate.

**Perfect-form test.** If the app did not exist, this would still be worth saying because every system that measures the same units through several sources under a budget faces one problem before any statistics: which observations to take so that the connections between the sources can be known at all, and the theory of that choice has been written for single instruments and for found data, not for many sources on chosen data.

**Setting.** A population of units. Units may stand in relations, pairs or groups, and time passes in periods. There are K sources. A source, applied to a unit at a period, yields a response through a measurement model from the unit's latent state, with a bias structure that depends on how the source collects, a cost in attention units, a custody class that says where its raw values may travel, and a grain: fixed, one value per unit; slow, changing across years; occasion, changing across periods; or relational, defined on a pair or group rather than a unit. Each unit supplies at most B attention units per period, and B may differ across units. The object of discovery is the coupling structure: how the sources' latent quantities covary across units, how they co-move within a unit across periods, and what a relation carries that its members do not. Nothing in this paper depends on what the units or the sources are.

**Abstract.** When data are found, the estimator is the whole of the method. When data are chosen under a per-unit budget, the estimator is downstream of the choice, and this paper is the theory of the choice. It states eight propositions with their arguments. A coupling between two sources is point-identified without assumption only from units observed on both; without co-observation it is an interval fixed by the marginals, and it is the strength of an untestable assumption, never the number of units, that closes the interval. The map of what can be known is therefore the weighted co-observation graph over sources, whose connectivity is necessary and not sufficient for a single latent space: the condition is rank, and the invariance of the linking measurement across the units it joins. Where a pair of sources was never co-observed the choice of what to observe is the whole answer, which is a theorem; elsewhere the choice's advantage over estimation is a quantity measured against a named comparison, and for the placement of one unit borrowing from the population dominates whenever the unit's own information is small relative to the between-unit variance it must resolve. The objective is expected information gain per attention unit, with the three criteria that appear in this paper named and kept apart, over a horizon in which enabling observations are scored as bundles, as an explicit scalarization of population-level and unit-level targets with a stated exploration rule, a hazard on the continuation term, and a coverage preference written down as one. Six constraints keep it honest, and they are three sides of one property, that selection remain estimable, plus three about linking and time. Allocation is planned missingness whose decision variable is the set of sources assigned to each unit and whose solution equalizes marginal return per attention unit. Roles attach to observations, not to pairs: a coupling is estimated only on records taken blind to the source that would serve as its prior. The value of a source is its saving relative to the current portfolio on a stream the policy never chose, and a new source is admitted by a test that splits the units that proposed it from the units that test it. And computation binds in exactly two places, which are named.

**1 · Why this is a problem of its own**

Most theory of finding structure in data assumes the data exist. The population has been measured, and the question is what to compute. The setting here does not have that shape. The data are a tensor of units by sources by periods that is almost entirely empty, and it is empty by design: each unit can contribute B attention units per period, the sources cost attention, and nothing is in the tensor until a choice puts it there. Two systems with identical units, identical sources and identical budgets, differing only in which cells they chose to fill, can differ by orders of magnitude in what can be learned from them.

The connections between sources are not in any source. A source alone yields a marginal: how its quantity is distributed. A connection is a joint fact, and a joint fact exists in the data only where two sources were observed on the same units. So discovery is not a matter of computing harder on what one has. It is a matter of having observed the right pairs on the right units, and that is decided by the choice. This paper is the theory of that choice, and it is a different theory from estimation, though it constrains estimation and is constrained by it.

**2 · Identification by co-observation**

*Proposition 1.* A coupling between sources j and k is point-identified without assumption only from units observed on both. Where no unit has been observed on both, the coupling lies in an interval fixed by the two marginals and narrowed by conditioning on any third source jointly observed with each; it is point-identified only by an assumption the design cannot test, conditional independence given that third source, or a latent model whose loadings the rest of the graph over-determines, and it is the strength of that assumption, never the number of units, that closes the interval.

*Argument.* A coupling is a property of the joint distribution of the two latent quantities. The marginals constrain the joint to the family of joint distributions with those marginals, whose width is a property of the marginals: where they are extreme the interval is narrow and marginal-only observation is nearly enough, where they are diffuse it is wide. A third source m observed jointly with j on some units and with k on others narrows the interval by conditioning, by however much of j and k it explains, and no assumption is needed for that narrowing. An assumption of conditional independence given m does not narrow the interval further; it selects a point inside it, the point at which m carries the whole coupling, and nothing in the data distinguishes that point from its neighbours. A latent model that fixes both loadings from elsewhere in the graph likewise yields a point, testable only where the graph over-determines it. Units observed on j alone or on k alone sharpen the marginals and with them the estimated endpoints of the interval; what they never move is its identified width, which the marginals fix.

*Two widths.* A coupling on an edge of weight zero is an interval fixed by identification. A coupling on a light edge is a point with sampling error. The two widths are not the same quantity, they respond to different remedies, joint observation for the first and more of it for the second, and every reported coupling states which it carries.

*Consequence: the co-observation graph.* Take the sources as nodes and weight the edge between two sources by the number of units observed on both, indexed by the covariates the coupling will be conditioned on. This graph is the map of what the system can know without assumption. It is not a fact about the population; it is a fact about the design, and it is the design's to change.

*Consequence: what links the graph.* Connectivity of the graph is necessary for one latent space and is not sufficient. What links two components is the rank of what they share: linking a d-dimensional space requires d linearly independent directions co-observed across every cut of the graph, not one edge of any weight, and weight buys precision on a link rank has already made identifiable. The bridge must also mean the same thing on both sides. Where the linking measurement's loadings, thresholds or bias differ across the units it joins, the common scale it appears to supply is an artefact, so invariance of the linking measurement across those units is an identification requirement of the same standing as connectivity, and the design buys the observations that test it. Linking is by content where units supply the same observations and only through the source's estimated parameters where they supply different draws from it; the second is the weaker link and is reported as one.

*Consequence: relations.* A relational source is jointly observed with a unit-level source only through units that stand in relations, so its edges are weighted by units that are both members of a recorded relation and observed on the unit-level source. And what a relation carries beyond its members is identified only by replication within the relation: the same pair observed more than once, or observed in both directions, separates the relationship-specific quantity from the members' own and from error. One observation per pair confounds all three however many pairs there are, so the design buys repeated and reciprocal pairs, and the relational quantity is reported as unidentified where it did not.

**3 · Two regimes: where the choice dominates and where it does not**

*Proposition 2.* Where a pair of sources has never been co-observed, the choice of what to observe is the whole answer. Elsewhere the choice's advantage over estimation for a population coupling is a quantity measured against a named comparison policy, and for the placement of one unit, borrowing from the population dominates the next observation whenever the unit's own information is small relative to the between-unit variance the placement must resolve.

*The theorem.* A cell never collected is a structural zero, and no estimator recovers it. For a population coupling on a pair of sources with no co-observed units, the design decides between an interval and a point, and that decision is the whole of what can be known about the pair. This is the part of the proposition that is a theorem, and it is the part the hierarchy of section 9 rests on.

*The weaker claim.* Between two policies that both co-observe every pair, the gap is a constant factor in effective sample size, which a better estimator can close and often does. The unbounded ratio between the information carried by the best and the worst single observation, an observation at a unit's frontier against one the model already predicts, shows only that some observations are worthless; it does not measure the gap between reachable policies. Design dominates where it creates or removes structural zeros; elsewhere its advantage over estimation is measured against a named comparison policy and not asserted. And an observation is worth nothing only inside the model that predicts it, which is why the exploration rule of section 4 does not price its observations this way.

*The unit regime.* A unit under a budget of B attention units per period yields, over P periods, at most B times P times the information a single response carries about the latent state, itself bounded by the response's entropy and strictly below it by the source's measurement error. Whether that total is small is not an absolute matter. The unit's position in a latent space is mostly a borrowed posterior when the per-unit information is small relative to the between-unit variance the placement must resolve, when the reliability of the unit-level estimate is low, and a heterogeneous population with informative responses can put a unit's own observations ahead of the prior at the same budget. The regime is a ratio, and the design computes it rather than assuming it. A system that confused the two regimes would either starve its couplings, by optimizing each unit's placement and never manufacturing the joint observations the couplings need, or over-ask its units, by serving observations whose information the prior already supplied.

**4 · The objective**

*Proposition 3.* The choice is well posed as expected information gain per attention unit, under a criterion named per target, over a stated horizon in which enabling observations are scored as bundles, as an explicit scalarization of population-level and unit-level targets, with an exploration rule stated in one of three forms, a hazard on the continuation term, and a coverage preference written down as a preference.

*Three criteria, kept apart.* Three criteria appear in this paper and they are not the same quantity: the posterior entropy of the coupling parameters, which section 6 minimizes; the code length of a stated finite window of future observations, which section 8 measures; and the posterior variance of one named coupling, which orders candidates differently again. They agree only in the limit of an unbounded window under a correct model. The objective names which one it optimizes for which target, and the price list of section 8 is stated in the predictive criterion because that is the one a held-out stream can measure.

*The scalarization.* Bits about the population's couplings, bits about a unit's placement and bits about a unit's dynamics are reductions in different posteriors. Summing them requires an exchange rate, and there is no theorem that supplies it: how many bits about the population are worth one bit about this unit is a policy parameter, stated, revisited, and never presented as a discovered quantity. Writing it down is what makes the objective a design rather than a slogan.

*The horizon, and bundles.* An observation whose worth is that it enables a later pairing is not valued by crediting it forward; that prices it at zero and it is never taken. Its value is the option the planned pairing gives it, so the two are scored as a bundle. Complementarity of that kind breaks the diminishing returns under which a greedy rule on gain per attention unit is near-optimal, so the per-cost ratio is the myopic part of the objective and not the objective, and a policy that drops the future term systematically undervalues the observations that create co-observation, which section 3 says are the ones that matter most.

*Exploration.* Expected information gain is computed inside the current model, and it cannot value an observation whose worth is that it would show the model to be wrong. So a share of the budget is allocated outside the model in a stated sense: by a non-adaptive rule; or by maximizing disagreement among a declared set of rival model classes; or by maximizing expected surprise under a nonparametric alternative. A value is always computed under some model, so what makes this share outside is that the rule spending it does not read the current model's posterior. The share names which of the three it uses and whether the uniform stream of section 5 is drawn from it or is a separate charge on the budget. Section 8's self-extension is funded by this share.

*The hazard.* A unit that stops supplying observations yields nothing forever. The value of an observation is its immediate gain plus its continuation value weighted by the probability the unit is still present, which depends on what the unit was asked this period; the hazard multiplies the continuation term and not the gain already collected. This is how the experience of being observed enters the objective without becoming the objective.

*Coverage, as a preference.* An information-seeking policy does not neglect the uncertain; it seeks them. Two other things starve a rare subpopulation. Bits weighted by population mass are worth more where the mass is, so a policy scored on the average unit serves the majority. And expected gain is computed inside the current model: where a subpopulation is not represented in the model at all, its variation reads as noise, noise carries no expected gain, and the parts of the population the model has never fitted are exactly the ones it does not know to ask about. The first is corrected by the weight and the second by the exploration share. Beyond those two corrections, valuing information about a subpopulation in proportion to how little the model can say about it is a stated preference of the same standing as the exchange rate: a policy parameter, written down, and not a consequence of the objective.

**5 · Constraints that keep the objective honest**

*Proposition 4.* Unconstrained maximization of expected information fails in at least six ways. Three of the failures are one property seen from three sides, that selection must remain estimable, and three concern linking and time.

*The floor.* A deterministic choice of the best observation gives every other eligible observation a probability of zero, and with that it destroys the overlap that makes selection estimable: no counterfactual to estimate the selection term against, no residual on observations never taken, no reading of what a unit would have supplied. Every eligible observation carries a probability bounded below by a floor, and the realised probability is logged with the response. The floor has a price: corrective weights run as large as the reciprocal of the floor, so the floor trades the variance of the corrected estimate against the budget it spends on observations the policy did not want, and it is chosen on that arithmetic.

*The log.* Every observation carries the reason it was taken, the target it served, its expected gain, the hazard and coverage terms, the unit's state, and its realised probability. Selection is then a known function of recorded variables. That makes the design ignorable given the log; it does not make an analysis that ignores the log valid, and it does not survive a misspecified outcome model: under adaptive selection an estimator fitted as though the data were drawn at random is biased even when every probability is known. So the propensity is entered, by weighting or by conditioning on the history, and inference is computed in a form valid under sequential selection. The log covers what was served, not who stayed: attrition is a second mechanism, not ignorable given the log, and it is carried by the hazard model of section 10. Steering on the current estimate of a coupling is not forbidden, since the estimate is a function of the past and selection stays ignorable given the log, but it makes the selection probability depend on the quantity being estimated, so the propensity enters that coupling's estimator rather than being ignored. What must not happen is steering on anything measured after, or jointly with, the response it selects.

*The evaluation streams.* A fixed fraction of each unit's periods is drawn uniformly from the eligible observations and never selected on. Drawn from the anchor set alone it calibrates the anchors and nothing else; drawn from the eligible set it can price any source, at the cost that each source's share of it falls as the number of sources rises. Both are needed and they are separate streams, reported separately, and neither is ever averaged with the adaptive stream, on which the policy can improve a model's number by taking predictable observations. The evaluation stream's size is set by what it must detect: separating a marginal code-length improvement of a given size takes a number of uniform observations of that source on the order of the response variance over the square of the improvement, so a source served rarely on the adaptive stream is served at a raised rate on the uniform one, and a source whose value is below the stream's resolution is admitted on the exploration share rather than on the value test.

*The anchor set.* A set of observations is taken on every unit regardless of expected gain, on a schedule. It places every unit in one space, and it gives every pair of sources it contains a floor of joint observation; flooring every pair is quadratic in the number of sources and unaffordable at any budget, so non-anchor pairs take their floor from the allocation of section 6. An anchor is retired only through a linking period in which the retiring anchor and its replacement are co-observed on a common sample, because retiring an anchor without that overlap breaks every comparison that ran through it. It is also the most repeated observation in the system and therefore the most exposed to learning, so its invariance is under continuous test: an anchor whose measurement model has drifted corrupts every comparison it carries while appearing to hold them together, and the design buys the observations that would detect it.

*Repetition.* Within-unit structure needs repeated observation of the same source on the same unit, at lags whose shortest is below the timescale of the change, since a band faster than the fastest lag realized is unidentifiable however the lags are spread, and unequally spaced so that periodic components are not aliased onto one another. Separating change from measurement error additionally needs replication at a lag short enough that true change is negligible; without it the state variance and the error variance are one quantity. Repetition also teaches, so schedules are bounded by each source's measured learning curve. Where a source is keyed, first exposure is staggered across units, which decorrelates practice from calendar time and not from age: age, period and cohort are linearly dependent by construction, so one of the three is fixed by assumption whatever the design, and the assumption is stated rather than hidden in the schedule.

**6 · Allocation as planned missingness**

*Proposition 5.* The allocation of budget across sources is a planned-missingness design whose decision variable is the set of sources assigned to each unit, whose objective is a named scalar summary of the uncertainty of the coupling structure, and whose solution equalizes the marginal reduction in that uncertainty per attention unit across pairs, every pair held at or above a floor.

*Argument.* Let each pair of sources carry a current uncertainty, an interval where never co-observed and a posterior width where co-observed, and a value, how much the rest of the structure would sharpen if this coupling were known. Minimizing a scalar summary of the structure's uncertainty subject to the budget equalizes marginal return per unit cost across pairs. Where a pair's uncertainty falls as the reciprocal of its co-observation count, that condition gives counts proportional to the square root of value times uncertainty over cost; where it falls differently the exponent moves with it, so the shape to state is the equal-marginal-return condition and not any one closed form. Which scalar summary is minimized is named, since different summaries rank allocations differently.

*The decision variable.* The decision variable is not the pair count. An observation is bought on a unit, and one observation of source k on a unit already observed on r other sources raises r pair counts at once, so pairs are purchased in bundles and the free variable is the set of sources each unit is assigned under its budget and each source's eligibility. The feasible co-observation matrices are those realizable by some assignment of sets; a target matrix outside that set is arithmetic, not a design. The population as a whole is then sampled in a matrix, each unit seeing a designed subset, and pairwise coverage identifies pairwise couplings and no more: a coupling conditional on a third source needs the triple co-observed on the same units, and a structure assembled from blocks estimated on different subsamples must be projected to a valid one, which is part of the estimator and is reported.

*The ledger.* The co-observation graph with its current intervals is the state of this allocation, recomputed as the model moves, and the policy acts on it. It is also the honest statement of what the system can currently know, which is why it is published alongside every coupling it bounds.

*Relations.* Where a source is relational, allocation extends to relations: the design assigns some pairs and schedules some relational observations, repeated and reciprocal as section 2 requires, rather than only recording the ones units chose, because chosen relations select on the very quantities the relational source measures, and a record of volunteered relations alone is a record of the relations that went well.

**7 · Roles, and the exclusion**

*Proposition 6.* A source used as a prior to reduce the observations needed on a target may not enter the records on which the coupling between them is estimated. Roles attach to observations, not to pairs: every measurement of a target is recorded blind or prior-informed, and a coupling is estimated only on blind records.

*Argument.* If source j informs how a unit is measured on k, whether by shortening the measurement, by steering which of k's observations the unit receives, or by entering the estimate of the unit's value on k, then the unit's value on k contains j, and their covariance across units is inflated by construction. Yet using j as a prior is the mechanism by which the system gets cheaper as it learns: fewer observations place a unit on k when j predicts k. Both are true, so the resolution is a role per observation. Using j as a prior for k on one set of units while estimating the j and k coupling on a disjoint blind set is valid, provided the split is fixed by design and never by an outcome. A pair's role is then a summary of its records rather than a state it can be stuck in, and a pair whose coupling must be re-estimated, because the population moved or the coupling drifted, is re-opened by buying a fresh blind subsample.

*Leakage through the prior.* Splitting units is not sufficient. Where the blind units' values are shrunk toward a population quantity computed from prior-informed units, j has entered k on every unit, including the blind ones. The discovery estimate is therefore computed on an information set that is prior-free entire, its own units and the population quantities those units alone support, or the prior-informed contribution enters with the weight by which it was injected. That weight is recorded, which is why a flat claim that no estimator removes what the design put in would be too strong: a known linear injection is removable, at a variance cost that can exceed the value recovered. What makes the exclusion necessary is that the injection is generally neither known nor linear.

*The cycle, stated with its exclusion.* Bits saved on one source lower the observations needed on another, which frees attention, which buys depth or breadth, which sharpens the couplings, which saves more bits. The cycle runs on prior-informed records and is kept honest by the blind ones, a designed share reserved for discovery so that the couplings the cycle rests on remain measurements rather than echoes of the priors that used them.

**8 · The value of a source, and the admission of a new one**

*Proposition 7.* The value of a source is its saving in the code length of the other sources' future observations, per attention unit it costs, relative to the current portfolio, measured on the evaluation stream. A candidate source proposed from the model's residuals is admitted only by a test that splits the units that proposed it from the units that test it and survives that stream.

*The value.* Marginal information is the exact meaning of one source making another worth more, and it is a set function, not a price. What a source saves depends on the set it is measured against: two sources carrying the same structure are each worth little beside the other and much beside neither. So the price list states each source's saving relative to the current portfolio, is valid only for that portfolio, and is recomputed when the portfolio changes. A source's worth alone, or averaged over the orders in which the portfolio could have been assembled, is a different number and is named where it is used; the gap between the two readings is what redundancy means here. The measurement is honest only on the evaluation stream, at the resolution section 5 says that stream can deliver for a source of that rarity.

*The admission test.* Where the joint model's residuals cluster, structure exists that no source captures, and a cluster is a candidate for a new source or a new observation. It is a candidate, not a specification, because residual structure on an adaptively sampled, heavily missing tensor reliably manufactures factors out of the missingness pattern itself. A candidate is admitted only if the units whose residuals proposed it are excluded from the units on which it is tested; if it survives on the evaluation stream; if it predicts observations on a source other than the ones its residual was computed in; and if it survives the serving propensity by weighting or matching on it, since entering a propensity as a term removes only what that term's form can express. Under that test the system is self-extending: its failures propose its next measurement, and the exploration share of section 4 funds finding out whether the proposal pays in the same currency as everything else.

**9 · The efficiency hierarchy**

*Proposition 8.* Design efficiency dominates where it decides between a structural zero and a point, which is Proposition 2's theorem. Statistical efficiency, estimating jointly so that every source borrows strength from every other and every unit borrows from the population, dominates for unit placement and for precision on what design has made identifiable, which is Proposition 2's ratio. Computational efficiency is the binding constraint in exactly two places: the horizon term, whose exact solution is intractable at population scale, so the future value Proposition 3 rests on is always an approximation whose error is indistinguishable in its effect from a design error; and custody, where a computation that may not move restricts which joint quantities are computable rather than how quickly they are computed. Elsewhere computation is not what is scarce, because an estimator has nothing to compute on a cell that was never collected, and that asymmetry, not emphasis, is what orders the three.

**10 · What would have to hold**

The expected gains are computable and the model that computes them is good enough that its errors do not compound. A wrong model chooses badly in a way that confirms itself, so the exploration share is what corrects the estimate of value, and the evaluation stream is what measures whether the model deserves the trust the policy places in it.

The hazard is estimated on everyone who was served, including those who left, which the log makes possible, and jointly with the response process, because survival in a budgeted population is correlated with what is being measured.

The anchor set is answered by every unit, and where it is not, the holes are reported as holes in the co-observation graph rather than as a design.

Costs are calibrated in the same attention units the budget is stated in, so that a source's value per cost and the budget are commensurable.

Observing does not change what is measured beyond the learning term section 5 bounds. Where it does, the coupling is a property of the design that measured it rather than of the population, and only the evaluation stream makes two designs' estimates comparable.

Budgets differ across units and are not independent of what is measured: a unit with more attention to give is not a random unit. Couplings are estimated within budget strata and reported with the budget distribution of the units that supplied them, or the structure reported is the attentive population's.

Couplings hold still, or weight is not information: where a coupling drifts, old co-observations are discounted at the rate the drift sets and the edge carries an effective weight rather than a count.

The log is an inference channel. An expected gain is a function of the values that produced it, so a reason that travels can reconstruct a raw value that may not, and what the log exports is restricted to the functions its custody class permits. Where a source's raw values may not leave the unit, the part of the choice that depends on them runs where they are.

Units are not adversarial. An information-maximizing rule is legible to a unit that knows it, and the frontier observations the policy values most are precisely the ones a unit wanting to move the conclusion would target; the probability floor is the partial defence and is stated as one.

**11 · The potential, and what remains open**

Fully realised, this is an experiment on a population that never ends, in which every period's observations are the ones that teach the most about what is not yet known, in which the map of what can be known without assumption is a published object that the design acts on and the reader can inspect, in which sources are priced by what they save relative to the portfolio they join and admitted by what they add, and in which the system's failures are its research agenda. It is the theory of the choice that estimation stands on, and wherever it decides between an interval and a point, it is where the value lives.

What remains open is research. Non-myopic design at population scale is intractable exactly, and every approximation trades the future term for tractability in a way that must be measured against the objective it approximates. The exchange rate between unit-level and population-level bits, and the coverage preference, have no theorem behind them and are stated as the choices they are. The allocation of Proposition 5 assumes coupling values that are themselves estimated on the couplings not yet known. The assumption that fixes one of age, period and cohort is a choice the schedule cannot make for the analyst. And the admission test of Proposition 7 has a threshold that decides how often the system chases noise against how often it misses structure, and setting it is a calibration the data itself would have to supply.
