# The discovery problem

*General theory, paper G1: how to find the connections between measurement sources on the same units as efficiently as possible, when observation is budgeted.*

**Status: theory research.** General track: this paper is about any population measured by many sources, and it names no particular product or source. `research/README.md` says what a paper is and how the tracks relate.

**Perfect-form test.** If the app did not exist, this would still be worth saying because every system that measures the same units through several sources under a budget faces one problem before any statistics: which observations to take so that the connections between the sources can be known at all, and the theory of that choice has been written for single instruments and for found data, not for many sources on chosen data.

**Setting.** A population of units. Units may stand in relations, pairs or groups, and time passes in periods. There are K sources. A source, applied to a unit at a period, yields a response through a measurement model from the unit's latent state, with a bias structure that depends on how the source collects, a cost in attention units, a custody class that says where its raw values may travel, and a grain: fixed, one value per unit; slow, changing across years; occasion, changing across periods; or relational, defined on a pair or group rather than a unit. Each unit supplies at most B attention units per period. The object of discovery is the coupling structure: how the sources' latent quantities covary across units, how they co-move within a unit across periods, and what a relation carries that its members do not. Nothing in this paper depends on what the units or the sources are.

**Abstract.** When data are found, the estimator is the whole of the method. When data are chosen under a per-unit budget, the estimator is downstream of the choice, and this paper is the theory of the choice. It states eight propositions with their arguments. A coupling between two sources is identified only from units observed on both, so the map of what can be known is the weighted co-observation graph over sources, and connectivity of that graph is the condition for a single latent space. Under a budget, the information about the coupling structure that a system can obtain is determined first by which unit, source and period triples it observes and second by how it estimates, in one regime, while in the other regime, the placement of a single unit, borrowing from the population dominates the next observation. The objective is expected information gain per attention unit under a named utility, over a horizon, as an explicit scalarization of population-level and unit-level targets with an exploration allocation, a continuation hazard and a coverage term. Six constraints keep it honest: an anchor set that links, a probability floor that preserves positivity, a log that makes selection ignorable, steering on pre-response variables only, a uniform stream never selected on, and repetition at unequal lags. Allocation is planned missingness with a stated solution shape. A source used as a prior for measuring a target may never be read against that target, so roles are assigned per pair and move only forward. The value of a source is the bits it saves predicting the others on the uniform stream, and a new source is admitted by a test that survives that stream. And efficiency is a hierarchy, design above estimation above computation, with attention as the denominator throughout.

**1 · Why this is a problem of its own**

Most theory of finding structure in data assumes the data exist. The population has been measured, and the question is what to compute. The setting here does not have that shape. The data are a tensor of units by sources by periods that is almost entirely empty, and it is empty by design: each unit can contribute B attention units per period, the sources cost attention, and nothing is in the tensor until a choice puts it there. Two systems with identical units, identical sources and identical budgets, differing only in which cells they chose to fill, can differ by orders of magnitude in what can be learned from them.

The connections between sources are not in any source. A source alone yields a marginal: how its quantity is distributed. A connection is a joint fact, and a joint fact exists in the data only where two sources were observed on the same units. So discovery is not a matter of computing harder on what one has. It is a matter of having observed the right pairs on the right units, and that is decided by the choice. This paper is the theory of that choice, and it is a different theory from estimation, though it constrains estimation and is constrained by it.

**2 · Identification by co-observation**

*Proposition 1.* A coupling between sources j and k is identified only from units observed on both. Where no unit has been observed on both, the coupling is bounded to a set determined by the two marginals and by any third source jointly observed with each, and no number of units observed on j alone or on k alone narrows that set.

*Argument.* A coupling is a property of the joint distribution of the two latent quantities. The marginals constrain the joint only to the family of joint distributions with those marginals, which for any two non-degenerate quantities is a wide set. A third source m observed jointly with j on some units and with k on others bounds the coupling between j and k through m only under an assumption about the conditional dependence of j and k given m, and the bound is an interval whose width is set by how much of j and k the third source does not explain. Only joint observation of j and k on the same units picks a point from the set.

*Consequence: the co-observation graph.* Take the sources as nodes and weight the edge between two sources by the number of units observed on both, further indexed by the covariates the coupling will be conditioned on. This graph is the map of what the system can know. A coupling on an edge of weight zero is an interval. A coupling on a light edge is a wide point. The graph is not a fact about the population; it is a fact about the design, and it is the design's to change.

*Consequence: connectivity.* For the sources to measure into one latent space, so that a unit's position is comparable across sources, the graph must be connected with sufficient weight on every edge the model reads. A disconnected graph yields separate spaces on incomparable scales, and units measured in different components cannot be compared. The connected core the design guarantees is the anchor set of section 5: a set of observations every unit supplies, whatever else it does.

*Consequence: relations.* A relational source is jointly observed with a unit-level source only through units that stand in relations. Its edges in the graph are weighted by units that are both members of a recorded relation and observed on the unit-level source, so the design that wants to connect relational structure to unit structure must allocate relations, not only observations.

**3 · Two regimes: where the choice dominates and where it does not**

*Proposition 2.* For a population-level coupling, the information obtainable is determined first by which triples of unit, source and period are observed and second by the estimator. For the placement of one unit, most of the obtainable information is borrowed from the population, and the choice of how to borrow dominates the choice of the next observation.

*Argument, first regime.* Consider one unit and one period. Among the observations the system could take, the information an observation carries about a population coupling ranges from nothing, when the observation falls on a pair of sources already estimated to the precision the model reads, or on a unit whose value is already predictable, to the observation's full entropy, when it falls on a coupling that is an interval and on a unit at the frontier of what the model can predict. The estimator cannot recover what the observation did not carry. So the ratio of information between a good and a bad choice, for that unit on that period, is unbounded above, and summed over units and periods the total information gathered by a good policy and a careless one differ by a factor no estimator closes. Where a pair of sources has never been co-observed, the choice is the whole answer, because no estimator recovers an uncollected cell.

*Argument, second regime.* A single unit under a budget of B attention units per period yields, over a year, a few hundred bits about itself. Its position in a latent space of even modest dimension is therefore mostly a posterior that borrows from the population: from the couplings already estimated, from the units like it, from the priors the sources supply. The estimator's choice of how to pool, what to shrink toward and how strongly, moves that posterior more than any one further observation does. Design still matters for the unit, since an observation at its frontier is worth more than one at its centre, but the marginal observation is not most of the value.

*The boundary.* The two regimes are separated by how much of the target's information lives in the unit versus in the population. A system that confused them would either starve its couplings, by optimizing each unit's placement and never manufacturing the joint observations the couplings need, or over-ask its units, by serving observations whose information the prior already supplied.

**4 · The objective**

*Proposition 3.* The choice is well posed as a scalarized expected information gain per attention unit, under a named utility, over a stated horizon, with an exploration allocation outside the current model class, discounted by the continuation hazard the observation induces and weighted by a coverage term.

*The currency.* An observation's value is the reduction in the expected code length of the system's future observations, under log-loss, which is what makes bits the unit. A target for which the natural criterion is instead the variance of an estimate, the precision of one coupling coefficient, orders candidate observations differently, so the objective names the utility it optimizes and does not pretend the orderings agree.

*Three targets, one scalarization.* Bits about the population's couplings, bits about a unit's placement and bits about a unit's dynamics are reductions in different posteriors. Summing them requires an exchange rate, and there is no theorem that supplies it: how many bits about the population are worth one bit about this unit is a policy parameter, stated, revisited, and never presented as a discovered quantity. Writing it down is what makes the objective a design rather than a slogan.

*The horizon.* An observation may be worth nothing today and much later. An observation on a source no other source has been paired with carries no coupling information until a pairing exists, so the value of the pairing is credited to the design that will make it and not to the source. A policy that drops the future term is the myopic special case, and it systematically undervalues the observations that enable couplings, which are exactly the ones the first regime says matter most.

*Exploration.* Expected information gain is computed inside the current model, and it cannot value an observation whose worth is that it would show the model to be wrong. So a share of the budget is allocated outside the current model class, to observations chosen for what they would reveal about the model's failures rather than for what the model expects them to teach. Section 8's self-extension is funded by this share and by nothing else.

*The hazard.* A unit that stops supplying observations yields nothing forever. Expected future information is therefore conditional on continued participation, and the value of an observation is discounted by the probability the unit is still present in the next period, which depends on what the unit was asked this period. This is how the experience of being observed enters the objective without becoming the objective.

*Coverage.* Left to itself, an information-seeking policy concentrates where the model is most uncertain, which over time is where it has learned least, which is disproportionately the units that are rare, that respond differently, or that were badly served early. Without a coverage term the policy starves exactly the parts of the population whose couplings differ from the majority's, and the estimator then reports the majority's structure as everyone's. So the objective values information about a subpopulation in proportion to how little the model can say about it.

**5 · Constraints that make the objective honest**

*Proposition 4.* An objective of pure expected information, applied without constraint, produces a dataset that is maximally informative and useless. Six constraints remove six ways it fails.

*The anchor set.* A set of observations is taken on every unit regardless of expected gain, on a schedule. It places every unit in one space, it gives every pair of sources a floor of joint observation, and it is never retired, because retiring an anchor breaks every comparison that ran through it. A new source enters against anchors already placed.

*The probability floor.* A deterministic choice of the best observation gives every other eligible observation a probability of zero, and with that it destroys the overlap that makes selection estimable: no counterfactual to estimate the selection term against, no residual on observations never taken, no reading of what a unit would have supplied. Every eligible observation carries a probability bounded below by a floor, and the realised probability is logged with the response.

*The log.* Every observation carries the reason it was taken: the target it served, its expected gain, the hazard and coverage terms, the unit's state, and its realised probability. Selection is then a known function of recorded variables, and inference conditional on the log is ignorable of the selection, which is the one defence a chosen dataset has against the charge that its patterns are patterns in the choosing.

*Pre-response steering.* Where the policy steers units toward sources to fill a gap in the co-observation graph, it steers on variables known before the response, which sources the unit holds and the counts in the graph, never on the current estimate of the coupling it is trying to identify. Steering on a posterior conditions on a downstream variable and biases the coupling it is meant to measure; where such steering is unavoidable, the coupling is reported with the steering propensity.

*The uniform stream.* A fixed fraction of each unit's periods is drawn uniformly from the anchor set and never selected on. It is the only stream on which a model's code length can be compared honestly, because on the adaptive stream the policy can improve the number by taking predictable observations. The two streams are reported separately and never averaged.

*Repetition at unequal lags.* Within-unit structure needs repeated observation of the same source on the same unit, at lags dense enough for the timescale of the change to be seen and varied enough that a continuous-time process faster than the mean interval remains identifiable. Repetition also teaches, so schedules are bounded by each source's measured learning curve, and where a source is keyed, first exposure is staggered across units so that practice is decorrelated from age and from calendar time by design rather than subtracted afterwards.

**6 · Allocation as planned missingness**

*Proposition 5.* The allocation of budget across sources and pairs of sources is a planned-missingness design whose decision variables are the co-observation counts, whose objective is the expected posterior uncertainty of the coupling structure, and whose solution allocates in proportion to uncertainty times value divided by cost, above a floor on every pair.

*Argument.* Let each pair of sources carry a current uncertainty, an interval where never co-observed and a posterior width where co-observed, and a value, how much the rest of the structure would sharpen if this coupling were known. Each observation on a unit that holds source j and is now observed on source k adds one to the co-observation count of the pair and costs the attention of k. Minimizing expected posterior uncertainty subject to the budget yields an allocation in which pairs with high uncertainty and high value per unit of cost receive more joint observations, every pair receives at least its floor, and the marginal value of one more jointly observed unit on a pair falls as the pair's count rises. The population as a whole is then sampled in a matrix: each unit sees a designed subset of sources, population estimands are identified across units without any one unit observed on everything, and expensive sources are paired by deliberate allocation rather than by hoping the overlap occurs.

*The ledger.* The co-observation graph with its current intervals is the state of this allocation, recomputed as the model moves, and the policy acts on it. It is also the honest statement of what the system can currently know, which is why it is published alongside every coupling it bounds.

*Relations.* Where a source is relational, allocation extends to relations: the design assigns some pairs and schedules some relational observations rather than only recording the ones units chose, because chosen relations select on the very quantities the relational source measures, and a record of volunteered relations alone is a record of the relations that went well.

**7 · Roles, and the exclusion**

*Proposition 6.* A source used as a prior to reduce the observations needed on a target may not then be read against that target. Roles are assigned per pair of source and target, prior or discovery, and a pair's role moves only from discovery to prior, never back.

*Argument.* If source j informs how a unit is measured on k, whether by shortening the measurement, by steering which of k's observations the unit receives, or by entering the estimate of the unit's value on k, then the unit's value on k contains j. Their covariance across units is then inflated by construction, and no estimator removes what the design put in. Yet using j as a prior is the mechanism by which the system gets cheaper as it learns: fewer observations place a unit on k when j predicts k. Both are true, so the resolution is a role per pair. While the coupling between j and k is being discovered, k is measured blind to j on the units that will supply the coupling. Once the coupling is read, j may serve as a prior for k on units that will not supply it again. The role moves forward only, and the record carries it, so that no coupling is ever estimated on units whose measurement of one side already contained the other.

*The cycle, stated with its exclusion.* Bits saved on one source lower the observations needed on another, which frees attention, which buys depth or breadth, which sharpens the couplings, which saves more bits. The cycle runs on the prior role and is starved by nothing but the exclusion, which reserves a designed share of units and observations for discovery, blind, so that the couplings the cycle rests on remain honest measurements rather than echoes of the priors that used them.

**8 · The value of a source, and the admission of a new one**

*Proposition 7.* The value of a source is the bits it saves in predicting all other sources' future observations, per attention unit it costs, measured on the uniform stream. A candidate source proposed from the model's residuals is admitted only by a test that survives that stream.

*The value.* Marginal information is the exact meaning of one source making another worth more. It is measured, not argued: the code length of the other sources' observations on the uniform stream, with and without the candidate source in the model, per attention unit the source cost. The measurement is honest only on the uniform stream, for the reason section 5 gives. Across sources this yields a price list: what each source is worth to the rest and what it costs, which is what lets the value of adding a source be compared with the value of asking more of an existing one.

*The admission test.* Where the joint model's residuals cluster, structure exists that no source captures, and a cluster is a candidate for a new source or a new observation. It is a candidate, not a specification, because residual structure on an adaptively sampled, heavily missing tensor reliably manufactures factors out of the missingness pattern itself. A candidate is admitted only if it survives on the uniform stream, predicts held-out observations of a different kind than those it was found in, and does not vanish when the serving propensity is entered into the model. Under that test the system is self-extending: its failures propose its next measurement, and the exploration share of section 4 funds finding out whether the proposal pays in the same currency as everything else.

**9 · The efficiency hierarchy**

*Proposition 8.* Efficiency in discovery is a hierarchy. Design efficiency, taking the observations that carry information about what is not yet known, dominates for population structure. Statistical efficiency, estimating jointly so that every source borrows strength from every other and every unit borrows from the population, dominates for unit placement and for precision on what design has made identifiable. Computational efficiency, incremental estimation on sufficient statistics with outputs bounded by the resolution at which they will be read, is necessary for the other two to run at all and is never the binding constraint on what can be known. Attention is the denominator of all three.

*Argument.* The first two orderings are Proposition 2 restated with the levels named. The third follows from the setting: computation is not what is scarce. A system that could compute anything but observed carelessly would know less than a system that computed crudely on observations chosen well, and the theory says so plainly so that effort goes where the information is.

**10 · What would have to hold**

The expected gains are computable and the model that computes them is good enough that its errors do not compound. A wrong model chooses badly in a way that confirms itself, so the exploration share is what corrects the estimate of value, and the uniform stream is what measures whether the model deserves the trust the policy places in it.

The hazard is estimated on everyone who was served, including those who left, which the log makes possible, and jointly with the response process, because survival in a budgeted population is correlated with what is being measured.

The anchor set is answered by every unit, and where it is not, the holes are reported as holes in the co-observation graph rather than as a design.

Costs are calibrated in the same attention units the budget is stated in, so that a source's value per cost and the budget are commensurable.

Custody constrains what the policy may see: where a source's raw values may not leave the unit, the part of the choice that depends on them runs where they are, and the logged reason travels without the raw.

**11 · The potential, and what remains open**

Fully realised, this is an experiment on a population that never ends, in which every period's observations are the ones that teach the most about what is not yet known, in which the map of what can be known is a published object that the design acts on and the reader can inspect, in which sources are priced by what they teach about each other and admitted by what they add, and in which the system's failures are its research agenda. It is the theory of the choice that estimation stands on, and for population structure under a budget it is where most of the value lives.

What remains open is research. Non-myopic design at population scale is intractable exactly, and every approximation trades the future term for tractability in a way that must be measured against the objective it approximates. The exchange rate between unit-level and population-level bits has no theorem behind it. The coverage term and the hazard can pull against each other where a rare subpopulation is also the one most likely to leave. The allocation of Proposition 5 assumes coupling values that are themselves estimated on the couplings not yet known. And the admission test of Proposition 7 has a threshold that decides how often the system chases noise against how often it misses structure, and setting it is a calibration the data itself would have to supply.
