**1 · The problem, stated structurally**

A unit-level design observes units and reads how they differ and how they move. Some of what it wants to know is not of that form. What one unit makes of another is not a property of either. What a pair does together is not the sum of what each does alone, or is, and which is the question. Whether two units who are alike chose each other for it or became alike through each other is a question about a pair's history, and a graph of who stands in relation to whom is a structure over units that no unit's record contains.

The series has handled this by naming a grain and pricing nothing at it. G1 defines a relational source and states an identification condition for what a pair carries; G2 stores an observation on a relation on the relation; G3 gives the relational level a between and a within and puts the exposure mapping on the graph; G5 makes a relation its own sequence; G6 says a pair is not a distance. Each defers the object to a paper that did not exist, and one of them states its condition wrongly. This is that paper, and its claims are the ones a design must make before its first relational observation is worth its cost: what the observation decomposes into, what identifies each part, which population the reading is about, and what the graph does to everything else.

**2 · Three things called a relation, three kinds of observation, and the difference between a pair and a graph**

*A membership, an observation, a latent.* The word names three objects. A recorded membership is a fact of the ledger: these units stand in a relation of this type from this period, recorded as a membership event because they said so or as a served one because the design prompted it. An observation on a relation is a response a relational source produced on it at a period. A relational latent is what the relation carries beyond its members — the quantity a relational source measures net of what each member brings to every relation — and it exists as an estimand whether or not any observation identifies it. A design that records memberships has a graph and no relational measurement; a design that takes relational observations without the designs of section 3 has measurements and no relational latent; and a claim about "the relation" says which of the three it is about.

*Three kinds of observation.* A directed observation about a member has a sender and a receiver, and it is the sender's raw value about the receiver: produced on the sender's side, about the receiver, and both facts matter for custody in section 9. A directed observation about the relation has a sender and no receiver — a member's report on the pair, or a non-member's — and section 3 says it decomposes into fewer terms. A symmetric observation is a property of the relation no member produced alone, in two kinds that section 4 shows identify opposite things: a comparison, which reports which member exceeded which, and a joint outcome, which reports what the members did together.

*Pairs and groups.* A group is a relation with more than two members, and its observations belong to it. Everything below is stated for pairs. Section 7 extends what extends: the decomposition gains a group term identified only where units cross groups; the coordination of a symmetric observation is over all members, so its completion probability is a product of as many propensities as there are members and its cost is that many budgets; and section 4's identification by the pairing graph is stated for pairs and is not extended to groups here, where the graph is a hypergraph and the algebra is different.

*A relation and a graph.* The recorded relations form a graph over the units. The graph is not a relation and not a population fact: it is the set of memberships the record holds, which section 5 shows is the product of two selections, and it is the object over which section 8's exposure mapping is defined and section 8's dependence runs. Two graphs are kept apart throughout: the relation graph, over units, and G1's co-observation graph, over sources, which gains a relational layer in section 5.

**3 · What a directed observation decomposes into, and the design that identifies each term**

*The decomposition.* A directed response by sender i about receiver j on one relational source is a population mean, plus what i brings to every relation as a sender, plus what j draws from every relation as a receiver, plus what the pair carries beyond both — the relationship term, which is directed, so that i's about j and j's about i are two quantities — plus error. Two reciprocities sit across the terms. Generalized reciprocity is the covariance, within a unit, between what it brings as a sender and what it draws as a receiver. Dyadic reciprocity is the covariance, within a pair, between the two directed relationship terms, and its correlation is that covariance over the relationship variance. A directed observation about the relation has no receiver and decomposes into the mean, the sender's term, the relation's term and error, so the receiver variance is not an estimand on such a source, and a non-member's report decomposes the same way with the non-member as sender.

*What is assumed before anything is identified.* The relationship terms are independent across pairs and of the members' terms; the errors are independent across observations; and no term is shared by several pairs. The last is not a convenience: section 7 names a context shared by pairs and a group term, and the last row below says what such a term needs. Each row holds under these assumptions, and the harness beside the series checks every row as an exact rank — a variance component is identified on a design if and only if its covariance matrix is linearly independent of the others' — so the table is a theorem about each design and not an estimate.

*The table.* The sender variance is identified where units send to several receivers, the receiver variance where units receive from several senders, and both with the generalized reciprocity where every unit is a sender in at least two relations and a receiver in at least two; degree in each role is what separates the pair's term from the members', and no connectivity of the pairing graph is needed for that — connectivity is section 4's condition, for attributing latents on a comparison or a sum network, and belongs there. The relationship variance separates from error only where the same directed pair is observed more than once — on repeated occasions inside a window shorter than the relationship's own timescale, in G3's sense of a window — and with one observation per directed pair it is identified with the error variance as a sum, whose share is an upper bound on the pair's. Replication by several indicators of one construct in one sitting separates the pair's term from item error only, leaves it confounded with G3's occasion method factor, which the pair's term and the sitting's conditions are two names for on a single-sitting design, and is admissible only under G3's randomized split of indicators across sittings with at least three conditionally independent indicators. The dyadic covariance is identified from one observation per direction, given the generalized reciprocity, provided the two directions' errors are independent, which they are not where the two reports share a sitting; the dyadic correlation needs replication besides, because its denominator is the relationship variance. A design in which each unit is in one relation identifies the sender, the receiver and the pair as one number, however many pairs there are. And a context or group term shared by several pairs is identified from two pairs that share the context and no member — a context of four units or more, whether or not any unit is in another — or from units crossing contexts; a context of three units whose members are in no other context confounds it with the members' and the pair's terms under any replication. An earlier draft said the crossing was the only way, and the rank says otherwise.

*What this corrects.* G1's second section says that the same pair observed more than once, or observed in both directions, separates the relationship-specific quantity from the members' own and from error, and that one observation per pair confounds all three; G3's second section says the pair's term separates from the members' only when both directions are observed. Both are superseded by the table: degree separates the pair's term from the members', replication separates it from error, reciprocal observation identifies the dyadic covariance and neither of the other two, and one observation per pair confounds the pair's term with error while leaving the members' terms identified wherever the degree condition holds. A design that followed the earlier sentence bought reciprocal pairs without replication and identified no pair's term. The two earlier sentences are corrected in place, and this paragraph is the record of why.

*Invariance across relation types.* The Setting gives a relation a type, and a relational source's measurement model is not assumed the same across types: a directed report inside a close relation and inside a working one may be two measurement models. G3 makes invariance across the groups a design compares an estimand and not a property assumed, and it is that here across types, tested before any reading pools them; where invariance is partial, the comparable type set is named per comparison and no chain of comparisons runs across differing sets; and the design buys the observations that test it, which are directed observations of one construct across types on the same senders, priced as a line beside the construct's own.

*The sender's own value enters the report.* A sender who reports on a receiver on a construct the sender also holds projects: the report carries the sender's own value on the same construct. The term is identified against a unit-level source on that construct — in the anchor set where the construct is an anchor, and bought otherwise at its per-unit cost — as the regression of a sender's reports on the sender's own value, net of the receivers' values as their other senders supply them. The link between the two sources is by content only where the sender supplied the same observation on both, and otherwise runs through the unit-level source's estimated parameters, which is G1's weaker link and is reported as one with the invariance it needs tested. Without a unit-level source on the construct, projection is confounded with the sender term, and a sender variance read on such a design is partly projection.

*Two constructs a directed source can measure.* A relational source that asks a sender what the receiver is measures the sender's view. One that asks the sender what the receiver will respond measures the sender's knowledge of the receiver's own view. They are two sources with two measurement models, and they come apart exactly where the receiver's own report is biased: a sender who knows the receiver's self-view and disagrees with it is accurate on the second and dissenting on the first. A design that asks only the second has measured knowledge of a self-report and called it agreement.

*The relationship term is the only part that belongs to the pair, and it is a shrunken posterior.* Everything else in the decomposition belongs to a unit, and a coupling on relations in G3's sense is a coupling among relationship terms across pairs, or between a relationship term and its members' positions — the interaction G6 called complementarity, a surface over pairs of positions that section 5 shows is observed only where pairs were assigned. Every relationship term is estimated from a handful of observations and is a shrunken posterior in G3's sense, reported with the shrinkage that produced it; a design that estimates one per observed pair will find its largest among its noisiest, so no pair's term is read as a finding on its extreme, and the complementarity surface is a fitted surface over shrunken terms with the attenuation carried, on the argument G6 makes for distances between sparsely observed units.

**4 · A symmetric observation identifies a quotient**

*The model class, stated.* A symmetric observation depends on the two members' latents through one function of them — their difference for a comparison, their sum for a joint outcome — plus a pair term with mean zero, independent of the pairing, plus error. That the response depends on the latents only through the difference is an assumption of the comparison's measurement model, and not a definition: it fails under a discrimination that varies with level, under a ceiling or a floor in the response, and under any link that reads the two latents separately. Where it holds, the results below are exact; where it fails, a common shift moves the response and reads as a contrast, and the assumption is testable only by the pins below, which are the same objects that recover what the cancellation hides.

*A comparison sees a difference.* Under the class, anything common to both members cancels exactly: a common level, a common function of time, a common function of the encounter's conditions. Where a condition of the encounter — a stake, a setting, an audience — enters both members' latents alike, the mean of its effect is not identified from comparisons, and a finding that the condition moves everyone alike is an invariance of the design reported as a result. What survives is every contrast: the between-unit variance of a condition's effect, and each unit's effect relative to the population's. Population-wide change over time cancels the same way, so a rate of change estimated inside a comparison network is a rate relative to a contemporaneous mean that itself moves with entry, exit and everyone else's change.

*A joint outcome sees a sum, and the pairing graph attributes it.* The fixed part of a sum-type response — the members' latents through their sum, with the pair term and error set aside — identifies each member's latent only where the pairing graph attributes it: on a component with an odd cycle, three pairs among three units determine each unit's latent from the three sums; on a bipartite component — two sides that only ever pair across — the latents are determined up to a constant that raises one side and lowers the other by the same amount, and nothing in the outcomes fixes it. A difference-type source determines the latents up to one common constant on each connected component; a sum-type source up to one alternating constant on each bipartite component and fully where a component has an odd cycle. Those are results about the fixed part. With the pair term present, a triangle has three sums and six unknowns and nothing solves; the members' latents are recoverable because the pair terms are mean-zero and independent of the pairing, which needs each unit in at least two pairs, and the pair term itself separates from error only by the replication section 3 requires. Degree buys precision on the members' latents at the rate of the number of pairs a unit is in, and the kernel result says which constant no degree buys.

*What pins the quotient, derived.* Each kind is pinned by an observation whose likelihood depends on one member's latent alone, and the Setting supplies three ways to have one. A unit-level source on the comparison's own latent — in the anchor set where that latent is an anchor, and bought at its per-unit cost where it is not, which is the ordinary case, since the anchor set is a fixed set of sources and not a counterpart for every relational construct — fixes the common constant of a comparison and the alternating constant of a sum; it links to the relational source through its estimated parameters, G1's weaker link, and the invariance of the linking measurement across the units it joins is an identification requirement priced with it. A condition applied asymmetrically to the two members of one encounter multiplies the difference of the members' effects by a difference of conditions rather than by a common one, so the mean effect of the condition returns as the coefficient on that difference, at the cost of encounters the members do not face alike. And a graded per-member outcome recorded inside the encounter is a unit-level source taken inside the relation, which pins the quotient on the first argument and raises the question the first argument raises: whether what a member does inside an encounter measures the latent the comparison is about, which is an invariance test between the two.

*The pairing rule is part of the measurement model, under G1's two-part rule.* Who was paired with whom decides what the observations can identify — the components, the cycles, the degrees — and the rule is recorded with every relational observation as G2 records a serving decision, with its realised probability. G1's rule on steering applies at this grain in both its parts. Pairing on any function of the logged past is permitted — on the members' current estimates, and on a predicted outcome of the pair or a predicted relational latent, which are functions of the log too — and it makes the selection depend on what is estimated, so the pairing propensity enters every relational coupling's estimator, by weighting or matching, with the weights' variance priced against the per-pair floor of section 5; recording the rule is not entering it, and an analysis that ignores the log is biased with every probability known. What is forbidden is pairing on anything measured in the same sitting as the relational response or after it — a screening item asked at the encounter, the members' contemporaneous state — because that selection is not a function of the past the log holds. And the informative pairing differs by target: for a member's latent, a partner whose own latent is well known near the member's estimate; for the pair term and its surface over positions, partners across the space the units would not have chosen, which is section 5's assignment.

*Roles at this grain.* G1 attaches a role to every observation — blind or prior-informed — and estimates a coupling only on blind records. A relational observation carries the same role, set by whether the pairing rule read the unit-level source the coupling is against: a pair formed by a rule that consulted the members' estimates on a source is a prior-informed record for every coupling between the relational source and that one, because the record's existence was decided by the source that serves as its prior. So a designed share of pairings is drawn by a rule blind to the unit-level sources in question, the relational couplings against those sources are estimated on the blind share only, and G1's leakage clause holds — the blind share is not shrunk toward population quantities the prior-informed pairs helped compute. The blind share is a fraction of the assigned stream stated in pairs, and section 9 puts it in the line.

**5 · The graph is a design object: two selections, two populations, and the assigned stream**

*Two selections.* Which relations the record holds is the product of two choices. Units chose to form relations, and formation depends on the relational latent and the members' own — G1's observation that chosen relations select on the very quantity the relational source measures, so that a record of volunteered relations is a record of relations that formed well. And the design chose which relations to observe, with a reason and a probability logged. The first selection is not logged, because the design did not make it; it is a fact about the units, and it is what makes the recorded graph a sample drawn by formation rather than by the design. A third selection sits on top of both for symmetric observations, since completion needs both members and section 9 says how much it removes.

*Two populations, and neither is wrong.* A relational reading is about one of two populations. The population of formed relations: how the relations that exist differ from one another and move. The population of possible pairs: how any two units would relate, which the formed set does not represent, because it was drawn from it by formation. Both are estimands. A coupling among relationship terms on the formed set is a fact about formed relations, and it is published as that; a claim about possible pairs is a different number, and the difference between the two is the formation selection, identified only by observing pairs the units did not form. Complementarity — the surface of the pair term over the members' positions — is where the difference is largest, because formed pairs concentrate where the surface is high and along the diagonal of similarity. Off the diagonal the formed set is not empty; it is observed at the density formation supplies, which is G1's light edge and supports an interval with the sampling error that density gives, and it is a structural zero only where that density is exactly zero. Both widths are published as the widths they are.

*The assigned stream.* Assignment is the design prompting a relation the units did not form. The assigned stream is the set of prompted relations, drawn from each unit's partner pool by a rule that read at most the logged past — uniformly, or adaptively on anything the log holds, with the propensity entered as section 4 requires — under a per-pair floor with the realised probability logged, and observed with the replication section 3 requires. It is not G1's eligible-drawn stream, and the two are kept apart by the settled rule: the assigned stream's uniform share — pairs drawn uniformly from the pools, never selected on — is the eligible-drawn evaluation stream at this grain, on which every relational source is priced and evaluated and which is never averaged with the adaptive share; the adaptive share is where possible-pair couplings are estimated with the propensity entered; and a stated fraction of both is the blind share of section 4. Relational couplings are estimated on the assigned stream, or on the formed set with the population named.

*The partner pool, and the four numbers that are one decision.* The per-pair floor cannot run over all pairs: with every unit a candidate partner of every other, each pair's probability is at most one over the population and the corrective weights, which run to the reciprocal of the floor, grow with the population until the smallest per-pair effect they can resolve is below any use; and per unit the floor's cost is linear in the number of candidate partners, so it is that count that must be bounded, not the pair count. So each unit has a partner pool of a stated size c, drawn from the consenting population by a stated rule, and the floor runs over it; pairs outside every pool are reported as never eligible. Four numbers follow from c and are chosen together, in the shape G1 gives its floor: the possible-pair frame is the union of the pools, which holds c over the population less one of all pairs and is spread over the complementarity surface as the pools' drawing rule spreads it; the per-pair floor is at most one over c; the corrective weights run to c; and the standing charge is the floor share of each unit's relational draws times the relational source's cost per member per period. At c of twenty and one floor draw per unit per period, the frame holds twenty over the population less one of all pairs, the weights run to twenty, and the charge is one relational observation's cost per unit per period per relational source floored. A pool drawn uniformly reaches the surface as the population does; a pool drawn to cover the surface — partners far from the unit in the space — reaches it faster and is a selection stated with the reading.

*The co-observation graph's relational layer.* A relational source is co-observed with a unit-level source only through units who are both members of a recorded relation and observed on that source, so its edges in G1's graph are weighted by those units, and the weight is an effective count under section 8's dependence composed with G1's own discount for drift. Two relational sources are co-observed only on relations observed on both. And a relational source's coupling with a unit-level source lives on members — what a unit brings as a sender, or draws as a receiver, against the unit's position — on the blind share.

*Survivorship is a third thing.* Formation selects which pairs enter the record; dissolution, in section 6, selects which remain, and the formed population at a period is the relations that have not dissolved. The two selections differ in what they select on and when, and a between-relation coupling at a period carries both: it is published with the age distribution of the relations it was read on, and the older they are the more the second has done.

*The first fork: whether the design may prompt a relation.* Assignment asks a unit to relate to someone it did not choose, which is an imposition of a kind no unit-level observation makes, and whether the design may make it is the owner's. Four shapes, each with what it identifies and what it costs. Record formed relations only: no imposition; the possible-pair population is unidentified, the complementarity surface is read at formation's density off the diagonal, and no relational source has a never-selected-on stream to be priced on. Prompt, with the offer drawn from the pool and acceptance free: the offer's effect is identified by design as section 6 states, the readings on accepted offers are complier readings under section 6's two restrictions, and the offer's own effect on a unit who declined is the violation named; this costs the unit an offer, and the design a pool per unit. Assign within a consenting pool for a bounded task — an encounter of stated length and kind: the possible-pair population is identified within the pool, complementarity is read off the diagonal, and the pool's consent is built at entry with the task stated; this costs the pool the task and the design the pool's representativeness, stated. Assign and compensate, which widens the pool and buys a selection on who accepts compensation, stated as a term. The owner rules, and whichever shape holds, the readings name the population they are about.

**6 · The relation's own time**

*Formation is an offer and an acceptance.* Where the design prompts a relation, the offer is a first-class event in G3's sense — the system's decision, randomizable — and the acceptance is the unit's. So formation is an instrument. One randomized offer identifies, by design, the offer's effect on each member as an intention-to-treat, and the effect of the relation formed on each member as a complier effect under two restrictions stated per relation type: that the offer moves the outcome only through the relation formed, which is doubtful because an offer declined is itself an event the unit learned something from, and is named as the violation it is; and monotonicity, that no unit forms a relation because it was not offered one. The asymmetry of the relation's effects on its two members is the contrast of those two design-identified effects and needs no second randomization; it is the influence asymmetry — how much more one member moved the other — only where the offer went to both members alike. Where the offer went to one member, that member initiated, and the contrast carries the initiation asymmetry too — the effect of having been offered rather than having offered — so separating influence from initiation needs the offer randomized in both directions, with the difference between the two contrasts as the estimand, and a one-sided offer identifies the contrast and not the split. Where the units formed the relation themselves, formation is a second-class event: its onset is confounded with what preceded it, and what follows it is read under sequential ignorability with the estimators G3 says that assumption admits, and no better.

*Within-relation dynamics.* How one relation moves needs the same relation observed across periods — the within level of G3 at this grain — with a window per dimension, a centre estimated jointly with the dynamics, and the sampling schedule bounding which dynamics exist as estimands. The relationship term has a timescale of its own, and section 3's replication is inside that timescale or it is measuring change and calling it error. A relation observed once per period per direction supports a within-relation reading and no separation of the pair term from error within the period; the design buys both, or states which it bought.

*Dissolution is informative exit, with a handle the unit case lacks.* A relation that ends leaves the record, and leaving is correlated with what the relational source measures, so the exit is a hazard beside the response process in the shape of G3's dropout term. It differs from that term in one thing: a unit that leaves the system is observed no further, but the members of a dissolved relation persist and are observed after it, so the hazard as a function of the observed history is estimable, and what is unidentified is only its dependence on the contemporaneous relational latent net of that history, carried as a stated assumption with a bound — and the members' post-dissolution observations are a check on the assumption that the unit case cannot run. Two consequences. The formed set ages into a set of relations that lasted, as section 5 says. And a within-relation series is right-censored by an event that depends on the series, so a within-relation coupling read on completed series is read on the survivors and stated as such.

*The between-relation level has a time.* The population of relations turns over faster than the population of units, since relations form and dissolve while their members stay; a coupling across relations is a fact about the relations that existed at a period and moves with formation, dissolution and the members' own change, and it carries a time as every level in G3 does.

**7 · Similarity among the related and its four causes; groups; structure proposed at this grain**

*Four causes.* Members of a relation resemble each other more than two units drawn at random, and the resemblance has four causes that a design must separate before it is a finding about any of them. Homophily: units formed relations with those already like them, a selection on similarity at formation. Influence: members became alike through the relation, a convergence after formation. Shared context: members were exposed to the same occasion, place or group, which moves them alike whether or not they relate. Observational selection: the record holds the pairs it holds, and section 5's selections put similar pairs in it.

*What separates them, and on which relations.* Homophily is read from the members' placements before the relation formed, so it is identified on the stratum of relations formed after both members were placed, and a design that places every unit at entry has that stratum from its first period; relations that predate the record have no pre-formation placement, and on that stratum homophily and influence are not separable by any design, so its share of the formed set is reported with every reading that splits them. On the identified stratum the placements are posteriors with error, and similarity measured between two point summaries is attenuated by that error and pushed into influence, so the split is computed net of the placements' posterior error with the shrinkage stated, as G3 computes a discrepancy between levels net of each level's error and G6 corrects a distance. Influence is read only under a randomized offer, per section 6, since units accept offers of those they were already becoming like and convergence on accepted offers is continued selection until the exclusion restriction is argued. Shared context is read from units who share the context and stand in no relation — G3's level of what units sharing a group or occasion share — which the design observes because every unit is placed whether or not it relates. Observational selection is read from the assigned stream against the formed set. A resemblance published without the four named has been attributed to whichever cause the reader prefers.

*Groups.* A group's decomposition adds a group term: what all of a group's observations share beyond what each member brings. It is identified, per section 3's last row, from two pairs inside the group with no member in common — a group of four or more, whether or not its units are in any other group — or from units crossing groups, the co-membership graph connected; a group of three in no other group confounds the term with its members' and its pairs' terms under any replication. A symmetric observation on a group of m members needs all m present, so its completion probability is the product of m propensities and its cost is m budgets and one coordination over m windows. Complementarity in a group is the dependence of the group term on the members' positions jointly, a surface of higher order than the pair's, read only on assigned groups for the reason section 5 gives, with the number of possible groups making any pool's coverage of it sparser than the pair's by orders. And section 4's attribution results are stated for pairs and not extended here.

*Structure proposed at this grain.* A relational structure proposed from residuals — a cluster of pair terms, a shape in the complementarity surface — is admitted under G3's five clauses and not otherwise, with two clauses sharpened. The propensity the candidate must survive is the pairing rule's, since the missingness pattern at this grain is the doubly selected graph of section 5, and a structure manufactured from formation is likelier here than one manufactured from serving is at the unit grain. And the rival the candidate must not be absorbed by is the four causes above: a shape in pair terms that a homophily term, an influence term or a shared context explains is those and not a relational structure. The published failure rate of admissions covers relational candidates as a class of their own.

**8 · What the graph does to every reading made at the unit grain**

*Dependence, with its form and its sign.* Units in relations are not independent draws, since section 7's causes make related units covary, and a between-unit interval computed as though they were is wrong by a design effect that every published between-unit number carries — G1's and G3's are stated per unit, and every edge weight in the co-observation graph is an effective count under it, composed with G1's discount for drift. Its form depends on the statistic. For a mean, under correlation on the graph's edges only and none beyond, the effective number of units is the raw number over one plus the product of the mean degree and the within-relation correlation of the residuals. For a coupling — a covariance or a slope — the inflation is one plus the product of the mean degree and the two sources' within-relation residual correlations, which is much smaller whenever both are below one; at a mean degree of ten and a correlation of a tenth, a mean's interval widens by two fifths and a coupling's, with both correlations a tenth, by a twentieth. Its sign follows the correlation's: where related units are positively correlated the naive interval is too tight, and where section 7's complementarity makes them negatively correlated on a source it is too wide, so the direction is read and not assumed. The edge-only assumption fails where influence propagates, since second neighbours then covary, and the effect at distance two is stated or bounded. And the estimator changes: G6's interval by resampling units assumes units independent, which the graph denies, so the valid resampling at this grain is over connected components of the relation graph, or a resampling built for a network, and G6's pair statistics on a related population owe the same replacement.

*Interference, with the bias bounded.* Every first-class event on a member reaches the members it stands in relation to, which is why G3 identifies a randomized event's direct effect only under an exposure mapping over the relation graph, assigns on clusters where the graph is dense, and names the direct and spillover parts apart. The relational grain is where that mapping is defined, and its validity is the record's completeness: an unrecorded relation is an unrecorded exposure path. The bias that puts on the direct effect has no fixed sign — it follows the spillover's sign and the arm composition of the unseen neighbours, inflating the direct effect where unrecorded neighbours share an arm with the exposed and attenuating it where clustered assignment puts them in the other — so what is published is a bound from the estimated share of relations the record does not hold and the spillover's estimated size, and the share is estimated by asking a sample of units to name their relations against the record, with every exposure mapping.

*Leakage.* An observation on a relation has every member as its unit of protection, per G2, so a release about one member's relations composes on the budgets of every other member in them, and a release that reaches a unit's relations reaches a neighbourhood the unit did not consent for. Under a dense graph the composition is fast: a release per unit is a release per unit times its degree, and the output-privacy budget is spent at the graph's rate, not the population's.

*The two interests in one observation, and the second fork.* A directed observation about a member is the sender's raw value and the receiver's information, and the two members' interests in it differ: the sender's candour depends on who will read it, and the receiver's interest is in reading it. Whether the receiver may read what senders reported about them is the owner's, in six shapes with their arithmetic. Nothing is shown: the sender's candour is protected and the receiver learns nothing from a source that is about them. An exact aggregate over the receiver's senders is shown, with the partition arithmetic stated: a receiver with s senders who can place s minus one of them — by relation type, by period, by who was present — recovers the last, so an exact aggregate protects a sender only where the receiver cannot partition the set, which is a property of the receiver's knowledge and not of s, and the design states which partitions the receiver could make. A coarsened aggregate is shown, at a resolution or under a noise parameter from which no single contribution is recoverable, charged to the output-privacy budget of every sender in it under G2's composition, with the parameter stated. A deferred disclosure, after a stated delay or after the relation has ended, which decouples candour from disclosure by as much as the candour effect decays over the delay, a rate the arm below measures. Per-sender disclosure with the sender's consent, obtained when the report is made and not after, so that the consent is built into the observation and the sender knows the reader as they write. Per-sender disclosure open, which is a different source: a report the sender knows the receiver will read is a message to the receiver and measures presentation toward the receiver as much as the receiver. That cost is a measurement claim the design can measure — a randomized disclosure arm, a first-class event, whose contrast against the undisclosed arm is the price of the open shape in the source's own units — under G3's conditions for such an arm: it is consented to in advance, so the cost is measured on the consenting population, which is the population least likely to show one, and the direction of that selection is stated; and it is assigned on senders and clustered under section 8's own interference rule, since candour is a sender's property and a sender's disclosure reaches their relations. The fork is put with the price measured rather than asserted, and the owner rules.

**9 · Custody, erasure, the sequence, value and cost**

*Where a relational observation lives, under the layout the fork sets.* The event lives on the relation, per G2, keyed by its members and a period, in the relation's own sequence whichever member wrote it. The raw value travels by its source's custody class. A directed observation's raw value is the sender's, produced on the sender's side and held by the sender's custodians, and the relation's sequence holds the event with a reference to it where the class forbids the value to travel and the value itself where it does not. A symmetric observation's raw value is produced by the design's instrument and not by a member, and where it lives is a consequence of section 8's fork and not prior to it: a public class is held by the population fit; a class that may not travel is held by a single designated custodian or under a two-party primitive between the members' custodians, and it is replicated to both members' custodians only where the fork's answer lets both read it, since a value on the receiver's device has already been shown. There is no custodian of the relation as such — custodians are the devices units hold — and a relation's record is the union of its members' and the fit's, with the loss of a member's custodian a hole in the co-observation graph as G2 reports one.

*Erasure by a member.* G2 makes erasure an ordered event with its reach stated, and at this grain a member's erasure reaches three kinds of thing. Its own directed observations and its membership events are its own and are removed, with the folds they entered recomputed. A symmetric observation on a relation it was in is every member's, and is removed from every fold that could return it about the erased member. A directed observation it received is the sender's raw value and the receiver's protected datum at once, and the design states which of three shapes holds: removed, which destroys the sender's own datum; retained with the receiver's identifier destroyed and the value unlinkable to them, which meets an erasure right without destroying the sender's raw value and is the shape a design under such a right takes; or retained whole, which meets no such right and is available only where none binds. Whichever holds, the relationship terms the observation entered are recomputed or marked, and the publication ledger row of every release that read it states whether the erasure reached it.

*The relation in the sequence: G5's double-counting rule.* G5 makes a relation its own sequence and owes this paper the rule for how its observations appear in each member's. The rule: a directed observation about a member appears in the sender's sequence as a produced response, scored by the response factor, and in the receiver's sequence as a received event with no response factor scored; a symmetric observation appears in the relation's sequence as the response and in each member's as a received event unscored; a directed observation about the relation appears in the sender's as a response and in the relation's as a received event. So every observation is scored once, projection is visible to the learner in the sender's sequence, and the dependence of section 8 enters each member's sequence as received events it may condition on. The rule is a cut decision recorded in the fold version, and a fold that scores an observation in two sequences has counted it twice in the loss.

*Value: what a relational source is worth, and what its worth cannot see.* A relational source's value is G1's, on the uniform share of the assigned stream: its marginal saving per target, per attention unit summed over every member it draws on, as the bundle it belongs to, in G4's value ledger. Two things about that entry. The bundle is the ordinary case at this grain: an observation whose worth is that it enables a later relational observation — placing a unit on the anchors before it is offered a relation, so that section 7's homophily is readable — registers at zero credited forward and is priced as the bundle, per G1, or the relational readings that depend on it are never affordable. And the ledger is blind to the pair's term: a relational source whose whole worth is to identify a relationship term saves little predictive code length, since the term is estimated from four observations and predicts little, so identification of the pair's term is tracked as a state beside the ledger, in G4's pattern for identification, and the move that changes it — replication in both directions on assigned pairs — is priced by what it identifies and not only by what it predicts.

*Where computation binds.* The relational level's variance components are pairwise quantities across units under custody, which is the binding point G3 names with a cost at population scale it does not know. Two things soften it by a constant and one does not. The between-relation components are statistics over relations that are consistently estimated on a random sample of them; observed relations already number the population times the mean degree over two, so the saving is a constant factor and not an order, and the effective sample size is set by the units the sampled relations share and not by the relations. The partner pool is bounded, so the pairs a unit's custodian ever computes against are its pool and not the population. What does not soften is the within-relation reading: it needs the same relation across periods, so sampling is over relations and never over a relation's observations, and a relation once sampled is held for its series.

*The cost line, derived.* A relational observation draws attention on the side that produces it, in the attention units the Setting gives it per member. A directed report costs the sender's budget and no coordination. A symmetric observation costs every member's budget and a coordination the unit-level design never pays — every member available inside one window — so where each member completes with probability p, a pair completes with probability p squared, and the pairs the design must offer are the pairs it needs over p squared: at p of seven tenths, two offered for each completed. The completing pairs are a further selection on top of formation and assignment, stated with the population. The line per unit per construct follows from section 3's table. The sender and receiver terms need each unit in at least two relations in each role; the relationship term needs at least two observations per direction; so a unit sends at least four directed reports per construct — two partners, twice each — and receives four, in the replication window, which is four times the report's cost per member against B per period for the construct, before the invariance test of section 3 and the blind share of section 4 add their lines. Resolution follows the terms: the sender and receiver variances resolve at the rate of the number of units; the relationship variance at the rate of the number of replicated pairs; and the reciprocity correlation at the rate of the number of pairs observed in both directions, divided by the squared reliability of the estimated relationship terms. To tell a reciprocity correlation of a tenth from zero at the conventional resolution takes pairs of the order of the square of the sum of the two conventional resolution factors over the square of the correlation, which is about eight hundred pairs with the terms known exactly; the terms are not known exactly, and at two replicates per direction a relationship term's reliability is its variance over that variance plus half the error variance — two thirds where the two variances are equal — so the requirement is about eight hundred over four ninths, near eighteen hundred pairs, each observed twice in each direction, which is seven thousand directed reports, or at a mean degree of four about nine hundred units sending eight reports each. The harness beside the series draws that line and finds it: power near four fifths at eighteen hundred pairs, one half at eight hundred. And the line is a lower bound, because the relationship terms are estimated after the members' terms are removed, and the removal has an error of its own — centring inside groups of six, the harness finds the observed correlation about six tenths of what the reliability alone predicts — which grows as groups shrink. Every construct is its own line, and the line is stated against B and against the pool's standing charge of section 5.

**10 · The pair in the space**

G6 says a pair's outcome is a function of the two members' coordinates and a relation-specific latent, never of distance alone. Section 3 says what that latent is — the relationship term — and what identifies it; section 5 says where its surface over positions can be read at all; and both consume G6's placements, so both owe G6's corrections: the surface is fitted over shrunken terms and shrunken placements with the attenuation carried, and a use that reads it for a target carries the distance validity G6 defines for that target. The relation graph embedded in the space draws homophily as a geometry, ties among the near, and section 7 says which of four causes drew it and under what design the arrow between two of them is read. The geometry is the picture, and the grain is the estimand.

**11 · What would have to hold**

*"Relation" names one of three objects in every claim* — a recorded membership, an observation on a relation, or a relational latent — every observation is one of three kinds, and a graph is a set of memberships.

*A directed observation about a member is decomposed into mean, sender, receiver, relationship and error*, with generalized and dyadic reciprocity across them, under the stated independence assumptions, and each term is published only where its identifying design was bought: two relations per unit in each role for the members' terms, replication of the same directed pair inside the relationship's timescale for the relationship term, both directions in separate sittings for the dyadic covariance and replication besides for its correlation; one observation per pair publishes the relationship and error variances as a sum and an upper bound; a term shared by pairs is published only where the context holds two pairs with no member in common or where units cross contexts.

*G1's and G3's condition on reciprocal observation is superseded by section 3's table*, and the two earlier sentences are corrected in place.

*A relational source's measurement model is tested for invariance across relation types before any reading pools them*, the comparable type set is named per comparison where invariance is partial, and the test's observations are a priced line.

*Projection is identified against a unit-level source on the same construct*, bought where the construct is not an anchor, linked through its estimated parameters with the invariance the link needs tested, and a directed source states which of two constructs it measures.

*Every relationship term is a shrunken posterior published with its shrinkage*, and the complementarity surface is a fitted surface over shrunken terms with the attenuation carried.

*The comparison's difference-only link is stated as an assumption of the measurement model*, a comparison publishes contrasts and never a mean, a joint outcome attributes latents only on components with an odd cycle, and each is pinned by a purchased unit-level source, an asymmetric condition or a per-member outcome inside the encounter, with the link's invariance priced.

*The pairing rule is recorded with every relational observation and its propensity enters every relational coupling's estimator*, pairing on the logged past is permitted and pairing on anything measured with or after the response is not, and a blind share of pairings, stated in pairs, is what every coupling against a unit-level source is estimated on, without shrinkage toward what the prior-informed pairs computed.

*Every relational reading names its population* — formed relations or possible pairs — and a claim about possible pairs is read on the assigned stream, whose uniform share is the eligible-drawn stream at this grain and is never averaged with its adaptive share.

*The partner pool's size, the per-pair floor, the maximum weight and the standing charge are one decision, stated as four numbers*, and pairs outside every pool are reported as never eligible.

*Whether the design may prompt a relation is the owner's fork*, in the four shapes of section 5, and the readings name the shape that held.

*Formation is read as an offer randomized and an acceptance chosen*, with the offer's and the relation's effects on each member identified by one randomization, their contrast the asymmetry of the relation's effects, and influence separated from initiation only under offers randomized in both directions, and formation the units made themselves read under sequential ignorability and no better.

*Within-relation readings are on the same relation across periods with a window and a jointly estimated centre*, and dissolution is a hazard beside the response, estimable on the observed history, with its dependence on the contemporaneous latent bounded and checked on the members' post-dissolution observations, and the survivors named.

*Resemblance among the related is published with its four causes separated on the stratum of relations formed after placement, net of the placements' posterior error* — homophily on pre-formation placement, influence under a randomized offer, shared context on units sharing it without a relation, observational selection against the assigned stream — with the pre-record stratum's share reported, and a group term identified only where units cross groups, with a group's completion a product of as many propensities as members.

*A relational structure proposed from residuals passes the five clauses against the pairing rule's propensity and the four causes as its rival.*

*Every between-unit interval and every co-observation weight carries the graph's design effect in the form its statistic takes and the sign its correlation has*, the assumption of no correlation beyond an edge is stated or bounded, and intervals are by resampling connected components or a network resampling, never units.

*Every exposure mapping is published with the estimated share of relations the record holds and a bound, not a sign, on the direct effect from the share it does not.*

*An observation on a relation has every member as its unit of protection*, and the budget composes at the graph's rate.

*Whether a receiver may read what senders reported is the owner's fork*, in the six shapes of section 8 with the partition arithmetic, the coarsening parameter and the delay stated, and the open shape's candour cost measured by a randomized disclosure arm on the consenting population with its selection's direction stated, assigned on senders and clustered.

*A relational observation's event lives on the relation and its raw value under its class*, a symmetric value is replicated to both members only where the fork lets both read it, there is no custodian of a relation as such, and a member's erasure reaches its own observations, the symmetric ones, and the directed ones it received in a stated one of three shapes, with the ledger row of every release stating whether the erasure reached it.

*Every observation is scored once across the relation's and the members' sequences*, under the double-counting rule recorded in the fold version.

*A relational source's value is its saving on the uniform share per attention unit summed over its members, as a bundle*, and identification of a pair's term is a state beside the ledger.

*The cost of a relational construct is stated as a line against B* — reports per unit per construct in the replication window, offered pairs over completed under two-sided non-response with p stated, the invariance test, the blind share, the pool's standing charge — and the pairs a reciprocity needs are derived with the relationship terms' reliability at the chosen replication.

**12 · The potential, at full scale**

At full scale — a population placed at entry so that relations formed afterward have their members' placements before them, a partner pool per unit with its floor, an assigned stream beside the formed relations with a uniform share and a blind share, and replication in both directions in separate sittings on the relations it holds — the relational grain becomes the level at which "you and them" is an estimand rather than a display. What is knowable through it that no unit-level design offers, each with its condition: what each unit brings to every relation and draws from every relation, apart from what any one relation carries, where every unit is in two relations per role; whether what a pair carries runs both ways, as a covariance where both directions were observed apart and as a correlation where they were replicated; whether those who give are given, on the same degree condition, with its interval under the graph's design effect; the surface of complementarity over positions, on the diagonal at formation's density and off it on the assigned stream, fitted over shrunken terms; whether the related are alike because they chose, changed, shared, or were recorded, as four numbers on the post-placement stratum under a randomized offer; what a comparison network is worth once a purchased unit-level source gives it a scale, which is every contrast it holds and no mean; how relations move and end, on the survivors, with the members' later observations as the check; and how far every unit-level interval in the design was wrong before the graph's dependence was in it, in the direction the correlation's sign gives. The conditions are the price, and this grain's price is stated as a line rather than ranked: four observations across two budgets and a coordination per identified relationship term against one observation per identified unit mean, a pool the design pays a standing charge for, an assignment the units did not choose and the owner must license, and a disclosure the design must price before it makes it.

**13 · What remains open**

Whether the partner pool can be drawn without the drawing rule itself selecting the possible-pair population the assigned stream exists to reach, and what pool size reaches the complementarity surface off the diagonal at a density any interval can use. Whether the complier readings on prompted relations describe anything but compliers, and what share of a population accepts a prompted relation at all under each of the four shapes. Whether the graph's design effect can be estimated on a record whose completeness is itself estimated, whether a network resampling is valid on a graph that is doubly selected, and whether the two errors compound past the interval's use. Whether the relationship term's timescale is short enough that replication inside it is affordable, or whether for most relational sources the pair's own term is an upper bound forever, and the reciprocity correlation's reliability keeps its line out of reach. Whether a group's complementarity surface is readable at any scale of assignment, given that the number of possible groups outruns every pool. What the pairwise primitive costs at population scale for the within-relation reading, which no sampling softens and which G2, G3 and G6 leave open with this paper. Whether the candour cost of open disclosure, once measured on the consenting population, transfers to the population that did not consent, or moves with every relation type and must be measured again wherever the fork is put. And whether the eligible-drawn stream at this grain — pairs drawn uniformly from pools that the design bounded — is uniform over anything a relational source's value should be read on, or whether bounding the pool has already selected the stream that was to be selected on by nothing.
