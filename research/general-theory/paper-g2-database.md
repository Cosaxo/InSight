# The database as the model

*General theory, paper G2: a data structure for many-source, many-grain, adaptively collected, custody-partitioned measurements of a population.*

**Status: theory research.** General track: this paper is about any population measured by many sources, and it names no particular product or source. `research/README.md` says what a paper is and how the tracks relate. It stands on paper G1, whose design layer produces the data this structure holds.

**Perfect-form test.** If the app did not exist, this would still be worth saying because a database is normally designed to hold what was observed, and data chosen under a budget, at several grains, under several custodies, mean nothing without the record of why each observation was attempted, what the unit's state was when it was, and which version of which model has read it since; the structure that holds all of that is a different object from a table of responses, and it has not been written down as one.

**Setting.** A population of units, standing in relations, measured across periods by K sources as paper G1 defines them: each with a measurement model, a bias structure, a cost, a custody class and a grain. A custody class is a constraint set on where a source's raw values may travel, and the classes need not be totally ordered. The observations were chosen by a policy that logged its reasons and its realised probabilities. The system has three readers: the unit's own custodians, the devices the unit holds, which keep that unit's raw values and compute on them; the population fit, which estimates the joint model; and a research and audit reader, which must be able to check any published number as it stood on any date. Models are revised over time. Some sources' raw values may not leave the unit's custodians. Nothing in this paper depends on what the units or the sources are.

**Abstract.** A database is usually a schema over what was observed. For data chosen under a budget, at several grains, under several custodies, by a policy that decided what to observe, that is not enough: a response without the decision that produced it cannot be modelled honestly, a response without the unit's state when it was given cannot be read for change, a statistic without the model version that defined it cannot be reproduced, and a value that may not leave its custodian cannot be joined by moving it. This paper argues that the right structure is the joint model itself, held as versioned model-relative summaries, beside an ordered record from which everything public can be re-derived and everything else verified. It states the atom of the record, the observation attempt with its decision, its outcome, its observed state and a reference to the estimate the policy used; the ordering the record can have across many writers; the three grains and the tensor as a view; the model store, including the participation record of every aggregation round and a publication ledger in which every release, the shipped model versions included, has a row; custody as physical layout, with a join computed by the custodians of the columns it touches, summaries that are not private for being summaries, secure aggregation stated with its conditions, and an output-privacy budget defined on a named unit of protection and composed across every release; time held as version intervals over ledger positions; folds whose equality between incremental and batch runs is stated as the algebraic condition it is; erasure as an ordered event, with what it does and does not reach; the type a published number must carry, which no fold may omit; and the three readers as releases with rows. It prices what custody costs, and it states the open problems, chief among them that a summary is sufficient only for the model that defined it while custody forbids asking the past a new question.

**1 · What the discovery problem demands of storage**

Paper G1 shows that data chosen under a budget carry their meaning in the choosing. Seven demands follow for whatever holds them, and a schema over responses meets none of them by default.

It must store why, and what was refused. Every observation was attempted for a reason, with a probability, and some attempts were declined or expired. Inference on chosen data is honest only conditional on that record, including the attempts that produced no response; a store that holds what was observed and not what was attempted holds a dataset whose selection cannot be modelled.

It must store the state at the time. A response is read for change only against what was true of the unit when it was given, and the unit's slow attributes, recent occasions and estimated position all move. The observed state must be frozen with the response, and the estimated state referenced, or both are lost.

It must hold several grains. Units, relations between units, and occasions are three kinds of thing, and an observation on a relation belongs to neither member. A store with one grain forces two of them into the third.

It must respect custody by construction. Some values may not leave the custodians that hold them, and a join that moves them is not a join the system may make. The structure must make the join computable without the move, and must not mistake a summary for a safe object.

It must version its summaries against the model that defined them. A summary is sufficient for one model iterate, and the model changes.

It must record what it published. Every release, an estimate, a count, a model version shipped to devices, spends against a privacy budget and creates a differencing surface against the next release, so the history of publication is part of the data.

And it must be checkable. Any number the system ever published must be reproducible as it stood on the day it was published where it was computed on public values, and verifiable against a recorded transcript where it was not, or the system cannot be audited and its claims cannot be checked.

**2 · The atom: the observation attempt**

*Proposition 1.* The native record is the observation attempt: one unit or relation, one source, one instrument version, one serving decision with its reason and its realised probability, the unit's observed state as of that time, a reference to the estimated state the policy used, and an outcome, which is a response, a refusal, or an expiry. Anything smaller loses meaning; anything larger is a fold.

*Argument.* Remove the decision and its probability and the response can no longer be modelled as chosen. Remove the refusals and expiries and non-response is confounded with non-selection, so propensities are recorded only where the unit complied and paper G1's ignorability is lost exactly where it matters. Remove the observed state and the response can no longer be read for change. Remove the instrument version and a change in the instrument reads as a change in the unit. Remove the time and the response cannot be placed on any within-unit series. Each field is load-bearing for a class of reading, and the classes are the ones paper G1 says the design exists to support. Conversely, anything assembled from several attempts, a unit's mean, a coupling, a posterior, is a derived quantity, reproducible from the attempts and a model version, and therefore a fold rather than a record.

*State, observed and estimated.* The observed state is the attributes and occasion facts recorded as of the time of the attempt, frozen in the event. The estimated state, the unit's position as the policy's model held it, is not copied into the event; it is referenced by model version and posterior identifier, because a copy would put a model's output inside the record from which that model must be re-derivable, and the model would then read the state the model wrote.

*Granularity.* Where one decision serves several responses, the decision is one event and each response is an event naming it, so a probability is recorded once and not once per response. Where a source is a stream the custodian folds before anything is reportable, the atom is the custodian's fold with its window and fold version, and the stream beneath it is never in the ledger.

*Admissibility.* A response is admissible only against a serving decision the design log issued, so the reason and the realised probability are the system's record and not the device's claim, and an event that names no decision is not an event. What a custodian may write is an outcome for a decision that exists. Without this, a device that lies moves the co-observation counts the design acts on, which is cheaper than poisoning a fit and reaches the whole population's allocation.

*The ledger and its order.* Events are appended and never edited; a correction is a new event that refers to the old one, and an erasure is an ordered event as section 8 describes. A late report, an observation supplied after the period it describes, is an event with two times, the period it describes and the moment it arrived, and both are kept. The ledger is totally ordered per unit and per relation, and only partially ordered across them: a globally ordered log across a population is either the single bottleneck or a fiction. A prefix is a cut closed under those orders, a fold's determinism is stated relative to a cut rather than to wall-clock time, and an event on a relation joins the relation's sequence whichever member wrote it.

**3 · Three grains, and the tensor as a view**

The unit grain holds fixed and slow attributes as versioned facts: a value, the time it became true, the time it stopped. The relation grain holds pairs and groups keyed by their members and a period, with membership itself as events, so that a relation's history is recoverable and an observation on a relation is stored on the relation and not on a member. The occasion grain holds everything that varies per period, which is most of the ledger.

The tensor of units by sources by periods that paper G1 reasons about is a derived view over these three, never a stored object, because it is almost entirely empty and its emptiness is design rather than data. What is stored about the emptiness is two things that are not the same kind of thing. The co-observation counts, for every pair of sources within every declared conditioning cell, are a fold over events and replay like any other. The identification intervals of the couplings are a model artefact: each carries the model version that produced it, the assumption under which a coupling never co-observed is bounded at all, and its own determinism boundary. The design reads both, and the difference between them is why they are stored as two things. The counts are exact inside the fit, where the design reads them. What the system publishes of them is a release with a row, under the same budget as any estimate, because a count over a fine cross-classification of a changing population, published twice, names whoever joined it.

**4 · The model store**

The structure's second half is the model, held as data. It contains, each versioned against the model that produced it: a posterior over each unit's position in the shared latent space and over each unit's within-unit parameters; the measurement parameters of each source, including each source's invariance status across the groups the system compares; the couplings between every pair of sources at each level, with their intervals, their identification basis and the co-observation counts that identify them; the relationship terms of the relation grain; the residuals, kept rather than discarded, because paper G1's self-extension reads them; the evaluation streams, tagged on every event so that no number computed on one is ever averaged with another; the participation record, one row per aggregation round, saying which custodians contributed, at which model version, and which contributions were rejected as stale; and the publication ledger, one row per release, where a release is any quantity that leaves the fit: a published estimate, a published count, every model version shipped to custodians or read outside the fit, and every audit export. A model fitted on a population discloses that population, and a sequence of versions shipped over a growing cohort differences like any other sequence, so it is budgeted like one.

*Proposition 2.* Holding the model as versioned data is necessary for a published number to be reproducible or verifiable and for the system to be auditable; together with the determinism record of section 7 and the participation record, it is sufficient. A statistic without its model version is a number with no definition; a release without its ledger row is a privacy expenditure nobody recorded.

**5 · Custody as layout**

*Proposition 3.* Custody classes are physically separate stores; a join is computed by the custodians of the columns it touches and never by moving them; and what crosses a boundary is a model-relative summary, never a row. Under that rule the crossings that summation reaches are possible at a stated cost, and the crossings that need a pairwise primitive are named as holes rather than assumed.

*The rule.* A public value may travel anywhere and is fitted centrally. A value that may not leave its custodians stays with them, and the custodian computes what the joint model needs from it: the expected sufficient statistics of the current model iterate where the model admits them, a clipped gradient at the current parameter where it does not, and the unit's own posterior. The server aggregates contributions and never holds what produced them. Where one custodian holds every private column a join touches, the join runs there. Where two do, a relatedness between two units, a component of population structure, a within-pair contrast, the join runs as a pairwise primitive, a two-party computation between the two custodians, and nowhere else; a second custodian that holds two units' data, an enclave, is itself a custody decision the structure states rather than a detail. Composition by summation reaches first-order statistics and no pairwise quantity, and the absence of the primitive is recorded as a hole in the co-observation graph.

*A summary is not private for being a summary.* A fixed-dimension statistic that does not grow with the data exists essentially only for the simplest model families; the object here is a latent model with nonlinear measurement models, so what a custodian sends is sufficient for one iterate of one model and worthless to the next version. And a contribution computed on one unit's small dataset can be inverted toward that data. What protects it is the input-privacy mechanism, and the summary's shape only bounds what that mechanism must hide.

*Input privacy.* Secure aggregation gives the server the sum over the participating set and nothing else, under a threshold of non-colluding participants, no collusion between server and participant, and a participating set the server does not choose. It fails not by being broken but by being run on a set of one, or on two sets differing by one, with the protocol satisfied throughout; so who is in a round is a fact the structure records in the participation record and constrains, rather than a scheduling detail. Robustness against a faulty or hostile custodian is bought at the custodian: a bound on each contribution proved to the server without revealing the contribution, and a robust aggregation operating on the protected sum. Both bias the estimate by an amount the structure bounds rather than states, since the bias depends on the distribution the protocol hides. A contribution computed against a superseded model version is stale, and the fold names which it does: rejects it, or corrects it to the current version.

*Output privacy.* Input privacy says nothing about what the published aggregate reveals. Output privacy is a separate mechanism with its own ledger: a budget defined on a stated unit of protection, a unit's whole contribution across all periods, and for an observation on a relation every member of it, composed across every release that read that unit under a composition rule the ledger names, with a release refused when the composition would exceed it. Differencing is defeated by composing the budget over the whole history and not by freezing a cohort: two releases on one frozen cohort compose, and two frozen cohorts difference on their overlap. Each release is computed on a cohort the ledger names and at a resolution from which no single contribution is recoverable, and the publication ledger of section 4 is where this is enforced, because a budget that is not recorded is not a budget.

*What custody costs.* Sufficiency is relative to a model iterate, so a model revision costs a round-trip to every custodian, and no question can be asked of the past that the previous model's summaries did not anticipate. Each aggregation round samples the custodians that were online, which is a selection by time zone, power and connectivity that enters every estimate and is why the participation record exists. Individual-level diagnostics, outlier inspection and record linkage are unavailable by construction on the custody side. And secure aggregation hides exactly the contributions one would inspect to detect a poisoned or faulty custodian, which is why robustness is bought at the source and costs bias. The partition is therefore explicit: public columns are fitted centrally and are the diagnostic surface, and the cost above is paid only for the joins that cross into custody.

**6 · Time, held as versions over positions**

*Proposition 4.* Every event carries a valid time, the moment it describes, with the observed state as of that moment. Every version of a derived quantity carries the transaction-time interval over which it was current and the ledger cut it was computed from, so the number as of a date is the version current then and is recomputable by replaying to that cut. A structure with only the first cannot be audited; with only the second it cannot read change.

*Argument.* Change is read against what was true then, so the observed state is frozen with the event and never updated when the unit's attributes later move; a unit that changes country does not have its past responses re-filed under the new one. Reproduction is a statement about what a derived quantity contained on a date, so each version records the interval it was current and the cut it saw, and a late-arriving event enters the derived quantity in a later version, which is exactly the fact an auditor needs to explain why a number published in one month differs from the same number recomputed in the next. Instrument versions are time-stamped changes to a source's measurement model, so that a change to a source's wording, a rescaled response format or a custodian's updated folding algorithm is a boundary the model can see rather than a change in the population. Transaction time is monotone by construction, and erasure is the one operation that moves it backwards, which is why section 8 makes erasure an ordered event and not a deletion.

**7 · Folds, and where their equalities end**

*Proposition 5.* Every read model is a function of a ledger cut, a model version and a fold version, and where the function is deterministic its replay reproduces the read model; where it is produced by a stochastic or distributed procedure, its record carries the seed, the participation set of each round and the reduction order, and reproduction is a re-run under that record. An incremental fold equals a batch fold only under a stated algebraic condition, and a retraction needs an inverse the fold may not have.

*Argument.* Determinism is what makes audit possible: a sampled replay from the ledger against the stored read model is a test the system can run on itself, and a mismatch is a defect in the fold, a corruption of the store, or a redaction the replay can name, never an ambiguity. Incremental folds equal batch folds where the fold is a homomorphism of a commutative monoid of events and both runs are cut on the same time axis: a count cut on transaction time and the same count cut on valid time are two numbers, and a late-arriving event moves the second and not the first. Retraction, which erasure requires, needs an inverse besides: counts and sums have one; maxima, distinct counts, quantiles and any saturating fold do not, and those are recomputed rather than adjusted. The structure records, per read model, which case it is in, so that a replay that disagrees on a saturating fold is read as the saturation it is and a replay that disagrees on a count is read as the corruption it is. Fits are not deterministic functions of a cut: initialisation, minibatch order, sampling, reduction order and above all which custodians were online each round all enter, which is why the participation record is part of the model store and why a fit is reproduced by re-running under its record rather than by replaying a fold.

**8 · Evolution and erasure as transactions**

*Proposition 6.* A change to the model or the schema is a named, ordered version whose transition commits all or none, while the rewrite of derived data behind it may be eager, lazy or incremental; and the erasure of a unit is an ordered event and a transaction across the ledger, the folds, the model store and the publication ledger, with a stated outcome for each.

*Evolution.* A version that lands half-applied leaves two definitions of every statistic alive at once, and a reader cannot know which one a number carries. So the transition is atomic in its definition even where the rewrite is not, every prior version remains reproducible from the ledger, and a derived quantity carries the version it was computed under until it is recomputed.

*Erasure.* A unit's payloads are kept out of immutable structures wherever possible, so that erasing the unit is the removal of its events and a recomputation of the folds they entered. The erasure is itself an ordered event carrying the identifiers and hashes of what it removed, so a replay stays deterministic given the redaction set and a mismatch has three readings rather than two. Where payloads must live in an immutable structure, erasure is destruction of the key that renders them readable, and the record states that this guarantee is computational and that derived quantities which already absorbed the plaintext are not reached by it. Fitted estimates that a unit's contribution entered are recomputed where the fold is reproducible, or record that a contribution was withdrawn where it is not. Published aggregates are not withdrawn, and what erasure did to them depends on how they were released: where a release carried a per-unit output-privacy guarantee, the unit's presence was already indistinguishable in it and the ledger row says so; where a release was coarsened or suppressed rather than noised, no such guarantee exists, and the row records it as a release erasure cannot undo. The publication ledger says which releases the unit's data entered, so the erasure can state what it did and did not reach.

**9 · The type of a published number**

*Proposition 7.* A published quantity is an object, not a scalar, and no fold returns a scalar: the type is the return type of every read model, and the publication ledger row is written from it. It carries its level, between units or within a unit or on a relation; its population and basis, including the consent and participation model that shaped who is in it; its uncertainty, as an interval or a standard error, with whether that interval is likelihood-based conditional on the log or a coverage claim; its identification basis, the co-observation weight it rests on and the assumption under which it is a point if it was never co-observed; its decomposition into the components the model separates where one is known, and a statement that none is where none is; the validity and invariance status of the sources that produced it; its design, including whether it was computed on the adaptive stream with propensities, on an evaluation stream, or on both reported separately; the model version and fold version that define it; the transformation applied before release, noise or coarsening, with its parameter; the identifier of the ledger row that authorised the release; and the date as of which it was true.

*Argument.* Every field names a way a bare number has misled a reader: a between-unit coupling read as a fact about one unit, a population read as everyone when it was the consenting or the persistent, a difference in level read as a difference in the quantity when the source was not invariant, an interval read as coverage when it was conditional, a noised number read as exact, a number quoted a year after the cohort that produced it moved. Enforcing the shape as a type is cheaper than correcting each misreading afterwards, and it is the only way the correction reaches every downstream reader at once.

**10 · Three readers, three layouts, each a release**

A unit is not a device. The unit's raw store has a stated set of custodians, the devices the unit holds, or a device and a backup whose key only the unit holds, and the loss of one is a loss of raw values that the co-observation graph reports as a hole rather than as an absence of the unit. The custodians hold the unit's raw values for every source whatever its custody, the unit's own posterior and within-unit summary, and the code to compute its contribution to any population statistic the current model version defines. They are the only places the unit's private raw values ever exist, and they are where the unit reads about itself.

The population fit holds the ledger of public events, the design log, the aggregated contributions, the participation record and the model store. It is the diagnostic surface for everything public and a blind aggregator for everything private, and the boundary between the two is the partition of section 5.

The research and audit reader is a release like any other and has a row. Inside the custody boundary and under a consent scoped to audit, it holds the design log and the per-unit posteriors, which are individual-level data on every unit however public the columns they were fitted from. Outside it, it holds them at the resolution the checks it must run require. Which of the two a deployment is, is a stated fact about it. From it any published number computed on public columns is reproducible as of any date. A number that crossed a custody boundary is verifiable rather than reproducible: the reader recomputes the public part, checks the aggregation transcript against the commitments the contributing custodians published, and redraws the release's noise from its recorded seed, without ever holding a contribution. The structure states, per number, which of the two it is.

These are three layouts of one record, not three systems. Each is a fold over the ledger and the model store at a resolution and a custody that suit its reader, and no layout carries a value the ledger and the model store cannot re-derive or verify.

**11 · Efficiency**

The efficiencies here are the ones that let the structure run at population scale without changing what it can know. Folds are incremental where section 7's condition holds, so that a new event updates the read models it enters rather than triggering a recomputation of everything. Read models are held at the resolution at which they will be read, so that per-read work is bounded by resolution rather than by record count. The design log is larger per observation than the response it annotates and is read by the fit and the audit rather than by any display, so its size is never the cost that decides anything. The index the design reads most is a per-unit bitmap of sources observed and a counter per pair per declared conditioning cell, maintained incrementally; the conditioning set is versioned with the model, and a revision that changes it rebuilds the view rather than amending it. None of these changes what is knowable; the discovery problem decides that. They decide whether the structure can hold it.

**12 · What would have to hold**

Custodians compute. A unit's custodian can hold its raw values, compute a posterior and a contribution, prove a bound on the contribution without revealing it, and take part in a pairwise primitive where one is needed.

The ledger is appended by every writer and edited by none, including the system's own corrections and erasures, which are ordered events; and no event is admitted that names no serving decision.

The participation record is written for every aggregation round, and no round runs on a participating set the server chose or on a set small enough to name a contributor.

The publication ledger is written before a release leaves the system, and a release without a row does not happen; a shipped model version and an audit export are releases.

Model versions are named, transitions are atomic in definition, and every version remains reproducible or verifiable from the record.

The evaluation streams and the adaptive stream are tagged at the event and never merged in any fold.

Consent is scoped to a use and recorded as an event with a valid time, so that the population of every statistic can be stated, and revocation is an event that the erasure transaction reads.

**13 · The potential, and what remains open**

Fully realised, this is a structure in which the model of a population is the database, every number it publishes carries what it means and can be rebuilt or verified as of any date, the joins that summation reaches run without any private value leaving the custodians that own it, the ones that need more are named as holes, and the design that chose the data is stored beside the data so that the choosing can be modelled. It is the structure paper G1's design needs and paper G3's mechanisms read from, and it is a different object from a table of responses.

What remains open is research. A summary is sufficient only for the model iterate that defined it while custody forbids asking the past a new question, and the trade between storing more general summaries at the custodian and revising models less often has no general solution. The output-privacy budget is finite while the set of releases a growing system could make, its shipped model versions included, is not, and how to spend a finite budget across an unbounded publication surface is a design problem with no settled shape. Pairwise primitives at population scale are costly and their cost curve is not known, and whether an enclave is an acceptable second custodian is a decision each deployment must make in the open. Erasure of a unit whose contribution entered a fitted estimate is exact only where the fold is reproducible, and how to state honestly what erasure did to an estimate that cannot be recomputed is a definition still owed. And the type of a published number is enforceable inside one system; how it survives the number's journey into prose, a reader's memory and another system's tables is not a database problem, and this paper claims no answer to it.
