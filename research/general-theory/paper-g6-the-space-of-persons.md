# The space of persons

*General paper G6: what the geometry every paper calls "one" actually is — three spaces that share a name — what a distance in it licenses and how that licence is earned, whether a population is regions or a continuum, how the space survives a new source, what a direction in it may be called, and what the space is for and must not do.*

**Status: theory research.** Nothing here describes any product; `research/README.md` says what a general paper is and what holds it to its setting. G1 supplies the co-observation graph and the two evaluation streams; G3 the classical floor, level separation and the admission test; G5 the learned representation, the probes and the interventions that read it; G2 the version row and the release a coordinate is; G4 the value ledger a new source is priced in.

**Perfect-form test.** If the app did not exist, this would still be worth saying because every design that measures a population on many sources ends by saying that each unit is a point in one space — and a point in a space is a promise about distances, that units near one another are alike in a way that matters, which is a claim that has to be earned against something the space did not use, and which most such designs never test; and because whether that space is a continuum or a set of regions, how many dimensions it needs, and what a direction in it may honestly be called, are questions about a population that only a space of this kind can pose and only a validated one can answer.

**Setting.** A population of units measured by K sources under an attention budget, as in G1, with the coupling structure across units, within units over periods, and on relations as the object of discovery. Add that the mechanisms of G3 and the learner of G5 each place every unit as a point in a space: the classical model in a latent space whose coordinates are named and whose covariance is estimated, the learner in a representation whose coordinates mean nothing that can be stated. Add that the space is used — to say which units are alike, to pair them, to rank them along a direction, to place a newcomer — and that each use is a claim. Nothing here depends on what the units, the sources or the uses are.

**Abstract.** The papers of this series say, in nearly every section, that a unit is a point in one geometry. This paper is about that geometry, and its first claim is that "one" names three. The classical model's latent space has coordinates that are named dimensions and a covariance that makes a distance meaningful under it; the learner's representation has coordinates that mean nothing and a distance that is whatever the predictive objective happened to make it; and the outcome space, in which two units are close if their futures are alike, is the one every use actually wants and neither of the others is. A distance in any of them licenses nothing until it is earned: proximity in the space must predict agreement on sources the space was not fitted on, on the eligible-drawn stream, and that validity is an estimand with a number, not a property a space has by being a space. From there the paper asks the four questions only a validated space can pose. Whether the population is regions or a continuum is a property of the space and of its metric together, and the honest default is a continuum until a multimodality test says otherwise, because a learner manufactures clusters from the missingness pattern as readily as from the people. How many dimensions the population needs is an intrinsic dimension per grain, with the between-unit and the within-unit geometries as two spaces that share nothing but the units, and the trait and the state as two subspaces of the second. What happens when a source is added is that the space rotates or grows, and a unit's coordinates before and after are comparable only under an alignment stated as predictive equivalence, never as coordinate survival. And what a direction in the space may be called is a claim earned by a probe with a known answer and an intervention, per G5, and withheld otherwise — a named direction is a hypothesis wearing a label. The paper ends with what the space is for and what it must not do: "units like this one" is a product only where the distance is validated; pairing units on proximity is an intervention that moves the phenomena the space describes; ranking units along a direction is a screening instrument and is the fork the declared-difference paper puts to an owner; and a unit's coordinates are a release with a row and a budget.

**1 · The problem, stated structurally**

A design that measures a population on many sources produces, at its end, a placement: each unit is a point, and the points sit in a space. The series says so from its first paper and never says what the space is. It is treated as a given, the way a corpus is treated as a given in the recipe of G5, and the same thing happens: the object nobody examines is the one carrying the promise.

The promise is about distances. To say a unit is a point is to say that two units near one another are alike, and that being alike matters — that they will answer alike, respond alike, pair well, or share a fate. None of that follows from having coordinates. A space is a set of numbers per unit with a metric on them, and the metric was chosen by whatever fitted the space, for that fitter's purpose. Whether the distances it induces mean anything for a purpose the fitter did not have is an empirical question, and the answer is a number.

This paper is that number, and the questions that become askable once it exists.

**2 · Three spaces that share a name**

*The latent space of the classical model.* G3's joint model places each unit on named dimensions — a coordinate per latent — with a covariance among them estimated from the population. Distances here are meaningful under that covariance: two units are close if they differ little along dimensions the population varies on much, and a distance is a statement in the model's own terms. Its coordinates can be named because the model was built from named measurement models. Its limit is its class: whatever structure is not covariance is not in the space.

*The representation of the learner.* G5's model places each unit as a vector whose coordinates mean nothing that can be stated, in a space whose metric is whatever made the predictive objective fall. Two units are close because the model found it useful to treat them alike when predicting their next observation — which is a real fact about them, and a fact in the model's terms, not the population's. The space can hold structure the classical class cannot, and it can hold the serving policy and the missingness just as readily, per G5.

*The outcome space.* Neither of those is the space any use actually wants. What a use wants is that two units near each other will be alike on what comes next — on held-out sources, on future periods, on how they respond to an event. That is a space defined by futures, and it is not directly constructible, because the futures are what is being predicted. The two constructible spaces are proxies for it, and the whole of section 3 is the measurement of how good a proxy each is.

*Why "one geometry" is a misleading phrase.* The series has used it to mean that every unit is placed in the same space, which is true and important — G1's anchor set is what makes it so. It has not meant, and this paper does not let it mean, that the three spaces above are one. A distance that means something in the classical space may mean nothing in the learner's, and neither is the outcome space until shown to be.

**3 · A distance licenses nothing until it is earned**

*Distance validity as an estimand.* For a space and its metric, the validity of distance is how well proximity predicts agreement on something the space was not fitted on: hold out a source, or a period, or a class of event; compute the distance between units in the space fitted without it; and read whether nearer units agree more on the held-out thing, on the eligible-drawn stream so the policy's selection is not what agrees. That is a number per space, per metric, per held-out target, and it is the licence every use below needs. A space with no such number is a set of coordinates, and a claim that two units are alike because they are close in it is unearned.

*Validity is per target and does not transfer.* A space in which distance predicts agreement on answers may not predict agreement on responses to an event, and a space validated for one held-out source may fail for another. So the licence is per use: the target the use cares about is the target the distance is validated against, and a space carries a table of validities and not a verdict.

*The classical space's validity is a bound the learner must beat.* Under G3's rule, the learner keeps its place only by beating the floor per estimand; here the estimand is distance validity per target, and a learned space whose distances predict less than the classical space's is a worse geometry however lower its loss, since loss and distance validity are different quantities and the second is the one a use needs.

*Two units close in every space are the test of the metric, not the reward.* Where the classical and learned spaces agree on who is near whom, the agreement is evidence about the population only if both were validated; where they disagree, the disagreement is a reading — the learner found a similarity the covariance cannot express, or the learner found the policy — and the admission test of G3 is what decides which.

**4 · Regions or a continuum**

*The question, and why it is about the metric too.* Whether a population falls into groups or spreads along a continuum is the question the declared-difference paper asks of one condition and this paper asks of everyone. It is a property of the space and of its metric together: a continuum under one metric can look clustered under another, and a learner's metric was chosen for prediction, not for whether it makes clusters. So the question is put to a validated space under a stated metric, and the answer is qualified by both.

*The honest default is a continuum.* A learner manufactures clusters from the missingness pattern — units served the same items look alike for that reason — and from the anchor set's grain, and from the policy's cohort-wise serving, as readily as from the people. A clustering that is not put to a test is a description of the design. So the default is that the population is a continuum, and a region is claimed only where a multimodality test rejects the continuum on the eligible-drawn stream, on held-out sources, with the propensity weighted, and where the region survives the five clauses of G3 as a proposed structure. A region that survives is a finding about the population; one that does not is a finding about the serving.

*What a region licenses, and what it does not.* A region is a density, not a boundary: units at its edge are not members or non-members, and a use that assigns membership has drawn a line the space does not contain. A region also does not license a name until section 6's probes say what the region is, and a region named for the units that were expected to be in it is the category door of the appearance paper, generalized.

**5 · Dimensions, and the two geometries that share only the units**

*Intrinsic dimension per grain.* How many dimensions the population needs is not the number of sources and not the number the fitter chose; it is the dimension of the manifold the units actually occupy, estimated from the space itself, and it is a finding. It is estimated per grain, because the between-unit geometry — how units differ from one another — and the within-unit geometry — how one unit moves across periods — are two spaces. G3's level separation says they are separate estimands; here it says they are separate spaces, with separate dimensions, that share only the units placed in both, and a distance in one says nothing about a distance in the other.

*Trait and state as two subspaces of the within-unit geometry.* Within a unit, the slow directions and the fast ones are two subspaces, and the split between them is the state-share question of the body paper made geometric: the fast subspace's dimension is how many independent ways a unit moves day to day, and its share of the within-unit variance is how much of the unit is weather. A learner's representation carries both mixed together unless the tokenization of G5 gave it time to read, and a space that mixes them places a unit at a point that is partly where they are and partly what day it is.

*The dimension a use needs.* A use that needs only "alike or not" needs few dimensions and a validated metric; a use that needs "alike in what respect" needs named dimensions, which only the classical space has and only a probed learned space earns. So the dimension question has a second answer per use, and a space projected to fewer dimensions for display has traded a validity the use may have needed for a picture, which section 8 prices.

**6 · Directions, and what a direction may be called**

*A direction is a hypothesis.* Any vector in the space is a direction, and a direction along which units vary is a candidate axis of difference. In the classical space directions are named by construction, since the dimensions are the measurement models. In the learner's space a direction has no name, and giving it one — this direction is sociability, that one is caution — is a claim, made with the same authority as any other label and usually with less.

*How a direction earns a name.* Per G5's tools. A probe with a known answer: if a direction recovers a placement the classical floor already makes, the direction encodes that dimension and may be called by its name, with the residual between them stated. A sparse feature under the admission test: a direction found by decomposition is a proposal until it survives the five clauses. An intervention: a direction is credited with a role only if moving a unit along it moves the model's predictions in the way the name implies, and a direction that predicts without responding to intervention is a correlate wearing a name.

*The declared-difference paper's rule, generalized.* That paper publishes the items that separate a declared group as directions and never as weights, and forks whether a unit may be projected onto them. The rule generalizes to every direction: a direction is publishable as what it is — a hypothesis with its probe, its test and its intervention — and a projection of a unit onto it is a per-unit quantity that is a screening instrument the moment it is ranked, which is section 8's fork and not this section's to decide.

**7 · The space under a new source, and relations in the space**

*A new source moves the space.* Adding a source to the portfolio refits the classical model and retrains the learner, and in both the space that results is not the space that was: dimensions rotate, a new one appears, distances change. A unit placed before and after is placed in two spaces, and the learned-representation paper states the rule this paper generalizes — comparability across versions is predictive equivalence, never coordinate survival. Two placements of a unit are the same placement if they make the same predictions about the unit on held-out targets, under a stated tolerance, and a version row records which pairs of spaces were aligned and how. A use that carried a unit's coordinates across a refit as if they were the unit's has confused the map with the territory it was redrawn from.

*What a new source does to distance validity.* It can raise it, where the new source carries a coupling the old space lacked; it can lower it, where the new source's tokens dominate the metric with a grain finer than the others and the learner reweights toward it. So the value of a source in G4's ledger has a geometric entry: the change in distance validity per target that adding it produced, which is a different number from its code-length saving and is stated beside it.

*Relations are not points.* A pair is not a point in the space and the distance between its members is one number about it, not the only one. Two units may be close and pair badly, or far and fit; similarity and complementarity are different geometric facts, and the second is a function on pairs that the space of units does not contain and G1's relational grain does. The tie graph, embedded in the space, gives homophily a geometry — ties form between the near — and the interests paper's rule holds here: proximity before a tie and convergence after it are separated only by an offer the units did not choose, and a space that shows ties among the near has not said which way the arrow runs.

**8 · What the space is for, and what it must not do**

*"Units like this one" is a claim, and the licence is section 3's number.* The commonest use of a space of persons is to show a unit the units near them. That is a product only where the distance is validated for the target the showing implies — near in what respect, predictive of what — and a display of neighbours in a space with no validity number is a display of the fitter's metric with people's names on it.

*Pairing on proximity is an intervention.* A system that pairs units because they are near has acted on the space, and the pairing changes the phenomena the space describes — the ties that form, the answers given afterward, the convergence the interests paper names. That is the loop of G5 and the performativity of the learned-representation paper in geometric form, and the space after the pairings is a space the pairings shaped. Randomized withholding of proximity-driven pairing is what identifies its effect, and a space that was not held out from its own pairings has measured its own influence.

*Ranking along a direction is screening, and it is the owner's fork.* Any direction, once a unit's projection onto it is computed and units are ordered by it, is an instrument for sorting people, and the declared-difference paper puts exactly that to an owner as three shapes — no projection computed, a projection shown to the unit alone, a projection stored. The same fork applies to every direction in the space, and this paper states it and decides nothing: the space can hold the projection, and whether it does is not a property of the space.

*A coordinate is a release.* A unit's position in the space is a per-unit quantity derived from everything about them, it identifies them to the degree the space is validated, and handing it to anything — a display, a pairing, another computation — is a release under G2's budget with a row in the publication ledger. The learned-representation paper forks whether a per-unit representation may be released at all; a coordinate in a validated space is that representation with a licence attached, and the fork stands.

*Projection for display is a trade.* A space of many dimensions shown in two has thrown away validity the use may have needed, and the picture's distances are not the space's. A display states its projection and the validity that survived it, or it is a picture of a picture.

**9 · What would have to hold**

*"One geometry" is read as one placement of every unit and never as one space*, and every claim names which of the three spaces it is made in.

*Every use of a distance carries a validity number for its target*, measured on held-out sources on the eligible-drawn stream, and a space carries a table of validities rather than a verdict.

*A learned space keeps its place only where its distance validity beats the classical space's per target*, since loss and distance validity are different quantities.

*The population is a continuum by default*, and a region is claimed only under a multimodality test on the eligible-drawn stream with the propensity weighted and the five clauses passed; a region is a density and never a boundary.

*Intrinsic dimension is estimated per grain*, the between-unit and within-unit geometries are two spaces, and trait and state are two subspaces of the second, separable only where the tokenization let the learner read time.

*A direction is named only by a probe with a known answer, a passed admission test, and an intervention*, and is otherwise published as a hypothesis.

*Placements across versions are compared by predictive equivalence under a stated tolerance*, recorded in the version row, and never by coordinate survival.

*A new source's geometric value — its change in distance validity per target — is stated in the value ledger beside its code-length saving.*

*Similarity and complementarity are two facts about a pair*, and a tie among the near is not read as an arrow.

*Neighbours are shown only under a validity number for the implied target; proximity-driven pairing is randomized and withheld for identification; a ranked projection is the owner's fork; a coordinate is a release with a row; a display states its projection and what survived it.*

**10 · The potential, at full scale**

At full scale — a population placed in a validated space, with the validity table maintained per target and per version — the space of persons becomes an object about a population rather than about a fitter. What is knowable through it that nothing else offers: whether the population is a continuum or has regions, decided by a test rather than by a picture, and if regions, what they are, decided by probes rather than by the names expected in advance; how many independent ways units differ, and how many independent ways a unit moves, as two numbers that are properties of the population; which directions of difference are real, with a name each has earned, and which are correlates of the design; how a new source reshapes what "alike" means, priced beside what it saves; and a licence, per use, for the commonest thing anyone does with such a space, which is to say who is like whom. The conditions are the price, and they are the ones the recipe's home setting never needed because it never showed a person their neighbours: a distance validated against what the space did not use, a region that survives a test, a name that survives an intervention, and a coordinate that is a release.

**11 · What remains open**

Whether distance validity on held-out sources transfers to validity on held-out events, or whether a space validated for answers is a different space from one validated for responses. Whether any learned space beats the classical space's distance validity on any target once the policy is weighted out, or whether the learner's advantage is all in prediction and none in geometry. Whether multimodality tests have power at the population sizes where regions would matter, or whether every population is a continuum at the resolution a test can reach. Whether intrinsic dimension per grain is stable across versions, or moves with every refit. Whether predictive equivalence can be made tight enough that a unit's placement is carried across years of refits without the tolerance swallowing the movement the placement was meant to show. And whether a direction that passes a probe, a test and an intervention inside the model has thereby earned its name in the population — which is G5's last question, that the step from a psychometrics of a representation to a psychometrics of persons is not taken there, and is not taken here either.
