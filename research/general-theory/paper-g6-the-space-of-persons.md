# The space of persons

*General paper G6: what the geometry every paper calls "one" actually is — two constructible spaces and the criterion both estimate — what a distance in it licenses and the statistic that earns the licence, whether a population is regions or a continuum, how many dimensions it needs on each level, what a direction may be called, how a placement survives a refit and a new source, why a pair is not a distance, where a pairwise read may run, and what the space is for and the forks its uses put to an owner.*

G1 supplies the co-observation graph, the two evaluation streams, the replication that identifies within-unit noise, the relational grain and the sizing of a difference; G2 the custody rule, the publication ledger, the version row and the type a published number carries; G3 the classical floor with its rotation convention, amortized placement with its errors, level separation, the causal readings and the five-clause admission test; G4 the value ledger and the state it keeps beside the ledger; G5 the learned representation, the probes and the interventions that read it, and the custody of a sequence.

**Perfect-form test.** If the app did not exist, this would still be worth saying because every design that measures a population on many sources ends by placing each unit as a point in a space, and a point in a space is a promise about distances — that units near one another are alike in a way that matters — which is a claim about a number the design has to compute against something the space did not use, and which the space does not carry by being a space; and because whether that space is a continuum or a set of regions, how many dimensions it needs, what a direction in it may honestly be called, and what a distance in it may honestly be used for, are questions a validated space can answer and an unvalidated one can only illustrate.

**Setting.** A population of units measured by K sources under an attention budget of B units per unit per period, as in G1, with the coupling structure across units, within units over periods, and on relations as the object of discovery. Add that the mechanisms of G3 and the learner of G5 each place every unit in a space: the classical model in a latent space whose coordinates are named, whose rotation convention is recorded with its version and whose covariance is estimated; the learner in a representation whose coordinates mean nothing that can be stated and on which a metric is a further decision. Add that a placement is a posterior summary and not a point, with an error that grows as a unit's own observations shrink. Add that the space is used — to say which units are alike, to pair them, to rank them along a direction, to assign them to a region, to place a newcomer, to draw them — and that each use is a claim. **Abstract.** The series says, wherever it places a unit, that the unit is a point in one geometry. This paper is about that geometry, and its first claim is that "one" names two constructible spaces and a criterion neither of them is. The classical model's latent space has named coordinates under a recorded rotation convention and a covariance that gives a distance a meaning in the model's terms; the learner's representation has coordinates that mean nothing and no metric at all until one is declared, since a predictive objective induces a function and not a distance; and the outcome criterion — two units are alike if their predictive distributions on what comes next are alike, under one stated divergence — is what every use wants, and both spaces are estimates of it whose error is measurable. That error is distance validity, and the paper states it as a statistic rather than a property: over pairs of units both observed on a held-out target on the eligible-drawn stream, the probability that the nearer of two pairs is the one that disagrees less, in the target's own scale — invariant to any monotone change of the metric, one half at no information, with a ceiling set by the within-unit noise the replication identifies, an interval from resampling units and not pairs, a pair count that thins quadratically with the number of sources, a shrinkage correction because placements are posteriors, and the type a published number carries. From it the paper asks five questions in turn. Whether the population is regions or a continuum is a question about the space and the metric together, a continuum is the default because missingness manufactures clusters, and a region is a mixture component that improves held-out prediction and survives the admission test — not a rejection of unimodality, which is a different property — and is a density that licenses membership only through a fork. How many dimensions the population needs is an intrinsic dimension per level, under the stated class, cut, capacity and scale, with the between-unit and within-unit levels as two variance decompositions on one measurement scale, and trait and state as bands whose separation from response error the short-lag replicate has to buy. What a direction may be called is earned by a probe read under the floor's rotation convention, a passed admission test and a representation-level intervention, and what is named is a decodable subspace, not a vector. How a placement survives a refit is predictive substitutability against a reference version under a stated tolerance — a licence to reuse a placement for prediction, not an identity, and not a preservation of distances — with coordinate comparison licensed only in the classical space under its recorded convention, and a source's geometric worth kept as a state per target rather than as a second ledger column. And why a pair is not a distance is that a relation carries a latent of its own, identified apart from its members by degree, apart from error by replication, and in its reciprocity by both directions. The paper then places every pairwise read against the custody rule and the binding point that names it, and ends with the uses: neighbours are shown only under a validity number for the implied target; pairing on proximity is an intervention identified under an exposure mapping and consent; ranking along a direction and assigning to a region are forks with their shapes priced; a coordinate is a release with a row; and a display states its projection and the validity that survived it.

**1 · The problem, stated structurally**

A design that measures a population on many sources produces, at its end, a placement: each unit is a point, and the points sit in a space. The series has placed units from its first paper and never said what the space is. It has been treated as a given, the way a corpus is treated as a given in the recipe of G5, and the same thing happens: the object nobody examines is the one carrying the promise.

The promise is about distances. To say a unit is a point is to say that two units near one another are alike, and that being alike matters — that they will respond alike, move alike, pair well, or share a fate. None of that follows from having coordinates. A space is a set of numbers per unit with a metric on them; the numbers are posterior summaries with an error each, and the metric was chosen by whatever fitted the space, or by nobody. Whether the distances it induces mean anything for a purpose the fitter did not have is an empirical question, and the answer is a number with an interval and a cost.

This paper defines that number, prices its estimate, and asks the questions that become askable once it exists.

**2 · Two spaces and a criterion**

*The latent space of the classical model.* G3's joint model places each unit on named dimensions — a coordinate per latent — under an identification and rotation convention recorded with the model version, with a covariance among the dimensions estimated from the population. A distance here is meaningful under that covariance: two units are close if they differ little along dimensions the population varies on much, and the distance is a statement in the model's own terms. Its coordinates can be named because the model was built from named measurement models, and they are comparable across versions because the convention is recorded. Two cautions follow it. A covariance estimated on the units it places makes an in-sample distance optimistic, so a validity read on it is read on a fit that held those units out. And its limit is its class: whatever structure is not covariance is not in the space.

*The representation of the learner.* G5's model places each unit as a vector whose coordinates mean nothing that can be stated. A predictive objective induces a function from a unit's record to a prediction, and not a distance: any invertible reparametrization of the representation leaves every prediction identical and changes every distance in it, so the choice among a Euclidean, an angular or a learned metric is a decision the objective did not make. The metric is therefore declared as an object of its own, with its own version row, and every distance claim below is about a representation and a metric together, not about the representation. Two units are close under the declared metric because the model found it useful to treat them alike when predicting their next observation — a fact about them in the model's terms, and one the policy and the missingness can supply as readily as the units can, per G5. The space can hold structure the classical class cannot; that is its claim on a place at all.

*The outcome criterion.* Neither of those is what a use wants. What a use wants is that two units near each other will be alike on what comes next — on a held-out source, on a future period, on how they respond to an event. State that as a divergence between the two units' predictive distributions on the target, under one stated divergence, on the eligible-drawn stream; the expected difference in log score between predicting one unit's response from its own distribution and from the other's is the one used here, since it is the scale the loss already carries. This is a criterion and not a third space, because it is defined on futures and is not constructible from the past, and because it is stated on predictive distributions and not on realized draws: a distance between two units' realized responses is dominated by within-unit noise and describes the draws. The two constructible spaces are estimates of the criterion, and section 3 is the measurement of each one's error.

*Why "one geometry" is a misleading phrase.* The series has used it to mean that every unit is placed in the same space, which is true and important — G1's anchor set is what makes it so. It has not meant, and this paper does not let it mean, that the classical space, the learner's space under its metric, and the criterion are one. A distance that means something in the classical space may mean nothing in the learner's, and neither approximates the criterion until shown to.

**3 · Distance validity: the statistic, and what it costs**

*The statistic.* Take a space with a declared metric, a held-out target — a source, a period, a category of event — and the set of pairs of units both observed on that target on the eligible-drawn stream, so that the policy's selection is not what agrees. For each pair, its disagreement on the target is the distance between the two responses in the target's own measurement model: a mismatch indicator for a categorical response, the model's scale difference for an ordinal or continuous one, so that the scale is the source's and travels with the number. Distance validity is the concordance: the probability, over two pairs drawn at random from that set, that the pair nearer in the space is the pair that disagrees less on the target, with ties split. It uses only the order of the distances, so it is invariant to any monotone reparametrization of the metric and cannot be raised by transforming distance alone; it is one half at no information whatever the target's marginal, and the marginal enters only through the disagreement scale, which is stated beside it. Its ceiling is below one: within-unit noise on the target, which G1's replication identifies, bounds the concordance the criterion itself would reach, so a validity is read beside its ceiling, and one near the ceiling is a space that has taken what the target's noise leaves.

*Its interval, and the stream it is valid on.* Pairs are dependent. A set of n units co-observed on the target supplies of the order of n squared over two pairs, and the effective sample size of a statistic over them is of the order of n, since every pair shares a unit with n others; so the interval is by resampling units — the variance of a pair-kernel statistic taken over units, or a jackknife over units — and an interval computed as though pairs were independent is too tight by a factor of order n. The pairs lie on the eligible-drawn stream, whose draws the policy did not choose, so the interval is valid under the sequential selection of everything else, as G1 licenses; a validity read on the policy's stream measures how well the space predicts what the policy served.

*What co-observation costs it.* The statistic exists only on pairs both observed on the target on the eligible-drawn stream, which is G1's constraint applied to this paper's own headline number. With K sources sharing an eligible-drawn share of the budget, a unit's chance of an eligible-drawn observation on a given source in a window falls with K, and the chance that two units both have one falls with its square; so the pair count per target thins roughly quadratically in K, and the units that supply the pairs are those the eligible-drawn design happened to reach on that source, which is the selection G1's first proposition is about. The co-observation graph carries, per target, the count of co-observed pairs on the stream, and a validity is published with that count; a target whose count is below what the next paragraph requires has no validity number, and the space's licence for that target is absent rather than weak.

*The arithmetic of a difference.* To resolve a difference δ between two spaces' concordances on the same target requires, by G1's sizing, units of the order of the kernel's unit-level variance over δ squared — units co-observed on the target, not pairs. Comparing the two spaces on the same pair set cancels the pair set's own contribution, which is what makes the comparison affordable at all. With a unit-level kernel variance of a few hundredths, a difference of one hundredth of concordance takes units in the low thousands co-observed on the target, and a difference of a tenth takes tens; so the eligible-drawn share the pairwise statistic requires is derived from the smallest concordance difference the programme needs to read, with the quadratic thinning of the pair count inside the derivation. That is G1's sizing rule one level up, and it says the geometric reading is more expensive per unit of resolution than the loss reading G5 sizes, because it consumes co-observed pairs and not observations.

*Placements are posteriors, and distance is contracted by shrinkage.* Every placement is a posterior summary: G3's full-information posterior for placement, partially pooled toward the population centre by an amount that grows as the unit's own observations shrink, and under a budget that is the ordinary case. A distance between two point summaries therefore understates the distance between two sparsely observed units and does so unevenly, so that validity is inflated where well-observed units dominate the pair set, the separation a region test looks for is suppressed, and an intrinsic-dimension estimate is collapsed toward the population value the pooling shrinks to. So each of the three numbers this paper defines is computed on a stated posterior, with the shrinkage stated, and either corrected — a distance between two posteriors, or the expected distance under posterior draws, in place of a distance between two summaries — or bounded, with the amortization gap and the family gap G3 measures carried as errors of the number. A validity table without that line is a table about the well-observed.

*The validated object and the deployed one.* Validity by holding out a source is a number about a space fitted without that source, and section 7 says that a space refitted with a source is not the space that was; so K source-ablation numbers are numbers about K spaces, none of them the one in use. Two protocols read the deployed space itself: holding out periods, so that the distance in the space fitted to the past is scored on agreement in the future — the deployed object exactly, for the use that shows neighbours — and holding out units, so that the placement of a newcomer in the deployed space is scored, which is the use that places one. A source-ablation validity transfers to the deployed space only under the assumption that adding the target does not reorder the distances among the co-observed units beyond the tolerance, which is checkable as the concordance between the two spaces' distance orders on those pairs, and it is published as an assumption checked, or as an assumption.

*Validity is per target and does not transfer.* A space in which distance predicts agreement on one target category may not predict agreement on responses to an event, and a space validated for one held-out source may fail for another. So the licence is per use: the target the use cares about is the target the distance is validated against, and a space carries a table of validities and not a verdict.

*Comparing the two spaces, and what the comparison gates.* The comparison is well posed only under one protocol: the same target, the same pair set, a held-out fit for each — the classical covariance fitted on units other than those it places, or its distance is optimistic — the same statistic, and a paired interval. And what it gates is the use of the distance, per target, and never the learner's place. G3's rule keeps the learner by its code length per estimand against the strongest classical floor, and that rule stands: a learner with a lower loss whose distances under its declared metric predict less than the classical space's keeps its place and loses the licence to have its distances used for that target, because loss and distance validity are different quantities and a use needs the second. A learned space that beats the classical space's validity on a target has earned a licence the classical space had, not a place the learner lacked.

*Two units close in every space are the test of the metric, not the reward.* Where the classical and learned spaces agree on who is near whom, the agreement is evidence about the population only if both were validated on the target; where they disagree, the disagreement is a reading — the learner found a similarity the covariance cannot express, or the learner found the policy — and the admission test of G3 is what decides which.

*The number's type.* A validity is a published quantity and carries what G2 says one carries: the level it is about, which is the between-unit level; the population and basis, which is the set of co-observed pairs on the eligible-drawn stream with its count; the uncertainty, which is the unit-resampled interval with whether it is likelihood-based or a coverage claim and that it is valid under sequential selection; the identification basis, which is co-observation; the invariance status of the target; the design, which is the stream; the model version, the metric version and the fold version; and the publication ledger row that authorised it. The table of validities per target is a table of such objects.

**4 · Regions or a continuum**

*The question, and why it is about the metric too.* Whether a population falls into groups or spreads along a continuum is a property of the space and of its metric together: a continuum under one metric can look clustered under another, and a learner's declared metric was validated for prediction of a target, not for whether it makes clusters. So the question is put to a space under a metric whose validity for the target the region claim is about has been read, and the answer is qualified by both; a region found in a metric validated for nothing is a statement about the metric.

*A region is not a mode.* A continuum and a unimodal density are different properties: a single continuous dimension can carry a bimodal density, and well-separated regions can project to a unimodal one. So a multimodality test answers a question this paper is not asking, and a region is defined by what a test can address: a region is a mixture component whose addition improves held-out prediction of a target category the fit did not use, on the eligible-drawn stream with the propensity weighted, and which survives G3's five clauses as a proposed structure. The test is the held-out comparison of a mixture with one more component against the mixture without it, in the validated metric, the null being that the components already present suffice; and the sample it needs is stated with the separation it resolves, in units of within-region spread, with the effective size counting units. Two components a full spread apart are resolved on the order of hundreds of units co-observed on the target; a third of a spread apart, on the order of thousands or more; and below what the test can reach the population is a continuum by this paper's default whether or not it is one, which the paper says rather than hides.

*The honest default is a continuum.* A learner manufactures clusters from the missingness pattern — units served the same items look alike for that reason — and from the anchor set's coarseness, and from the policy's cohort-wise serving, as readily as from the units. A clustering that is not put to the test above is a description of the design. A region that survives is a finding about the population under the stated metric and target; one that does not is a finding about the serving.

*What a region licenses.* A region is a density, not a boundary: a unit at its edge has a posterior membership, not a membership. Whether a membership may be computed at all is a fork with three shapes, and the owner rules. No membership is computed, which exposes nothing and forgoes every use that needs one. A posterior membership probability is shown to the unit alone, which is a number about the unit derived from everything about them, released to one reader under a row of its own, and stays a probability. A hard assignment is stored, which is a label that outlives the posterior that produced it, is the cheapest shape to misuse as a screen, and is the only shape a downstream use that needs a category can consume. Each shape's exposure and cost are stated with it, and section 9 puts the same fork to ranking.

*What a region is called.* A region licenses a name only through section 6's probes. A name chosen before the test is a hypothesis about the region's content, and the test of that hypothesis is a probe with a known answer, not the name; a region named for the units that were expected to be in it has had its category imposed on the space, which G3's admission test — proposer and tester apart — forbids.

**5 · Dimensions, on two levels of one scale**

*Intrinsic dimension, scoped.* How many dimensions the population needs is not the number of sources and not the number the fitter chose; it is the dimension of the manifold the placements occupy, estimated from the space itself. It is a finding about the population as the stated model class, cut and capacity see it, at a stated scale: measurement error makes the observed cloud full-dimensional at fine enough scale, so the estimate carries a scale parameter above the noise and a noise model, with the noise identified by G1's replication, and it drifts with sample size, so it is reported with the number of units it was read at. Section 7 says every refit can move it, and section 12 asks whether it settles; until it does, it is a property of a version.

*Two levels on one scale.* It is estimated per level, because how units differ from one another and how one unit moves across periods are two variance decompositions of one measurement scale. G3's level separation does not make them two spaces: it estimates the within-unit centre jointly with the dynamics, computes a per-dimension discrepancy between the levels on the common scale the measurement model fixes and net of each level's own error, and carries a cross-level term — whether a unit's position on the between-unit level predicts its own within-unit coupling strength. So the between-unit and within-unit geometries share a scale, each has an intrinsic dimension of its own, G3's discrepancy is defined between them, and the cross-level term is the one thing a distance on one level is licensed to say about the other. The relational grain is a third object and section 7's.

*Trait and state as bands, not subspaces.* Within a unit, the slow and the fast variation are two bands, and the split between them is a choice of centre in G3's sense — a fixed level, a slow trend, a seasonal profile — which selects which band the within-unit coupling is about, so the split is declared and not discovered. At a declared band, the fast band's dimension is how many linearly independent directions a unit moves in from period to period, its share of the within-unit variance is the state share, and both are separable from response error only under the short-lag replicate G1 requires — a repeat inside a window shorter than the state's timescale — which the design has to buy, or the state share is an upper bound. A learner's representation carries both bands mixed unless the cut of G5 gave it time to read, and a space that mixes them places a unit at a point that is partly where they are and partly what period it is.

*The dimension a use needs.* A use that needs only "alike or not" needs few dimensions and a validated metric; a use that needs "alike in what respect" needs named dimensions, which only the classical space has and only a probed learned space earns. So the dimension question has a second answer per use. And a space projected to fewer dimensions for display has traded validity for a picture, priced as the surviving validity: section 3's statistic recomputed on the projected distances, published beside the full space's number, so that the trade is two numbers and not a caption.

**6 · Directions, and what a direction may be called**

*A direction is a hypothesis.* Any vector in the space is a direction, and a direction along which units vary is a candidate axis of difference. In the classical space directions are named by construction, since the dimensions are the measurement models, under the recorded convention. In the learner's space a direction has no name, and giving it one — this direction is the one along which units who respond thus lie — is a claim made with the same authority as any other label and usually with less.

*How a direction earns a name.* Per G5's tools, with their conditions. A probe with a known answer: where a linear probe recovers a placement the classical floor already makes, the representation encodes something within the span of the floor's rotation convention, recorded with its version — not the quantity as the floor means it — with the residual between them computed net of the floor's own posterior error. And what the probe finds is a read-out direction: the set of directions with the same projection onto the decodable subspace is an equivalence class, none of whose members is the direction the model varies the latent along, so the named object is a decodable subspace and not a vector. A sparse feature under the admission test: a direction found by decomposition is a proposal until it survives the five clauses. An intervention: a direction is credited with a role only if moving a unit along it moves the model's predictions in the way the name implies, scoped to the model as G5 scopes it, and a direction that predicts without responding to intervention is a correlate wearing a name.

*Publication.* A direction is publishable as what it is — a hypothesis with its probe, its test and its intervention, and the subspace it names. A projection of a unit onto it is a different object: a per-unit quantity that becomes a screening instrument the moment units are ordered by it, which is section 9's fork and not this section's to decide.

**7 · Refits, a new source, and relations**

*A refit moves the space.* Adding a source to the portfolio refits the classical model and retrains the learner, and in both the space that results is not the space that was: dimensions rotate, a new one appears, distances change. In the classical space the recorded convention makes coordinates comparable across versions, and that is the one place coordinate comparison is licensed. In the learner's space nothing survives a refit by construction, and comparability is a licence stated as predictive substitutability: a placement in the new version may stand in for the placement in the old, for a prediction on a target, where the two predictive distributions differ by less than a tolerance in the criterion's divergence. Three things about that licence. It is a licence to reuse a placement for prediction and not an identity of placements: G3 says two models can agree on every prediction and disagree about why, and a placement's region, its named direction and its distances are the why. It does not preserve distance: every invertible reparametrization satisfies it and changes every distance, so a distance comparison across versions needs a further object — an alignment between the two metrics, with its own validity number read in the new space. And it is not transitive: a chain of pairwise-substitutable refits drifts without bound, so substitutability is read against a reference version fixed by policy rather than against the predecessor, every version compared to the reference, and the reference retired through a linking period as G1 retires an anchor set. The tolerance is a policy parameter in the criterion's units — a stated fraction of the target's noise floor per observation — recorded in the version row with the pairs of versions it was read on.

*What a new source does to validity, and where its worth is kept.* A new source can raise a target's validity, where it carries a coupling the old space lacked, and lower it, where its tokens dominate the declared metric at a resolution finer than the others' and the learner reweights toward it. That change is a fact about the source, and it is not a column in G4's value ledger: G1 says that summing changes in different posteriors needs an exchange rate no theorem supplies, G4 keeps identification as a state beside the ledger for the same reason, and G5 says the geometric and the code-length readings are not commensurable. So a source's geometric worth is kept as a state per target beside the ledger, in G4's pattern, read under G4's bundle-and-horizon rule: a source whose whole geometric worth is to enable a later co-observation registers zero alone and is priced as the bundle it belongs to at the horizon, which is G1's exact case and the ledger's exact blindness.

*Relations are not points, and a pair is not a distance.* A pair is not a point in the space, and the distance between its members is one number about it. G1's relational grain says why it is not the only one: a relation carries a quantity specific to the pair, identified apart from the members' own only where each member is in several relations, apart from error only where the same pair is observed more than once, and in its reciprocity only where both directions are observed — G7's table. So a pair's outcome is a function of the two units' coordinates and a relation-specific latent, and never of distance alone — distance is one symmetric function of the coordinates that discards direction and the latent both. Similarity and complementarity are therefore two facts: the first is in the space, the second needs the replication G1 prices. The relation graph embedded in the space gives homophily a geometry — ties form among the near — and says nothing about the arrow: proximity before a tie and convergence after it are separated only under a randomized offer, per G3's causal readings, and a space that shows ties among the near has not said which way it runs.

**8 · Where a pairwise read runs**

Every number this paper defines is a read across pairs of units — validity, the region test, the intrinsic dimension, and the neighbours a display shows — and G3 names the pairwise primitive across units under custody as one of the two points where computation binds, with a cost at population scale it does not know. G2 requires a join to be computed by the custodians of the columns it touches, and G5 assembles a sequence only where one custodian holds every column. The columns here are two: the placements, held by whoever ran the fit, and the held-out target, held by its source's custodian. Where one custodian holds both, every read runs there. Where they differ, validity is computed by either from what the other emits — the order of distances over the co-observed pairs, or the order of disagreements — and each is a pairwise object about units, a release with a row and a budget charge, that identifies the units' proximity and not their coordinates. The region test and the intrinsic dimension need the placements only and run where they are held. A neighbour display for one unit is a one-against-all read, linear in the population per query, and each query is a release. And the cost of the all-pairs reads is not what it seems: each is a statistic over pairs that is consistently estimated on a random sample of them, so the cost is linear in the pairs sampled, and since the effective sample size is the units in any case, sampling pairs loses less than it appears to. What binds is the neighbour read at population scale, which is G3's open cost, and this paper leaves it open.

**9 · What the space is for, and the forks its uses put to an owner**

*"Units like this one" is a claim, and the licence is section 3's number.* Showing a unit the units near them implies a target — near in what respect, predictive of what — and is a product only under a validity number for that target, read by the period-holdout protocol on the deployed space. A display of neighbours in a space with no such number is a display of the declared metric with the units' names on it. The recipe's home setting shows neighbours without a validity number; this setting cannot, because a shown neighbour is a claim about a unit.

*Pairing on proximity is an intervention.* A system that pairs units because they are near has acted on the space, and the pairing changes the phenomena the space describes — the ties that form, the responses given afterward, the convergence a tie produces. That is the loop of G5 in geometric form, and the space after the pairings is a space the pairings shaped. Its effect is identified by randomized withholding of proximity-driven pairing under the conditions G3's causal reading sets: an exposure mapping over the relation graph, since a pairing reaches both members and, through their ties, their neighbourhoods; assignment on clusters where the graph is dense; and consent, so that the estimand is the consenting population's and is published as such. A space that was not held out from its own pairings has measured its own influence.

*Ranking along a direction is screening, and it is a fork.* Any direction, once a unit's projection onto it is computed and units are ordered by it, is an instrument for sorting them, and whether that instrument may exist is not a property of the space; it is put to the owner as three shapes with their exposures. No projection is computed, which exposes nothing and forgoes the use. A projection is shown to the unit alone, which releases one number about one unit to one reader under a row. A projection is stored, which is the screening instrument itself, available to any use that can read the store, and the shape every downstream ranking needs. Section 4's membership fork is the same fork with a category in place of a direction, and the two are decided together or the space has one door and a window.

*A coordinate is a release.* A unit's position in the space is a per-unit quantity derived from everything about them; it identifies them to the degree the space is validated, and handing it to anything — a display, a pairing, another computation — is a release under G2's budget with a row in the publication ledger. Whether a per-unit coordinate may leave its custodian at all is a fork of its own, with the shapes: never, so that every use runs where the placements are held; to the unit alone; to a named reader for a named use with a row each; or to a display, which is the widest. A coordinate in a validated space is a per-unit representation with a licence attached, and the licence does not decide the fork.

*Projection for display is a trade with two numbers.* A space of many dimensions shown in two has thrown away validity the use may have needed, and the picture's distances are not the space's. A display states its projection and the surviving validity of section 5 beside the full space's, or it is a picture of a picture.

**11 · The potential, at full scale**

At full scale — a population placed in a validated space, with the table of validities maintained per target and per version — the space becomes an object about a population rather than about a fitter. What is knowable through it that nothing else offers: whether the population is a continuum or has regions, decided by a held-out test rather than by a picture, and if regions, what they are, decided by probes rather than by the names expected in advance; how many independent ways units differ and how many a unit moves, as two numbers under the stated class and scale, with what a position on one level says about the other; which directions of difference are real, with a subspace each has earned, and which are correlates of the design; how a new source reshapes what "alike" means, kept beside what it saves; and a licence, per use, for saying who is like whom. The conditions are the price, and they are the ones the recipe's home setting never met because it shows neighbours without a validity number: a distance validated against what the space did not use, on pairs the design had to buy; a region that survives a test; a name that survives an intervention; and a coordinate that is a release.

**12 · What remains open**

Whether distance validity on a held-out source transfers to validity on a held-out event, or whether a space validated for responses is a different space from one validated for what follows an event. Whether any learned representation under any declared metric beats the classical space's validity on any target once the policy is weighted out, or whether the learner's advantage is all in prediction and none in geometry. Whether the region test has power at the population sizes and co-observed pair counts where regions would matter, or whether every population is a continuum at the resolution the pairs can reach. Whether intrinsic dimension per level settles across versions at a stated scale, or moves with every refit. Whether substitutability against a reference version can be held tight enough across years of refits that the reference's own retirement does not swallow the movement the placement was meant to show. What the neighbour read costs at population scale under custody, which G3 left open and this paper leaves open. And whether a direction that passes a probe, a test and an intervention inside the model has thereby earned its name in the population — which is G5's last question, that the step from a psychometrics of a representation to a psychometrics of units is not taken there, and is not taken here either.
