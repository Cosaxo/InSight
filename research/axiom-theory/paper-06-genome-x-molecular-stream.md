# A stream from the body, beside the genome

*Paper 6 of the axiom series: a continuous molecular stream from a passive wearable, crossed with the genome and with every repeated measure, and what would have to be true for it to be worth what it looks worth.*

**Status: theory research.** Nothing here describes the app or proposes a change to it; `research/README.md` says what a paper is and how one reaches the product. Paper 5 is the body axis this paper extends with a measured channel; papers 1 and 3 supply the genetics it crosses; paper 4 supplies the declared-difference readings it feeds; paper 0a the design it runs on.

**Perfect-form test.** If the app did not exist, this would still be worth saying because a wearable that draws fluid through a passive gel and reads several molecules continuously would be the cheapest molecular occasion stream a population could ever supply, and what such a stream is worth turns on two things nobody states when the idea is first drawn: which of its channels is a scaled reading of the blood and which is a reading of the skin's own metabolism, and what stands beside it — a genome, a diary, a fixed challenge, and an outcome years later.

**Abstract.** A passive wearable that draws fluid from the skin without exercise and reads a small molecular panel continuously would turn the body axis's perceived channel of paper 5 into a measured one, at the cadence of hours rather than days, on a population rather than a ward. This paper's first move is to refuse the single sentence such concepts are usually built on. There is no one transfer model for such a stream: one analyte may be a small person-specific fraction of the blood quantity, another is manufactured by the gland the fluid comes from and is a reading of that gland and of secretion rate rather than of the blood at all, and others are too slow or too faint to carry an hourly signal. Which is which is an empirical question with a cheap answer, and it decides everything downstream. The second move is to state the horizon. A person-specific ratio that holds for weeks supports every reading taken inside weeks on one scale, and every comparison across quarters or years crosses an unobserved re-scaling, so a phenotype that spans epochs must be a functional invariant to that scale — a time to return to the window's own baseline, not a peak or an area in absolute units — and the anchor that estimates the scale must be sampled at twice the drift's own rate, which a yearly clinic panel is not. From those two facts the program follows. Crossed with a genome sequenced inside the program rather than imported, the stream makes knowable the genetics of dynamic phenotypes nobody has measured, and creates one confound the crossing invents: the transfer ratio is a property of skin and glands and is itself heritable, so a scale-dependent curve parameter carries a heritable multiplier and a study of it finds loci for sweating. Crossed with every repeated measure, the stream raises the body covariate's resolution to the grain the body moves on, without identifying anything paper 5 left unidentified. Three uses are ranked by what the perfect form makes knowable, with the hardware's limits stated as conditions beneath each rather than as the ranking itself: the genetics of response shapes, the intervention readout in weeks, and detection before the next scheduled contact — the last rescoped hard, because the injury markers a trial watches are proteins the fluid never carries, so what the stream sees is late decompensation and the claim to see early toxicity is withdrawn. Vector dosing is rescoped to personal reference ranges for the same reason. Two measurements would decide the program's shape before any of the rest is built, and four decisions that cost no engineering move its worth by more than any sensor.

**1 · The problem, stated structurally**

Almost everything measured about a body is measured at a visit. A blood draw once a year, a panel at a clinic, a sample when something is already wrong. Between visits the body does everything that matters — responds to meals, sleep, illness, stress and treatment — and none of it is recorded.

The quantity that most deserves continuous measurement is not a level but a return. How fast a person comes back to their own baseline after a load is a curve, and a visit is a point; a series of points years apart cannot recover the curve between them at any sample size, because the curve's own duration is hours. This is the shape of the ability that is thought to decline with age, and it is exactly the shape a visit cannot hold.

One quantity is already read continuously, through an inserted sensor, on people who have a reason to accept the insertion. What has not existed is a passive stream of several quantities on people who did nothing to supply it, held beside their genome, their diary, a fixed challenge, and what happened to them afterwards. Paper 5 built the body axis on daily self-report and, where consented, a worn device folded to one number a day, and left the measured channel there because no such stream existed to theorize. This paper is that channel at its perfect form: hourly, multi-analyte, passive, and crossed with everything else.

**2 · The two axes alone, and the transfer model that is not one model**

*The device, and the one compartment it can have.* The stream is a gel worn on intact skin that draws micro-volumes of fluid by an osmotic gradient, without power and without breaking the skin, and reads a small panel by electrochemistry. Those three properties fix the compartment. What crosses intact skin unaided is eccrine sweat. Reaching interstitial fluid takes a current, a microneedle array or a suction blister — a power source or a breach, each of which the device is defined as not having. So the honest specification is a sweat device, and every reading in this paper is a sweat reading, with sweat's lag and sweat's confounds. A future instrument that adds an interstitial channel by a current or an array is a second compartment with its own lag, its own dilution behaviour and its own bias term, not the same stream at higher fidelity, and a program that writes the two as one fluid has hidden a measurement model inside a slash.

*The transfer model is per analyte, and this is the paper's first correction.* The concept as usually drawn asserts one relation for the whole panel: each analyte appears in the fluid at a small, person-specific fraction of its blood concentration, stable within a person, so the stream tracks trends and not values. That is a defensible model for some analytes and false for at least one, and the difference is not a detail.

There are three kinds, and each reading must say which it is using.

*Scaled from the blood.* An analyte that reaches the fluid by diffusion from plasma and is not made or consumed at the sampling site appears at some fraction of the blood level, the fraction differing between people and holding within a person over some horizon. Glucose is the candidate case. For these, the stream reads within-person trends and states no absolute value, and the whole edifice below is available.

*Made in the gland.* The eccrine gland is metabolically active and produces lactate glycolytically at the site. Sweat lactate therefore runs above blood lactate rather than below it, and its concentration moves inversely with secretion rate, so what the sensor sees is gland metabolism divided by flow. No normalizer recovers a blood quantity that was never sampled: here the ruler and the signal are the same variable, and dividing by the ruler does not reveal a blood signal, it reveals a different function of the gland. Lactate may still be a phenotype — gland metabolic capacity is a real property of a person, and it may even be an interesting one — but it is not a metabolic readout of the body, and every reading that treated it as one has to be withdrawn or re-derived.

*Too slow, or too faint.* An analyte whose body pool turns over on a scale of days carries nothing about an hourly response no matter how well it transfers; urate is the case. An analyte whose fed-state blood level sits near the bottom of the physiological range has a fraction of that level at or under the detector's limit, and a meal moves it downward rather than upward, so a post-meal parameter built on it is a decrement from a floor; ketones are the case. Neither is useless — a multi-day urate trend and a fasting ketone excursion are both real readings — but neither belongs in a curve measured over a morning.

*And the sampling rate is a quantity, not an adjective.* "Hourly" is asserted in every drawing of this concept and derived in none. Resting passive extraction delivers micro-volumes per hour; the shortest window the stream can resolve is the volume the detector needs divided by that flux, and it is a number the hardware owes before any curve is claimed. A meal glucose response peaks well inside an hour, so its shape needs a window shorter than its peak, and whether the flux supplies one is the first thing a program should find out. Where it does not, the stream reads daily and weekly structure and the response curve is not available at all.

*The genome, and one change from the papers above.* Papers 1 and 3 take the genome as a consumer file the person already holds. This paper's genome is sequenced inside the program, because the designs below require it: carriers cannot be recruited deliberately from files people happened to buy, and loss-of-function calls are exactly what array-plus-imputation cannot make. Three things change with it. Coverage reaches variants a consumer file cannot call. The batch structure is no longer chip, version and vendor but capture chemistry, coverage depth and joint-calling batch, and that is what enters every model. And selection is the program's own recruitment rather than who chose to buy a test, which makes it a designed quantity for once. Every other condition of papers 1 and 3 carries unchanged.

*Where each stops.* The stream alone is a diary of the skin's chemistry with nothing to explain: it can say a person is unlike their own usual today and cannot say why, what it means, or whether it matters. Its ceiling is meaning and consequence. The genome alone is a fixed fact that predicts nothing without a phenotype measured well on the same people, and inherits the mixture of whatever phenotype trained its weights; its ceiling is a phenotype worth having. Papers 1 and 3 argue that the phenotypes worth having are the ones no cohort could afford to collect, and a passive stream is the cheapest deep phenotype there is — subject to everything above about which of its channels are readings of the body at all.

**3 · The crossing, and the two facts that rank everything**

Paper 0 states the general argument and paper 5 the level: nothing here is a claim about how people differ unless it says so, and most of it is about how a person moves around their own centre.

*The first fact is the transfer model, stated per analyte and per reading.* Every use that needs an absolute number is out for all analytes. Every use that needs "this person is unlike their own normal" is in for the analytes that scale from the blood, and is a statement about the skin for the ones that do not.

*The second fact is the horizon, and it is the one the concept always omits.* Suppose the person-specific ratio holds for weeks. Then two challenges thirteen weeks apart are separated by at least one unobserved re-scaling, and a decade of quarterly challenges is a chain of concatenated unknown scale factors. A reference sampled once a year against a drift whose own time constant is weeks does not identify the scale; it aliases it, by a factor of tens. Two consequences follow and both are load-bearing. A phenotype compared across epochs must be a functional invariant to a person-and-epoch scale factor: the time to return to that same window's own baseline, an excursion expressed as a ratio to that same window's baseline, the shape of a decay rather than its height. A peak, an area and a slope in absolute units are scale-dependent, and they are the parameters such concepts always name first. And the calibration anchor must run at twice the drift's rate or faster, which makes it a frequent event with a cost, not an annual formality.

*The stream is a covariate, not a second opinion.* Paper 5 routes a perceived channel against a measured one because both are instruments for one construct — the night's sleep — which is what makes their disagreement decompose into perception bias, device bias and true difference. A metabolite trace and a report of energy are not two measurements of one quantity, so that decomposition has no referent here and is not imported. The stream enters as a distinct construct at finer grain. It is not the truth against which the report is scored, and the design does not rank the two.

*What the cadence changes.* At daily grain paper 5 reads a state share and a lag structure in days. At sub-hourly grain the response to a single load becomes a curve, and the curve's scale-free functionals are each a phenotype in their own right. Paper 1's argument that trajectory parameters are phenotypes, made there for a test repeated over years, is made here for a meal repeated over a morning — with the horizon rule above deciding which of those parameters survive being compared across years.

**4 · What becomes knowable**

*4.1 Resilience, as a phenotype defined on a fixed challenge and expressed scale-free.* A recovery from an unknown load is a recovery from nothing, so the phenotype exists only against a fixed challenge repeated identically. The challenge menu is constrained by the instrument in a way the concept's usual list ignores: it must not perturb the sampler. A stair climb moves secretion rate along with the metabolite, so the ruler and the signal move together; a cold exposure suppresses secretion and starves the sampler of volume. What is left is loads that leave sweating alone — a fixed meal, a fixed fast — and those are the challenge.

The schedule is a design problem too. A quarterly challenge is confounded with season, and season drives ambient temperature and humidity, which drive secretion, which is the ruler. So the challenge times are randomized within the quarter and season enters the model as a term, or the cadence is chosen to separate them; a fixed calendar slot each quarter aliases the year onto the instrument.

The phenotype is then the scale-free functional of the response — time to return to that window's own baseline, excursion as a ratio within the window — repeated across years. Its slope across a person's own years is the aging phenotype, and it needs the years; it cannot be bought forward. Sensor generations will change under it over a decade, so generation enters as a term and the design buys an overlap period in which both generations are worn by the same people. Without that overlap the aging slope and the engineering's slope are the same number.

*4.2 The genetics of response shapes, and the confound the crossing itself creates.* Papers 1 and 3 say the phenotypes worth crossing with the genome are the ones nobody phenotyped, and a dynamic functional is the clearest case there is.

The crossing invents its own confound and this paper will not pass it over. The transfer ratio is set by gland density, maximal secretion rate, skin barrier properties and body composition, all of which are substantially heritable. A scale-dependent parameter therefore carries a heritable multiplier, and a genetic study of it will find loci for skin and sweat physiology and publish them as loci for metabolic resilience. Three conditions follow. The ratio is estimated per person from the calibration channel and entered. Its own heritability is estimated and published as a finding about the instrument, not about metabolism. And no scale-dependent parameter enters a genetic model at all, which is the second place the horizon rule pays for itself.

Power is the product of the number of people observed on both axes and the phenotype's reliability, so a novel functional's single-administration reliability is a number the design owes before it states a target, and repeated challenges are how it is raised. This is where the count of section 7 binds.

The strongest form is the natural experiment paper 4 makes available — people born with a gene switched off, measured continuously against matched people who are not — and it has to be priced rather than promised. Heterozygous loss-of-function carriers for any given gene run on the order of one in a thousand to one in ten thousand in an outbred population, and complete absence is rarer by orders. A million sequenced participants therefore yields ones to tens of carriers per gene, and a continuous functional of unstated reliability on ten people estimates nothing. So the design is a gene-set burden design with a stated carrier count and a detectable effect size, or a cohort in which consanguinity raises the rate, and never a single-gene portrait. This reading suspends papers 1 and 3's common-variant condition, and may: that condition was a property of imputed consumer files, not of genomes, and sequencing inside the program is precisely what lifts it. Finally, a lifelong germline absence is not an acute somatic edit — developmental compensation and lifetime exposure are the standard reasons the two do not transfer — so the reading is a lifetime-exposure analogue and is stated as one.

*4.3 The metabolic shadow, and what would actually validate it.* The panel carries no proteins. Cytokines are too large and too dilute to cross into sweat in readable amounts, so nothing here is an immune readout, and section 6 keeps that as a standing condition rather than a caveat.

What remains is a hypothesis: that a systemic inflammatory response leaves a metabolic footprint in the panel's own quantities. That is an unproven mapping, not a fact, and everything downstream that leans on it is conditional on it — including, as section 5 admits, a use this paper once ranked first.

A routine vaccination is the wrong event to test it with, though it is the one such concepts always reach for. Routine adult vaccines are mostly locally reactogenic; the metabolic footprint of the hours afterwards is dominated by behaviour, less intake and more rest and altered sleep; and where fever does occur it raises sweating directly, which moves the ruler rather than the signal. A null result would be uninterpretable — no way to separate a stream that failed to detect activation from an activation that was never there. A validation event has to be large, timed and known: a fixed oral load, a controlled fast. Vaccination stays in the design as a dated observational event, stratified by reported reactogenicity, worth recording because the population receives it anyway and not worth resting a claim on.

*4.4 Detection before the next scheduled contact, rescoped honestly.* The concept's headline safety claim is that a dense personal baseline catches vector toxicity before the next visit, and the example always given is the liver. That example does not survive its own panel. Early vector hepatotoxicity is an asymptomatic rise in transaminases, read with bilirubin and coagulation indices — proteins and functional assays, every one of them something section 6 says the fluid cannot carry. Derangement of glucose, lactate and ketones is the signature of hepatic decompensation, which is to say of a person already clinically ill, and that is the regime in which the visit schedule was never the binding constraint. The same sentence section 5 uses to rescope dosing — none of it appears in the fluid — applies here and is applied.

What survives is narrower and still real: the stream can detect late systemic decompensation earlier than the next scheduled contact would, and it detects nothing earlier than that. The comparator is the trial's actual safety pathway — scheduled laboratory panels, participant-reported symptoms, a line staffed around the clock — and not a fortnightly visit, which is a comparison chosen to be beaten.

The arithmetic is the rest of the use. An hourly multi-analyte anomaly detector over autocorrelated, meal-driven, non-Gaussian traces fires constantly under any nominal rule, because the null is never exactly right. Against a serious-event base rate of a few percent per participant over months, the question is entirely the positive predictive value, and so the design owes three numbers before this is a use rather than a nuisance: a target alert rate per participant-week, the specificity that yields a tolerable predictive value at that trial's own event rate, and the named person who acts on an alert. A system that says "call this participant today" without those three has moved work rather than found anything.

*4.5 What an intervention does, within weeks, and what that costs.* Whether a compound flattens a person's metabolic variability, or shortens the return in 4.1's challenge, is readable within weeks rather than over the years an outcome trial takes. It needs no absolute accuracy and no outcome linkage, and that is a real saving.

It is not free of everything else, and an earlier draft of this paper claimed it was. Within-person variability is dominated by diet, activity and sleep, so a compound's effect is separable in weeks only with the diary, a randomized comparator or a crossover with washout, and a measurement window that sits inside one calibration epoch — otherwise a cartridge change or the behaviour shift that comes with entering a trial is read as the compound. With those three it is the least demanding reading in the paper. Without them it measures enrolment.

*4.6 The occasion covariate at finer grain.* Paper 5 made the body the covariate under every within-person reading in the series. The stream raises that covariate's resolution to the grain the body moves on, so paper 1's test occasion, paper 2's reveal, paper 4's variability signature and the bank's state items each gain a body covariate measured within the hour rather than reported for the day.

What it does not do is identify anything. Paper 5 identifies the state share only where the occasion carries a second indicator of the same dimension or the item is re-served inside the contemporaneity window; a finer covariate is a predictor, not a second measurement of the item's dimension, so the within-window replicate remains the allocation the design must buy and is carried forward here unchanged.

**5 · The return path, and the uses ranked by what they make knowable**

*The body axis.* Its perceived channel gains a distinct measured construct at finer grain to be read beside — not against — and paper 5's cross-lagged asymmetry gains a channel recorded before the morning's mood could colour it.

*The genome.* It gains a class of phenotype no biobank holds, the dynamic functional, on which paper 3's never-phenotyped argument bites hardest, and it gains one new instrument confound to model, which is 4.2's.

*The bank and the trait scales.* Every repeated item gains a body covariate at the grain the body actually moves on, with paper 5's identification requirement intact.

*The declared difference.* Paper 4's variability signature gains a measured within-person variability beside the reported one, and its carrier cohorts gain a continuous phenotype — priced as 4.2 prices it.

*The design layer.* The fixed challenge is a system-controlled event of paper 0's first class, so its timing can be randomized, which is what section 4.1 spends to separate season. The vaccination is a second-class observational event. Both feed paper 0a's event-triggered serving.

*The ranking.* These are ordered by what the perfect-form stream makes knowable, with the hardware's limits stated as conditions beneath each rather than used as the order itself. First, the genetics of response shapes and the resilience phenotype under it, because a dynamic phenotype with a genetics does not exist at population scale in any form, and its conditions are the scale-free functional, the per-person ratio entered as a covariate, the reliability stated per group, and the overlap-times-reliability target of section 7. Second, the intervention readout, whose conditions are the diary, a randomized comparator and one calibration epoch, and which returns an answer in weeks. Third, detection before the next contact, rescoped to late decompensation, whose conditions are the three numbers of 4.4 and whose ceiling is set by an inflammation-to-metabolite mapping this paper lists as unproven.

*And the rescoping.* Dosing of gene-therapy vectors is not on the list. Vector handling is governed by pre-existing antibodies, phagocytic uptake, complement and receptor expression, none of which appears in the fluid, so the stream cannot set a dose. What it can do is define each participant's own normal, so a post-dose deviation is read against them rather than against a population, and if a baseline feature ever predicts tolerability that is a hypothesis tested prospectively, never a dosing rule.

The order a program would build in is not this order. It is set by which conditions are cheapest to establish, and section 8 names the two measurements that decide it.

**6 · What would have to hold**

*The transfer model is per analyte, and every reading names which kind it uses.* Scaled from blood, made in the gland, or too slow or too faint to carry the signal being claimed. A panel-wide sentence about trends is not a transfer model.

*The compartment is named and singular.* Sweat, for a passive device on intact skin. An interstitial channel is a second instrument with its own lag and bias, admitted separately or not at all.

*Trends, never values, and the horizon stated per use.* Hours for a response curve, weeks for an intervention readout, quarters and years only for functionals invariant to a person-and-epoch scale.

*A calibration channel exists, and it is priced in the same attention currency as the diary.* Enzymatic sensors decay over days to weeks, the osmotic agent is consumed and the dilution changes as it depletes, so drift is not separable from physiology without a reference. For most of the panel that reference is a blood draw; for glucose an inserted sensor is a comparator with its own error in another compartment, not a truth. Sampled at twice the drift rate, that is a recurring invasive act, and the honest headline for the whole program is therefore low effort rather than none. A paper that concedes the diary's cost and passes over calibration's has hidden the larger of the two.

*The ruler is modelled, and the list is longer than sweat rate.* Secretion rate itself; ambient temperature and humidity, and season through them; skin site and wear position; occlusion, because a continuously worn gel hyperhydrates the site and reduces output over hours in a shape that mimics a personal drift; hydration status; contamination at the sampling site; and medications that change sweating, which anticholinergics, many antidepressants and some antihistamines suppress while other agents raise. Each of these moves the ruler and not the signal, and the last is precisely what a clinical trial and a decade of life produce.

*The panel is enumerated, and the enumeration is the panel.* Glucose, lactate, ketones and urate, read electrochemically, mostly enzymatically. No proteins, therefore no cytokine and no transaminase. A steroid such as cortisol, read by affinity rather than by an enzyme, is a separate channel with its own drift behaviour and its own validation, and any stress or aging reading states which channel it came from rather than borrowing the panel's.

*The diary exists and is cheap.* Meals, caffeine, alcohol, medication, sleep, illness and cycle day, one tap each, enterable the next day. Without it most deviations are lunch.

*Whether the wearer sees the stream is stated, because it decides what is measured.* A visible continuous trace is a far stronger intervention on the phenotype than a daily tap: seeing one's own glucose changes eating, and eating is the variability 4.5 reads and the response 4.1 compares. So paper 5's reactivity estimand is carried forward in its strong form — a randomized arm differing in visibility — and a program that shows the trace to everyone has chosen a phenotype rather than measured one.

*Wear is a person-level selection, not only a day-level gap.* People remove a wearable when ill, in hospital, charging or swimming, which are the days the stream most wants; wear state is recorded as data, the report is retrospectively enterable, and the propensity is modelled per occasion beside the leaving hazard. Beyond that, precision rises with wear time and wear time is engagement, which correlates with health, age, employment and disposition, so paper 5's rule applies with full force: any group comparison or heritability on a stream-derived phenotype states the reliability attained in each group and is not read where they differ. And the non-ignorability parameter is redefined for this axis: paper 5's "how much worse the unreported days were" has no referent for a chemistry trace with no self-report attached, so the band is stated as an explicit shift in the analyte's distribution on unworn occasions.

*The genome's conditions apply as amended.* Sequenced inside the program; batch is capture chemistry, depth and joint-calling batch; ancestry modelled; identity established from the data; discovery items served blind to the genome; selection anchored from outside. Common-variant claims only, except where 4.2's burden design states its suspension and the reason sequencing licenses it.

*Consent is per use, revocable, and at family scope for the genome*, which describes relatives who never consented. Consent to be monitored in a trial and consent to contribute to discovery are separate decisions, and no declaration or sample is taken from a minor.

*Custody is at the custodian.* The stream and the genome stay on the person's devices; only model-relative summaries leave; quantities defined on pairs of people need their own primitive; and every published statistic, per-variant results above all, spends against a recorded output-privacy budget released per frozen cohort. A pooled store of genomes beside continuous chemistry is the most identifying dataset a person can produce, and one breach ends recruitment permanently.

*The models are named by their interfaces, not by their genre.* One model takes the trace and the fixed columns and returns a per-person predictive distribution over the next window together with the challenge functionals and their posterior precision; a classical model is kept permanently as the floor it must beat out of sample. The evaluation surface has to be defined afresh, because paper 0's uniform stream does not exist here: a passive sensor is not served by a policy, so there is no unchosen serving to hold out. What stands in its place is held-out persons, held-out time blocks, and the randomized challenge timings, which are the only chosen events in the design — and the wear-propensity model stands where paper 0's serving-propensity model stood, as the thing that must enter before any residual structure is believed. A separate reasoning model that reads those outputs, proposes analyses and writes them up is a different system with a different estimand, and it does not ingest an electrochemical current; a diagram that says it does costs the program its credibility in the first room of biologists.

**7 · The requirements the design generates**

*The overlap, stated correctly.* A coupling between the stream and the genome is point-identified only from people observed on both; without them it is an interval fixed by the marginals. Participants with only one of the two do narrow that interval — they sharpen each marginal, the phenotype's reliability estimate and the ancestry reference — and what they cannot do is collapse it to a point. Power for the genetics use is the product of the overlap count and the phenotype's reliability, so the target is stated as that product, with the effect size and the reliability it assumes named beside it. Deeper phenotyping raises the second factor, and a novel scale-free functional with low single-administration reliability lowers it, possibly by an order of magnitude, until repeated challenges bring it back. A headline metric that is quoted without its reliability is half a number.

*Age spread from the start, named for what it buys.* Recruiting the old alongside the young buys a between-person age gradient in the third year, and that gradient carries cohort effects and survivor selection, because the oldest recruited are the healthiest of their generation. It is not the within-person aging slope of 4.1, which requires the years and cannot be bought forward, and the two are reported as different quantities.

*Sequencing before the wearables ship*, because deliberate recruitment of families and carriers is impossible afterwards, and because the burden design of 4.2 needs its carrier counts known in advance.

*Families and carriers recruited deliberately*, at the counts 4.2's arithmetic demands rather than at whatever the population supplies.

*Ancestry spread*, so that any genetic finding travels.

*Two anchors, not one.* A calibration anchor at twice the drift rate, which is the frequent invasive one; and a yearly clinical panel on a stable instrument, which anchors the outcomes and supplies the molecules the fluid cannot carry. Collapsing them into one annual event is the aliasing error of section 3.

*Linked outcomes over decades, consented at enrolment*, because retrofitting outcome consent to an existing cohort is expensive and often impossible.

*A published access process*, because a cohort open to hundreds of groups is used hundreds of ways and a closed one is used by its analysts, and that difference is most of what separates a good dataset from a field-defining one.

*A randomized challenge schedule*, a recorded vaccination register, a target-graduation ladder written before the first hit with its failure rate published, and computation at the custodian from the first day rather than after the first breach.

None is a reason to want less. Each is what a fully populated stream beside a fully populated genome looks like from the inside.

**8 · What would decide it**

Two measurements sit under everything above, and both are cheap relative to the program.

*The transfer model, per analyte, over ninety days.* For each analyte the design depends on, the stream is worn against a co-sampled reference — a venous draw at the sampling times, with an inserted glucose sensor admitted as a comparator rather than a truth, since it is itself a modelled estimate in another compartment. What is estimated is not accuracy. It is the distribution across people of the sweat-to-blood ratio, its stability horizon within a person, and its drift rate, each with an interval; and for lactate specifically, whether any blood-borne component is separable from gland production at all once secretion rate is in the model. The decision rule is written before the data: a within-person correlation between the stream's change and the reference's change, over a stated horizon, above a stated threshold, with drift below a stated rate per month, per analyte. An analyte that fails is not modelled around. It loses its blood-reading role and stays, if at all, as a reading of the skin — which is a finding, not a failure, and is the outcome this paper expects for at least one member of the panel.

*Lead time and false-alarm rate, estimated together.* A detector reported without a denominator is trivially successful, so the estimand is a pair: the lead time before a recorded clinical event, and the alert rate over event-free periods of the same participants. Retrospectively, the only archives of continuous molecular data are inserted single-analyte glucose traces from diabetes trials — another device, another compartment, another population — and that study is worth running with its transferability limit stated rather than hidden. Prospectively it needs the device, worn by the cohort the first measurement is already running, and this paper says so rather than claiming the pair costs no hardware.

Neither measurement needs the learned model or the genome cohort, and between them they decide whether the program leads with discovery or with safety, and whether 4.1 exists at all.

**9 · The potential, priced against what those measurements would return**

A ten on this scale is a dataset after which a field's standard design changes — after which the ordinary way to ask a question in that field is to go to the data rather than to run a new cohort. A five is a dataset that answers questions the field already had. Each score below is conditional, because a score that does not name its condition is an assertion.

*Aging and resilience.* Conditional on at least one analyte returning a ratio stable across a quarter and on a calibration anchor at twice the drift rate: seven, rising with the years, because a dynamic resilience phenotype with a genetics does not currently exist at population scale in any form. If drift wins, two — a decade of concatenated scale factors is not a slope, however many people wear it.

*The genetics of response shapes.* Seven at the overlap-times-reliability target of section 7, with the transfer ratio entered and the functionals scale-free. Below either factor's target it is not a low score but an unidentified one.

*Trials.* Six for how they are run, on the intervention readout, which needs the least of anything. Three for detection, because the panel reads decompensation rather than early injury and the alert arithmetic is unsolved.

*Immune biology.* Two, and entirely conditional on an inflammation-to-metabolite mapping that this paper lists as unproven and 4.3 says how to test.

*Stress and hormones.* Four, on the affinity channel's own conditions, and nothing at all without them.

Overall, the program as this paper specifies it is a five or a six that becomes a seven or an eight the moment the first measurement returns a stable ratio for more than one analyte. The single largest mover is not a sensor and not a model: sequencing before recruitment, recruiting families, asking for outcome consent on day one and publishing under an access process are four decisions that cost no engineering and are worth more than any improvement to the panel.

Inside the system, it is paper 5's measured channel at the grain the body moves on, the covariate under every within-person reading in the series, and the first dynamic phenotype papers 1 and 3 could cross with a genome.

**10 · Open questions the data would have to answer**

Whether any analyte beyond glucose transfers from the blood at all, or whether the rest of the panel reads the skin — which the first measurement answers analyte by analyte, and which reshapes half this paper if the answer is the second. Whether the ratio's stability horizon reaches a quarter for any analyte, which decides whether 4.1 exists as written or only within an epoch. Whether the resting flux supports a window short enough to resolve a meal response's shape, or only its presence. Whether a systemic inflammatory response has a readable metabolic footprint at all, which 4.3's controlled event tests and on which a whole use depends. Whether scale-free curve functionals are reliable enough per administration to reward the overlap, and how many repeats it takes to make them so. How much of the transfer ratio's own variance is heritable, which is a finding about the instrument that decides how much of 4.2 is a finding about metabolism. Whether wear-time selection can be bounded rather than only modelled. And whether a decade of sensor generations can be linked into one series at all, or whether the aging slope is readable only within a generation and the decade is a set of segments.
