# A stream from the body, beside the genome

*Paper 6 of the axiom series: a continuous molecular stream from a passive wearable, crossed with the genome and with every repeated measure, and the program that would earn it.*

**Status: theory research.** Nothing here describes the app or proposes a change to it; `research/README.md` says what a paper is and how one reaches the product. Paper 5 is the body axis this paper extends with a measured channel; papers 1 and 3 supply the genetics it crosses; paper 4 supplies the declared-difference readings it feeds; paper 0a the design it runs on.

**Perfect-form test.** If the app did not exist, this would still be worth saying because a wearable that draws sweat and interstitial fluid through a passive gel and reads several metabolites continuously would be the first molecular occasion stream a population could supply without effort, and what such a stream is worth depends almost entirely on what stands beside it: the genome, a diary, a known challenge, and an outcome years later.

**Abstract.** A passive wearable that draws fluid from the skin without exercise and reads glucose, lactate, ketones, uric acid and their kin continuously turns the body axis's perceived channel of paper 5 into a measured one, at the cadence of hours rather than days, on a population rather than a ward. This paper states what that stream is and is not: a reading of a small and person-specific fraction of the blood quantity, stable enough within a person over weeks to read trends, and not a reading of absolute values. From that one fact the whole program follows. Crossed with the genome, the stream makes knowable the genetics of dynamic phenotypes nobody has measured, recovery slope, excursion area and time to baseline under a standardized challenge, and the genetics of resilience across a life, provided the phenotype is defined on a fixed challenge, the sensor's decay is separated from the person's, and an outcome is linked. Crossed with every repeated measure, it makes within-person change interpretable at hourly grain, and it supplies the covariate that says which changes are the body. Three uses are ranked by what the hardware can deliver rather than by what it promises: earlier warning in trials, which needs only within-person precision and should be first; the genetics of response shapes, which is the strongest science and second; and dosing of gene-therapy vectors, which the stream cannot support and is rescoped to personal reference ranges. The conditions are the paper's spine: a calibration channel, a sweat-rate normalizer, a diary, the bad-day wear gap, the absence of immune molecules from the panel, consent at family scope, custody at the custodian, and the overlap of stream and genome as the number that gates every genetic claim. Two cheap experiments decide the program's shape before any of the rest is built, and a short list of lifts that cost decisions rather than engineering moves its worth by more than any sensor.

**1 · The problem, stated structurally**

Almost everything measured about a body is measured at a visit. A blood draw once a year, a panel at a clinic, a sample when something is already wrong. Between visits the body does everything that matters, responds to meals, sleep, illness, stress and treatment, and none of it is recorded. Aging is described as a decline in the ability to return to baseline after a load, and that ability is exactly what a visit cannot see, because a visit is a point and resilience is a curve.

The one continuous molecular measurement in wide use reads a single quantity through an inserted sensor, and it has already shown how much a person's daily response differs from the population's. What has not existed is a passive stream of several quantities on people who did nothing to supply it, held beside their genome, their diary, a known challenge, and what happened to them afterwards. Paper 5 built the body axis on daily self-report and, where consented, a device band folded to one number a day. This paper is that axis's measured channel at its perfect form: hourly, multi-analyte, passive, and crossed with everything else.

**2 · The two axes alone, and where each stops**

The stream is a passive wearable that draws micro-volumes of sweat and interstitial fluid through a gel by an osmotic gradient, without exercise or power, and reads a small panel of metabolites by electrochemistry. What such a device can be expected to deliver, and what it should be held to before any claim is made on it, is this: the fluid it reads carries a small fraction of the blood concentration of each quantity, the fraction differs between people, and within a person it holds for weeks. So the stream tracks trends within a person and does not state absolute values, and the fraction drifts over longer horizons unless recalibrated. Alone, the stream is a diary of the body's chemistry with nothing to explain: it can say a person is different from their own usual today and cannot say why, what it means, or whether it matters. Its ceiling is meaning and consequence.

The genome, imported as a file the person already holds, is a fixed fact that predicts nothing without a phenotype measured well on the same people, and inherits the mixture of whatever phenotype trained its weights. Its ceiling is a phenotype worth having. Papers 1 and 3 say that the phenotypes worth having are the ones no cohort could afford to collect, and a passive stream is the cheapest deep phenotype there is.

Each axis's ceiling is the other axis. The genome gives the stream a cause to test against; the stream gives the genome a phenotype measured hourly on a population, which no biobank has held.

**3 · The crossing, and one fact that reorders everything**

Paper 0 states the general argument and paper 5 the level: nothing here is a claim about how people differ unless it says so, and most of it is about how a person moves around their own centre.

Three parts are specific to this pair. The stream is a measured channel beside the perceived one, so paper 5's routing applies: each channel is read against the outcomes it should govern, and their disagreement decomposes into perception bias, device bias and true difference, with only the third a reading. The device is a second instrument, not the truth: its output is a model's estimate from an electrochemical current, with its own bias term, an error that depends on sweat rate and skin condition, and an algorithm version that travels with every value.

The stream's cadence changes what a within-person phenotype can be. At daily grain paper 5 reads a state share and a lag structure in days; at hourly grain the response to a single load becomes a curve, and the curve's parameters, its peak, its slope back to baseline, its area, its time to return, are each a phenotype in their own right. Paper 1's argument that trajectory parameters are phenotypes, made there for a test repeated over years, is made here for a meal repeated over a morning.

And the fact that reorders everything: the stream reads trends and not values. Every use that needs an absolute number is out. Every use that needs "this person is different from their own normal" is in. That single line ranks the three uses of section 5, sets the conditions of section 6, and decides which experiment runs first in section 8.

**4 · What becomes knowable**

*4.1 Resilience, as a phenotype defined on a fixed challenge.* A person's return to baseline after a load is the aging concept the field names and cannot measure at scale. It becomes measurable only against a fixed challenge, because a recovery slope from an unknown load is a slope from nothing: one standardized meal, one fixed stair climb, one short cold exposure, taken on a schedule and identical every time. The response curve to that challenge, repeated quarterly across years, is the resilience phenotype, and its own slope across the years is the aging phenotype. This is what no cohort has held, because no cohort can bring people in quarterly for decades and a passive wearable can.

*4.2 The genetics of response shapes.* Papers 1 and 3 say the phenotypes worth crossing with the genome are the ones nobody phenotyped. Here they are the curve parameters of 4.1, on a population, under paper 3's conditions: common-variant claims only, ancestry modelled, batch entered, identity established from the data, and the item served blind to the genome where its genetics is being read. It becomes knowable whether recovery speed is heritable, whether its genetics is the genetics of the level run backwards or something else, and which parts of the body's response to a load are disposition and which are circumstance. The strongest form is the natural experiment paper 4 makes available: people born with a gene switched off, measured continuously against matched people who are not, which is the closest thing to a human knockout that exists and directly answers what editing that gene would do.

*4.3 The immune event that costs nothing.* The panel carries no immune molecules, and section 6 says why that matters. What the stream can read is the metabolic shadow of an immune event: the shift in glucose, lactate and ketones that accompanies systemic inflammation even when the inflammatory proteins themselves are unreadable. Every routine vaccination in the population is a dated, consented, real immune perturbation happening for free, and the stream's response to it, hour by hour, is the closest thing to an immune readout a metabolite panel can supply. It is also the ideal validation event for the claim that the stream detects activation within hours, because it is common, ethical, and needs no trial.

*4.4 Earlier warning than a visit.* In a trial, a participant is seen every week or two. A dense personal baseline read hourly can show a deviation before the next visit, and for a safety signal the system need not know what is wrong, only that something is: a trial has clinicians, and the stream's job is to say "call this participant today." Liver toxicity from a vector develops over days and shows in metabolic markers; a systemic inflammatory response moves the panel even when the cytokines do not cross into the fluid. The comparison is against a fortnightly visit, not against a perfect sensor, which is why this reading survives the panel's limits when the immune-guardrail claim does not.

*4.5 What an intervention does within weeks.* A longevity compound is judged today by whether lifespans extend, which takes years. Whether it flattens a person's metabolic variability, or shortens their recovery after the fixed challenge, is readable within weeks from within-person precision alone, with no absolute accuracy and no outcome linkage required. This is the reading that needs the least of anything and changes how such trials are run.

*4.6 The occasion covariate at hourly grain.* Paper 5 made the body the covariate under every within-person reading in the series. The stream makes it hourly. Paper 1's test day, paper 2's reveal, paper 4's variability signature and the bank's state items all gain a covariate that says what the body was doing at the hour of the observation, not the day, and a covariate that was measured rather than perceived.

**5 · The return path, and the three uses ranked**

*The body axis.* Its perceived channel gains a measured one to be routed against, and paper 5's direction reading, the cross-lagged asymmetry in both directions, gains a channel recorded before the morning's mood could colour it.

*The genome.* It gains the deepest passive phenotype battery there is, and a new class of phenotype, the curve parameter, on which the never-phenotyped argument of paper 3 bites hardest.

*The bank and the trait scales.* Every repeated item gains an hourly body covariate, so the state share of paper 5 is read at the grain the body actually moves on.

*The declared difference.* Paper 4's variability signature gains a measured within-person variability rather than a reported one, and its carrier cohorts gain the continuous phenotype that makes a human knockout readable.

*The design layer.* The standardized challenge is a system-controlled event of paper 0's first class, so its timing can be randomized; the vaccination is a dated event of the second class, and both feed paper 0a's event-triggered serving.

*The three uses, ranked by what the hardware delivers.* First, earlier warning in trials, 4.4 and 4.5, because it needs only within-person precision, earns first, and produces the data the other two need. Second, the genetics of response shapes, 4.1 and 4.2, the strongest science and the one gated by the overlap count of section 7 and by a validation chain no sensor shortens: fine-mapping, causal gene assignment, tissue, direction of effect, pleiotropy, functional work, each a rung on a ladder written in advance with its failure rate published. Third, and rescoped: dosing of gene-therapy vectors. Vector handling is governed by pre-existing antibodies, reticuloendothelial uptake, complement and receptor expression, none of which appears in the fluid, so the stream cannot set a dose. What it can do is define each participant's own normal, so a post-dose deviation is read against them rather than a population, and if a baseline feature ever predicts tolerability that is a hypothesis tested prospectively, never a dosing rule.

**6 · What would have to hold**

Trends, not values. The device tracks a person-specific fraction of the blood quantity, and every reading is stated as a within-person change; no reading states an absolute level.

A calibration channel exists. Enzymatic sensors decay over days to weeks, the osmotic agent is consumed, and the dilution changes as it depletes, so drift is not separable from physiology without a reference: a co-worn reference during periodic calibration windows, a replaceable sensor cartridge on a schedule matched to its measured decay, and the standardized challenge as a known input. Without these, a failing sensor and a sick person produce the same trace, and the aging reading of 4.1 is indistinguishable from the engineering failure of a decade-old sensor.

A sweat-rate channel is read as a ruler, not a signal. The fraction depends on how much the person sweats, and a change in sweating reads as a change in chemistry until it is normalized out. Skin contamination on the finger is a documented confound and is modelled, not assumed away.

The panel is the panel. Immune molecules are proteins at concentrations and sizes that do not cross into the fluid in readable amounts, so no reading here claims to see a cytokine; 4.3 reads a shadow and says so. Cortisol is a steroid read by affinity rather than by an enzyme, is further along than a research project and short of a decade of drift, and any stress or aging reading built on it states which channel it came from.

The diary exists and is cheap. Meals, caffeine, alcohol, medication, sleep, illness and cycle day, one tap each, enterable the next day. Without it most deviations are lunch. Passive chemistry plus a small active context channel is worth far more than passive chemistry alone, and the paper says so rather than promising zero effort.

The bad-day gap is designed against, not modelled away. People remove a wearable when ill, in hospital, charging or swimming, which are the days the stream most wants. Wear state is recorded as data, the report is retrospectively enterable, the response propensity is modelled per occasion beside the leaving hazard, and every coupling carries paper 5's sensitivity band over how much worse the unworn days were.

The genome's conditions of papers 1 and 3 apply unchanged: common-variant claims only, ancestry modelled, batch and vendor entered, identity established from the data, discovery items served blind to the genome, and selection anchored from outside.

Consent is per use, revocable, and at family scope for the genome, which describes relatives who never consented. Consent to be monitored in a trial and consent to contribute to discovery are separate decisions, and no declaration or file is taken from a minor.

Custody is at the custodian. The stream and the genome stay on the person's devices; only model-relative summaries leave; quantities defined on pairs of people need their own primitive; and every published statistic, per-variant results above all, spends against a recorded output-privacy budget released per frozen cohort. A pooled store of genomes beside continuous chemistry is the most identifying and most attractive dataset a person can produce, and one breach ends recruitment permanently.

The AI is two layers, named correctly. A purpose-trained model over the time series with the genome beside it does the ingestion and the pattern finding; a reasoning model above it writes analyses, reads, proposes and interprets. A classical model stays as the floor the learned one must beat on days the policy did not choose. A general reasoning model does not ingest an electrochemical current, and a diagram that says it does costs the program its credibility in the first room of biologists.

**7 · The requirements the design generates**

The overlap count is the program's headline metric: the number of people who supplied both the stream and a sequenced genome, per phenotype, because a coupling is identified only by people observed on both and no quantity of stream-only or genome-only participants narrows it. Deeper phenotyping buys effective sample size through lower measurement error, a small multiple and not an order of magnitude, so the count is stated as a target per discovery claim. Age spread from the start, including the oldest, so the age curve is readable in the third year rather than the twentieth. Families, and carriers of switched-off genes, recruited deliberately, which is possible only if the genome is sequenced before the wearable ships. Ancestry spread, so that any genetic finding travels. A yearly clinic panel, on a stable instrument, as the anchor that does not drift and the source of the molecules the fluid cannot carry. Linked outcomes over decades, consented at enrolment, because retrofitting outcome consent to an existing cohort is expensive and often impossible. None is a reason to want less. Each is what a fully populated stream beside a fully populated genome looks like from the inside.

**8 · Two experiments that decide the shape, and the lifts that cost decisions**

Before any of the rest is built, two cheap experiments settle whether the program has the shape this paper gives it.

The first is ninety days of one analyte against a reference, in a small cohort, reporting within-person precision and drift separately from absolute accuracy. If within-person precision survives ninety days, every reading in section 4 is reachable and the rest of the program is a matter of scale. If drift or sweat-rate noise overwhelms it, no model downstream recovers the lost baseline, and the program is rebuilt around whatever analyte does survive.

The second is a retrospective look at one completed trial with timestamped adverse events, on archived continuous data or a comparable cohort wearing the device, asking whether a deviation from the personal baseline was visible before the clinical event was recorded. Hours to days of warning makes the safety use the program's first product; events invisible until symptoms means the program leads with discovery instead. Neither experiment needs the learned model, the genome cohort or a new sensor.

The lifts below cost decisions rather than engineering, and together they move the program's worth by more than any sensor could. Sequence before shipping wearables, so recruitment can be deliberate. Recruit families, not individuals. Ask for outcome linkage on day one. Publish under a data-access process, because an open cohort is used by hundreds of groups and a closed one by a few analysts, and that difference is what separates a good dataset from a field-defining one. Schedule the standardized challenge. Record every vaccination the population already receives. Write the target-graduation ladder before the first hit, and publish its failure rate. Compute at the custodian, before the first breach rather than after.

**9 · The potential, at full population, priced**

Fully populated and built in the order above, the stream beside the genome is the first passive, hourly, multi-analyte phenotype battery held beside genomes, diaries, a fixed challenge and outcomes, on a population. Its worth by field, on a scale where ten is a dataset that changes how a field works: aging, seven, and eight or nine once outcomes and years accumulate, because resilience becomes a measured phenotype with a genetics; the genetics of response shapes, seven at the overlap counts section 7 sets, and gated below that; trials, eight for how they are run and six for how fast biology learns from them; immune biology, three, until proteins can be read continuously; stress and hormones, five, on the affinity channel's own conditions. Overall, near seven as the hardware validates, and four as the concept was first drawn, because the concept's headline claims were the ones the panel cannot carry.

Inside the system, it is paper 5's measured channel at the grain the body moves on, the covariate under every within-person reading, and the phenotype battery papers 1 and 3 were waiting for.

**10 · Open questions the data would have to answer**

Whether within-person precision survives ninety days for any analyte beyond glucose, which the first experiment answers. Whether the metabolic shadow of an immune event is early enough to be useful, which the vaccination reading answers. How wide the sensitivity band over unworn days is once the report is the cheapest act, and whether next-day entry recovers what the gap costs. Whether the curve parameters of a standardized challenge are heritable enough to reward the overlap count, which only the overlap count answers. And how far a decade of sensor generations can be linked into one series without the aging signal being the engineering's, which the yearly anchor is designed to answer and may not.
