# What is seen first

*Paper 9 of the axiom series: physical appearance, from photographs and in person, as two instruments that must be kept apart — what a body is, and what others read from it — with the two doors through which a face can predict a person named, and only one of them open to design.*

**Status: theory research.** Nothing here describes the app or proposes a change to it; `research/README.md` says what a paper is and how one reaches the product. Paper 2 supplies the observer structure this axis both confounds and decomposes, and the interference design it borrows; paper 8 the cancellation and the two-sided budget; papers 5 and 6 the body's other channels and the drift discipline; paper 7 the verification chain it can supply one claim to; papers 1 and 2 the rule against difference scores.

**Perfect-form test.** If the app did not exist, this would still be worth saying because appearance is what others observe about a person before anything else is known, so it enters every social measurement through two paths that are rarely written down — who is paired with whom, and what the observer brings — and because a face can predict a person through two doors that no instrument blind to the face can tell apart: the biology that shaped both, and the treatment the face drew over a lifetime. Which door is open is not a question a criterion answers. It is a question a design partly answers, and stating what the partial answer is worth is the paper.

**Abstract.** Appearance is measured by two instruments. Morphology is geometry: what a body is, read from images against a capture standard or in person, a physical measurement whose problems are calibration and drift. Perception is what others read from a body, an observer rating of exactly paper 2's kind with a face as its stimulus, and its defining property is that readers agree with each other far more than the face agrees with the person — which is reliability, and is about the readers. An earlier draft of this paper took the next step the subject always takes: score the readers' consensus against the trait measured by an instrument that never saw the face, and call the result validity with the readers removed. That step is not available. An instrument can be blind to a face and its criterion still be downstream of it, because appearance draws treatment and treatment over a lifetime shapes self-concept, schooling, encouragement and the behaviour others produce in a person's presence — so the bank is a treatment history, the keyed score is partly one, and enacted behaviour under an opponent who saw the face is one too. A face therefore predicts a person through two doors, shared biology and treatment history, and no criterion blind to the face separates them; a nonzero coupling is ambiguous between the two and a zero is ambiguous between no signal and two signals cancelling. So the estimand is restated as the coupling and its doors, with what each design can and cannot say: the treatment door's existence and per-exposure size are identified by randomizing whether one person is displayed to another, as an interaction with appearance rather than as a visibility effect, at the level of the choice set and against a placeholder rather than an absence; the treatment door's lifetime integral is not identified by anything; and within-person appearance change — weight, age, injury, a changed regime — is the one timing handle, because a face is not fixed. The remainder follows from that: paper 2's category term decomposed rather than corrected; self-presentation as three separate acts; the body's visible state as a fourth instrument that is itself a perceived signal; two clocks as residuals on chronological age validated against outcomes and not against each other; a session-binding channel for paper 7 under an enrolment requirement that paper must carry; and two decisions stated as enumerated shapes with what each exposes and what each kills, for the owner.

**1 · The problem, stated structurally**

Every social measurement of a person is taken after that person has been seen. So appearance enters those measurements through two paths, and neither is usually recorded. The first is pairing: who rates whom, who follows whom, who is chosen as an opponent, is decided after a face was seen, so the set of pairs on which any social reading is computed was assembled partly by appearance. The second is the observer's contribution: whatever a rater brings to a target, part of it was brought to the face. A reading taken on appearance-assembled pairs by appearance-influenced observers carries appearance as a term it cannot name.

The subject has a second problem that is its own. Readers agree about what a face conveys. That agreement is the statistical signature of a reliable instrument, and the instrument is reliable — about the readers. The step from there to a claim about the person has always been taken by scoring the consensus against something else, and the trap in that step is not that the something else might be unreliable. It is that the something else is not independent of the face even when it never saw it. Section 3 states why, and what is left.

**2 · The axis alone, and the instruments in it**

*Morphology is a physical measurement.* Shape, proportion, height, build, colouring, symmetry, the features a geometric model can locate, read from images against a capture standard or in person. Its problems are calibration and drift: pose, distance, lens, lighting and expression each move a two-dimensional measurement of a three-dimensional body, and camera pipelines change over years. Alone it is anthropometry — precise, heritable to whatever degree paper 3's conditions measure, and silent about the person.

*Perception is an observer measurement.* What a panel of readers assigns to a face, and it inherits paper 2's structure: a normative component every reader gives every face, a stereotype component a reader gives a face of a recorded category — the categories being the anchors both sides carry, and nothing looser — and a residual that is specific to this face. That residual is the object of every perceived-signal reading below, and an earlier draft defined it out of existence by writing that a stranger's reading is normative plus stereotype and nothing else. It is not nothing else: it is the face-distinctive part, and whether it carries anything is the question.

*Presentation is a set of choices, not one.* Which of many images a person supplies; how it was produced — angle, lighting, grooming, cosmetics, clothing; and whether it is what it claims to be — edited, old, someone else's. Three acts with three meanings, and a design that reads them as one parameter reads none.

*State is a time-varying channel.* Visible fatigue, weight, skin, posture, repeated over time — an occasion measure that a second person reads, which makes it a perceived signal in its own right and not an instrument exempt from the discipline the perceived channel is under.

*The other axes alone have never had the terms.* Paper 2's observers rated targets whose appearance was neither recorded nor controlled; paper 8's opponents and every tie were chosen after a face was seen. None of those readings is wrong. Each carries a term it cannot name, and this axis is what names it.

**3 · The doors, and what each design can say**

*3.1 Agreement is reliability, and the arithmetic is about the readers.* With k readers whose average inter-reader correlation is r, the consensus has reliability k·r divided by 1 plus (k−1)·r. At an average inter-reader correlation of two tenths, twenty readers give a consensus above eight tenths. That figure is a total-variance figure: reading within demographic strata removes the between-strata part of the target variance, so the within-stratum inter-reader correlation is lower — roughly one tenth if half the target variance lies between strata — and the panel that reaches the same consensus reliability is closer to forty. The correlation of a consensus with a criterion is bounded above by the square root of the product of the two reliabilities, and that bounds the *observed* coefficient; the paper publishes the observed coefficient, the ceiling, and the disattenuated value beside each other, and says which one a sentence is about. And the sampling unit for a coefficient is the target, not the reader: a standard error of five hundredths on a small coefficient needs about four hundred targets per stratum, each read by the panel above — which, across the strata of section 6, is tens of thousands of readings, and is stated in section 7 as the requirement it is.

*3.2 Two doors, and no criterion tells them apart.* A face predicts a trait through shared biology — whatever shaped the face also shaped the person — or through treatment history — the face drew a lifetime of responses from others, and the responses shaped the person. The second door is not a hypothesis to be tested against the bank; the bank is its product. Self-concept is what treatment leaves; a keyed score is partly what encouragement and schooling left; behaviour enacted in an encounter is behaviour produced under an opponent who saw the face and behaved accordingly. So blindness of the instrument to the face does not make the criterion exogenous to it, and the earlier draft's estimand — validity with the readers removed — is not defined once the second door is admitted. A nonzero coupling between a face and a criterion is real and predictive and says nothing about which door; a zero is compatible with no signal and with two signals of opposite sign. The question "do faces reveal character" is therefore ill-posed, and the paper replaces it with two that are not: how much does a face predict, and what does each design say about the door.

*3.3 The treatment door is identified by design, per exposure, as an interaction.* Any system that controls whether one person is displayed to another can randomize that display, and the difference in how the person is then treated is identified. Four things about that estimand, each of which the earlier draft got wrong. It is an interaction, not a main effect: showing against withholding identifies what *an* image being visible does, and the effect of *this* appearance is the interaction between assignment and the person's morphology or perceived score — a between-person contrast that needs the assumption that nothing else correlated with appearance moderates visibility, and that assumption is stated because the face of a real person cannot itself be randomized. It lives at the level of the choice set: a follow from a slate or an opponent chosen from a list is a comparison, so by paper 8's cancellation a visibility effect common to every candidate vanishes and only relative appearance survives — and withholding one candidate's image changes every other candidate's outcome in the same set, so the unit of assignment is the set and the design carries paper 2's interference discipline on the graph. Its control is not absence: a blank where an image is expected reads as evasive or false, so the design has three arms — the image, a uniform placeholder identical across people, and nothing — and the appearance estimand is read against the placeholder. And what it identifies is the door's per-exposure size in one context; the lifetime integral of the same door, which is what the bank carries, is identified by nothing, and the paper says so.

*3.4 A face is not fixed, and change is the timing handle.* Weight, age, injury, surgery, a changed regime of grooming: a person's appearance moves, and when it moves the treatment door predicts that treatment changes after it and self-concept after that, on the same person, in that order. That is paper 5's within-person timing applied to appearance, and it is the only handle on the lifetime door — partial, because the changes are not assigned, and real, because the order is observable.

*3.5 A supplied image is chosen; an observation in person is not.* The supplied image carries the three acts of section 2. The in-person observation removes the choice of image and adds an observer whose presence is reactivity and whose reading is an instrument with its own bias. What separates them is three latent factors — selection, production, misrepresentation — each with its own reliability, never a raw difference, and never a sign, since a sign across unlinked scales is an artefact of centring as paper 8 already found for its own discrepancy.

*3.6 A face at the camera binds a body to a moment, and only that.* Paper 7 found nothing in the worn stream binds a body to the session where the answers are produced. A face at the screen supplies one claim: an enrolled face was in front of this session at this moment. It supplies continuity not at all — paper 7 gives the worn stream that claim precisely because it is observed always rather than at a gate, and a camera check is a gate. And it supplies session binding only under a requirement paper 7 must carry: the face is enrolled to the same chain as the device and the genome, at the supervised event that paper already has, with a stated false-accept rate — otherwise it is a fifth identity tied to none of the other four, and the confederate whose face is at the screen while the device's wearer sits in the next room passes it.

**4 · What becomes knowable**

*4.1 The coupling, per trait, with its doors named.* Blind, independent panels read standardized images in randomized order; the consensus's face-distinctive residual is scored against each criterion the series holds — self-report, the keyed score, enacted behaviour, the body's channels — within strata, with the observed coefficient, its ceiling and its disattenuated value all published. What is published beside the number is which door it could have come through, which by 3.2 is both, and what the design of 3.3 says about the treatment door's per-exposure size for that trait. The paper commits to no prior on magnitude; it states what each outcome would mean. A coupling for a trait with a large randomized treatment effect and no within-person timing signal is compatible with the treatment door alone; a coupling with no treatment effect in any context is evidence the shared-biology door is open; a zero is reported as a zero with both readings.

*4.2 Paper 2's category term, decomposed.* With the perceived channel measured, that paper's category component separates into what a category is, read from members' criteria, and what it looks like, read from strangers' consensus on members' faces. An acquaintance's rating can then be decomposed into a face-predictable part and a residual — and that is a decomposition, not a correction, because under 3.2 partialling appearance out of an accuracy reading removes true score along with reader bias and biases distinctive accuracy downward. Which part is the target quantity is stated per reading.

*4.3 The treatment door, by design.* Under 3.3: the interaction of assignment with appearance, at the choice-set level, against the placeholder arm, on follows, opponent choice and sealed predictions in any system that controls display. And a second layer, because readers are measured too: whether the effect differs by the reader's own categories, which makes the treatment door a relation between kinds rather than a property of faces.

*4.4 Self-presentation, as three parameters.* Multiple supplied images per person, an observation in person for a modelled subsample, and the three factors of 3.5, each with its reliability and no sign. Then crossed: whether selection, production or misrepresentation is the parameter that travels with the bank's response style and with paper 8's reported-against-enacted discrepancy, or whether the acts are different acts.

*4.5 Visible state, as the fourth instrument and a perceived signal.* Paper 2 already supplies a same-day acquaintance report of whether a person seemed tired or unwell, so a blind standardized capture is the fourth instrument for the body's state, not the third, and what it adds is a stranger's reading with no acquaintance and a fixed capture — which lets the acquaintance's report be split into what was visible and what was known. It is read by a person, so it is a perceived signal under 3.1's discipline and is scored against paper 5's reports and paper 6's stream as its criterion. Its error is non-ignorable and correlated with the latent: an announced capture invites grooming, and people who feel worst groom least or groom most to conceal, so the channel is scoped to what an announced capture can carry or paired with an unannounced or passive arm.

*4.6 Two clocks, as residuals validated against outcomes.* Perceived age is a reader consensus subject to grooming, cosmetics, weight, ancestry and cross-group estimation bias, and chronological age is known from the anchors; the face clock is the consensus residualised on chronological age inside a latent model with its own reliability, never the difference. Paper 6's within-person resilience slope is a second residual on the same person. Their agreement is not validation — both can track chronological age imperfectly partialled, or a shared third cause such as adiposity or sun that moves skin and metabolism together — so each is validated against linked outcomes, and their disagreement, once both are, is the finding about whether there is one clock or several.

*4.7 The mediation, with its identified link stated honestly.* Genome to face is heritability under paper 3's conditions. Face to treatment is identified as 3.3's interaction, under 3.3's assumption, per exposure, in the contexts a system controls. Treatment to outcome is observational. The chain is not identified and the paper does not claim it; one link of it becomes experimental under a stated assumption.

*4.8 One claim for paper 7.* Session binding, as 3.6 defines it, under the enrolment requirement and its false-accept rate, with a presented image, a recording and a synthesized face as its attacks and an interactive challenge as its answer, and with paper 7's rule that a template is irrevocable.

**5 · The return path, the two decisions, and the ranking**

*Observers.* Paper 2 gains the term it lacked and a decomposition of its category component.

*The bank and the instruments.* Every self-report gains the treatment door as a named alternative to a face-reading explanation, and 4.4's three presentation parameters beside its response style.

*The logic test.* The reading that ability can be seen in a face is scored against a keyed score — and the keyed score is named as partly a treatment history too.

*Arranged competitions.* Paper 8 gains appearance as an assigned treatment on its opponents' behaviour and on its comparison effect, and a second manipulable variable beside the stakes, under the same cancellation.

*Ties.* The follow graph and the choice of opponent become readable as partly appearance-driven, at the choice-set level, by design.

*The body.* A fourth state instrument and a second clock.

*The genome.* One link of a mediation made experimental under a stated assumption.

*Verification.* One of five claims, under an enrolment requirement.

*The first decision the paper does not make: what a person is shown about their own face.* Four shapes. Nothing shown, and the person told that readings exist and what they are used for. The population's findings shown — what faces predict in general — and no personal value. The person's own values on request, with the reliability, the ceiling and the doors beside them. The person's own values by default. In every shape the readings exist and every reading of section 4 survives; display changes nothing measured. What differs is what is exposed and to whom — in each shape only to the person, about themselves — and the cost: the first shape is the failure the rest of this series refuses, a person read by others and not told what was read; the fourth is a number about one's own face arriving unasked. The smallest shape that avoids both is the third. The decision is the owner's.

*The second decision: the custody of the image.* Three shapes, and each kills something. Device-only, with derived features leaving: no reader ever sees a face, so 4.1, 4.2, 4.5 and 4.6 do not exist, and what survives is morphology and the treatment door. Transient display to readers with no stored image: readers read a face that is never kept, derived features and consensus are stored, 4.1 and 4.3 survive, and cross-year comparability for 4.5 and 4.6 survives only if the feature extractor is frozen or the same-person overlap of section 6 is bought with re-captures rather than re-derivations. A stored standardized capture under the strictest custody the series has: everything survives, and the store is the one whose breach is a breach in the street. The smallest shape that gets the perceived channel at all is the second; the cost of the third is stated by paper 7's template rule, which applies to a face more than to anything. The decision is the owner's.

*Ranked by what they make knowable.* First, the coupling with its doors and the treatment door by design, which are one program and which answer, for the first time on one population, both of the questions that replace the ill-posed one. Second, paper 2's decomposition, which changes what every observer reading in the series means. Third, the two clocks, which need years and outcomes. The mediation is a link and is ranked as one.

**6 · What would have to hold**

*Agreement is reported as agreement, with the three numbers.* Every perceived-signal claim publishes the observed coefficient, the ceiling, and the disattenuated value, says which it means, and never reports a consensus as a property of the person.

*Both doors are named on every coupling.* No coupling between a face and a criterion is published as face-reading; each carries what 3.3 found for the treatment door in that trait and what 3.4 found within person, and is otherwise reported as compatible with both.

*The between-strata term is unidentified, and stratification has a cost.* A between-strata difference in a coupling is confounded among a trait difference, differential treatment and non-invariance of the criterion or the perceived scale across strata, so it is published as unidentified among the three, with an invariance test across strata and roles required before any signed gap is read, as paper 2 requires. And the strata are visible categories on the treatment door's own path — appearance, then categorization, then treatment — so reading within them conditions on a mediator, and the within-stratum coupling is stated as the coupling net of the categorical part of the treatment door, not as the coupling.

*Readers are independent, ordered at random, and described.* Independent readings, randomized stimulus order per reader, a stated stimulus load, each reader's own categories recorded, and overlapping panels across years, because reader norms drift and a drifting reader is a drifting instrument.

*Both instruments carry a drift model.* Capture generation enters every morphological and every perceived model as a term; a same-person overlap across capture generations is bought, as paper 6 buys one across sensor generations; and a drift rate is stated for the camera, for the pipeline and for the reader cohort — otherwise the face clock, the camera clock and the reader clock are one number.

*The capture standard names what it controls and what it does not.* Expression, pose and lighting are controlled by a guide; distance is fixed by a physical scale reference of known size in frame or a depth estimate, because a phone cannot fix distance otherwise and perspective at short range is the largest morphological artefact there is. Haircut, cosmetics, tan, weight and eyewear are not controlled by the anchor, so the anchor is not presentation-free, morphology is measured on it with those entered as terms, and the supplied image is read against it as presentation with the same terms. A manipulation detector owes the alert rate and predictive value paper 6 demands of any detector, and the in-person channel is its ground.

*Exposure randomization has a denominated two-sided budget and its own consent.* The budget is in impressions and days: a bound on the cumulative hidden share per person per window, because a withheld-face trajectory accumulates forgone contacts the way paper 8's loss trajectory accumulates losses, and a per-encounter bound does not bound a trajectory. The induced effect on the withheld person is published beside the estimate, which is a disclosure and not a protection, and the protection is the bound. And the consent the design needs is consent to be *withheld* at random, which is a decision of its own, separate from consent to be shown.

*The in-person channel runs under a protocol and its entry is modelled.* What is recorded, by whom, blind to what; the observer's reading as an instrument with a bias term; the observer's presence as a reactivity term; and selection into the subsample modelled as papers 5 through 8 model theirs.

*The state channel's error is non-ignorable.* Grooming on capture days moves with the state being measured, so 4.5 is scoped to what an announced capture carries or paired with a passive arm, and paper 5's randomized-schedule arm does not make an announced capture unobtrusive and is not claimed to.

*Consent is per purpose, and the purposes are six.* Being measured; being read by strangers; being shown to others; being withheld from others at random; being observed in person; being matched across accounts. None implies another. A minor's image is a consent requirement satisfied by building the consent — a guardian's, and the minor's own assent — not by deciding it away. A third party in a frame is a fork rather than a rule: excluding such frames removes candid capture, group settings and much of the in-person channel, and admitting them requires the third party's consent to be built, and the owner chooses which cost.

*Custody is in whichever of section 5's shapes the owner chooses, built before the first capture.*

*The verification channel carries its requirement.* Face enrolled to the device and genome chain at the supervised event, a stated false-accept rate, no continuity claim, and paper 7's irrevocable-template rule.

*The rule against difference scores holds everywhere* — three presentation factors, the face clock as a residual, and never a sign across unlinked scales.

**7 · The requirements the design generates**

*Panels of about forty readers per stratum*, independent and randomly ordered, sized on within-stratum agreement, with reader categories recorded and overlapping across years.

*About four hundred targets per stratum for a standard error of five hundredths on a small coefficient*, which across the strata of section 6 is tens of thousands of readings and is a recruitment requirement the axis states.

*A capture standard with a scale reference, and several supplied images per person*, since presentation is three parameters and one image identifies none.

*Captures repeated on a schedule with a passive or unannounced arm*, for 4.5 and 4.6.

*Criterion measurements on the same people*, with each criterion's own exposure to the treatment door named.

*Randomized display in every interaction a system controls*, assigned at the choice-set level, with a placeholder arm, the realised probability recorded, the cumulative hidden share bounded, and consent to be withheld obtained.

*A same-person overlap across capture generations*, and stated drift rates for camera, pipeline and readers.

*An in-person subsample under protocol*, with its entry modelled, sized for three presentation factors and for the manipulation check.

*Face enrolment at the supervised event*, if the verification channel is used, with its false-accept rate.

*Custody built before the first capture*, in the shape the owner chooses.

None is a reason to want less. Each is what a fully populated appearance axis looks like from the inside.

**8 · What would decide it**

*The calibration question comes first.* Whether a supplied image can be placed against the capture standard closely enough for morphology to be read from it at all, and whether a phone capture with a scale reference fixes distance well enough that perspective is a term rather than the measurement. If not, morphology is read on the anchor alone and 4.4's production factor is the only thing a supplied image carries.

*Does display change treatment, as an interaction?* Randomize display at the choice-set level, against the placeholder, in any system that controls it, and read the interaction of assignment with appearance on follows, opponent choice and sealed predictions, with the cumulative hidden share bounded. This identifies the treatment door's per-exposure existence and size, which is what every reading in 4.1 needs beside it.

*Does the face-distinctive residual couple with anything?* Panels at section 7's sizes, targets at section 7's counts, within strata, with the three numbers published — and each coupling read beside the treatment result above. The study does not decide whether faces reveal character, because 3.2 says that question is not posed. It decides how much a face predicts and, trait by trait, what the treatment door accounts for per exposure.

**9 · The potential, priced with each score's own condition**

A ten is a dataset after which a field's standard design changes; a five answers questions the field already had. Each score names its condition, and every one is conditional on the calibration question of section 8.

*The perception of persons.* Seven, conditional on panels and targets at section 7's sizes. It sits above five because what it changes is not the answer to a question but the design available to anyone who asks one — a population with the criteria measured, the readers measured, and the treatment door randomized — and because 3.2's restatement of the question is itself the contribution.

*The treatment door.* Seven, conditional on randomized display being permitted at the choice-set level with a placeholder arm; two without it, since observation identifies no door.

*Paper 2's decomposition.* Six, conditional on the invariance tests of section 6, because it changes what every observer reading in the series means.

*The body's state and the two clocks.* Four, conditional on repeated captures, a passive arm, the drift overlap and linked outcomes, rising with years.

*The verification channel.* Four, conditional on enrolment to the chain at the supervised event; it supplies one claim of five under a requirement paper 7 must carry, and nothing without it.

*The mediation.* Three, because one link is made experimental under an assumption and the chain is not.

**10 · Open questions the data would have to answer**

Whether a supplied image can be placed against the capture standard, which precedes everything. How large the treatment door's per-exposure effect is, as an interaction with appearance, and whether it differs by who is looking. Whether any face-distinctive residual couples with any criterion once both doors are named beside it, and for which traits the treatment door accounts for what is found. Whether within-person appearance change is followed by treatment change and then by self-concept change in that order, and over what horizon. Whether selection, production and misrepresentation are one act or three. Whether an unannounced capture can be obtained under consent at all, on which 4.5 depends. Whether the face clock and the metabolic clock predict outcomes at all, and if both do, whether they predict the same ones. Whether a face enrolled to the chain holds a usable false-accept rate against a synthesized face. And what a person is owed about a reading others have made of their face — which is the first decision of section 5, and the one question here that no study answers.
