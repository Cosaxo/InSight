# What is seen first

*Paper 9 of the axiom series: physical appearance, from photographs and in person, as two instruments that must be kept apart — what a body is, and what others read from it — and as the covariate under every social reading in the series.*

**Status: theory research.** Nothing here describes the app or proposes a change to it; `research/README.md` says what a paper is and how one reaches the product. Paper 2 supplies the observer structure this axis both confounds and completes; paper 8 the randomized exposure design it borrows; papers 5 and 6 the body's other channels it is read beside; paper 7 the verification it can supply a channel to; paper 1 the rule against difference scores it obeys.

**Perfect-form test.** If the app did not exist, this would still be worth saying because appearance is the one thing about a person that others observe before anything else is known, so it sits under every social measurement anyone takes; because reading character from faces is the oldest measurement error in the study of persons and fails for a precise reason — agreement among readers about what a face means is a fact about the readers; and because the only instrument that can separate what a face says from what its readers bring is a population on which the traits being read are measured independently, which is what the rest of this series is.

**Abstract.** Appearance is measured by two instruments that the history of the subject has never kept apart. Morphology is geometry: what a body is, read from calibrated images and in person, a physical measurement with a calibration problem. Perception is what others read from a body, an observer rating of exactly paper 2's kind with the face as its stimulus, and its defining property is that observers agree with each other about what a face means far more than the face agrees with the person. That agreement — consistent, reproducible, high — is the trap: it looks like reliability and it is, and it has been taken for validity for as long as faces have been read. Validity requires a criterion the readers never saw, and this series has the criterion measured on the same people: the bank, the keyed test, the enacted behaviour of an arranged competition, the body's own channels, the genome. So the axis's first product is not a reading of persons but a reading of readings — how much, per trait, a face says once what its readers bring is removed — and the honest prior is that the answer is near zero for character and small but real for state and health. Three further facts organize the paper. Appearance changes how a person is treated, which changes their self-concept, which changes what they report, so a correlation between faces and self-report is evidence of a treatment history and not of face-reading; separating the two needs within-person timing and an intervention. The intervention exists: a system whose own interactions show or withhold a face can randomize exposure, which identifies the effect of appearance on treatment as cleanly as paper 8's assigned stakes identify a slope, with the same two-sided cost. And a photograph is chosen while an in-person observation is not, so the two differ by a self-presentation act that is itself a reading, and edited or generated images are the manipulation the in-person channel anchors against. What becomes knowable: the validity of perceived signals with the stereotype removed; paper 2's category component split into what a category is and what it looks like; the treatment effect of appearance identified by design; self-presentation as a person parameter; appearance as a repeated state channel beside the body's others; apparent age against metabolic aging as two clocks; the mediation from genome through face to treatment; and a session-binding channel paper 7 lacked. Two decisions are the owner's and the paper decides neither: whether a person is ever shown a reading of their own appearance, and the custody shape of the most identifying image a person can supply.

**1 · The problem, stated structurally**

Every social measurement of a person is taken after that person has been seen. An observer who rates an acquaintance saw a face first; a stranger who chooses an opponent, follows an account, or answers a sealed prediction about someone chose after seeing whatever was shown. So appearance is not one axis among the others — it is the covariate that was present, unrecorded, under every reading in this series that involves a second person, and a covariate that is present and unrecorded is a confounder.

The subject has a second problem that is its own, and it is a measurement problem before it is anything else. When people read traits from faces they agree with each other. The agreement is high, stable across readers and reproducible across studies, and it is the statistical signature of a reliable instrument — which it is. What it is reliable *about* is the readers: the shared stereotype that a face of this shape belongs to a person of that kind. Whether the person is in fact of that kind is a separate question, and it has been answered far less often than the first, because answering it requires the trait measured on the same person by an instrument that never saw the face. Reliability without that criterion is the mechanism by which reading character from faces persisted as a discipline for centuries and returns under new names whenever a new way of reading faces is invented.

The criterion is what this series has. A population on which self-report, a keyed test, enacted behaviour, the body's channels and the genome are all measured is a population on which the validity of any perceived signal can be estimated, per trait, with what the readers bring subtracted. That is the axis's first product, and it is a finding about readers as much as about faces.

**2 · The axis alone, and the two instruments in it**

*Morphology is a physical measurement.* Face shape, body proportions, height, build, colouring, symmetry, the features a geometric model can locate — read from images with a known capture geometry, or in person. Its problems are calibration problems: pose, distance, lens, lighting and expression all move a two-dimensional measurement of a three-dimensional body, so a morphological reading is a measurement only against a capture standard, exactly as paper 6's stream is a measurement only against a calibration channel. Alone, morphology is anthropometry: precise, heritable, and silent about the person.

*Perception is an observer measurement.* What a panel of readers says a face conveys — attractiveness, apparent age, apparent health, and the traits readers will assign when asked: competence, warmth, trustworthiness, dominance. It is paper 2's instrument with a stimulus in place of an acquaintance, and it inherits paper 2's whole structure: a normative component every reader supplies for every face, a stereotype component a reader supplies for a face of a given kind, and a distinctive residual. What it lacks, by construction, is the third thing paper 2's observers have — acquaintance — so a stranger's reading of a face is normative plus stereotype and nothing else, and its agreement with other strangers is agreement about the stereotype.

*Presentation is a choice, and it sits between the two.* Grooming, clothing, expression, angle, which photograph a person supplies at all. A supplied image is a self-presentation act, and reading it as morphology mistakes a decision for a body. An observation in person removes the choice of image and adds an observer.

*State is a time-varying channel.* Visible fatigue, weight change, skin, posture — appearance repeated over time is an occasion measure, and it is the only one of the body's channels in this series that a second person can read without an instrument.

Alone, the axis has all four and can distinguish none of them from the others. Its ceiling is the criterion.

*The other axes alone have never had the covariate.* Paper 2's observers rated targets whose appearance was neither recorded nor controlled, so its category component is a mixture of what a category is and what it looks like. Paper 8's opponents, and every tie in the follow graph, were chosen after a face was seen. None of these readings is wrong; each carries a term it cannot name.

**3 · The five structural facts**

*3.1 Agreement is not validity, and the criterion must be blind.* A perceived-trait score is reliable in proportion to its readers' agreement, and a panel of readers produces a consensus whose reliability rises with panel size in the ordinary way: with single-reader agreement of two tenths, a panel of twenty gives a consensus reliability above eight tenths. That number is real and it is about the readers. Validity is the correlation of that consensus with the trait measured on the person by an instrument that never saw the face, and it is bounded above by the square root of the product of the two reliabilities — the consensus's and the criterion's. So the validity of a perceived signal is estimable only where the criterion exists and is reliable, which is a requirement the series meets for some traits and not for others, and the paper says which.

*3.2 The feedback loop manufactures correlation without validity.* Appearance changes how a person is treated; treatment over years changes what a person believes about themselves; self-concept is what the bank measures. So a face that readers call competent will, on average, belong to a person who has been treated as competent, who may therefore report themselves as competent, and the correlation between the perceived signal and the bank is real and is not face-reading. It is a treatment history. Separating the two needs either within-person timing — a change in treatment preceding a change in self-report, on the same person, which the repeated designs of paper 5 make possible — or the intervention of 3.3. A between-person correlation between a face and a self-report identifies neither.

*3.3 Exposure is a system-controlled event, so the treatment effect is identified by design.* A system whose own interactions display a person to others — an opponent card, a follow suggestion, a sealed prediction's reveal — can show or withhold the image at random, and the difference in how the person is then treated is the effect of appearance on treatment, identified as cleanly as paper 8's assigned stakes identify a slope. This is paper 0's first class of event and it is the axis's causal handle. It carries paper 8's lesson: the manipulation has a cost that falls on the person whose face is withheld, so exposure randomization is a per-person budget for being the hidden side, not a free draw, and its induced effects on the person's own experience are reported beside the treatment effect it identifies.

*3.4 A photograph is chosen and an observation is not.* The supplied image is self-presentation; the in-person observation, under protocol, is not — but it adds an observer, whose presence is a reactivity term and whose own reading enters as an instrument with its own bias. The difference between what a person supplies and what an observer records is a self-presentation parameter, of the same kind as paper 8's reported-against-enacted discrepancy, and it obeys the same rule: it is a discrepancy factor in a latent model with its own reliability, never a raw difference between two scales. Edited and generated images are the attack on this channel, and the in-person observation and paper 7's verification are the anchor against it.

*3.5 The face is the standard biometric, and paper 7 lacked one at the screen.* Paper 7 concluded that nothing in the worn stream binds a body to the session where the answers are produced. A face at the screen does, partly: it places a body in front of the session at a moment, which is the session-binding channel that paper needed and could not build from a sensor on the skin. It carries the standard attacks — a presented image, a recording, a synthesized face — and the standard answers, an interactive challenge that a replay cannot answer, and it carries paper 7's rule that a template is irrevocable. What it adds to that paper is real: one of the five claims moves from "no instrument" to "an instrument with a threat model".

**4 · What becomes knowable**

*4.1 The validity of perceived signals, per trait, with the readers removed.* Blind panels read standardized images; the consensus is scored against the same trait measured on the person by an instrument that never saw the face — the bank for self-concept, the keyed test for ability, paper 8's enacted behaviour for disposition under stakes, paper 5's reports and paper 6's stream for state and health. What is published per trait is the validity coefficient with the ceiling of 3.1 stated beside it, within demographic strata, and the between-strata component reported separately as what it is. The honest prior is stated in advance so the finding can contradict it: near zero for character traits, small but real for apparent health and fatigue, and real for apparent age — which is the reading 4.6 builds on. A validity of zero is not a null result here; it is the measurement of a stereotype's reach, which is a finding about readers that has never been made at this scale with the criterion in hand.

*4.2 Paper 2's category component, split.* That paper decomposes an observer's prediction into what the population would say, what the target's category would say, and what only acquaintance supplies. With appearance measured, the category term splits again: what a category *is*, read from members' criteria, and what it *looks like*, read from strangers' perception of members' faces. An acquaintance's rating gains appearance as a covariate, so what is left after it is closer to what acquaintance actually knows, and paper 2's accuracy readings become readings net of the face — which is the correction every observer study has needed and none has had.

*4.3 The treatment effect of appearance, by design.* Under 3.3's randomization: how appearance changes who is followed, who is chosen as an opponent, how a sealed prediction about the person is made, and how the matcher's counterparties behave — each an intention-to-treat effect of showing the image, with the same discipline paper 8 applies to assigned opponent strength. And a second layer, because the readers are also measured: whether the treatment differs by the reader's own category, which turns the treatment effect into a reading about who treats whom differently, on the anchors both sides carry.

*4.4 Self-presentation as a person parameter.* The discrepancy factor of 3.4 between the supplied image and the observed body, published with its reliability, and then crossed: whether it is the same parameter as paper 8's reported-against-enacted discrepancy and the bank's own response style, or a different one. If a person who presents an improved image also reports an improved self, that is one trait with two readings; if not, presentation and report are different acts, which is itself the finding.

*4.5 Appearance as a state channel beside the body's others.* Standardized captures repeated on a schedule give a visible occasion measure — fatigue, weight, skin, posture — read against paper 5's reports and paper 6's stream on the same days. It is a third instrument for the body's state, and paper 5's three-way routing applies here for once, because a visible sign of fatigue and a reported night's sleep are two instruments for one construct in a way a metabolite trace and a mood report are not. Its identification requirement is paper 5's, unchanged.

*4.6 Two clocks.* Apparent age from a face is a biological reading, and paper 6's within-person resilience slope is another, on different tissue by a different mechanism. Read together on the same people over years, their agreement validates both as aging measures; their disagreement — a face that ages faster than metabolism, or slower — is a finding about whether there is one clock or several, and it is available only because both are measured on one population. The face's reading is cheap, which makes it the one that can be taken often.

*4.7 The mediation, with its identified link named.* Morphology is substantially heritable, which is not the finding. The finding is the chain: genome to face, face to treatment, treatment to outcome. The middle link is identified by 3.3's randomization; the first is heritability under paper 3's conditions; the last remains observational and is stated as such. So the chain is not identified as a whole and the paper does not claim it is — what is new is that one link of it becomes experimental rather than assumed.

*4.8 A channel for paper 7.* The face at the screen as session binding and as continuity, with its own threat model, which lets that paper's five claims all have an instrument for the first time.

**5 · The return path, the two decisions, and the ranking**

*Observers.* Paper 2 gains the covariate it always lacked and a split in its category term.

*The bank and the instruments.* Every self-report gains the treatment-history term of 3.2 as a named alternative to a face-reading explanation, and 4.4's presentation parameter beside its response style.

*The logic test.* The stereotype that ability can be read from a face is tested against a keyed score, which is the one criterion for it that cannot be argued with.

*Arranged competitions.* Paper 8 gains appearance as a treatment on its opponents' behaviour and on 4.5's social-comparison effect, and a second manipulable variable beside the stakes.

*Ties.* The follow graph and the choice of opponent become readable as partly appearance-driven, by design rather than by inference.

*The body.* A third state channel and a second clock.

*The genome.* One link of a mediation made experimental.

*Verification.* The channel paper 7 lacked.

*The two decisions the paper does not make.* Whether a person is ever shown a reading of their own appearance — the validity of what strangers read from their face, their apparent age, their presentation parameter — is a decision with a harm on one side and a value on the other, and both are real: the harm is what a number about one's own face does to a person, and the value is that the reading exists about them whether or not they see it, and a person who is read by others and not told is the failure the rest of this series refuses. The paper states both and decides neither. And the custody shape of the image — the most identifying datum a person can supply, the one that names them to a stranger in the street — from device-only with derived features leaving, through a stored standardized capture under the strictest custody the series has, to whatever an in-person protocol records: the smallest shape that still gets the value is the question, and it is the owner's.

*Ranked by what they make knowable.* First, the validity of perceived signals with the readers removed, because it is the measurement the subject has lacked since it began and the series is the instrument for it. Second, the treatment effect by design, because it is cheap, fast and identified. Third, paper 2's split and the two clocks, which need years. The genetics is a link, not a chain, and is ranked as one.

**6 · What would have to hold**

*Agreement among readers is reported as agreement among readers.* Every perceived-signal claim is scored against a criterion the readers never saw, publishes the validity ceiling of 3.1 beside the coefficient, and never reports a consensus as a property of the person.

*Demographic strata are the unit of every validity claim.* Perceived traits are confounded with age, sex, ancestry and every visible category, and a signal that is really a difference in how categories are treated will present as a difference in the trait. So validity is read within strata, the between-strata component is reported as a treatment finding, and the readers' own categories enter as terms, since a stereotype is a relation between a reader's kind and a face's kind.

*A face-to-self-report correlation is a treatment history until shown otherwise.* Per 3.2: only within-person timing or randomized exposure separates the two, and a between-person correlation is published as compatible with both.

*The image has a capture standard and the supplied image is read against it.* A neutral expression at a fixed distance under known lighting is the anchor; the supplied image is a presentation reading relative to it; and morphology is measured on the anchor only, with pose, lens and distance calibrated. Edited and generated images are detected as manipulation, the detector owes the alert rate and predictive value paper 6 demands of any detector, and the in-person channel is the ground it is checked against.

*Reader panels are blind, sized, and described.* Blind to every other axis; large enough that the consensus reliability of 3.1 is stated rather than assumed; with each reader's own categories recorded, because the reading is a relation.

*Exposure randomization is a two-sided budget with its own consent.* Withholding an image is a treatment on the person withheld, with paper 8's floor logic applied: a per-person budget for being the hidden side, the induced effect on the person's own experience reported, and consent to be shown to others separate from consent to be measured.

*The in-person channel runs under a protocol.* What is recorded, by whom, blind to what; the observer's reading entered as an instrument with its own bias term; the observer's presence as a reactivity term; and who the observers are, since they are readers too.

*Repeated captures are a reactivity estimand.* Being photographed on a schedule changes presentation, and possibly the body; paper 5's randomized-schedule arm carries forward.

*Consent is per purpose and the purposes are more separable here than anywhere.* Being measured, being read by strangers, being shown to others in the system's interactions, being observed in person, and being matched across accounts are five decisions; none implies another; and no image of a minor, and no third party in an image, is ever material.

*Custody is the strictest in the series*, in whichever of the shapes the owner chooses, because the image identifies its subject to anyone at all, and a breach of a face store is a breach in the street.

*The verification channel carries paper 7's conditions.* A presented image, a recording and a synthesized face are its attacks; an interactive challenge is the answer; the template is irrevocable; and it binds a body to a moment at the screen, not to the interval between moments.

*The rule against difference scores holds.* Every discrepancy — supplied against observed, perceived against measured — is a latent factor with its own reliability, per papers 1 and 2.

**7 · The requirements the design generates**

*A capture standard, and the supplied image beside it*, on every person, since a presentation parameter needs both.

*Captures repeated on a schedule*, for 4.5 and 4.6, with the schedule's reactivity arm.

*Reader panels of the size 3.1's arithmetic sets*, blind, with their own categories recorded, per trait and per stratum.

*Criterion measurements on the same people* — which is the whole series, and is the reason this axis is worth more here than anywhere else it could be built.

*Randomized exposure in the system's own pairwise interactions*, with the realised probability recorded and the two-sided budget kept.

*Demographic strata large enough to read validity within*, which is a recruitment requirement the axis states rather than a limit it accepts.

*An in-person subsample under protocol*, sized for the presentation parameter and for the manipulation check.

*A manipulation detector with its numbers, and the verification anchor under it.*

*Custody built before the first capture*, in the shape the owner chooses.

None is a reason to want less. Each is what a fully populated appearance axis looks like from the inside.

**8 · What would decide it**

*Does any perceived signal survive the criterion?* Standardized images of a cohort; blind panels at 3.1's size; consensus per trait; validity against the criterion measured on the same people, within strata, with the ceiling stated. The prior is written before the panels read: character traits near zero, apparent health and fatigue small and real, apparent age real. The study decides whether the perceived channel is a measurement of persons or of readers, per trait, and either answer reshapes the axis — a validity near zero for character does not close the axis, it settles what it is for.

*Does showing a face change treatment?* Randomize the image's visibility in the system's own pairwise interactions and read the intention-to-treat effect on follows, opponent choice and sealed predictions, with the hidden-side budget kept. This is cheap, fast, and identified, and it is the study to run first because it needs no panel and no criterion.

*And the calibration question that precedes both.* Whether a supplied image can be read against a capture standard at all — whether presentation and morphology separate in practice or whether the supplied image is too far from the anchor to place — decides whether 4.4 exists and how much of 4.1 rests on the anchor alone.

**9 · The potential, priced with each score's own condition**

A ten is a dataset after which a field's standard design changes; a five answers questions the field already had. Each score names its condition.

*The perception of persons.* Eight, conditional on the criterion study running at 3.1's panel sizes and on strata large enough to read within. The subject has lacked a criterion since it began, and a population carrying self-report, a keyed test, enacted behaviour and the body's channels is that criterion; the finding is worth this whether it is a validity or a zero.

*Treatment effects of appearance.* Seven, conditional on exposure randomization being permitted in the system's own interactions; three without it, since observation identifies nothing here.

*The body's state and two clocks.* Five, conditional on repeated captures and on paper 6's slope existing, rising with years.

*Verification.* Not priced on this scale; it is the channel paper 7 needed and its value is that paper's.

*The mediation.* Four, because one link is made experimental and the chain is not.

The value concentrates in the first two, and they are the cheapest.

**10 · Open questions the data would have to answer**

Whether any character trait is readable from a face at all once its readers are removed, which the criterion study settles per trait and on which the axis's meaning depends. Whether apparent health and fatigue are read validly enough to be a state channel, or only enough to be a stereotype about the tired. How large the treatment effect of a face is in a system's own interactions, and whether it differs by who is looking. Whether the presentation parameter is the same trait as the bank's response style and paper 8's discrepancy, or a third thing. Whether the face's clock and the metabolic clock agree, and if not, which one outcomes follow. Whether a supplied image can be placed against a capture standard closely enough for morphology to be read from it, or whether the anchor is the only measurement. How the feedback loop of 3.2 can be timed within a person, and over what horizon treatment precedes self-concept. And what a person is owed about a reading others have made of their face, which is the decision this paper hands to the owner and the one question here that no study answers.
