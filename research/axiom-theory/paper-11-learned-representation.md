# One geometry, learned

*Paper 11 of the axiom series: the representation of a person learned from every axis at once, the distilled layer that may stand in for it but is never read as its structure, the value of each axis measured per unit of attention by what its removal costs, and the loop in which the model both chooses the data it learns from and moves the phenomena it predicts.*

**Status: theory research.** Nothing here describes the app or proposes a change to it; `research/README.md` says what a paper is and how one reaches the product. Paper 0 supplies the joint model this paper conditionally exceeds and the distilled layer it keeps; paper 0a the policy the representation feeds, and the two evaluation streams — anchor-drawn and eligible-drawn — it is judged on; G3 the classical floor, the five-clause admission test and the claim types code length does not settle; G2 the version ledger, the custody and the differencing budget a released model lives under; G4 the value ledger this paper is the instrument for and the identification state that ledger cannot see; paper 9 the two-doors lesson every prediction here inherits; paper 12 the custody-preserving computation this paper's training is an instance of.

**Perfect-form test.** If the app did not exist, this would still be worth saying because a population measured by many sources has structure no single-source model expresses and no covariance model holds — structure conditional on the order and context of a person's own history, running across sources — and above a volume that must be measured rather than assumed, a model trained to predict each unit's next observation from its whole record captures that structure and cannot say what it captured; so the questions worth stating are what such a representation makes knowable that the readable models do not, what it is worth per unit of attention axis by axis, how its unreadability is made safe rather than tolerated, and what happens when the model that learns from the observations both chooses them and changes what it is trying to predict.

**Abstract.** Paper 0 built the joint model of a person as a covariance structure over every axis and said in one paragraph that above some volume a learned model would find structure that class cannot express, be unreadable, and be kept beside a distilled layer whose faithfulness is reported. This paper is that paragraph at full length, and it is careful about four things the paragraph left short. The learned representation is where the axes meet: a reading on any axis predicted from every other is what it computes, and that transfer is conditional on a person's history in a way covariance cannot express, which is the real contrast with the joint model rather than the false one that covariance is symmetric. Its transfer map's entry is defined as the fraction of a target axis's own code length that a source axis saves at a stated grain, so an axis is not credited merely for being long. It is the instrument on which the value of an axis is measured — the code-length saving on the eligible-drawn stream, per unit of attention the axis costs, marginal to the portfolio and scored on the same held-out target for every axis so the rows compare — which is the number G4's value ledger runs on, priced honestly as an exact enumeration over subsets with sampled orders as the fallback and retraining variance stated. It carries three cautions that are conditions, not remarks. Code length prices prediction, and G3 says it is nearly flat where structural couplings live, so the coupling column of the ledger is priced in posterior information and named as a different currency. Every prediction leans partly on the anchors, and ablating them bounds category use from below only, because the anchors are recoverable from the other axes, so the anchor share is published as an interval and never as a guarantee. And the loop is two hazards, not one: the model learns from data a policy chose, corrected by importance weighting toward the evaluation stream and the admission test rather than by feeding the propensity to a predictor that has no estimand; and the model's outputs, once they drive the pairings and displays that constitute the axes, move the phenomena it predicts, which is identified only by randomized withholding. The representation is a release that memorizes: trained under paper 12's custody, its version sequence differenced and budgeted as G2 requires, its leakage a lower bound against a named adversary and never a guarantee. Whether the volume has been reached at all, for which target, is the measurement that precedes every reading, and whether a per-person representation may be released at all is one of two decisions this paper puts to the owner rather than settling.

**1 · The problem, stated structurally**

Every axis in this series has a model, and paper 0 joined them: a covariance structure over every source, each axis a measurement model on a shared latent, a person one point in one geometry. That model is readable, its couplings are estimands with identification, and it is the floor everything else stands on.

It is a class, and a class has things it cannot say. A covariance structure expresses how quantities co-vary. It cannot express that what a person answered yesterday changes what they answer today in a way that depends on what was revealed between; that a body reading matters for a test on one day and not another; that the order of two events carries what the events do not; that a tie forming rewrites how an answer should be read after. Those are conditional on a person's own history, they run across axes, and paper 0 said in one paragraph that above some volume a model trained to predict each person's next event from their whole record learns them and cannot be read.

This paper is that paragraph made into an axis paper. Its subject is not whether such a model exists — G3 states that as a proposition with a measurement attached — but what it makes knowable across axes that the readable models do not, what it is worth per unit of attention, and how it is kept honest.

**2 · The three objects, and where each stops**

*The floor is readable and bounded by its class.* The strongest classical model the mechanism can fit — paper 0's joint model with every bias term G3 gives it — expresses covariance at every level with a time on each, and is auditable. Its ceiling is its class: whatever the population's structure has that is not covariance is invisible to it, and no data changes that.

*The teacher is unbounded by class and unreadable.* A model trained to predict each person's next observation from their whole record, with the fixed columns as context and reveals, occasion reports and tie changes as events, learns whatever predicts, conditional on history. Its internal state is a high-dimensional representation of a person whose coordinates mean nothing that can be stated. Its ceiling is that nothing in it is a claim: it has no couplings, no estimands, no identification, and it has learned the serving policy and the missingness as readily as the person.

*The distilled layer is a licence to substitute, never a reading.* A factor layer fitted to the teacher's representation in named dimensions, under a rotation convention fixed and versioned with it, with faithfulness reported as the expected log-ratio of the teacher's predictive distribution to the surrogate's under a distribution fixed in advance. G3 states what that number is: a bound on the extra code length substitution costs on that distribution, and therefore a licence to use the surrogate there for prediction. It is not a statement that the surrogate's factors are the teacher's structure — two models can agree on every prediction and disagree entirely about why — and no reading in this paper depends on such a statement. When a distilled factor enters the admission test of section 4, it enters as a predictive constraint proposed for testing, not as a structure asserted; that is the only sense in which the layer is read at all, and the paper's subtitle says substituted-for, not readable.

Each stops where the next begins: the floor says what is claimed, the teacher says what is predictable, the layer says where one may stand in for the other, and none is the joint model of a person alone.

**3 · Five claims**

*3.1 The representation is where the axes meet, and its transfer is conditional on history.* A reading on any axis predicted from every other is what the teacher computes. That is the crossing this series exists to draw, and the map of it is a matrix whose entry, from source axis to target axis, is the fraction of the target axis's own marginal code length that adding the source saves, at a stated observation grain so that a long axis is not credited for having more to predict. The map is not symmetric, and the reason is not the false one an earlier draft gave — that covariance is symmetric and the teacher is not, which is wrong twice, since mutual information is symmetric for any joint distribution including the teacher's, and a covariance model's conditional predictions are already asymmetric through differing marginal entropies. The real contrast is that the teacher's transfer is conditional on a person's history — the order and context of what came before — which is exactly what a covariance model cannot carry, so a map entry that moves with history is structure the floor cannot express. The map is measured on the eligible-drawn stream and it is the first thing this paper makes knowable that the joint model does not.

*3.2 The value of an axis is what its removal costs, per unit of attention, on one shared target.* G1 defines a source's value as its code-length saving on the evaluation stream, per attention unit it costs, marginal to the portfolio; the teacher makes that a computation, and an earlier draft dropped two of its four qualifiers. The saving is per unit of attention the axis costs, because an axis read from ten observations a day and one read from a single upload are not the same move and G4's decision rule compares moves by what they buy per unit spent. And the saving is scored on one target set fixed across all axes — the future observations of every axis but the ablated one, as G1 specifies — because scoring each ablation on a target set that includes the ablated axis would fold that axis's own unpredictability, which is its row in 3.1's map, into its value, double-counting the map beside it, and scoring each on a different target set would make the rows incomparable, which is the one thing a ledger may not be. The saving is measured on the eligible-drawn stream at its stated per-source rates, reweighted to a population quantity, not on the anchor-drawn stream that serves only the anchors.

*3.3 The currency is not one currency, and the coupling column is priced apart.* An earlier draft priced "per target — population couplings, person placement, within-person dynamics" in code length throughout, and G3, its cited authority, says the opposite: code length settles predictive claims and is nearly flat in the parameters structural couplings live in. So placement and within-person dynamics, which are predictive, are priced in code length; the population-coupling target is priced in posterior information about the coupling under the prior-free information set, a different object; and the ledger states the two are not interchangeable rather than summing them.

*3.4 The loop is two hazards.* The model learns from data a policy chose, and the model's outputs change what it predicts, and these are different.

Selection first. A missingness pattern generated by an information-seeking rule is structure a predictor will fit, and an earlier draft proposed to remove it by feeding the serving propensity to the teacher as a feature — which cannot work, because a predictor has no estimand for a person-versus-policy decomposition to be about, and the propensity as a feature is used exactly insofar as it predicts, including as a proxy for the state that drove selection. What the training does instead is import-weight the training stream toward the evaluation stream, so the teacher is fitted toward the distribution it will be judged on; the residual is stated as a bound, not assumed away, because an ablated model's training data was itself collected under a policy that used the ablated axis, so the ablation is not a clean counterfactual and its value carries that bias. And every structure the teacher proposes is a candidate under G3's five-clause admission test in full: the units whose residuals proposed it are excluded from the units that test it; it survives on the evaluation stream; it predicts a source other than the one its residual was computed in; it survives the serving propensity by weighting or matching rather than by entering it as a term; and it is not absorbed by a widened bias model. An earlier draft named five clauses, ran three, and required the propensity clause in the form G3 forbids.

Performativity second, and an earlier draft missed it entirely. The teacher feeds the policy and predicts who will be paired with whom and who will follow whom; once its outputs drive those pairings and displays, they change the ties, reveals and encounters that constitute the axes it predicts. That is paper 9's treatment door applied to the model's own output, and no selection correction touches it, because the phenomenon has moved rather than the sample. Its size is identified only by randomized withholding of teacher-driven pairing, at the choice-set level, as paper 9 identifies a display effect.

*3.5 The representation is a release, and it memorizes.* A learned representation of a person is a compact identifying object, and a trained model carries its training data in recoverable form to a degree that is measured against a named adversary and is a lower bound, not a determination. Three things follow from G2. The model is trained under paper 12's custody, as a multiparty computation whose rounds, participation and staleness that paper states and this one does not re-derive. Every version of the model is a release, and because the loop retrains continuously, the sequence of versions over a growing cohort differences like any release sequence and is budgeted as one — which is the paper's largest release surface. And a per-person representation handed to anything is a release of that person, whose mechanism and parameter must be named before any cost can be composed, and whose membership and reconstruction rates are lower bounds against a stated adversary class and access model, never the guarantee a conditions list will read them as.

**4 · What becomes knowable**

*4.1 The transfer map.* For every ordered pair of axes, the normalized, grain-stated, history-conditional entry of 3.1 on the eligible-drawn stream, with the anchors' contribution held per 4.5. It says where the series' couplings are strong enough to draw and where they are noise, and a nearly empty row is one of three things — an axis nothing predicts, an axis that is highly unreliable, or an axis that carries little about anything — which 4.2 and 4.8 tell apart. It is a predictive map and the word upstream is not used of it.

*4.2 The value ledger.* Per axis, per target, the code-length saving per unit of attention on the eligible-drawn stream, marginal to the portfolio, on the shared target of 3.2, with the coupling column priced apart per 3.3. This is the number G4's programme runs on. The exact quantity averaged over orders needs one fit per subset — for a dozen axes, every subset, which is the honest requirement; sampled orders are the fallback, they save less than an order of magnitude and carry a permutation-sampling error, and each fit carries retraining variance from seed and ordering that on a thin stream can exceed a single axis's marginal contribution, so the ledger states both error sources and the seeds per subset needed to resolve its smallest entry. The averaged-over-orders reading splits credit symmetrically between two redundant axes, so it is used for fair attribution and never for retirement, which takes the marginal reading — the same split G4 draws.

*4.3 The placement curve.* Paper 0's currency — observations to place a newcomer — priced here in the teacher's own terms: the expected code length of a person's next observations after their first n, for the floor and for the teacher, on held-out newcomers. An earlier draft claimed the teacher places a newcomer further from the same few observations; paper 0 says the opposite at the person level, that partial pooling is worth more than the next item and the teacher's edge is sequential structure a three-observation person barely has, so the gap is narrowest exactly at small n and the paper states that rather than selling the reverse. The curve is where the gap opens as n grows, and that is the honest reading of what the learned model is worth to a person over time.

*4.4 Structure beyond the class, admitted or not.* Sequential and contextual structure the floor cannot express appears as the teacher's code-length gain over the floor and as clusters in the floor's residuals. Each is a candidate and becomes a finding only under the five-clause test of 3.4 in full. A gain over the floor that fails any clause is a gain the loop made, and the test's failure rate is published.

*4.5 How much of a prediction is the person, and how much their categories — as an interval.* A teacher predicts from the anchors because they predict, and a prediction leaning on them is paper 9's stereotype learned by a machine. Ablating the anchor inputs gives the lower bound on category use only, because the anchors are recoverable from the other axes and a teacher deprived of them reconstructs them and predicts through the proxies — paper 9's two-doors lesson, that blindness of the instrument does not make the input exogenous. So the anchor share is published as an interval: the lower bound from ablating the anchor inputs, and an upper bound from a model given only the reconstructed anchors, or from the anchor-predictability of the representation itself. The interval is the answer, per cohort and per axis, and a single-prediction difference of two models' log-losses is not a share, being sign-indefinite and unbounded on one outcome, so it is never published per prediction.

*4.6 What a new axis does, and how absence is carried.* When an axis is added the retrained teacher is a different model with a different geometry, and persons under the old one are not placed under the new one. Absence of an axis is a pattern in the input a predictor will and should condition on, so there is no representational state that is absent rather than valued unless the architecture supplies one: a per-axis presence indicator with a stated invariance requirement, or a masked objective, and the paper specifies that rather than wishing it. Conditioning on absence gives axis-lacking people a systematically different representation, which is the missingness hazard of 3.4 and is tested as one. Whether a person placed before an addition survives it is a predictive-equivalence test across retrainings, not a question about coordinates, which the teacher does not have.

*4.7 Shift, detected by two calibrations rather than one gap.* A learned model's failure under shift — a new cohort, a changed policy — an earlier draft proposed to read from the teacher-minus-floor gap closing, on the assumption the floor's smaller class moves less. That conflates estimation variance with bias under shift, which for a misspecified small class can move arbitrarily, since the floor's bias terms were fitted to the old cohort. So the detector is each model calibrated against its own training cohort, reported as two numbers rather than their difference, and a reading on a cohort where either calibration has broken is not trusted.

*4.8 The volume, measured.* Whether the learned model earns its place is the teacher's code-length advantage over the floor on the eligible-drawn stream, per target, as a function of population size and observations per person, fitted from subsamples and stated as an extrapolation beyond the sizes that exist rather than a reading at them. Where the advantage is not positive at the actual size, the teacher is not run for that target, and everything in this section that depends on it waits.

**5 · The return path, one worked crossing, and the ranking**

*Every axis* gains its row in the transfer map and its line in the value ledger, a number no axis paper computes for itself.

*One crossing worked, so the map can be falsified.* Take the body and the bank. The floor can say the two co-vary; it cannot say that a body reading on the morning of an answer predicts that answer while the same reading a week stale does not. The teacher's map entry from body to a bank item, computed at the day grain and again at the week grain, is a number that differs if and only if the coupling is contemporaneous, and a population where the two entries are equal falsifies the claim that the body's predictive reach into the bank is a within-day thing. That is a specific prediction the map makes about a specific pair, and it is the shape every other cell of the map is read in.

*The genome* gains a phenotype only in a restricted sense an earlier draft overstated: a prediction computed from a person's own record is a deterministic function of its inputs and adds no measurement, and a prediction leaning on the anchors recovers population structure, which is paper 3's stratification confound. So the derived-trait claim is restricted to predictions of held-out observations of a named axis, computed from inputs excluding the anchors, under a frozen model version stated with the result — and nothing looser is a phenotype.

*The design layer* takes the teacher as the policy's input, kept honest by the floor, the two streams, the admission test and the randomized withholding of 3.4.

*The programme* runs G4's two levers on 4.2's ledger and 4.7's detector; this paper is the instrument G4 describes.

*Ranked by what they make knowable.* First, the value ledger and the transfer map, the numbers the series has needed and no readable model supplies. Second, the anchor-share interval, because without it every teacher reading is a category until shown otherwise. Third, the placement and volume curves, which say what the learned model is worth and when. Structure beyond the class is last, because most of what the loop proposes fails the test, and the paper prices it that way.

**6 · What would have to hold**

*The floor runs permanently and the teacher keeps its place only by beating it*, per target, on the evaluation stream, continuously, by code length and never a metric the teacher's builders defined.

*Two evaluation streams, named apart* — anchor-drawn and eligible-drawn — with axis values priced on the eligible-drawn one at its per-source rates, reweighted to the population, never on a single stream called uniform.

*The training stream is import-weighted toward the evaluation stream*, and the ablation counterfactual's residual bias is stated as a bound, because feeding the propensity to a predictor does not decompose it.

*Faithfulness is an expected log-ratio under distributions named in advance*, licensing substitution for prediction there and nothing else; no distilled factor is read as the teacher's structure, and one entering the admission test enters as a predictive constraint under a versioned rotation convention.

*Every prediction carries its anchor-share interval*, lower and upper, per cohort and per axis, never a per-prediction difference.

*Every proposed structure passes the five-clause admission test in full or is a candidate*, propensity handled by weighting or matching, with the failure rate published.

*The value of an axis is marginal, per unit of attention, on the shared target, on the eligible stream*, with the exact subset enumeration as the requirement, sampled orders as the fallback, and both the permutation and retraining error stated.

*Performativity is identified, not assumed absent*, by randomized withholding of teacher-driven pairing.

*The representation is trained under paper 12's custody and released under G2's budget* — the version sequence differenced and budgeted, a per-person representation released only by a named mechanism with a parameter, leakage a lower bound against a stated adversary.

*Shift is detected by two calibrations*, each model against its own training cohort, and a reading on a broken cohort is withheld.

*Absence is a specified representational state*, a presence indicator or a masked objective, tested for the missingness hazard.

*The volume is measured per target*, and the teacher runs only where its advantage is positive at the actual size.

**7 · The requirements the design generates**

*An eligible-drawn evaluation stream sized to resolve the smallest ledger entry the paper wants readable* — which for a per-axis, per-target, per-cohort ablation finer than retraining noise is a standing charge on the one-observation-a-day budget, and its share is derived from that smallest entry rather than chosen freely; at a dozen axes and a demand to resolve an axis worth a few percent of a target's code length against seed noise, it is a multi-percent share of every person's daily observation for the life of the programme.

*Retraining capacity for the ledger* — exact subset enumeration where affordable, sampled orders with a stated seed count per subset otherwise — on a cadence given a number rather than called recurring.

*A held-out newcomer set* for the placement curve, never trained on.

*An anchor-free teacher* trained alongside the full one, for 4.5's lower bound, and an anchors-only model for its upper.

*Distributions named in advance for faithfulness*, including a shifted cohort.

*Paper 12's multiparty training protocol* with its trust assumptions and leakage, since the teacher over every custodian's columns is the largest computation the system runs.

*Membership and reconstruction measured per version against a stated adversary*, published as lower bounds beside the version's row.

*A randomized-withholding arm* for performativity.

*The volume curve run before the teacher is relied on*, per target, with its extrapolation stated.

None is a reason to want less. Each is what a representation learned from a fully populated system looks like from the inside.

**8 · What would decide it**

*Has the volume been reached, for what?* The teacher against the floor on the eligible-drawn stream at the actual size, per target, with the curve against size fitted from subsamples and the population-size extrapolation stated as one. Where the advantage is not positive the teacher does not run for that target. This precedes everything.

*Is the transfer map anything, and is the empty-map reading powered?* The map's history-conditional entries against an anchors-only predictor — the honest form of the null, since a conditional-independence-given-anchors claim has no assumption-free test — and an empty map read as "axes coupled only through categories" only under a power calculation from the volume curve, since an empty map is equally the signature of an underpowered learner on a thin stream.

*Does the value ledger resolve its smallest entry against retraining noise?* The order-averaged value with its permutation error and its seed-to-seed variance separated, at a stated number of orders and seeds; a ledger whose smallest entries are inside the noise is one whose stream is too thin, which is a statement about the stream and not about the axes' path dependence.

**9 · The potential, priced with each score's own condition**

A ten is a dataset after which a field's standard design changes; a five answers questions the field already had. Every score is conditional on the volume having been reached for the target it concerns.

*The value ledger and the transfer map.* Eight, conditional on the volume and on the ledger resolving its entries against noise, because a value per source relative to a portfolio, per unit of attention, on a stream the policy did not choose, is what G4's method needs and what a science of persons has lacked. Four where the stream cannot resolve the small entries, since the large ones still order the axes.

*The anchor-share interval.* Seven, conditional only on the volume, because it is what separates a learned reading of a person from a learned reading of their kind, and an interval that is honestly wide is still the finding.

*The placement curve.* Six, conditional on the held-out newcomers, because it is what a system should quote when it says a representation helps.

*Structure beyond the class.* Five where the admission test passes anything, and a finding about the loop where it does not, since a loop that admits nothing has measured its own selection.

*Custody-preserving training and per-person release.* Not priced here; they are paper 12's, and the release is a decision put to the owner in section 5's terms below.

The value concentrates in the first two, both conditional on a volume the fully populated system is assumed to exceed and this paper insists on measuring.

*The two decisions this paper puts to the owner.* Whether a per-person representation may be released at all, and to whom, stated as three shapes with what each exposes and what each kills: no per-person representation leaves, and only population-level ledger and map are published, which keeps every reading in section 4 and forgoes any per-person product; a representation released to the person it describes and to nothing else, which the version-differencing budget of 3.5 must price before it is allowed; a representation released to other axes' computations under the budget, which is what 5's genome crossing needs and is the most identifying release the series has proposed. The paper states the three and decides none.

**10 · Open questions the data would have to answer**

Whether the volume has been reached for any target at all, which precedes everything and is assumed rather than known. Whether the transfer map survives against the anchors-only null at adequate power. Whether the value ledger resolves its small entries against retraining noise, and how many seeds and orders that takes. How wide the anchor-share interval is for a typical prediction, and whether the upper bound can be driven down or only reported. Whether performativity, once identified by withholding, is small enough to leave the map meaning what it says. What a per-person representation leaks at population scale against the strongest adversary rather than the one that was run. Whether the version sequence's differencing budget can afford continuous retraining at all. And whether the loop can run for years without the admission test's failure rate rising, which decides whether a system that chooses its own observations and moves its own phenomena learns or confirms itself.
