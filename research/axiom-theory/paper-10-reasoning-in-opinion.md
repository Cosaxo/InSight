# Reasoning in the clothes of opinion

*Paper 10 of the axiom series: the key that lives in the relation between two opinions rather than in either, the bank as a reasoning instrument with no test situation, and what separates once the same ability is measured twice — once as a test and once as a life.*

**Status: theory research.** Nothing here describes the app or proposes a change to it; `research/README.md` says what a paper is and how one reaches the product. Paper 1 supplies the keyed test and the residual discrimination this paper extends past; paper 0a the serving policy, the repetition schedule and the floor it allocates under; paper 5 the body covariate and the state timescales; paper 2 the reveal as a randomizable event; paper 4 the declared positions a stake is read from; paper 8 the cancellation it must check its own comparisons against.

**Perfect-form test.** If the app did not exist, this would still be worth saying because every measurement of reasoning ever taken has been taken as a test — with effort summoned, a score expected, and the situation itself part of what was measured — while the reasoning that matters is done in the ordinary run of holding opinions, and a bank of opinions with no right answer to any item can still carry a right answer to the relation between two of them, which is a key nobody has to be told about and a measurement nobody has to sit for.

**Abstract.** A keyed reasoning test measures ability in a situation of its own making: the person knows they are being scored, summons what effort they will, and answers under whatever a test does to them. Paper 1 crossed that score with an unkeyed opinion bank and found the bank could learn which of its items tilt with ability, while stating plainly that an ability tilt does not give an opinion a right answer. This paper takes the next step, which is that the key can live in the *relation* between two items instead of in either. No opinion has a right answer; a pair can have a right relation — one entails the other, the two exclude each other, a conjunction cannot be more probable than either conjunct, the same proposition in two frames should draw one answer — and a person's two answers can be scored against the relation without either item being a question anyone could fail. So an unbounded bank of opinions is, at depth, a reasoning instrument with no test situation, and the same ability is measured twice: as a test and as a life. What that pair makes knowable follows from the pairing. Whether tested and wild reasoning are one construct, which decides whether the bank is a free, continuous, unobtrusive test or a different instrument; the test-situation effect per person, as a latent and never a difference; motivated reasoning as a person-by-topic phenotype, read where incoherence concentrates in the topics a person has a declared stake in, with ability and the relation's difficulty held; framing susceptibility by design, from the same proposition served in two frames at a lag beyond recall; social influence on reasoning, identified because a reveal of the population's answer can be randomized; the state-dependence of reasoning, from the body's channels on the same days; and the bank's items classified by their relations rather than by declaration. The conditions are severe and specific. A relation is a key only if it survives the strongest reinterpretation of both items, so every relation carries a written defence and a contested one is an opinion pair; the lag between the two items must exceed recall and fall short of opinion change, and that window can be empty; the second item must never be served because of the first answer; the reveal contaminates the relation unless it is withheld or timed; and disguising reasoning as opinion is a cover story whose consent shape is a decision the paper states and the owner makes.

**1 · The problem, stated structurally**

Reasoning is measured as a test. A person sits, knows they are scored, summons effort, and the score carries the situation: what a test does to that person on that day, how much they cared, whether they were tired, and the knowledge that a right answer exists and someone will check. That is one construct, and paper 1 measured it well. It is not the construct that decides what a person believes.

The reasoning that decides beliefs is done without a test in sight: holding two opinions that cannot both be true, revising one position when another is stated, treating a proposition differently depending on how it is put. Nobody scores it, so nobody measures it, and the assumption everywhere is that the tested construct stands in for it. Whether it does is an empirical question that has never had the instrument to be asked, because the wild construct has no test to be measured by.

It does not need one. A single opinion has no right answer, but two opinions can stand in a relation that has one, and a person who answers both — days apart, as opinions, with no situation — has been scored on the relation without being tested on either item. That is the instrument: an unbounded bank of opinions in which the key lives between the items and not in them.

**2 · The two axes alone, and where each stops**

*The keyed test alone measures a situation as well as an ability.* It is reliable, repeatable and language-light, and its score is a real quantity — paper 1's whole argument rests on it and this paper does not withdraw a word of that. What it cannot do is separate the ability from the sitting. Effort on an unproctored test, the day's state, the knowledge of being scored: each is inside the number, paper 1 names them as conditions, and no repetition of the same kind of measurement takes them out. Its ceiling is the situation.

*The bank alone has no key and, at depth, more structure than any key.* Paper 1 gave each item a residual discrimination — how sharply its split moves with ability once the profile facts and shared dispositions are conditioned out — and that map says which opinions are partly reasoning. It says nothing about whether any given answer was reasoned, because a single item cannot. What the bank has, at unbounded depth, is *pairs*: items that entail each other, exclude each other, bound each other, or restate each other, and a pair's relation is a key that does not depend on anyone agreeing what the right opinion is. The bank's ceiling alone is that nothing scores the relations; its answers are a record of positions and not of the reasoning between them.

*Each axis's ceiling is the other.* The test gives the wild instrument a criterion for what it is measuring; the wild instrument gives the test the one thing it cannot supply for itself, a second measurement of the same ability taken with no situation attached.

**3 · The relation as the key, and five facts that follow**

*3.1 A relation is a key, and only a defended one.* Four kinds. Entailment: an answer to one item commits the person to an answer on another. Exclusion: two positions cannot both be held. Bound: a conjunction cannot be more probable than either of its conjuncts, and a whole cannot be smaller than its part. Equivalence: one proposition in two framings should draw one answer. Each is scored on a pair, and a person is not wrong on either item — they are incoherent on the relation. What makes a relation a key is that it survives the strongest reinterpretation of both items. Most apparent relations do not: an "and" can be read as a sequence, a probability as a frequency or as a degree of belief, a framing as carrying information the other frame does not, and under that reading two answers that look incoherent are coherent about a different question. So every relation in the bank carries a written defence against its best reinterpretation, and a relation whose defence fails is an opinion pair and is read as one. This is the condition that separates the instrument from the oldest way of manufacturing errors, which is to score a person against a reading of the question they did not share.

*3.2 The lag has two bounds, and the window between them can be empty.* If the second item is answered with the first answer in memory, coherence is recall and not reasoning, so the lag must exceed the item's recall horizon — which is measurable, as the learning curve paper 0a already measures for any repeated item. If the second item is answered after the person's actual position has moved, incoherence is change and not reasoning, so the lag must fall short of the position's own timescale — which paper 5 measures for state items and this paper must measure for opinions. Between the two bounds is the window in which a relation reads reasoning. For a slow opinion and a forgettable item the window is wide; for a salient item on a fast-moving position it can be empty, and a relation with an empty window is not an instrument on that person.

*3.3 The second item is never served because of the first answer.* A policy that serves the entailed item to people who answered a certain way on the first has selected the pair on the answer, and every coherence rate afterwards is a function of the policy. So a relation's two items are scheduled as a pair in advance, under paper 0a's floor, with the realised probability recorded — and the pair's scheduling competes with every other allocation the policy makes, which is a cost the design prices rather than hides.

*3.4 A reveal is a treatment on the relation.* A system that shows a person how the population answered, after their own answer, has intervened between the two items of every relation: the second answer is now made with the population's position on the first in view. That contaminates the coherence reading and identifies something else — social influence on reasoning — and paper 2 already treats the reveal as a randomizable event of paper 0's first class. So the reveal is withheld on the first item of a relation, or timed after the second, or randomized between the two, and which of those was done is recorded with every relation.

*3.5 The cover is a cover, and its consent is a decision.* A reasoning relation in the clothes of two opinions is unobtrusive precisely because the person does not know the relation is there, and telling them per item would put the situation back. The consent shape that keeps the measurement is general disclosure — that some items relate to others in ways that have a right answer, and that which ones will not be said — against the alternative of per-item disclosure, which is honest and destroys the instrument. That is a fork with a cost on each side and it is the owner's; the paper states it and decides nothing.

**4 · What becomes knowable**

*4.1 Whether tested and wild reasoning are one construct.* A person's coherence rate across relations, net of the relations' difficulty, is the wild measurement; paper 1's keyed score is the tested one; and their disattenuated correlation on the same people is the finding. Near one, and the bank is a free, continuous, unobtrusive test that measures what the sitting measures, without the sitting. Well below one, and either the situation is a large part of the tested score or wild reasoning is a different construct, and 4.2 and 4.7 say which. The relation's difficulty is estimated from the population as an item parameter, exactly as a test item's is, so the wild instrument has a measurement model of the ordinary kind and its reliability per person is a number — from n relations at an incoherence rate p, a standard error near the square root of p(1−p)/n, which at one relation in five and fifty relations is under six hundredths, and that is the count section 7 asks for.

*4.2 The test-situation effect, per person, as a latent.* What a test adds to or subtracts from a person's reasoning is the gap between the tested and the wild measurement — read as a latent factor with its own reliability inside a bivariate model, never as a raw difference between two unlinked scales, per the rule papers 1 and 2 state and paper 8 relearned. Its sign per person is not read; its variance across people is, and where it is large the tested score has been carrying a person parameter that is not ability.

*4.3 Motivated reasoning, as a person-by-topic phenotype.* Incoherence is not uniform across a person's beliefs. Where it concentrates in the topics a person has a stake in — a declared position, a group membership from paper 4's anchors, a preference from the interests axis — and does not in the topics they are indifferent to, with ability and the relations' difficulty held, the concentration is a reading of motivated reasoning: reasoning that bends where the conclusion matters. Its identification needs relations matched on difficulty across topics, since a hard relation in a charged topic is confounded with the charge; the stake measured rather than assumed; and the reading published as a person-by-topic variance component beside the person's overall rate, because a person who is incoherent everywhere is not motivated, they are inconsistent.

*4.4 Framing susceptibility, by design.* The same proposition in two frames, served at a lag inside 3.2's window, draws one answer from a person who is reading the proposition and two from a person who is reading the frame. The difference per pair is a framing effect; across pairs it is a person parameter with a reliability set by the pair count; and it is crossed at once with the keyed score, to ask whether susceptibility is low ability or something orthogonal to it, and with the body, to ask whether it moves within a person.

*4.5 Social influence on reasoning, by randomized reveal.* Under 3.4's randomization, the effect of seeing the population's answer to the first item on the coherence of the second is identified by design — and it is a reading of a specific thing: whether a person made coherent by the crowd is reasoning better or conforming, which the direction of the population's own answer on the relation decides. Where the crowd is incoherent on a relation, a person who follows it becomes less coherent, and the two effects are separable because the crowd's position varies across relations.

*4.6 The state-dependence of reasoning.* Relations answered on days paper 5's reports and paper 6's stream mark as bad, against the same person's good days, give the within-person coupling of reasoning to the body — a reading no test gives, because a test is sat on one day. It carries paper 5's conditions unchanged: the state share is identified only with the within-window replicate, the bad-day gap is a sensitivity band, and the coupling is an asymmetry and never a cause.

*4.7 The bank classified by its relations.* Paper 1 classified items by their ability tilt. This adds a second classification that does not depend on the keyed score at all: an item's loading on the coherence structure — how many relations it enters, how often those relations are violated in the population, and whether its violations track ability, stake or state. An item with many relations and violations that track ability is a reasoning item wearing an opinion's clothes; one whose violations track stake is a charged item; one with no relations is taste. The classification is estimated, revised as relations are added, and never declared.

*4.8 The return to the genome.* Wild coherence, the test-situation latent, motivated-reasoning concentration and framing susceptibility are four phenotypes that do not exist at population scale, and under paper 3's conditions each has a genetics — with the reliability attained stated per group, as paper 5 requires, since every one of them rises in precision with the number of relations a person has answered, and that number is engagement.

**5 · The return path, and the ranking**

*The logic test.* It gains what no repetition of itself can give: a second measurement of ability with the situation removed, and with it the test-situation effect as a person parameter it can be read net of.

*The bank.* It gains a key without any item having one, a second classification of its items that needs no external score, and a reason to be designed rather than only collected — relations are built in, at depth, deliberately.

*The instruments and the lenses.* Each trait scale that describes how a person thinks gains a behavioural counterpart in how that person's opinions cohere, and the discrepancy is read as a factor.

*The declared difference.* Paper 4's readings gain a reasoning-in-the-wild channel beside the tested one, so a group difference on the keyed score can be read against the same group's wild coherence — and a gap that exists on the test and not in the wild is a finding about the situation, not the group.

*The body.* One more within-person reading, on the covariate paper 5 built.

*Observers.* Paper 2's raters can be asked to predict a person's coherence, which is a behavioural criterion for distinctive accuracy that no self-report supplies.

*Arranged competitions.* Paper 8's stakes can be attached to a relation — answer the second item for something — and the stakes slope on coherence is then paper 8's parameter on a reasoning outcome, under that paper's cancellation and its two arms.

*The design layer.* Relations are scheduled pairs with a lag window, the reveal is a timed or randomized event, and both are allocations paper 0a's policy prices against everything else it wants to serve.

*Ranked by what they make knowable.* First, whether tested and wild reasoning are one construct, because everything else here and much of paper 1 reads differently under each answer. Second, motivated reasoning as a person-by-topic phenotype, because it is the reading nothing else in the series can make and the one the bank's charged topics have been waiting for. Third, framing and social influence by design, which need only the schedule and the randomization the design already runs. The state-dependence and the genome are crossings of what the first three produce.

**6 · What would have to hold**

*Every relation carries a written defence, and a contested relation is an opinion pair.* The defence states the strongest reinterpretation of each item under which the two answers would be coherent, and why that reinterpretation is not available; where it is available, the pair is not a key. Bound relations are the hardest to defend, because a probability is read in more than one way; equivalence relations are the easiest, because a frame that carries no information is a frame; entailment and exclusion sit between, and depend on the items being read as the same proposition by everyone, which is a cross-cultural condition and is tested as one.

*The lag window is measured per relation and per person, and an empty window is recorded as one.* The recall horizon is the item's learning curve; the position's timescale is estimated as paper 5 estimates a state item's; and a relation served outside the window is not scored.

*The pair is scheduled, never triggered.* Under paper 0a's floor, with the realised probability recorded, and never conditioned on the first answer.

*The reveal is withheld, timed or randomized, and which is recorded.* A relation whose first item was revealed before its second was answered reads social influence and not coherence, and is filed under 4.5.

*The pair is not recognisable as a pair.* Two items that visibly belong together are answered together in the person's mind whatever the lag, so the cover is a design constraint on the items themselves, and a relation whose items are recognised as related — measured by asking — is served no more.

*Difficulty is matched across topics before motivation is read*, and the stake is measured, per 4.3.

*Skipping is data.* A person who declines a hard-looking item has answered something, and a coherence rate over answered relations alone is selected on the very thing it measures, so the skip propensity is modelled per relation beside the answer, and paper 5's band applies.

*Repetition teaches.* A person who has met many relations becomes more coherent by exposure, so the coherence rate is a trajectory and paper 1's separation of practice from growth applies — first exposure staggered by design, the gain decorrelated rather than subtracted.

*Comparisons are checked against paper 8's cancellation.* Wherever a reading is a difference between two people's rates rather than a rate, whatever is common to both cancels, and a reading of the level needs a rate.

*The consent shape is the owner's decision, stated as a fork.* General disclosure that some items relate to others with a right answer, against per-item disclosure; the first keeps the instrument, the second is more honest per item and destroys it; and the paper does not choose.

*Paper 1's conditions on the keyed score apply unchanged*, since half of every reading here is that score.

**7 · The requirements the design generates**

*Relations designed into the bank at depth*, hundreds per topic, each with its defence, because the instrument is the relations and a bank that was only collected has none.

*Fifty relations per person for a person-level rate*, and fifty per topic per person for 4.3 — which, at two items per relation and one item a day, is a hundred days per topic and years for a topic map, and is the reason this is a slow instrument that must be allocated deliberately.

*Twenty to thirty framing pairs per person* for a susceptibility parameter of usable reliability.

*The keyed test on the same people*, under paper 1's conditions, since 4.1 and 4.2 are pairings.

*Lag windows measured before scoring*, per relation, with the empty ones recorded.

*A randomized reveal on relations*, with the assignment recorded.

*Relations matched on difficulty across topics*, and the stake measured from declared positions.

*Within-window replicates on a designed share of relations*, since 4.6 inherits paper 5's identification requirement.

*Staggered first exposure to relations*, so practice is decorrelated from tenure.

None is a reason to want less. Each is what a bank built to carry its own key looks like from the inside.

**8 · What would decide it**

*Is wild reasoning the tested construct?* A cohort with the keyed score and fifty defended relations each, served inside their windows with the reveal withheld; the disattenuated correlation between the coherence rate and the score, with the bivariate model of 4.2 fitted beside it. A pre-specified equivalence bound decides "one construct"; below it, the test-situation latent's variance says how much of the gap is the sitting. Nothing about this needs the topic map, the genome or the body.

*Does incoherence concentrate where the stake is?* Relations matched on difficulty across at least three topics, one of them charged for each person by their declared positions; the person-by-topic variance component of incoherence net of ability and difficulty, against an equivalence bound. If the component is null, motivated reasoning is not a trait at this grain and 4.3 closes; if it is substantial, its correlation with the declared stake is the finding.

*And the defence question, which precedes both.* Whether defended relations exist in enough number — whether, once the strongest reinterpretation is written for each candidate, enough survive to build the instrument. This is a question about the bank's content and it is answerable by writing the defences.

**9 · The potential, priced with each score's own condition**

A ten is a dataset after which a field's standard design changes; a five answers questions the field already had. Each score names its condition, and every one is conditional on the defence question of section 8.

*The measurement of reasoning outside the test situation.* Eight, conditional on the first study returning either answer at the stated equivalence bound. If wild and tested reasoning are one construct, every population that answers opinions has a reasoning measure it did not know it was taking; if they are two, the test-situation effect becomes a measured person parameter. Either changes the standard design.

*Motivated reasoning as a phenotype.* Seven, conditional on the second study's variance component surviving its bound; three if it does not, since the topic map still classifies items.

*Framing and social influence by design.* Six, conditional on the lag windows being non-empty for enough pairs and on the reveal being randomizable.

*The bank as a self-classifying corpus.* Five: a structural result about any opinion bank, free once the relations exist.

*The state-dependence of reasoning and its genetics.* Four, conditional on the crossings above and on the counts of section 7, rising with years.

The value concentrates in the first two, and both are decided by studies that need the bank and the test and nothing else.

**10 · Open questions the data would have to answer**

Whether enough relations survive their own defence to build the instrument at all, which precedes everything. Whether tested and wild reasoning are one construct, and if not, how much of a test score is the sitting. Whether incoherence concentrates where the stake is once difficulty is matched, or whether motivated reasoning dissolves into difficulty and charge. How wide the lag windows are for the relations that matter, and for how many people they are empty. Whether framing susceptibility is ability, a trait orthogonal to it, or a state. Whether a crowd's revealed answer makes a person more coherent or more conforming, and whether that depends on whether the crowd was coherent. How much of a coherence rate is practice, and whether the wild instrument teaches itself faster than the test does. And whether people who are told that some items relate answer differently from people who are not — which is the cost of the honest half of the consent fork, and is measurable before the owner chooses.
