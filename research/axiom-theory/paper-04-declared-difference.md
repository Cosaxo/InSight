# The shape of a declared difference

*Paper 4 of the axiom series: a self-declared neurotype read across every axis at once, including how the person is seen and how they see others.*

**Status: theory research.** Nothing here describes the app or proposes a change to it; `research/README.md` says what a paper is and how one reaches the product. Paper 0 carries the general argument; paper 1 supplies the keyed-ability readings, paper 2 the second-person readings, and paper 3 the item-level genetics this paper composes.

**Perfect-form test.** If the app did not exist, this would still be worth saying because what is known about attention-deficit, autism and their kin is known one instrument at a time, in clinics, on people who reached a clinic, and any population that carries a declaration beside thirty measurements of the person, including how they are seen by people who know them, would read the condition as a shape rather than as a score.

**Abstract.** A condition such as attention-deficit or autism is studied through a diagnosis and a handful of instruments built for it, on people who reached a clinic, compared with people who did not. The comparison is one trait at a time, the instruments were written for the condition and so find it, and how the person is seen by the people who know them is not measured at all. This paper describes a population in which a person may declare such a condition beside everything else the system measures: self-report trait scales, an unbounded item bank, a keyed reasoning test, declared interests, occasion reports of the body, day-to-day variability, and a dyadic record of how they are read by others and how they read others. It argues that the declaration crossed with all of that yields a signature: the joint shape of a declared group across every axis, not a list of single-trait differences, and it names the readings that no clinic and no single-instrument study can produce. Whether the difference in reading other people lies in reading people in general or in reading the specific people one knows, and whether observers who share the declaration read declared targets better. Whether legibility, how readable a person is to those who know them, differs, which is a property of the target no self-report reaches. Whether the within-person variability that a condition is said to carry is visible in a person's own answers across days and how it couples to sleep. Which of an unbounded bank's items separate the declared from the undeclared, on items nobody wrote for the purpose. And how the genetic architecture of the declaration overlaps each trait axis, and whether the signature is continuous into the undeclared population. It treats the declaration as a measurement with its own bias model, states the boundary that keeps a population description from becoming an individual screen, and leaves one design question open for the owner: the custody of a health-adjacent declaration in a system whose answers are otherwise public.

**1 · The problem, stated structurally**

A condition of the mind is studied through its diagnosis. Someone reaches a clinic, an instrument built for the condition is administered, a threshold is crossed, and the person joins a group that is then compared with people who did not. Three things follow from that shape, none of them anyone's fault.

The people studied are the people who reached a clinic, which selects on access, on age, on country, on gender, on whether the condition was visible enough to the right adult at the right time. What is described is the diagnosed, not the condition.

The instruments were built for the condition, so they measure what the condition was already believed to be. A trait the instrument does not ask about is not part of the description, and the description cannot surprise its authors.

And the comparison is one trait at a time. A group scores higher here and lower there, and the shape of a person who carries the condition, how the traits sit together in them, how they vary from day to day, how the people around them experience them, is not what the design measures. In particular, nothing in the design measures the person from outside: how legible they are to the people who know them, and how well they read those people, are second-person facts that a clinic does not have and a questionnaire cannot ask.

**2 · The two axes alone, and where each stops**

The declaration is one item: whether a person says they have the condition, and at what level of certainty, diagnosed, suspected or neither. Alone it is a label with a base rate. It cannot say what it is a label for, and it carries the biases of who declares, which are the biases of who was diagnosed plus the biases of who says so. Its ceiling is content.

The other axes, taken together, measure the person from many sides and cannot say which of what they measure hangs together as a condition. A trait scale sees a trait; the reasoning test sees ability and effort; the dyadic record sees legibility and perceptiveness; the occasion reports see variability; the bank sees everything it asked. None of them knows that a subset of the population shares a shape, or which subset, or what shape. Their ceiling is the label.

Each axis's ceiling is the other axis. The declaration says where to look; everything else says what is there.

**3 · The crossing, and why it is a shape and not a score**

Paper 0 states the general argument; the specific part here is what a crossing with a label yields when the other side is many axes rather than one.

A label crossed with one trait yields a difference: the declared group is higher or lower. A label crossed with many axes yields a distribution: the joint shape of the declared group across all of them, including how the traits covary within the group, which may differ from how they covary outside it. That covariance structure is the part no single-instrument study can see and the part that carries the information. Two people can share a label and sit at opposite ends of one trait while sharing a pattern across ten others, and a description in terms of the pattern is what the label was standing for all along.

Two further parts are specific. The declaration is a measurement with a bias model, exactly as paper 0's section 3.2 treats every axis: who declares depends on who was diagnosed, which depends on access, age, gender and country, and on who is willing to say so, which depends on culture and on what the person believes the declaration will cost them. The signature is therefore read conditional on that model, not as a fact about a condition in the abstract. And the signature is a description of a population, never a rule about a person. Nothing here screens anyone: a person's position relative to the signature is a similarity, stated with the signature's own spread, and the boundary between describing a group and diagnosing a member is a design choice this paper fixes in section 6.

**4 · What becomes knowable**

*4.1 The signature itself.* Across the trait scales, the minor scales, the bank, the reasoning test, declared interests and the occasion reports, the joint distribution of people who declare against people who do not: where the centres differ, where the spreads differ, and where the covariances differ. The last is the finding a one-trait design cannot produce. If two traits that move together in the undeclared population move independently in the declared one, or a trait that is stable in most people is a state in the declared group, that is a fact about the condition's structure and not about any one trait's level. Read through the profile facts, the signature also carries its own selection: how it differs by the age at which the person says they were diagnosed, by country, by gender, which is where the bias model of section 3 becomes data rather than caveat.

*4.2 Reading others: people in general, or the people one knows.* Paper 2 splits a prediction about another person into the normative part, what anyone clever infers from the population's modal answer, and the distinctive part, what only acquaintance supplies. The question of whether a condition affects reading other people can then be asked precisely: does the difference, if there is one, lie in the normative part, in the distinctive part, or in neither. A difference in the normative part is a difference in modelling people in general. A difference in the distinctive part is a difference in reading the specific people one knows. And the dyadic record can ask the question that a clinic cannot form: whether observers who share the declaration read declared targets better than observers who do not, which is the relationship term of paper 2's section 4.1 read by whether the two people share the label. If they do, the difference is not a deficit in one party but a mismatch between two, and the data says which.

*4.3 Legibility.* How readable a declared person is to the people who know them, on the distinctive part, against how readable undeclared people are. Legibility is a property of the target and cannot be self-reported, so no instrument built for the condition has ever measured it. Whether declared people are read worse, read the same, or read better by some kinds of tie than others, and whether the observability map of paper 2's section 4.2 differs for them, which items of theirs are public and which private, is knowable only in this crossing.

*4.4 Variability and the body.* A condition described as one of attention and regulation should, if the description is right, show in how a person's own answers move across days: in the spread of their state reports, in the consistency of their answers to repeated items, in the latency and time-of-day pattern of their responding, and in how all of that couples to sleep and energy occasions. It becomes knowable whether the declared group's within-person variability differs, on which dimensions, and whether the body couplings that paper 0's section 6 reads as observational events run differently for them. The condition is read as a dynamics as well as a level, which is a description no one-shot instrument can give.

*4.5 Interests.* Declared interests carry structure: how many, how concentrated, how stable over time, how far they are shared with the people one is tied to. Whether a declared group's interests are narrower and deeper, broader and shallower, more stable or less, is an old question asked here on declared interests across a population rather than on a clinic's impression, and paper 2's observer view of interests adds whether the people who know a declared person would name the same interests they declare.

*4.6 Ability, and effort as signal.* The keyed reasoning test gives a level and, under paper 1's conditions, a profile across item kinds and an effort model from the response record. For a condition of attention, the effort model stops being a nuisance and becomes a reading: whether declared people's measured ability differs from their believed ability more or less than others', paper 1's self-assessment gap, and whether their response record shows the signature of variable effort that the description predicts. Ability level, ability profile and effort pattern are three different readings and the crossing keeps them apart.

*4.7 The bank's own items.* Across an unbounded bank, which items separate the declared from the undeclared, on items nobody wrote for the purpose. Some will be the items an instrument would have asked. Some will not, and those are the description's chance to surprise its authors: the everyday preferences, habits and opinions that travel with the declaration and that no clinic thought to ask about. Under paper 3's map, each such item also carries whether its variance is heritable or circumstantial, which is where the next reading begins.

*4.8 The genetic overlap, and the continuum.* Given paper 3's crossing, the declaration itself has a genetic architecture and so does every trait axis, and their overlap can be read: which of the traits in the signature share genetic architecture with the declaration and which travel with it for other reasons. And the question that decides what kind of thing the condition is becomes askable: whether a genetic signal for the declaration predicts the signature's shape in people who do not declare, which would say the condition is a region of a continuous space rather than a category, and how far into the undeclared population the shape extends. Named genes are not the object; shared architecture is, for the reasons paper 1 and paper 3 give, and the reading is stated under their common-variant and ancestry conditions.

**5 · The return path: what the crossing gives back to every other axis**

*The self-report trait scales.* If a scale's factor structure differs for the declared group, its items mean something different to them, and the scale is not invariant across the declaration. That is a finding about the scale, not an error to be adjusted away, and it tells the scale where its reach ends.

*The bank.* Items that separate the declared from the undeclared gain a tag beside paper 3's, and the bank learns which of its everyday items are about neurotype without anyone having written them to be.

*Ties.* Paper 2's legibility, perceptiveness and relationship terms gain a moderator that changes what they mean: whether reading and being read are properties of a person or of the match between two people.

*The body.* The occasion reports gain a population in which variability is expected to be part of the picture, which is the population on which the within-person model of paper 0's section 5.2 can be tested hardest.

*The keyed ability axis.* The effort model of paper 1 gains a use beyond correction: it is a reading in its own right for a condition of attention.

*The genome.* The declaration is a phenotype no biobank holds beside thirty other measurements of the same person, and the continuum reading of 4.8 is a question about the genome axis's own object: whether the things it predicts are categories or regions.

*The profile facts.* Declaration rates by country, age and gender are a map of diagnosis and disclosure, not of the condition, and the anchors gain a way to read the difference between the two.

**6 · What would have to hold**

The declaration is one consented item, at three levels, diagnosed, suspected, neither, and it is a measurement with a bias model. Who declares is modelled from the profile facts and from the response record, and every reading in section 4 is stated conditional on that model.

The custody of the declaration is decided, and it is an ask rather than a default. In a system whose answers are otherwise public, a health-adjacent declaration may be public like any answer, or it may live in a consented tier with its own custody. The two shapes buy different things. Public, the declaration can be read by observers, so paper 2's readings can ask whether knowing of the declaration changes how a person is rated. In a consented tier, the declaration is safer to make, so more people make it and the signature's selection is smaller. Which is the better trade is the owner's call, and this paper carries both shapes until it is made.

Observers' knowledge of the declaration is recorded. Whether an observer knows that a target declared changes the ratings they give, so the record carries whether they knew, and the readings of 4.2 and 4.3 are made both ways.

The signature describes a population and never screens a person. A person's position relative to the signature is a similarity stated with the signature's spread, shown to the person as a reading about themselves against a group and never as a claim that they have or lack a condition. Nothing in the system offers a diagnosis, and the boundary is a design choice stated in advance, not a limit discovered later.

The instruments are tested for invariance across the declaration before a difference in level is read as a difference in the trait. A scale that means something different to the declared group produces an apparent level difference that is an artefact, and the test comes before the reading.

Reactivity is modelled. Declaring may change how a person answers afterwards, and being shown one's position against a signature certainly may, so the record states which answers came before and after each.

The conditions of papers 1, 2 and 3 apply to the readings that lean on them: effort modelled from the response record, the aggregate-only rating channel, the two-question observer design, the common-variant and ancestry conditions on every genetic reading.

**7 · The requirements the design generates**

A declared group large enough for a covariance structure to be a shape rather than a coincidence, in every profile-fact cell the signature is read by. Declared people who are also targets and observers in the dyadic record, in pairs that both share and do not share the declaration. Occasions on declared people, because 4.4 has no reading without them. Genotyped declared people, because 4.8 has none. None is a reason to want less. Each is what a fully populated declaration beside fully populated axes looks like.

**8 · The potential, at full population**

Fully populated, this crossing is a portrait of neurodivergence that no clinic can paint: the joint shape of the people who carry a declaration across everything a population measures about itself, including how they are seen and how they see, how they vary day to day, what they are into, what their reasoning and their effort look like, and how far the shape extends into the population that does not declare. It replaces a diagnosis-and-instrument description with a distribution over thirty measurements, it can surprise its authors because the bank asks what no instrument asked, and it can say whether the differences in reading other people are in one party or between two.

The method is general. Any declared condition, identity or circumstance can be read the same way, as a shape across every axis, with the same bias model on the declaration and the same boundary between describing a group and screening a member. The neurotype is the first case because it is the one the description most obviously fails today, and because the second-person readings of paper 2 are the ones it has most obviously lacked.

**9 · Open questions the data would have to answer**

Whether the declared group's covariance structure differs from the undeclared group's at all, or only its centres, which decides whether the condition is a shape or a shift. Which part of reading others differs, if any, and whether the relationship term by shared declaration is large enough to say the difference is between two people rather than in one. How much the custody choice of section 6 moves the declaration rate, which is the number that decides the trade. Whether the everyday items that separate the groups are stable across countries or are local. And whether the genetic signal for the declaration predicts the signature's shape in the undeclared, which is the continuum question, and how far.
