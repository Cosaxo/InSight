# The shape of a declared difference

*Paper 4 of the axiom series: a self-declared neurotype read across every axis at once, including how the person is seen and how they see others.*

**Status: theory research.** Nothing here describes the app or proposes a change to it; `research/README.md` says what a paper is and how one reaches the product. Paper 0 carries the general argument; paper 1 supplies the keyed-ability readings, paper 2 the second-person readings, paper 3 the item-level genetics and paper 5 the occasion covariate this paper composes.

**Perfect-form test.** If the app did not exist, this would still be worth saying because what is known about attention-deficit, autism and the conditions that travel with them is known through a recorded diagnosis and a few dozen variables per person, and any population that carried a declaration beside every axis it measures, including how the person is read by the people who know them, would describe the condition as a shape rather than as a score.

**Abstract.** A condition of attention or of social perception is studied through a recorded diagnosis. Whole-population registries and birth cohorts reach everyone, but through the same recorded diagnosis, so they inherit its ascertainment and hold a few dozen variables per person; the instruments were written for the condition and so find what it was already believed to be; and how the person is seen by the people who know them enters only as a diagnostic aid, one or two informants chosen by the clinic, on scales about symptoms, never as a property of the person graded against their own sealed answer. This paper describes a population in which a person may declare such a condition, per condition and with the conditions that co-occur with it, beside everything else the system measures, and argues that the declaration crossed with all of that yields a signature: the joint shape of the declared across every axis rather than a list of single-trait differences. It names what becomes knowable and the artefact each reading has to beat. Whether the difference in reading other people lies in modelling people in general or in reading the people one knows, computed against the target's own group norm so that base rates do not manufacture it. Whether legibility differs, standardized by how much there is to read, and whether it differs across kinds of tie, which is the signature of effortful presentation rather than of opacity. Whether day-to-day variability differs, under a designed repetition schedule and the aliasing bound the daily budget imposes. Which everyday items separate the declared from the undeclared, published as directions and never as weights, because a weighted item map is a screening instrument handed to third parties. And whether the condition is a region or a category, decided by tests that can tell the two apart, which prediction of the signature in the undeclared cannot. It treats the declaration as three measurements rather than three points on one scale, requires treatment to be declared beside the condition, states a no-screening rule that is a design rule rather than a sentence, and puts the custody of a health-adjacent declaration to the owner as three shapes with the trade named.

**1 · The problem, stated structurally**

A condition of the mind is studied through its recorded diagnosis. Someone is assessed, an instrument written for the condition is administered, a threshold is crossed, and the person joins a group that is compared with those who did not. Registry and birth-cohort designs reach whole populations rather than a clinic's patients, and they reach them through the same recorded diagnosis, so they inherit its ascertainment, which selects on access, on era, on country, on gender and on whether the right adult noticed at the right time. What is described is the recorded, not the condition.

The instruments were built for the condition, so they measure what the condition was already believed to be. A trait the instrument does not ask about is not part of the description, and the description cannot surprise its authors. Where the people who know the person are asked, they are asked as a diagnostic aid: one or two informants the clinic chose, on scales about the person's symptoms, about someone who was being assessed, and never as a measurement of the person in their own right, graded against the person's own sealed answer, taken on people who were not being assessed at all.

And the comparison is one trait at a time. A group scores higher here and lower there, and the shape of a person who carries the condition, how the traits sit together in them, how they vary from day to day, how the people around them experience them and how they experience those people, is not what the design measures. What no design has had is depth per person and the second-person record on the same people, and that is what this crossing supplies.

**2 · The two axes alone, and where each stops**

The declaration is a set of items rather than one: whether a person says they have each of a named list of conditions, attention-deficit, autism, and the conditions that commonly travel with them, an anxiety or a mood disorder, a sleep disorder, a learning disorder, each at one of three levels, diagnosed, suspected, neither; beside each, whether the person is treated for it, currently, formerly or never; and for a diagnosis, when it was made and by whom. Alone it is a set of labels with base rates. It cannot say what the labels are labels for, and it carries the biases of who declares, which are the biases of who was recorded plus the biases of who says so. Its ceiling is content.

The other axes, taken together, measure the person from many sides and cannot say which of what they measure hangs together as a condition. A trait scale sees a trait; the minor scales, the short instruments beside the main trait scales, see the finer traits; the reasoning test sees ability and effort; the dyadic record sees legibility and perceptiveness; the occasion reports see variability; the bank sees everything it asked. None of them knows that a subset of the population shares a shape, or which subset, or what shape. Their ceiling is the label.

Each axis's ceiling is the other axis. The declaration says where to look; everything else says what is there.

**3 · The crossing, and why it is a shape and not a score**

Paper 0 states the general argument; the specific part here is what a crossing with a label yields when the other side is many axes rather than one.

A label crossed with one trait yields a difference: the declared group is higher or lower. A label crossed with many axes yields a distribution: the joint shape of the declared group across all of them, including how the traits covary within the group, which may differ from how they covary outside it. That covariance structure is the part no single-instrument study can see and the part that carries the information. Two people can share a label and sit at opposite ends of one trait while sharing a pattern across ten others, and whether the pattern is what the label was standing for is the finding, not the premise: the pattern could be a pattern of declaring, of being treated, or of the distress that travels with the condition, and the readings below are built to tell those apart.

Three further parts are specific. The declaration is a measurement with a bias model, as paper 0's section 3.2 treats every axis: who declares depends on who was recorded, which depends on access, age, gender and country, and on who is willing to say so, which depends on culture and on what the person believes the declaration will cost them. The signature is read conditional on that model. The three levels are three measurements, not three points on one scale: a diagnosis measures access, era and whether an adult noticed; a suspicion measures exposure to a description, identification with it, and distress attributed to it; and whether the suspected sit between the other two on the signature is a finding and not an assumption, so the record asks what separates them, the age at diagnosis, who made it, whether an assessment was sought and refused or never sought, where the suspicion came from. And the signature describes a population and never screens a person, by a rule section 6 fixes in the design rather than in a sentence.

**4 · What becomes knowable**

*4.1 The signature itself.* Across the trait scales, the minor scales, the bank, the reasoning test, declared interests and the occasion reports, the joint distribution of people who declare against people who do not: where the centres differ, where the spreads differ, and where the covariances differ. The last is the finding a one-trait design cannot produce, and it is also the reading with the most ways to be an artefact. Observed covariance differs between two groups when the instrument's loadings or thresholds differ between them with the latent structure unchanged; when one group answers more variably, which adds measurement error and attenuates every correlation, so that traits appear to decouple, and the group this paper predicts to be more variable is the declared one, so 4.1 and 4.4 would otherwise manufacture each other; when one group is range-restricted by selection on severity or insight; when the group is a mixture of co-occurring conditions; and when response styles differ. So every covariance difference is reported disattenuated, with both groups' reliabilities beside it, after invariance is established for loadings as well as levels, within co-occurrence cell before pooling, and a difference that survives none of that is a finding about the instrument. One instance of the reading it is for: whether the coupling between a stable trait and its day-to-day expression, which in most people is tight, is loose in the declared group, so that a trait that is a trait for most is a state for them.

*4.2 Reading others: people in general, or the people one knows, against the right norm.* Paper 2 splits a prediction into the normative part, inferred from a population's answer, the stereotype part, inferred from the answers of people who share the target's profile facts, and the distinctive part, which only acquaintance supplies. A global norm confounds the question this paper asks with base rates: a declared observer predicting a declared target against the whole population's mode shows a normative deficit that is arithmetic, because the mode does not fit the target. So the decomposition is re-centred before the question is asked. The distinctive part is departure from the mode of people like the target including the target's declaration, and the normative part is computed twice, against the whole population's mode and against the target's group-conditional mode. The difference between those two accuracies is the estimand: an observer who reads targets well under one norm and badly under the other is modelling the wrong population, and that is the claim that the difference lies between two people rather than in one, stated as a number. The relationship term by shared declaration, whether declared observers read declared targets better, is read within tie type, matched on tie age and contact frequency, within perceiver and within target where possible, with assumed similarity entered as paper 2 requires, and priced as a contrast of variance components across four pair cells, the rarest of which is the shared one.

*4.3 Legibility, and effortful presentation as its first competitor.* How readable a declared person is to the people who know them, on the distinctive part, standardized by the target's own distinctive variance and compared at matched variance, because a raw accuracy difference between declared and undeclared targets is a difference in how far they sit from the mode, which 4.1 already reports. Legibility is a property of the target and cannot be self-reported, so no instrument built for the condition has ever measured it. Its first competitor is effort: a person working hard not to be read produces the same low legibility as a person who is hard to read, and the two come apart across kinds of tie. A legibility deficit is flat across ties; presentation effort predicts a steep within-person gradient, legible to a partner and opaque to a colleague. So the discriminating estimand is the within-person spread of legibility across tie types, not its mean, and it is read by gender, because the effort and the age at diagnosis both are.

*4.4 Variability and the body, under a designed schedule.* A condition described as one of attention and regulation should, if the description is right, show in how a person's own answers move across days. But under a daily budget with one item drawn from an unbounded bank, an item recurs at a lag of months, so inconsistency computed off the ordinary serving order confounds day-scale instability with change over months, with item context, and with the serving policy itself, and paper 0's section 5.2 bounds what a daily sample can resolve at all. The variability readings therefore exist only under a designed repetition schedule: chosen items returned at stated lags, some within the same occasion, so that state variance is separable from unreliability, with the aliasing bound quoted with every dynamic. Response latency is read within person and within item, standardized against the person's own distribution, with a model for the interrupted and backgrounded tail, and never between people; time of day is a reading only where the system randomized the time it asked. Under those conditions, and with paper 5's body covariate, it becomes knowable whether the declared group's within-person variability differs, on which dimensions, and whether the body couplings run differently for them.

*4.5 Interests.* Declared interests carry structure: how many, how concentrated, how stable over time, how far they are shared with the people one is tied to. Whether a declared group's interests are narrower and deeper, broader and shallower, more stable or less, is an old question asked here on declared interests across a population, and paper 2's observer view adds whether the people who know a declared person would name the same interests they declare.

*4.6 Ability, and effort measured from outside the response record.* The keyed reasoning test gives a level and, under paper 1's conditions, a profile across item kinds. Paper 1 estimates effort from the response record in order to remove it from ability; that estimate cannot then be read as a symptom, because a quantity fitted as a nuisance and renamed as a finding is the circularity paper 0's section 3.2 forbids, and it is computed from the same features as 4.4's variability, so the two would be one statistic reported as two confirmations. The effort reading therefore uses an effort measure identified outside the response record: self-reported effort at the sitting, a same-day state report, or a randomized deadline or incentive on the sitting that the system controls. With that, it becomes knowable whether declared people's measured ability differs from their believed ability more or less than others', paper 1's self-assessment gap, and whether their effort under a randomized condition differs. Ability level, ability profile and effort are three readings and the crossing keeps them apart. Where only the response-record model exists, 4.4 and 4.6 are one finding and are stated as one.

*4.7 The bank's own items, published as directions and never as weights.* Across an unbounded bank, which items separate the declared from the undeclared, on items nobody wrote for the purpose. Some will be the items an instrument would have asked. Some will not, and those are the description's chance to surprise its authors: the everyday preferences, habits and opinions that travel with the declaration and that no clinic thought to ask about. Across an unbounded bank the search is a multiple comparison, so separations are reported with the selection that found them and confirmed on people who were not in the discovery set. And the map's misuse is by third parties on people who are not in it, which no participant's consent reaches: an item map with weights is a screening instrument anyone can apply to an employee, an applicant or a child. So the map publishes which items separate and in which direction, never their weights, and never enough of them jointly to score a person; per-item effects are released coarsened, the joint discriminant is not released at all, and an item that separates strongly enough to screen on its own is reported as a fact about the item and withheld as a weight.

*4.8 The genetic overlap, and what would decide the continuum.* Given paper 3's crossing, the declaration has a genetic architecture and so does every trait axis, and their overlap can be read, with one qualification first: a genetic signal trained on a self-declared phenotype is a signal for diagnosis and disclosure, so it partly indexes access, education and help-seeking, and its correlations with the trait axes carry that. The question of whether the condition is a region of a continuous space or a category is not decided by whether the signal predicts the signature in people who do not declare. It does under both models: a threshold on a continuous liability predicts that people below the threshold show subthreshold expression, and undiagnosed cases in the undeclared produce the same pattern. What discriminates is three things read jointly: the genetic correlation between the declaration and a continuous signature score, tested against unity; the shape of the function from genetic signal to signature, tested for a threshold or a kink against linearity; and whether the signature's own distribution in the population is better fit by one distribution than by a mixture. Prediction in the undeclared is the precondition for those tests and not the answer. Named genes are not the object; shared architecture is, under paper 1's and paper 3's common-variant and ancestry conditions.

**5 · The return path: what the crossing gives back to every other axis**

*The self-report trait scales.* If a scale's loadings differ for the declared group, its items mean something different to them, and the scale is not invariant across the declaration. That is a finding about the scale, not an error to be adjusted away, and it tells the scale where its reach ends.

*The bank.* Items that separate the declared from the undeclared gain a direction beside paper 3's tags, and the bank learns which of its everyday items are about neurotype without anyone having written them to be, under the publication rule of 4.7.

*Ties.* Paper 2's legibility, perceptiveness and relationship terms gain a moderator that changes what they mean: whether reading and being read are properties of a person or of the match between two people, and whether low legibility is opacity or effort.

*The body.* The occasion reports gain a population in which variability is expected to be part of the picture, which is the population on which paper 5's within-person model can be tested hardest, and on which treatment on the occasion is a covariate the body axis has to carry.

*The keyed ability axis.* Effort identified outside the response record, which paper 1 needs as a correction, becomes a reading in its own right for a condition of attention, and the randomized sitting conditions that identify it are a design the ability axis gains for everyone.

*The genome.* The declaration is a phenotype no biobank holds beside every other measurement of the same person, and the continuum tests of 4.8 are questions about the genome axis's own object: whether the things it predicts are categories or regions.

*The profile facts.* Declaration rates by country, age and gender are a map of recording and disclosure, not of the condition, and the anchors gain a way to read the difference between the two.

**6 · What would have to hold**

The declaration is per condition and multi-select, at three levels each, with co-occurring conditions and treatment declared beside it, and every reading in section 4 is estimated first within co-occurrence cell and within treatment status, and pooled only where the cells agree. A signature reported over the union of conditions is a mixture and is labelled as one, and a diagnosed-against-suspected contrast that ignores treatment is partly a treated-against-untreated contrast.

The undeclared are three groups, those without the condition, those who have it and do not know, and those who know and do not say, and every reading states which it means. The second group is bounded from the declaration rate's own dependence on country, age and gender, and no contrast against the undeclared is reported without that bound, because undiagnosed cases in the reference group attenuate every centre difference and produce the pattern a continuum would.

The declaration is anchored across languages. The labels travel badly, so a small set of behavioural items asked identically everywhere anchors the declaration, and a cross-country difference in the signature can be told from a cross-country difference in what the word means.

The declaration is consented separately from every other answer, for a stated purpose, and it is revocable: on revocation it leaves the estimates at the next fit, and any observer's recorded knowledge of it leaves with it. Because a published declaration cannot be recalled, publication is a second consent and not a consequence of the first. No declaration is taken from a minor, and an age at diagnosis is a recollection, not a record.

The custody of the declaration is decided, and it is an ask with three shapes rather than two. Public, like any answer, which uniquely buys that a stranger may read it, and which costs that an employer, an insurer or a border may. Held in a consented tier and read only in aggregate. Or disclosed per tie at the person's choice, visible to the people they tell, with the telling recorded and dated. The third shape gets what the first was wanted for, because observer knowledge is recorded as data under every shape, and it gets more: disclosure becomes an event with a time, so 4.2 and 4.3 can be read within a tie before and after it. Which trade is right is the owner's call, and this paper carries all three until it is made.

Visibility is dated, because it changes which ties exist. Under any shape in which others can know, who follows, plays with and rates whom is caused in part by the declaration, so the clean reading is within a tie across the date the declaration became visible, and the cross-sectional comparison of declared and undeclared ties is reported beside it as the confounded version it is.

The stigma term is identified, not only recorded. Whether an observer knew of the declaration is data; the knowing and not-knowing observers of the same target are the contrast that measures what knowing does to a rating, and where a keyed item exists the rating can be checked against a truth, which bounds how much of a self-observer gap is the observer's frame. Until it is bounded, no difference in the gap is read as a property of declared people, and paper 2's reveal of the aggregate is withheld where the aggregate would deliver a stigma shift to the person it is about.

No per-person composite similarity to the signature is computed for display, stored, or exported. The signature is estimated at population level; a person sees only their own values on each axis against that axis's population distribution, never combined into a single distance, never ranked, and never labelled by the declaration. The declared group's parameters are published under the rule of 4.7; the projection of an individual onto them is not a quantity the system produces. This is the no-screening rule, and it is a rule about what exists rather than about what is said.

The instruments are tested for invariance across the declaration before any difference is read, of level, spread or covariance, and the covariance readings additionally require equal reliability or disattenuation, per 4.1.

Reactivity is modelled. Declaring may change how a person answers afterwards, and the record states which answers came before and after each declaration and each disclosure.

The conditions of papers 1, 2, 3 and 5 apply to the readings that lean on them: the aggregate-only rating channel and its release schedule, the two-question observer design, the common-variant and ancestry conditions on every genetic reading, and the designed repetition schedules and body covariate on every within-person one.

**7 · The requirements the design generates**

A declared group large enough, within each co-occurrence and treatment cell, for a covariance structure to be a shape rather than a coincidence, and large enough within the profile-fact cells the signature is read by. Declared people who are also targets and observers in the dyadic record, in pairs that both share and do not share the declaration, matched on tie type, tie age and contact, because the shared cell is a variance contrast and the rarest cell at once. Designed repetition on declared people, because 4.4 has no reading without it. Genotyped declared people, because 4.8 has none. And randomized sitting conditions on the keyed test, because 4.6's effort reading needs an identification the response record cannot give. None is a reason to want less. Each is what a fully populated declaration beside fully populated axes looks like.

**8 · The potential, at full population**

Fully populated, this crossing is a portrait of neurodivergence that no recorded diagnosis can paint: the joint shape of the people who carry a declaration across everything a population measures about itself, including how they are seen and how they see, how they vary day to day, what they are into, what their reasoning and their effort look like under conditions the system randomized, and whether the shape is a region or a category, decided by tests that can tell. It replaces a diagnosis-and-instrument description with a distribution over every axis at once, it can surprise its authors because the bank asks what no instrument asked, and it can say whether the differences in reading other people are in one party or between two, against the norm that makes the question fair.

The method is general. Any declared condition, identity or circumstance can be read the same way, as a shape across every axis, with the same bias model on the declaration, the same rule that a mixture is labelled as one, and the same boundary between describing a group and producing a projection of a person. The neurotype is the first case because it is the one the description most obviously fails today, and because the second-person readings of paper 2 are the ones it has most obviously lacked.

**9 · Open questions the data would have to answer**

Whether the declared group's covariance structure differs from the undeclared group's after disattenuation and within cells, or only its centres, which decides whether the condition is a shape or a shift. Which part of reading others differs, if any, once the norm is the target's own group, and whether the relationship term by shared declaration survives matching. Whether low legibility across ties is flat or steep, which decides whether it is opacity or effort. How much each custody shape moves the declaration rate and the composition of who declares, which is the number that decides the trade. Whether the everyday items that separate the groups are stable across countries once the label is anchored behaviourally. And whether the three continuum tests of 4.8 agree.
