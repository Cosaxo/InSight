# What follows an event

*Paper 13 of the axiom series: the event as the unit of causal reading across every axis — the events the system controls, the events the world supplies, the third class the system influences without controlling — the impulse-response of a person as a phenotype, and the causal budget every randomization spends.*

**Status: theory research.** Nothing here describes the app or proposes a change to it; `research/README.md` says what a paper is and how one reaches the product. Paper 0 supplies the two classes of event this paper builds on; G3 the two identification routes and the multiplicity discipline; paper 0a the allocation every randomization competes in; paper 5 the within-person series and the regression-to-the-mean artefact; papers 2, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10 and 11 the randomized events this paper gathers into one budget; paper 3 the conditions on any genome moderation; paper 6 the resilience functional this paper makes behavioural.

**Perfect-form test.** If the app did not exist, this would still be worth saying because a population observed continuously across many sources is also a population to which things happen, and what follows an event on every source at once is a causal reading no cross-sectional design reaches — while a system that itself causes some of those events, at times it can randomize, holds a causal instrument that is also an allocation, so that every randomization is an observation with a cost and the set of events it may cause is the programme's causal budget; and because the events it merely influences, through what it displays and pairs and predicts, are a third class neither randomized nor exogenous, which most designs mistake for one of the first two.

**Abstract.** Paper 0 sorted the events in a person's record into two classes — those the system controls and may randomize, and those the person or the world supplies, which are observational — and G3 gave each its identification route. This paper makes the event the unit of a reading across every axis. An event is an impulse; the person's series on every source afterwards is the response; and the response, read event-time by event-time against the person's own pre-event series, is a phenotype — a psychological resilience functional of the kind paper 6 defines for a metabolic challenge, here for a loss, a reveal, a tie ending, an illness — with a between-person variance that is the first thing the design must show exists. Five facts organize the paper. The system's randomizable events — reveals, displays, assigned stakes and opponents, fixed challenges, disclosure arms, offers, the withholding of model-driven pairing — are one causal budget, each spending attention and each competing with measurement in paper 0a's allocation, so a randomization is priced like any observation. World events are identified only under sequential ignorability, with the onset time as the unit, the report of the event modelled as the state-dependent act it is, and regression to the mean as the artefact the curve must beat. A third class exists that an earlier framing missed: events the system influences without controlling — a pairing its model proposed, a display its policy chose — which are neither randomized nor exogenous, and which become readable only where a withholding arm makes them partly first-class. Effects are read at event time, never pooled across people at different distances from their events, because pooling with heterogeneous effects averages with weights that can be negative. And anticipation is a measured quantity, since a person who knows an event is coming moves before it, which contaminates the pre-period unless timing is unpredictable or the anticipation window is modelled. What becomes knowable: the impulse-response of every axis to every randomizable event, with an unbounded outcome set held by shrinkage; behavioural resilience as a phenotype and its genetics for randomized events; cross-axis chains with the identified link named; and the observational events under their stated assumption and band. Which events may be caused at all is put to the owner as forks, because a randomized event is a treatment on a person's life.

**1 · The problem, stated structurally**

A population measured continuously across many sources is also a population to which things happen. A tie ends; an illness begins; a contest is lost; a face is shown to a stranger; the crowd's answer is revealed; a person moves. Each is an event, and each is followed by readings on every source the person supplies — so the record after an event is a response on every axis at once, against the record before it.

Cross-sectional designs cannot read that. They compare people who had the event to people who did not, and the two differ in everything that made the event happen. A within-person design can: the person's own series before the event is the counterfactual for the series after, and the difference, read source by source and day by day, is what the event did — subject to conditions this paper states, since the person's own series is also moving for reasons that are not the event.

The series has been reading events throughout without treating them as an axis. Paper 2 randomizes a reveal; paper 5 reads what follows an illness onset; paper 6 randomizes a challenge; paper 8 assigns stakes and opponents; paper 9 randomizes a display; paper 10 randomizes disclosure; paper 7 randomizes an offer; paper 11 withholds model-driven pairing. Each paper built its own event and its own reading. What none states is that they are one thing — one causal budget, one identification discipline, one phenotype family — and that the events the system merely influences are a class of their own that the two-class sort of paper 0 does not hold.

**2 · The two classes, restated, and the third**

*First class: events the system controls.* The timing of a reveal, the order of items, which face is shown, which stake is assigned, when a challenge is served, whether an offer is made. Each can be randomized outright, and what follows a randomized event is identified by design, under an exposure mapping that says who was exposed to whose event — since a reveal to one person of a group's answers exposes the group — and under consent to be randomized, which is per event family and is one of the owner's forks in section 5. This is the one exogenous source the structure owns.

*Second class: events the world supplies.* An illness, a tie ending, a move, a bereavement, a job change. Observational. Onset is confounded with state; the reporting of the event is itself state-dependent, because a bad day is reported on days a person also answers differently; and past outcomes affect future exposure, which biases the naive within-person estimator. Paper 0 states the route: an estimand defined under sequential ignorability — no time-varying confounder affected by prior outcomes — with the onset time as the unit of analysis and the report of the event modelled beside the event.

*Third class: events the system influences without controlling.* A pairing the model proposed and the person accepted; a display the policy chose and a stranger acted on; a contest the matcher offered and the person entered. The system did not randomize the event — the person or another person completed it — but the system chose the opportunity, so the event is neither exogenous nor assigned. An earlier framing filed these with the second class, which treats the system's own influence as world noise, or with the first, which treats an accepted opportunity as an assignment. Neither holds. The third class is readable only where the opportunity itself is randomized — a withholding arm, as paper 11 uses for model-driven pairing and paper 9 for a display — which makes the opportunity first-class and the completion a second-class event conditional on it. So a third-class event is read as an intention-to-treat on the opportunity plus a complier reading on the completion, and never as either alone.

**3 · Five facts about reading an event**

*3.1 The response is a curve at event time, and pooling across distances is wrong.* An event's effect depends on how long ago it happened: a loss stings on the day and fades; a tie ending changes the record for months. So the reading is event-time-specific — the effect at one day, one week, one month after — read against the person's own pre-event series. Pooling people at different distances from their events into one coefficient, as a single before-after contrast does, averages the event-time effects with weights set by the distribution of distances, and where the effect is heterogeneous over time those weights can be negative, so the pooled number can carry the wrong sign. The paper reads at event time and publishes no pooled coefficient.

*3.2 The person's own series is the counterfactual, and it moves on its own.* The pre-event series is not flat: it drifts with season and tenure, it has the state structure paper 5 measures, and — for world events — it is at an extreme at the onset, because an illness is reported when a person feels worst and a tie ends when a relationship is at its lowest. So the post-event recovery contains regression to the mean, which paper 5 names as the artefact any illness curve must beat, and the design reads a world event's curve against the person's own matched days — days with the same pre-event state but no event — rather than against the raw pre-event level. For a randomized event the onset is exogenous and the artefact is absent by construction, which is the whole reason the first class is worth its cost.

*3.3 Anticipation moves the pre-period.* A person who knows a contest is scheduled, a reveal is coming, or a challenge is due changes before it — prepares, worries, avoids. The pre-event series is then already a response, and a before-after contrast underestimates or misreads the effect. Two designs handle it: unpredictable timing, which paper 7 uses for a liveness challenge and paper 2 for a reveal and which makes anticipation impossible; or a modelled anticipation window, where the pre-period is split into a clean segment and an anticipating one and the effect is read against the clean segment. Which is used is stated per event family, and the size of anticipation — the pre-event drift for scheduled against unscheduled events — is itself a reading, since how much a person moves before a known event is a trait.

*3.4 Every randomization spends, in paper 0a's currency.* A randomized event is an observation the design chose: it costs the attention the event consumes, it costs the person whatever the event does to them — a withheld face, an assigned loss, a delayed reveal — and it competes with measurement for the one observation a day. So the system's randomizable events are one causal budget, each entry priced by what its randomization identifies against what it costs, allocated by the policy beside every item it might have served instead, and recorded with its realised probability like any serving decision. A programme that randomizes freely because randomization is clean has forgotten that the person on the other side of the assignment is also the person answering tomorrow, and paper 8's floor and paper 9's hidden-side budget are the two places the series already prices this.

*3.5 The outcome set is unbounded, and shrinkage is the discipline.* After an event, every item of a bank with no ceiling, every trait scale, every body channel and every tie is an outcome, so the number of event-by-outcome readings has no bound and the multiplicity is not a footnote. G3's discipline applies: effects are estimated jointly with shrinkage across outcomes, published with the shrinkage stated, and a single outcome's effect is never read as if it were the only one tested. Where the bank's items load on paper 0's factors, the reading is on the factors first and on items only as their residuals.

**4 · What becomes knowable**

*4.1 The impulse-response of every axis to every randomizable event.* For each first-class event family — reveal, display, assigned stake, assigned opponent strength, fixed challenge, disclosure, offer, withheld pairing — the event-time curve on every axis, against the person's own pre-event series, with the multiplicity held. This is the causal map of the system's own actions: what a reveal does to the next week's answers, to reported sleep, to who is followed; what a loss does to persistence in the bank and re-entry in the arena; what a withheld face does to the withheld person. It is available only because the events are randomized, and it is what every paper that randomized an event was reading one row of.

*4.2 Behavioural resilience as a phenotype.* Paper 6 defines resilience as the return to baseline after a fixed metabolic challenge, in scale-free functionals; this paper defines its behavioural counterpart: the return to a person's own centre after a randomized adverse event — an assigned loss, a withheld display, a reveal that placed them below the crowd — as time-to-return and excursion-as-ratio on each axis, repeated across events. Its between-person variance is the first thing the design must show exists, since a population that all recover alike has no phenotype here; its stability across event families says whether resilience is one trait or many; and its relation to paper 6's metabolic functional on the same people is a crossing neither paper makes alone — whether the body and the person return on one clock.

*4.3 Gene-by-event interaction, identified for the first class.* Whether the response to a randomized event differs by genotype is a phenotype papers 3 and 6 want and the design supplies: the event is randomized, the genotype is fixed, and the interaction is identified for the event's effect — under paper 3's conditions, with the genotype's main effect confounded by ancestry as always, and the interaction read within ancestry. For second-class events the interaction inherits the second class's assumption and is stated as such.

*4.4 Cross-axis chains, with the identified link named.* An event on one axis followed by a change on another followed by a change on a third — a loss, then reported mood, then answers; a tie ending, then sleep, then a test day — is a chain, and paper 9's rule holds: one link is identified where the event was randomized, and the chain is not. The paper reads the first link causally, the later links as event-time associations conditional on the first, and never publishes the chain as a mediation unless a later link was itself randomized.

*4.5 The world's events, under their assumption and band.* Illness, tie endings, moves, read as paper 0 specifies: onset time as unit, sequential ignorability stated, the report modelled, regression to the mean beaten by matched days. Two additions. The reporting model carries paper 5's non-ignorability band, because an unreported event is the missing-worst-day problem in event form. And the onset's own selection — that a person reports an illness when it is bad enough to report — makes the read curve a curve for events severe enough to be reported, which is stated as the population it is.

*4.6 The third class, made partly readable.* Under a withholding arm on the opportunity, a model-driven pairing or a policy-chosen display becomes an intention-to-treat on the opportunity and a complier reading on its completion, and the series' largest source of events — the system's own suggestions — stops being filed as world noise. What is identified is the effect of being offered; what is described is the effect among those who completed; and the gap between them is the selection the system's own influence induces, which is a reading about the system.

*4.7 Anticipation as a trait.* The pre-event drift before a scheduled event, against the flat pre-period before an unscheduled one, per person and per event family: how much a person moves toward an event they know is coming, which is a disposition no one-shot instrument holds and which the two timing designs of 3.3 measure by contrast.

**5 · The return path, the forks, and the ranking**

*Every axis* gains an event-time response curve to every randomized event, which is a causal reading of what the system does to that axis.

*The body* gains the behavioural resilience phenotype beside its metabolic one, and the clock question of 4.2.

*The genome* gains gene-by-event interactions on randomized events, the cleanest gene-by-environment reading the series can offer, since the environment is assigned.

*Observers, ties, the arena and the bank* each gain their randomized events read as one family rather than one paper's row.

*The learned representation* gains the third class: its own pairings, read as offered-versus-completed under a withholding arm, which is also paper 11's performativity measured.

*The design layer* gains the causal budget as one line beside measurement, priced in the same currency.

*The forks the owner rules — because a randomized event is a treatment on a person's life.* Which event families may be randomized at all: a delayed reveal is mild, an assigned likely loss is not, a withheld face has a cost paper 9 denominates, and the owner sets the set. Whether negative events may be assigned deliberately — a stake likely to be lost, an opponent likely to win — which the resilience phenotype of 4.2 needs and which is a decision about what the system may do to a person for a reading. What consent shape each family carries: general consent to be randomized, per-family consent, or per-event consent, with what each costs the identification. And whether unpredictable timing, which defeats anticipation, is an acceptable experience or an intrusion, per family. The paper states each and decides none.

*Ranked by what they make knowable.* First, the impulse-response map of 4.1, because it is the causal reading of the system's own actions and nothing else in the series assembles it. Second, behavioural resilience and its genetics, because a phenotype defined on assigned events is the cleanest gene-by-environment object the series has. Third, the third class made readable, because it turns the largest event source from noise into a reading. The world's events are ranked by their assumption, which is untestable, and the chains last, because most are one link.

**6 · What would have to hold**

*Effects are read at event time and never pooled across distances*, because pooled weights under heterogeneous effects can be negative.

*The counterfactual is the person's own series, corrected for its own motion* — matched days for world events, since onset sits at an extreme; and the state structure of paper 5 entered, since the series moves without any event.

*Anticipation is handled per family* — unpredictable timing or a modelled window — and its size is published as a reading.

*Every randomization is priced and logged* — attention, cost to the person, and the realised probability — as an entry in the one causal budget allocated beside measurement.

*The exposure mapping is stated* for every event on a relation graph, since a reveal or a display exposes more than one person, and paper 2's interference discipline carries.

*Consent to be randomized is per family and is one of the forks*, never assumed from consent to be measured.

*Sequential ignorability is stated for every world event, with what would violate it*, and tested where a negative control — an outcome the event cannot affect — exists.

*The report of a world event is modelled as state-dependent*, with paper 5's band over unreported events, and the read population is named as events severe enough to be reported.

*The third class is never filed as the first or the second*, and is read only under a withholding arm, as offered-versus-completed.

*Multiplicity is held by shrinkage across the unbounded outcome set*, read on factors first, with the shrinkage stated.

*A chain is published as one identified link plus associations*, never as a mediation, unless a later link was randomized.

*Paper 3's conditions apply to any genome moderation*, with the interaction read within ancestry.

**7 · The requirements the design generates**

*Events per person per family in the dozens* before an event-time curve is a curve rather than a point, and in the hundreds across the population per family before its between-person variance is estimable — which, at the rate a system can ethically cause adverse events, is years.

*A causal budget line in paper 0a's allocation*, with each family's cost to the person denominated as papers 8 and 9 denominate theirs.

*Unpredictable timing or a modelled anticipation window per family*, and both for at least one family so the contrast of 4.7 exists.

*Matched-day controls for every world event*, which need the pre-event state measured densely enough to match on — paper 5's daily report at minimum.

*A withholding arm on every system-influenced opportunity that is to be read*, sized for the intention-to-treat.

*A negative-control outcome per world-event family*, so sequential ignorability is tested rather than only stated.

*An exposure mapping on the relation graph*, maintained as ties change.

*A reporting model for world events* with its band.

*Shrinkage across the outcome set*, with the factor structure of paper 0 as its first level.

None is a reason to want less. Each is what a population read event by event looks like from the inside.

**8 · What would decide it**

*Does behavioural resilience vary between people?* One randomized adverse event family — the mildest the owner permits — repeated across a cohort with the person's own pre-event series dense enough to read a curve; the variance component of time-to-return across people, against an equivalence bound. Null, and there is no phenotype here and 4.2 and 4.3 close; substantial, and its correlation across event families in the same cohort says at once whether it is one trait.

*Does the world's curve survive its own artefacts?* One world event family — illness, since paper 5 already reads it — read against matched days with the reporting model and a negative-control outcome; whether the curve that survives regression to the mean and the band is distinguishable from zero, and whether the negative control stays flat. A curve that vanishes under matching was the artefact; a negative control that moves violates the assumption, and the family is published as unidentified.

*And the third-class question that decides how much of the record is readable.* A withholding arm on one system-influenced opportunity — a proposed pairing — read as offered-versus-completed; whether the intention-to-treat is distinguishable from zero and how far the complier reading departs from it. If the offer does nothing the third class is inert and the system's influence is not a confound; if the complier reading departs sharply, the system's suggestions were selecting, and every second-class reading that ignored them was wrong.

**9 · The potential, priced with each score's own condition**

A ten is a dataset after which a field's standard design changes; a five answers questions the field already had. Every score is conditional on the owner's forks in section 5, since a randomized event is a treatment.

*The impulse-response map of the system's own actions.* Eight, conditional on the event families permitted and on the causal budget being funded beside measurement, because a population with randomized social, competitive and informational events read on every source at once does not exist at any size, and the reason is that no one has held both the events and the sources.

*Behavioural resilience and its genetics.* Seven where the variance survives its bound and adverse events may be assigned; three where only mild events are permitted, since the phenotype then exists only on the events that barely move anyone.

*The third class made readable.* Six, conditional on withholding arms, because it converts the largest event source from noise to reading and measures the system's own selection.

*The world's events.* Four, conditional on the negative controls staying flat, because sequential ignorability is untestable in general and the reading is honest only where a control exists.

*Anticipation as a trait.* Four, conditional on both timing designs running in one family.

The value concentrates in the first two, and both wait on the forks before they wait on any data.

**10 · Open questions the data would have to answer**

Whether behavioural resilience varies between people at all, and whether it is one trait across event families or several. Whether it shares a clock with the body's metabolic resilience on the same people. How large anticipation is before a scheduled event, and whether defeating it by unpredictable timing changes the event itself. Whether the world's curves survive matched-day correction and their negative controls, or whether most of what follows an illness is regression to the mean. How far the complier reading departs from the offer effect for the system's own suggestions, which decides how much of the record the third class has been contaminating. What the causal budget costs against measurement over years, and whether a programme can afford to randomize enough adverse events to read resilience without the events themselves becoming the reason people leave. And which events the owner permits at all — which is the question under every score above and the one no data answers.
