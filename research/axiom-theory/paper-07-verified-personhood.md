# One living person, and whose genome it is

*Paper 7 of the axiom series: five separable claims usually bundled as "verification", the instrument that serves each, and the sequencing and subsidy designs that make what they buy estimable.*

**Status: theory research.** Nothing here describes the app or proposes a change to it; `research/README.md` says what a paper is and how one reaches the product. Paper 6 supplies the worn instrument and every condition on it; papers 1 and 3 supply the sequence matching that turns out to carry most of the identification; paper 2 supplies the observer structure this paper both protects and constrains; paper 0a the design it allocates under.

**Perfect-form test.** If the app did not exist, this would still be worth saying because every population statistic ever computed from voluntary self-supplied data is a statistic about accounts rather than about people, the gap between those two objects decomposes into five claims that are almost always treated as one, and the instruments that serve them are so unequal that the honest architecture is the opposite of the one such systems reach for.

**Abstract.** Every axis in this series is self-supplied: the person answers, uploads, wears. A self-supplied axis cannot verify itself, so every count is a count of accounts, every within-person series belongs to whoever held the account that week, and every genome coupling is a coupling to a file rather than to the body it came from. This paper's first move is to refuse the word. "Verification" bundles five claims with different evidence requirements and wildly different instruments: that no two accounts are the same person, that a living body is present now, that it is the same body as before, that it is the subject of the genome on file, and — the one such designs forget — that the body wearing the sensor is the one operating the session where the measurements are actually produced. Once separated, the instruments sort themselves, and not as expected. Duplication across accounts is a one-against-many problem needing tens of bits of discriminating power, which a handful of correlated physiological scalars do not have and which sequence matching supplies with room to spare, inside the data, with no wearable at all; the paper's earlier draft built that detector out of the wrong axis. What the worn stream of paper 6 supplies is the one-against-one problem: this window belongs to the enrolled owner of this account, checked continuously rather than at moments, using a feature that must be chosen to be stable exactly where paper 6's phenotypes are chosen to move. Liveness is an interactive challenge with a latency set by physiology, which bounds what it can gate. Attribution needs an observed sample — and the recurring calibration draw paper 6 already funds is one, which dissolves the tempting architecture of "expensive once, cheap forever" and replaces it with a chain re-anchored on a schedule. Session binding is a separate channel and without it none of the rest reaches an answer. The design conclusions follow from estimators rather than from budgets: attenuation from misattribution is recoverable by randomized rollout order, by a validation subsample, and by corrupting known links at known rates, so a permanently unverified population is not required and the earlier draft's headline conclusion is withdrawn; and a subsidy is an instrument only under an exclusion restriction that money paid to a person whose metabolism is the outcome does not satisfy, so the instrument is the offer's logistics, randomized within stratum, with a cash-only arm to bound what money does directly.

**1 · The problem, stated structurally**

A population statistic is a claim about a set of people. What is computed is a claim about a set of accounts, and the map between the two is unknown in both directions. One person may hold several accounts, so a count is inflated by an unknown factor and a mean is taken over a mixture of people and their copies. One account may be operated by several people, so a series that looks like a person changing is sometimes a person being replaced. And an uploaded file is attached to whoever uploaded it, which is a different claim from the one every genetic reading in this series makes.

The gap is not a nuisance term. It is the ceiling on everything else, and its worst property is that the trace it leaves is not marked as itself: a misattributed genome produces a coupling attenuated toward zero, and attenuation from misattribution is indistinguishable from a smaller true effect by anything computed inside the affected data.

It does not follow — and an earlier draft of this paper said it did — that only an outside instrument can close it. Part of the gap is visible from inside. A sequence is the most individually specific object a person can supply, and two accounts holding the same sequence are the same person with a certainty nothing else in this series approaches. That detector needs no device, no challenge and no payment; it needs only that the files exist, which papers 1, 3 and 6 already require. What genuinely needs an outside instrument is everything about the *present*: that a body is here now, that it is the body from before, and that it is the one at the keyboard. Sorting which part of the problem needs which instrument is most of this paper.

**2 · The two axes alone, and what each actually proves**

*The genome alone is a powerful identifier and a useless presence proof.* A sequence file identifies a body absolutely and is perfectly transferable: the same bytes describe the same body whoever uploads them, and a file is as readable from a person who died as from one who never consented. So it has no liveness and no binding to a holder. What it does have, and what the earlier draft failed to use, is discriminating power against *other files* — enough that duplicate holdings are detectable outright, and enough that degrees of relatedness are readable too, so a relative is distinguishable from a copy rather than confused with one. Its ceiling is the present tense.

*The worn stream alone proves a body, not a person.* A continuous trace with plausible physiological structure is strong evidence that a living body was inside the device, and no evidence about which body, because paper 6's panel reads quantities everybody has. Its usual failure is exactly the attack: the device is removed and worn by somebody else, and every reading afterwards is a valid reading of the wrong person. Its ceiling is identity.

*And neither says anything about the keyboard.* This is the gap both axes miss and the one that matters most for what the app actually collects. The stream is on skin; answers, sealed predictions and ratings are produced through a screen. Every proof either axis can offer is a proof about a body somewhere, and none of them is a proof about the hand typing.

**3 · Five claims, and the instrument that serves each**

*Uniqueness.* No two accounts are the same person. This is a one-against-many problem: at a population of size N the comparisons run as N², so false matches grow quadratically while true duplicate pairs grow linearly, and the per-comparison false-accept rate must fall proportionally to N to keep the two comparable. Tens of bits of stable, independent discriminating power are needed, and that number is the reason the instrument must be the sequence. Four correlated physiological scalars read through a drifting sweat instrument do not carry tens of bits, and a design that tries is deconvolving a true count out of an overwhelming false one. Sequence matching carries them by construction, and the same matching separates a duplicate from a sibling, which no metabolic feature can do.

*Liveness.* A living body is at the point of measurement now. Passive classification of a trace as alive is a marginal-distribution problem and marginals are cheap to synthesize; what resists replay is joint structure under an unpredictable perturbation, which makes liveness an interactive protocol rather than a classifier. It has a property such designs consistently ignore: a *latency*. Paper 6's admissible challenges are a fixed meal or a fixed fast, because exertion moves the sampler and cold starves it, so a metabolic challenge is answered in hours. Liveness therefore cannot gate a moment. It can gate an interval, and any operation that needs proof of presence *now* needs a faster channel than this one.

*Continuity.* The body measured now is the body measured before, on this account. This is one-against-one, which is a different and far easier problem than uniqueness: the question is whether this window matches this account's enrolled owner, not which of a million people it is. Here the worn stream is the right instrument and its advantage over ordinary biometrics is real, because it is checked always rather than at moments, so a handover is a discontinuity in a continuously observed quantity rather than something to be caught at the next gate.

*Attribution.* The body in the chain is the subject of the file. Nothing worn reads DNA, so this needs an observed sample — and paper 6 has already funded a recurring one. That paper requires a reference draw at twice the drift rate to separate sensor decay from physiology, which means a biological sample is taken from this person on a schedule, forever. Supervise it and attribution is re-established at every calibration rather than once at enrolment; leave it unsupervised and it is the single most dangerous operation in the system, because substituting the calibration sample silently re-anchors the person-specific ratio to a new body, which is the one act that overwrites the identifier continuity depends on.

*Session binding.* The body wearing the device is the one producing the observations. None of the four above touches this, and without it a verified person can wear a device while any number of others operate the account — which is both the permanent-delegation attack and the coordinated-account attack, and which no physiology answers. It needs a channel of its own: a proof of co-presence between device and session, or a challenge whose response must be produced inside the session within a window short enough that relaying it is work. This paper does not solve it and does not pretend the other four are worth anything without it; every reading in section 4 is conditional on it.

*Which yields the architecture, and it is not the tempting one.* The tempting architecture is attribution once, continuity forever. It is wrong here for a reason that is a gift rather than a cost: a supervised biological sample recurs anyway, so attribution is a repeated anchor and continuity's job is to span the intervals between anchors rather than to carry a decade. That is a much weaker requirement on the biometric, and it is the requirement section 8 should test. What must be published with a verified statement is therefore not a boolean and not, as an earlier draft had it, the age of its weakest link — an age says nothing about discrimination, and a fresh link over a weak check is worse than an old link over a strong one. It is an identity posterior, or the accumulated log-likelihood ratio the chain's evidence composes to, with the multiplicity of a long chain accounted for, because a chain of many checks gives an adversary many attempts.

**4 · What becomes knowable**

*4.1 The duplication rate, from the sequences.* Where files exist, duplicate holdings are detectable directly and relatedness is readable alongside, so the rate is measured rather than assumed, and it is measured without requiring anybody to wear anything. Two limits are structural and must travel with the number. It is a rate among accounts that supplied a sequence, which is a selected set, so its relation to the whole population is identified only through the sequencing design of section 5. And it counts one person's accounts, not one person's coordination with others, which is a different behaviour and stays invisible.

*4.2 Attenuation correction, with the assumption written down.* A misattributed genome attenuates a coupling toward zero by a factor set by the misattribution rate. An earlier draft proposed estimating it by comparing the same coupling across a verified and an unverified tier, which requires that the true coupling be identical in both — no effect modification by verification status or by anything driving selection into it — and section 6 concedes that selection correlates with age, health, occupation and disposition, every one a plausible moderator. So the cross-tier difference is attenuation plus effect modification plus differential reliability, and reporting it as attenuation is wrong.

Three estimators do the job without that assumption and none needs a permanently unverified population. Randomize the *order* of a rollout and the pre-verification state of each stratum is observed at random times, which recovers the rate as it was. Verify a random subsample of the not-yet-verified and the classical double-sampling estimator applies, with the subsample's randomness supplying the invariance rather than assuming it. And corrupt a random set of known-good links at known rates and the attenuation curve is traced inside the verified population entirely, which also calibrates the other two. The verified population's own residual misattribution — supervised collection has sampling, labelling and custody error of its own — enters all three as a term with a rate rather than being set to zero.

*4.3 A within-person series that belongs to one person, if the features can be chosen apart.* Paper 5's state share, paper 6's aging slope and paper 4's variability signature are all within-person quantities that assume one body throughout and cannot check it. Continuity turns the assumption into an observation — but only if the identifying features and the phenotypic ones are different features, which is a constraint the earlier draft violated by naming paper 6's challenge-response functionals as both. Those functionals are chosen precisely because they move across a decade; a feature that identifies across a decade is one that does not. So the identifying set is selected for small within-person drift against between-person spread over the phenotype's own timescale, and if no such set exists, continuity spans anchors and nothing longer.

The detector also needs a false-positive rate and it does not currently have one. Paper 6's own conditions enumerate the things that step these features: occlusion, which reduces output over hours in a shape that mimics a personal drift; secretion rate, temperature, humidity and season; skin site and wear position; hydration; medications that suppress or raise sweating; and body composition, which is one of the ratio's own determinants and which moves with weight change and pregnancy. Each produces a step, and a detector that reads a step as a handover reproduces the failure it exists to fix with the sign reversed. So a target false-positive rate per person-year is stated, and the detector is evaluated on labelled non-handover perturbations — a medication start, a relocation, a weight change, a cartridge swap — before continuity is claimed.

*4.4 Pairwise structure, on the pairs it actually covers.* A sealed prediction against a duplicate of oneself is not a prediction, and paper 2's decomposition requires the observer to be a different person from the target. Uniqueness supplies that, on pairs where both sides carry the evidence, which is a doubly-selected subgraph on top of the selections paper 2 already models. It defeats invented raters and does nothing against a target recruiting real acquaintances to rate favourably, so collusion is named here as unanswered rather than folded into the guarantee. And a caution paper 2 imposes on this paper rather than the other way round: publishing a tier label beside an aggregate hands the target a new attribute over their own observer set, which is exactly the partitioning that paper forbids, so the label is suppressed on observer aggregates or that paper's release rule is amended deliberately.

*4.5 Who duplicates and who delegates, with one reading marked unavailable.* Rates have correlates, and whether duplication is deception or household practice decides whether an unverified statistic is diluted or biased — dilution is correctable with a rate, bias correlated with the phenotype is correctable only among the people an instrument moves, which section 4.6 states precisely. The household reading is the one this instrument cannot deliver. A household shares ancestry, which compresses sequence distance; and diet, meal timing, hydration, ambient temperature and humidity, and sleep schedule, which are paper 6's own ruler movers and are the inputs to every continuity feature. So all the evidence degrades simultaneously in exactly the population the reading is about, and it is available only if section 8's study returns separation within co-resident relatives.

*4.6 What a subsidy identifies, and under what restriction.* Where the system pays for the device, payment is a treatment and take-up an outcome, and randomizing it is tempting as an instrument for selection into the verified set. The exclusion restriction it needs is that the payment affects the outcome only through take-up, and for this series' outcomes that is false twice over: cash moves diet, food security, stress and sleep directly, and the device itself moves metabolism through paper 6's reactivity condition, since seeing one's own trace changes eating. So the instrument is not the money. It is the offer's logistics — a randomized appointment slot, a queue position, a randomized reminder — which plausibly move take-up and nothing else, randomized within stratum rather than targeted at reluctant strata, since a subsidy assigned where uptake was expected to be low fails independence and is worth less than the design that earned it. A cash-only arm with no device bounds what money does directly. And what the instrument returns is a complier-restricted quantity: the phenotype difference among those the offer moves, with never-takers unidentified, which is a real answer and not the population one.

**5 · The return path, and the design conclusions**

*The genome.* Its couplings stop being couplings to files, and it acquires a second role it was not built for: the uniqueness instrument for the whole system.

*The body.* Paper 6's stream gains a second purpose at no extra hardware, and its calibration draw — a recurring cost that paper treats as a burden — becomes the anchor of the verification chain, which is the best argument for paying it.

*The bank, the instruments and the lenses.* Counts and distributions acquire a denominator that means what it says.

*Ties, observers and any arranged competition.* Pairwise readings become defensible on the pairs that carry evidence, and a competitive axis with anything at stake becomes possible at all.

*The design layer.* Verification events are allocations in paper 0a's currency and they collide with measurement in a way that is now precise rather than vague: paper 6's measurement challenge must be repeated identically for its phenotype to exist, while a security challenge must be unpredictable and, against an adversary that learns, varied. Those are two event classes with two budgets, not one event with a scheduling conflict.

*The first conclusion, which reverses an earlier draft's.* That draft concluded that verification must never be universal, because an unverified population is needed to calibrate the verified one. Section 4.2 shows that is false: randomized rollout order, a validation subsample and known-rate corruption each recover the correction inside a fully verified population. The conclusion was also an argument for less of this paper's own axis, which the series' second rule forbids for good reason. What survives is a sequencing result rather than a rationing one: verify everyone, in randomized order, with a validation subsample carried through the rollout, and the calibration comes from the sequence of the rollout rather than from anyone being left out.

*The second conclusion.* Verification is allocated by strata and randomized within them. Coverage of the strata where misattribution might differ is what the correction's precision depends on; randomization within each stratum is what keeps the offer an instrument. The earlier draft asked for stratified targeting and randomized offers in successive sections without noticing that the first defeats the second.

**6 · What would have to hold**

*The five claims are never substituted for one another*, and every verified statement names which it rests on and publishes an identity posterior rather than a boolean or an age.

*Session binding exists as its own channel.* Without a proof tying the device to the session that produced the observation, the other four claims verify a body somewhere and nothing about the data. Its mechanism is stated with its assumptions, and permanent delegation and coordinated multi-person operation are its threat model rather than physiology's.

*Device attestation is theorized, not deferred.* A sensor that fabricates its own output is answered by no physiological argument, and fabricating at the instrument is the cheapest attack on any physiological verifier, which makes attestation the binding constraint on every rate and correction in section 4 rather than a footnote. It is a separate mechanism with its own trust assumptions — what signs a reading, what the signing key is bound to, and what a compromised unit can produce before the compromise is visible — and every score in section 9 is conditional on it.

*Relatedness is a stratum in every identification claim, not a caveat.* Paper 6 argues the transfer ratio is set by heritable physiology, and a heritable feature has compressed within-family variance, so a first-degree relative sits far closer than a stranger and identical siblings may be indistinguishable. The same heritability that would let a sequence predict the ratio makes the ratio worse at telling relatives apart, and any statement of identification strength is published per relatedness class rather than as one number.

*A biometric template is irrevocable, which is a liability and not a defence.* An earlier draft argued that a physical property cannot be stolen because it is not a secret. The correct property is the opposite: the feature vector sits in a store, is exactly what a breach exfiltrates, and cannot be reissued, so one leak defeats that person's continuity check permanently. Template breach is in the threat model with its re-enrolment consequence, and a system that cannot re-enrol has to say so.

*The calibration draw is an event in the verification chain.* Supervised, it re-anchors attribution on a schedule; unsupervised, it is the re-anchoring attack, in which a substituted sample rebinds the ratio to a new body. There is no third option in which it is merely a measurement.

*Uniqueness runs where a pairwise computation can run.* An all-pairs match over a population is the canonical quantity paper 6 says needs its own primitive, and there is no single custodian in that architecture to run it at. So it is specified as a named multiparty or enclave protocol with its trust assumptions, its leakage — a returned rate is still a function of every pair — and its output-privacy budget, and any operation returning identities rather than rates is separately consented with its own record.

*Coercion and delegation are behaviours as well as attacks.* A device worn under pressure or as a favour produces a technically valid chain attached to the wrong intent, physiology does not distinguish them, and a verification rate reported without that sentence is compliance reported as consent.

*Exclusion is measured, not assumed away.* Whoever cannot or will not wear a device is a population correlated with age, health, occupation and trust; the rollout of section 5 keeps them in the statistics by reaching them in randomized order rather than by leaving them out.

*Consent is per purpose and the purposes differ.* Being measured, being identified, being matched against other accounts and contributing to discovery are four decisions. A subsidy buys none of them, which is the condition a paid device most needs because payment and consent are easiest to confuse in the population a subsidy reaches.

*Paper 6's measurement conditions apply unchanged*, since this is the same instrument: the per-analyte transfer model, the compartment, the ruler, the wear-time selection at person level. Two of them bite here specifically. The panel is thin as a feature source — one analyte has no blood transfer ratio at all, one turns over too slowly, one sits near the detector's floor when fed — so the identifying vector is close to a single scalar plus timing structure, and a circadian feature needs a sub-daily resolution paper 6 lists as a number the hardware still owes. And the days people remove a device are the informative days, so the verified series is systematically unverified across exactly the occasions the readings want, while re-binding after a long gap happens when drift is largest and discrimination lowest.

*Re-binding has a decision rule, not a gesture.* The rule is a function of gap length and of whether the wear site changed, and below a stated identity posterior re-binding requires a supervised event rather than a challenge — because a challenge proves liveness and not identity, and re-attachment at a new site is indistinguishable from re-attachment by a new person.

**7 · The requirements the design generates**

*A session-binding channel*, built before any of the rest is worth anything.

*Attestation with stated trust assumptions*, for the same reason.

*Sequences broadly enough held that uniqueness is measurable*, since the duplication rate is otherwise a rate among the sequenced.

*A randomized rollout order, recorded*, which is what supplies the attenuation correction and replaces the withheld arm.

*A validation subsample carried through the rollout*, randomly verified ahead of its stratum.

*A known-rate corruption calibration*, run deliberately on links whose truth is known, which traces the attenuation curve without waiting for the rollout.

*Randomized offer logistics within stratum*, with a cash-only arm, since that and not the payment is the instrument.

*Supervised calibration draws*, which is the recurring anchor, and the capacity to perform them at the cadence paper 6's drift sets.

*An identifying feature set chosen apart from the phenotypic one*, with its drift measured over the phenotypes' own timescale.

*A stated false-positive budget for discontinuity detection*, evaluated against labelled non-handover perturbations.

*Identification strength published per relatedness class*, and a re-enrolment path for a breached template.

None is a reason to want less. Each is what a verified population looks like from the inside.

**8 · What would decide it**

*Can the stream tell one person from their own past, over the interval between anchors?* This is the one-against-one question and it is the only one the stream must answer, which is a far weaker requirement than the earlier draft imposed. A cohort wears the device; a feature set chosen for stability is extracted; a held-out window is scored against the account's enrolled owner. What is estimated is a false-accept and false-reject pair as a function of window length, elapsed time since the last anchor, and relatedness class — unrelated, first-degree, identical sibling — with co-residence as a named stratum because that is where 4.5's reading lives. If the false-reject rate at a usable false-accept rate is high at gaps as short as the calibration interval, continuity does not span anchors and the architecture reduces to supervised events alone.

*And the honest note about what such a study cannot reach.* A uniqueness check needs a per-comparison false-accept rate that falls with population size, and a cohort of hundreds observes zero false matches at any such rate; the tail is extrapolated from a parametric model of the feature distribution's extremes, not measured. That is the arithmetic that moved uniqueness onto the sequence, and any future claim that a metabolic feature set does uniqueness states its tail model and validates it rather than quoting a within-cohort rate.

*How well does a sequence predict the ratio?* This is attribution's screening strength and it is not heritability, which is an upper bound the predictor will not reach; it is the predictor's out-of-sample explained variance and its calibration. Estimating it needs a training cohort whose genome-to-body binding is already established by supervised collection, which is the population the screen was meant to economize, so the screen is a refinement of an anchored system rather than a substitute for anchoring. Its cohort size is a requirement, not an afterthought.

*And the falsification an adversary runs.* Resistance to synthesized liveness responses is a red-team estimand — the effort required to produce a passing response under unpredictable challenge timing, measured by people trying — re-estimated as synthesis improves. A protocol whose resistance was measured once has unknown resistance now.

**9 · The potential, priced with each score's own condition**

A ten is a dataset after which a field's standard design changes. Every score below is conditional on session binding and attestation existing, because without either the paper verifies a body and not an observation.

*Duplication and delegation rates in voluntary populations.* Six, conditional on sequences being widely enough held. These are quantities everyone assumes and nobody has, and the sequence route makes them measurable without new hardware — which also means the finding is cheaper and smaller than the earlier draft implied.

*Attenuation correction across the series' genome couplings.* This is where the value concentrates and it needs its own arithmetic rather than a multiplier. Correcting attenuation scales a coefficient up by the inverse of the attenuation factor and scales its variance with it, and an attenuation factor estimated on a small validation subsample adds variance of its own, so a correction can widen every interval it touches. The reading is worth having when the rate is large enough that the bias exceeds the added variance, and the design's job is to estimate the rate precisely enough that it does. Priced honestly: five, and higher only where the duplication rate turns out to be substantial.

*Continuity for within-person trajectories.* Five, conditional on an identifying feature set existing apart from the phenotypic one and on a false-positive budget being met. If the study of section 8 returns poor separation at the calibration interval, two.

*Physiological biometrics as a field.* Five: a continuously worn, anchor-spanning one-against-one check with a stated relatedness curve is a different object from match-at-a-moment biometrics, and the curve is the finding whether it is favourable or not.

*The instrument design for voluntary cohorts.* Six as a methods contribution, and it does not depend on section 8 at all: randomized rollout order as an attenuation estimator, and randomized offer logistics with a cash-only arm as a selection instrument, generalize to every voluntary measurement programme anyone runs.

The paper's expected value is dominated by two things that are not its own measurements: whether session binding can be built, and whether the duplication rate is large enough for the correction to be worth its variance.

**10 · Open questions the data would have to answer**

Whether a device can be bound to a session at all without a second device or a second person, which decides whether any of this reaches an answer. Whether an identifying feature set exists that is stable over the interval between anchors while the phenotypic features move, since the two requirements pull on the same physiology. How the identification curve degrades with relatedness, and whether identical siblings are separable at all. What the discontinuity detector's false-positive rate is against paper 6's enumerated ruler movers, which decides whether continuity protects a trajectory or corrupts it. How far a sequence predicts the transfer ratio out of sample, and how large the anchored training cohort must be. Whether duplication is deception or household practice, which the instrument is least able to see in exactly the households it concerns. Whether the exclusion restriction survives for any offer-logistics instrument, or whether every route to the verified tier moves the phenotype somehow. And what a breached template costs a person for whom re-enrolment is not available, which is the one failure in this paper with no technical remedy at all.
