# One living person, and whose genome it is

*Paper 7 of the axiom series: continuous biological verification as the axis that makes every other axis a statement about people rather than about accounts, and the subsidy that pays for it read as a randomized instrument.*

**Status: theory research.** Nothing here describes the app or proposes a change to it; `research/README.md` says what a paper is and how one reaches the product. Paper 6 supplies the worn molecular stream this paper turns to a second purpose; papers 1, 3 and 6 supply the genome crossings it protects; paper 2 supplies the observer structure it makes real; paper 0a the design it allocates under.

**Perfect-form test.** If the app did not exist, this would still be worth saying because every population statistic ever computed from voluntary self-supplied data is a statistic about accounts rather than about people, the gap between those two objects is not estimable from the data itself, and a continuously worn biological instrument is the first thing that could close it without a state, a document or a photograph — while creating, in the same act, the most identifying record a person can hold.

**Abstract.** Every axis in this series is self-supplied: the person answers, the person uploads, the person wears. A self-supplied axis cannot verify itself, so every count is a count of accounts, every within-person series belongs to whoever held the account that week, and every genome coupling is a coupling to a file rather than to the body it came from. This paper argues that a worn molecular stream, present for a different reason, closes that gap — and that closing it is worth more than anything the stream measures on its own. Verification is first decomposed into three claims that are usually bundled and have completely different evidence requirements: that a living body is present now, that it is the same body as before, and that it is the subject of the genome on file. The stream supplies the first through an interactive challenge whose timing the wearer cannot predict, because a replayed trace has to reproduce a joint physiological structure rather than a marginal; it supplies the second through an inversion this series should have expected, since the person-specific transfer ratio that paper 6 spends its length controlling for is a stable biological property of an individual, which makes the nuisance parameter of one reading the biometric of another; and it supplies the third only in part, because a metabolite sensor reads no DNA — so attribution is established once under observation and then held by continuity, which is what converts a one-time cost into a standing guarantee. From there the paper states what verification makes every other axis worth: duplication and delegation become measured rates rather than unknown ones, the attenuation they impose on every genome coupling becomes correctable, a within-person trajectory becomes one person's, and pairwise structure — duels, observer ratings, and any competition the system arranges — stops being defensible only by assumption. Two design conclusions follow and neither is a concession to cost. Verification must be a labelled tier and never a gate, because the unverified population is the thing the verified population exists to calibrate, and gating destroys the comparison that gives verification its value. And where the system pays for the device, the payment is not a cost to be minimized but the strongest instrument in the series: randomize the subsidy and take-up acquires an instrument, which turns selection into the verified tier from an assumption into an estimate. The conditions are a threat model with the attacks physiology cannot answer named honestly, a removal protocol, the custody of a cross-account biometric join, and the heritability that sets attribution's strength — which is an open question of paper 6, so that paper's instrument finding is this paper's identification strength.

**1 · The problem, stated structurally**

A population statistic is a claim about a set of people. What is actually computed is a claim about a set of accounts, and the map between the two sets is unknown in both directions. One person may hold several accounts, so a count is inflated by an unknown factor and a mean is taken over a mixture of people and their copies. One account may be held by several people over time, so a series that looks like a person changing is sometimes a person being replaced. And an uploaded file — a genome above all — is attached to whoever uploaded it, which is a different claim from the one every genetic reading in this series actually makes.

The gap is not a nuisance term. It is the ceiling on everything else, and it has a property that makes it worse than ordinary measurement error: it is not estimable from the data that suffers from it. A duplicated account is not marked. A delegated session is not marked. A misattributed genome produces a coupling attenuated toward zero, and the attenuation is indistinguishable from a smaller true effect. No sample size, no model and no amount of cleverness recovers a rate that leaves no trace. Only an outside instrument does.

The instruments the world uses for this are documents, photographs and payment records, and each is an identity claim about a person's relationship to an institution rather than about a body. A document proves someone was issued a document. A photograph proves an image exists. A payment proves an account was funded. None of them proves a living human being is present at the moment a measurement is taken, and none of them binds that human to a genome.

**2 · The two axes alone, and what neither proves**

*The genome alone proves nothing about who holds it.* A sequence file is the most individually specific object a person can produce and it is also perfectly transferable. It has no liveness — a file is as readable from a person who died as from a person who never consented — and no binding to a holder, because the same bytes describe the same body no matter who uploads them. So the genome's identifying power runs the wrong way for this problem: it identifies a body absolutely, and says nothing about whether that body is the one operating the account. In its own right it is not an identity instrument at all but a *sample* instrument: it identifies whoever the sample came from, and everything therefore turns on how the sample was collected.

*The worn stream alone proves a body, not a person.* A continuous metabolic trace with plausible structure is strong evidence that a living body was inside the device. It is no evidence at all about which body, because the panel of paper 6 reads quantities that everybody has. Worse, its usual failure mode is exactly the attack: the device is removed and worn by somebody else, and every reading afterwards is a valid reading of the wrong person. Alone, the stream's ceiling is liveness without identity.

*Each axis's ceiling is the other axis, and unusually literally here.* The genome identifies a body and cannot tell whether it is present. The stream shows a body is present and cannot tell which. Crossed, and only crossed, they support the claim the whole series needs: this living body, present now, is the subject of that file.

**3 · The crossing, and the three claims that are usually one word**

"Verification" bundles three claims with completely different evidence requirements, and almost every failure in this area is the substitution of one for another.

*Liveness.* A living human body is at the point of measurement now — as against a replayed recording, a synthesized trace, a bench rig, an animal, or a body that has stopped. This is a claim about the present moment and it decays instantly; a liveness proof from yesterday proves nothing about today.

*Continuity.* The body measured now is the same body measured before, in an unbroken chain back to some anchoring event. This is a claim about a sequence, and its strength is the strength of its weakest link rather than of its most recent one.

*Attribution.* The body in the chain is the subject of the genome on file. This is a claim about a sample-collection event in the past, and no amount of present-tense evidence establishes it if the collection was unobserved.

Liveness without continuity gives a live human at every session who may be a different one each time. Continuity without attribution gives one consistent person whose file may be someone else's. Attribution without liveness gives a matching sequence from a sample that could have come from anywhere, including from a relative, which is the one substitution a sequence match nearly tolerates.

*How the stream supplies liveness: by asking, not by watching.* Passive classification of a trace as "alive" is a marginal-distribution problem, and marginal distributions are cheap to synthesize. What is expensive to synthesize is joint structure under an unpredictable perturbation. A living body's channels are coupled by physiology: secretion rate, the analytes that scale from blood and the analyte the gland makes itself do not move independently, the coupling has a shape, and the whole system carries a circadian phase. So liveness is an interactive protocol rather than a classifier. The system issues a challenge whose timing the wearer cannot anticipate — the same fixed load paper 6 uses as a measurement, or a prompted brief fast — and checks that a response arrives with the right latency and the right joint shape across channels. A recording cannot answer a question it was not asked, and this is the only property that resists replay. It also means liveness has a *rate*: it is established at each challenge and decays between them, so a system that needs continuous liveness is paying for challenges continuously, and a system that needs it only at moments of consequence issues them there.

*How the stream supplies continuity: the nuisance becomes the biometric.* Paper 6 spends its length controlling for a person-specific transfer ratio — the factor relating what the sensor sees to what the body holds, set by gland density, secretion capacity, skin barrier properties and body composition, stable within a person over some horizon, and argued there to be substantially heritable. Everything that makes it ruinous for measurement makes it excellent for identification: it is a stable biological property of the individual, it is measured continuously and for free by an instrument already present, and it is not a secret that can be stolen because it is not a secret at all — it is a physical property of a body, which is what a biometric is. The same is true of the shape of a person's challenge response, of their circadian phase, and of the joint structure across channels.

This is the inversion the series should have expected and did not: **the same quantity is noise in one reading and signal in another**, and a paper that treats it only as noise has thrown away an axis. Continuity is then the ordinary biometric problem, with one enormous advantage over the usual kind. A fingerprint is checked at moments; a worn stream is checked always. So a swap is not something to be caught at the next check — it is a *discontinuity* in a continuously observed quantity, and the strength of the method is the size of the between-person spread in these features against their within-person drift, which is the same ratio paper 6 needs for its own purposes and would estimate anyway.

*How attribution is supplied, and where it is not.* A metabolite sensor reads no DNA, so nothing worn can establish by itself that a body is the subject of a file. Two routes exist and their strengths are very different.

The first is a supervised collection: the sequencing sample is taken while the subject is observed, and the observation binds body to sample. This is expensive per person, happens once, and is the only route that gives attribution outright.

The second is a predicted-versus-measured check, and it exists only because of paper 6's own finding. If the transfer ratio and the gland physiology under it are partly heritable, then the genome predicts a distribution over what the device should measure, and the measured value can be scored against it. This is a weak check — it is a continuous prediction with a wide interval, not a match — and its strength is set exactly by how much of the ratio's variance is heritable, which paper 6 lists as an open question about its instrument. So one paper's nuisance-parameter heritability is the next paper's identification strength, and the same experiment answers both. Its honest use is as a screen that flags gross mismatch, never as a positive identification.

*Which yields the architecture.* Attribution is expensive and happens once. Continuity is cheap and happens always. Liveness is cheap per challenge and decays between them. So the standing guarantee is: attribution established once under observation, held thereafter by continuity, refreshed at consequence by liveness. Verification is not a state a person is in. It is a chain with a beginning, a maintenance cost, and a moment at which it can break — and the design question is not whether someone is verified but *how old the weakest link is*, which is a number and should be published as one wherever a verified statistic is.

**4 · What becomes knowable**

*4.1 The rates themselves, which are currently not quantities at all.* Duplication and delegation are today unmeasured because they leave no trace. With a verified subpopulation they become estimable, and by a route that does not require catching anyone: the same biometric that binds a person to an account matches that person across accounts, so the duplication rate within the verified tier is directly observed rather than inferred. Delegation shows as a discontinuity rate in the continuity chain. These are the first honest numbers anybody would have for either, and they are findings about voluntary populations in general rather than about one system.

*4.2 Attenuation correction for every genome coupling in the series.* A misattributed genome attenuates a coupling toward zero by a factor that depends on the misattribution rate, and the factor is not estimable inside the affected data. It is estimable *across tiers*: the same coupling computed in the verified tier and in the unverified tier differs by the attenuation, so the verified tier calibrates the unverified one. This is the sentence that decides the design in section 5 and it is worth stating exactly: the value of a verified sample is not that its own statistics are clean, it is that it is the only thing that says what the unverified statistics mean. Papers 1, 3 and 6 all publish couplings that need this and none of them can supply it.

*4.3 A within-person trajectory that belongs to one person.* Paper 5's state share, paper 6's aging slope, paper 1's trajectory phenotypes and paper 4's variability signature are all within-person quantities, which is to say all of them assume the series belongs to one body throughout. None can check it. A series that changed hands does not produce noise; it produces a *step*, and a step in the middle of a series is read as change in the person. Continuity turns that assumption into an observation, and the aging slope of paper 6 — the reading most vulnerable to it, because it spans the most years — gains the check it most needs.

*4.4 Pairwise structure that is real rather than assumed.* A sealed duel against a duplicate of oneself is not a duel. An observer rating supplied by the target is a fabricated observation, and paper 2's whole structure — the population's answer, the category's answer, and what only acquaintance supplies — decomposes a prediction that must come from a different person. A competition the system arranges, with stakes and a ranking, is worth exactly nothing if entrants can be duplicated, and it is the axis on this list with the least tolerance for the problem, because a competition converts the flaw directly into a prize. Verified personhood is what makes every pairwise and competitive reading a reading rather than a hope.

*4.5 Who duplicates and who delegates, as a finding about people.* Once the rates exist they have correlates, and the correlates are substantive. Whether duplication concentrates in particular cohorts, whether delegation is a household practice rather than a deception, whether a shared account is one person's or a family's — these are readings about how people actually use systems, and they are also exactly the covariates that decide whether an unverified statistic is biased or merely diluted. Dilution is correctable with a rate. Bias correlated with the phenotype is not, and it is the case worth knowing about.

*4.6 What a subsidy buys, measured rather than argued.* Where the system pays for the device, the payment is a treatment and take-up is an outcome. Randomize the subsidy across recruits and take-up acquires an instrument: the induced variation in who ends up verified is exogenous, so selection into the verified tier becomes an estimate instead of an assumption, and the same instrument identifies whether the phenotypes themselves differ between those who would and would not accept. This costs nothing that was not being spent anyway. It is the strongest identification opportunity in the series and it exists only because a device has a price.

**5 · The return path, and the two design conclusions**

*The genome.* Its couplings stop being couplings to files. It also gains an unusual second role: as the object that supervised collection binds, it becomes the anchor of the whole chain rather than one more uploaded column.

*The body.* Paper 6's stream gains a second purpose that costs no extra hardware and pays for a share of the device, and its nuisance parameter gains a use.

*The bank, the instruments and the lenses.* Every count and every distribution acquires a denominator that means what it says, and every published number gains a tier label.

*Ties, observers and arranged competitions.* Pairwise readings become defensible, and a competitive axis becomes possible at all.

*The design layer.* Verification events are allocations in paper 0a's currency, and they collide with measurement: a liveness challenge is also a load, so the system either spends one event on both — efficient, but verification timing is then confounded with phenotype timing — or spends two and keeps them clean. That is a real allocation choice and the paper does not pretend it is free.

*The first conclusion: a labelled tier, never a gate.* The strong temptation is to require verification for participation. The theory says do not, and the reason is not fairness or cost but identification. The unverified population is the object the verified population exists to calibrate; gate participation and the comparison disappears, and with it every attenuation estimate of 4.2 and every selection estimate of 4.6. A fully verified population is not the ideal case with the budget removed — it is a population that has lost the ability to say what verification was worth or whom it excluded. So a deliberately unverified arm is a design requirement rather than a concession, and every published statistic states the tier it was computed on and the age of the weakest link behind it.

*The second conclusion: allocate verification, do not maximize it.* If verification's value is calibration, then it obeys calibration's arithmetic: precision in the correction comes from the verified sample's size and, far more, from its *spread across the strata where misattribution might differ*. A verified tier that is large but concentrated in the people most willing to be verified corrects nothing where correction is needed. So verification is allocated deliberately — stratified across the cohorts whose rates might differ, with the randomized subsidy of 4.6 as what reaches the reluctant strata — and the target is coverage of the strata rather than a fraction of the population. This follows from the estimator and would hold with unlimited money.

**6 · What would have to hold**

*The three claims are never substituted for one another.* Every verified statement names which of liveness, continuity and attribution it rests on, and the age of the weakest link in its chain. A system that reports "verified" as a boolean has thrown away the only part of the information that decays.

*The threat model is written down, including the attacks physiology cannot answer.* Duplication is answered by cross-account biometric matching. Delegation is answered by continuity, provided removal is treated as a security event. Replay and synthesis are answered by unpredictable interactive challenge and by nothing else. Genome substitution at enrolment is answered by supervised collection and only weakly by the heritable-ratio screen — and a close relative's sample is the substitution that screen is least able to catch, which matters because a close relative is also the most available sample. A compromised sensor that fabricates its own output is answered by none of the above: no physiological argument reaches below the instrument, so device attestation is a separate mechanism with separate assumptions, and a paper that lets a physiology argument stand in for it is claiming something it has not got.

*Removal is a security event and re-attachment requires re-binding.* Continuity is a chain and a removed device breaks it. The protocol therefore records removal, treats the gap as unverified time rather than as missing data, and re-establishes binding on re-attachment by challenge — which is also the point at which the wear-gap conditions of papers 5 and 6 apply, because the days a person removes a device are not random days.

*Coercion and delegation are behaviours, not just attacks.* A device can be worn under pressure or worn as a favour, and both produce a technically valid chain attached to the wrong intent. Nothing in the physiology distinguishes them, and a paper that reports a verification rate without saying so is reporting compliance as consent.

*The cross-account join is the most dangerous operation in the system.* Matching a biometric across accounts is precisely what detects duplication and precisely what defeats every separation the rest of the architecture maintains: it is a global identifier computed over people who each consented to a local one. So it runs at the custodian, returns a rate rather than a list wherever a rate is what is needed, and any operation that returns identities is a separate, separately consented act with its own record. A verified population is the most re-identifying dataset this series has proposed, and it is proposed in the same paper as the genome that makes it more so.

*Exclusion is measured, not assumed away.* Whoever cannot or will not wear a device is a population, and it is not a random one — it correlates with age, health, occupation, disposition and trust. The tier labelling of section 5 is what keeps them in the statistics rather than out of them, and the subsidy instrument of 4.6 is what estimates how they differ.

*Attribution strength is inherited from an open question, not asserted.* The heritable-ratio screen is exactly as strong as the ratio's heritability, which paper 6 does not know. Until that number exists, attribution rests entirely on supervised collection and the screen is a flag, and any claim that the device alone proves whose genome it is, is a claim about an unmeasured quantity.

*Consent is per purpose and the purposes are genuinely different.* Consent to be measured is not consent to be identified; consent to be identified is not consent to be matched against other accounts; consent to contribute to discovery is a third thing. A device accepted for one is not permission for the others, and a subsidy does not buy any of them — which is the condition a paid device most needs, because payment and consent are easy to confuse in exactly the population a subsidy reaches.

*The measurement conditions of paper 6 apply unchanged*, because this is the same instrument: the transfer model per analyte, the compartment named, the ruler modelled, the wear-time selection at person level, and the horizon over which the identifying features themselves are stable — for a biometric that drifts, the re-binding cadence is set by the drift the same way the calibration cadence is.

**7 · The requirements the design generates**

*A verified tier stratified rather than sized.* The target is coverage of every stratum in which the misattribution rate might differ — age, country, tenure in the system, and the cohorts of paper 4 — rather than a headline fraction. A verified tier that is large and homogeneous corrects less than a small one that is spread.

*A deliberately unverified arm, maintained rather than tolerated*, because it is the comparison that produces every number in 4.2 and 4.6.

*Randomized subsidy levels*, recorded per recruit, which is what makes take-up an instrument rather than a selection problem. The randomization has to be real and pre-committed; a subsidy offered where uptake was expected to be low is an instrument correlated with the outcome and is worth less than no instrument at all.

*Supervised collection capacity*, since attribution can only be established by an observed act, and the number of such acts the system can perform is the true bound on the verified tier's size.

*A challenge budget*, because liveness decays and every refresh is an allocation that competes with measurement, and because challenges are also loads.

*A re-binding protocol*, with removal recorded, gaps labelled as unverified time, and re-attachment challenged.

*The biometric's own validation*, which is the identification experiment of section 8 and precedes every claim above it.

*Custody built before the first cross-account match*, not after, and a published record of every operation that returned identities rather than rates.

None is a reason to want less. Each is what a verified population looks like from the inside.

**8 · What would decide it**

*Can the stream tell people apart at all, and for how long?* The experiment is an identification study and it is cheap: a cohort wears the device; features are extracted from each person's stable structure — the transfer ratio, the challenge-response shape, the circadian phase, the cross-channel coupling; and a held-out window is matched against the enrolled set. What is estimated is not an accuracy but a curve: the identification rate as a function of the population size it must discriminate within, the window length available, and the elapsed time since enrolment. Continuity's strength is the between-person spread against the within-person drift, so the same study returns the re-binding cadence directly. If a held-out day cannot pick its owner out of a few hundred, continuity is not available and the architecture of section 3 collapses to supervised events only.

*How much of the transfer ratio is heritable?* This is paper 6's open question about its own instrument, and it is this paper's attribution strength. The same ninety-day study that estimates the ratio per person, run on a sample containing relatives, returns it. A low number means attribution rests entirely on supervised collection forever; a high one means the device screens gross mismatch by itself, which changes what supervised capacity has to be built.

*And the falsification that matters most*, because it is the one an adversary runs: can a plausible trace be synthesized well enough to pass a challenge? The honest form of this is a red-team estimand rather than a claim — the protocol's resistance is stated as the effort required to produce a passing response under unpredictable challenge timing, measured by people trying, and it is re-estimated as the models available for synthesis improve. A liveness protocol whose resistance was measured once is a protocol whose resistance is unknown now.

**9 · The potential, priced against what those studies would return**

A ten on this scale is a dataset after which a field's standard design changes. Each score is conditional, because the identification study of section 8 sits underneath all of them.

*The credibility of every other axis.* This is the one that matters and it is not a field score but a multiplier: nothing else in the series moves every published number at once. Conditional on the identification study returning usable continuity, the couplings of papers 1, 3, 5 and 6 gain an attenuation correction that cannot be obtained any other way, and the within-person readings gain a check they currently do without. Priced as a field contribution in its own right — the measurement of duplication and delegation rates in voluntary populations, with their correlates — a seven, because these are quantities nobody has and everybody assumes.

*Identity and biometrics as a field.* Six: a continuously-worn physiological biometric with an interactive liveness protocol is a different object from the match-at-a-moment biometrics in use, and the identification curve of section 8 is the finding, favourable or not.

*Pairwise and competitive structure.* Five on its own and much more as an enabler, since an arranged competition among strangers is not a measurable object at all without it.

*The subsidy instrument.* Six as a methodological contribution, because a pre-committed randomized subsidy in a voluntary measurement programme is a clean instrument for a selection problem that is normally assumed away, and it generalizes to every voluntary cohort anyone runs.

Overall the paper is worth more than its own measurements, which is unusual and is the point: it produces few readings and it makes every other reading in the series mean what it says. If the identification study fails, almost none of it survives, and the honest expected value is dominated by that one curve.

**10 · Open questions the data would have to answer**

Whether a worn molecular stream discriminates individuals at population scale or only within small groups, which the identification study answers and on which everything rests. How fast the identifying features drift, which sets the re-binding cadence and decides whether continuity is a standing property or a frequently renewed one. How much of the transfer ratio is heritable, which sets attribution's strength and is the same number paper 6 needs for a different reason. Whether liveness challenges can be made unpredictable enough to resist synthesis by models that will be better than today's, and how that resistance is re-measured rather than assumed. Whether duplication is deception or household practice, since the correction differs. Whether the phenotypes of those who decline verification differ from those who accept, which the randomized subsidy identifies and which decides whether the unverified tier is diluted or biased. And whether a verified tier can be maintained without becoming the population — because the moment verification is worth something to the person rather than to the system, everyone acquires it, and the comparison this paper is built on is gone.
