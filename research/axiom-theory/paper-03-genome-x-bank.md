# The genetics of what was never asked

*Paper 3 of the axiom series: imported genomes crossed with a question bank of unbounded depth.*

**Status: theory research.** Nothing here describes the app or proposes a change to it; `research/README.md` says what a paper is and how one reaches the product. Paper 0 carries the general argument, paper 0a the design layer, and paper 1 the keyed-ability crossing this paper partials on.

**Perfect-form test.** If the app did not exist, this would still be worth saying because genotyping is cheap and phenotyping is expensive, so every genotyped population has been measured on a short list of traits chosen in advance, and any population in which asking is the product and the list has no end would carry a genetics of everything it asked.

**Abstract.** A genetic architecture is read from a trait measured on many genotyped people, and the traits that have one are the traits someone decided in advance to measure. Phenotyping costs attention per item, so genotyped cohorts are wide and shallow: a committee's list, asked once. Everything never on a list has no genetics, and the lists are short and conservative by construction. This paper describes a population that answers an unbounded bank of items over years and holds its own genome files, and argues that the crossing inverts the shape of the field: depth set by the bank rather than by a budget, items admitted by what they add rather than by what a committee predicted, and a battery that grows after the genotypes exist. Four things become knowable. An item-level map of which everyday preferences, habits and opinions track common genetic variation and which track circumstance and culture, comparative within one estimator and one population. The genetic structure of the bank's own factors, derived from how people actually answer rather than authored, and the places where genetic and phenotypic structure disagree, which is where culture, not disposition, correlates two items. How the heritable share of an opinion varies with country at fixed ancestry, which is where culture overrides disposition. And which of the bank's genetic signal is ability genetics in disguise, read through paper 1's keyed score. The return path gives the bank a tag per item that no bank computes about itself, gives the derived trait scales an external test of whether their structure is genetically coherent, and gives the design layer a currency for admitting items: genetic information beyond what the items already held recover. Two conditions are the paper's own. The serving policy must not use the genome to choose which discovery items a person is asked, or the sample for every item is selected on the variable being studied. And an unbounded bank against a finite output-privacy budget cannot publish per-variant statistics per item; it publishes low-dimensional summaries, and the budget is spent by design.

**1 · The problem, stated structurally**

To read the genetic architecture of a trait you need the trait measured on many people who are also genotyped. Genotyping is cheap and getting cheaper; a file from a consumer service costs less than a meal and covers the common variation. Phenotyping is not cheap, because every item costs a person's attention, and attention is the one input that does not scale with a budget. So a genotyped population is phenotyped on a list, the list is short because each item is expensive, and it is chosen in advance by whoever funds the cohort, which means it is conservative: the traits already known to matter, measured the way they were measured before.

Two consequences follow, and both are structural rather than anyone's failing. Everything never on a list has no genetics, and the set of never-listed traits is most of what people are like: what they eat for breakfast, whether they believe in luck, how they take being interrupted, what they find funny, how they argue, what they collect. And the list cannot learn. A cohort phenotyped in one decade is analysed in the next on the items it happened to ask, so the battery is fixed before anyone knows which items would have carried signal.

A population in which asking is the product has the opposite shape. Its battery is as deep as its bank, the bank has no end, and items are admitted by what they add, continuously, after the genotypes already exist. Whatever is worth asking can be asked next week, of the same genotyped people.

**2 · The two axes alone, and where each stops**

The bank is a set of unkeyed items with no ceiling on its size, answered at depth over years. Alone it measures what people say, and with enough items it recovers the structure of what they say: which items go together, which factors organise them, how the factors vary by profile fact. It cannot say why any of that structure exists. A factor may be a disposition, a culture, an age, an economy, or a way of reading questions, and nothing inside the answers separates the cases. Its ceiling is cause.

The genome is a fact about the person that predicts nothing without a phenotype on the same people, and inherits the mixture of whatever phenotype it was trained on. Its ceiling is meaning: it needs something measured, on the same people, to be about. What it lacks is exactly what the bank has too much of.

Each axis's ceiling is the other axis.

**3 · The crossing, and the two roles an item can play**

Paper 0 states the general argument; three of its parts are specific here.

A covariance is a third measurement. For each item, the covariance between answers and common genetic variation across a population is a fact neither axis contains: how much of the variation in this answer tracks genotype, and which other items' genotype-tracking variation it shares. The first is an item's heritable share; the second is genetic correlation between items; and across an unbounded bank both form a map that exists only in the pair.

Depth substitutes for breadth. Many items answered by the same person are not independent measurements; they are correlated views of a smaller number of latent dispositions. That correlation is the asset. A latent disposition measured by many items is measured more reliably than any item measures it, so its genetic signal is estimated more precisely than any single item's; and items impute each other, so a person who answered half the bank supplies information about the other half. Depth therefore raises the effective size of the genotyped sample per latent trait without a single additional person, which is the compounding paper 0 names, stated for genetics.

An item plays one of two roles, and the roles must not be confused. A placement item is served to place a person in the between-person geometry, and for that purpose the genome may serve as a prior, so that fewer items place the person: paper 0's cycle. A discovery item is served so that its genetics can be read, and for that purpose the genome may not influence whether or when the person is asked it, because a sample that answered an item partly because of their genotype is selected on the variable being studied, and the item's heritable share is biased by the selection. The serving policy's own record, paper 0a's section 4.6, makes such selection estimable in principle, but estimable at a cost is not the same as absent by design. So every item carries its role, the discovery role is served blind to the genome, and an item may change role only forward, from discovery to placement once its genetics is read.

**4 · What becomes knowable**

*4.1 The item-level map of heritable and circumstantial.* For every item in the bank, the share of its variation that tracks common genetic variation, estimated by one method on one population and compared only within that frame. A heritable share is a property of an estimator, a phenotype definition and a population, and moves by a factor of two or more when any of the three moves, so the map is comparative rather than absolute: it ranks the bank's items against each other on one scale, and never quotes a bare figure as a fact about a trait. On that scale, items separate. Some carry a heritable share in the range that dispositions do: they are asking about something the person is. Some carry none: they are asking about the person's circumstances, their culture, their year, or how they read the question. That separation, item by item across everything anyone ever asked, is the map, and the value of the map is that it sorts items no committee ever funded, which is most of the bank. What would have to hold is the list of section 6, and one thing more: the map is ancestry-conditional, because an item's heritable share can differ by ancestry for reasons of tagging rather than of biology, so it is read within ancestry before across.

*4.2 The genetic structure of the bank's own factors, and where it disagrees with the phenotypic one.* A bank of unbounded depth defines its own factors from how people answer, not from what an author named, so the factors are not authored constructs but recovered structure. Crossing with the genome asks of that structure two questions. Which recovered factors are genetically coherent: do the items that load together also share genetic architecture, so that the factor is a disposition and not an artefact of wording or culture. And where the two structures disagree: two items correlated in what people say but not in what their genomes share are correlated by culture, education or circumstance; two items uncorrelated in what people say but sharing genetic architecture are two expressions of one disposition that the culture has pulled apart. The disagreement map is the finding. It says which of the bank's phenotypic structure is disposition and which is the world, and it is available only because the bank is deep enough to recover structure and the genome is present to test it.

*4.3 Where culture overrides disposition.* Held at one ancestry and read across countries, an item's heritable share can change. Where it falls, the environment in that country is doing more of the deciding on that item; where it holds, the disposition is expressed the same way everywhere. Across the bank this gives a map of which everyday traits are cultural in the strict sense, that culture changes how much of their variation is heritable, and which are dispositional wherever they are asked. The two-experiment discipline of paper 1's section 4.2 applies without change: across countries at fixed ancestry reads culture and environment, across ancestries within a country reads tagging, and a population spread across both is what makes the experiments available on one bank.

*4.4 How much of the bank's genetics is ability genetics.* Answers to opinion items tilt with reasoning ability through many routes, and ability has a genetics of its own, so an item can carry a heritable share that is ability's rather than the item's. Paper 1's keyed score, on the same people, lets the ability component be partialled out of every item's genetic signal. What remains is the genetics of the opinion or the preference itself, and the difference between the two readings, per item, is a map of which of the bank's apparent dispositions are reasoning wearing the clothes of taste, which is paper 1's return-path reading with the genome added as the third axis.

*4.5 Depth as a multiplier, made a number.* For a latent disposition the bank recovers, the precision of its genetic signal rises with the number of items that measure it and with the number of people who answered enough of them, and the two trade against each other under a fixed attention budget. It becomes knowable, per disposition, how many items and how many people bought a given precision, which is the price list of the genetics of that disposition in the currency of paper 0a. And it sets the admission bar for a new item in the same currency: a candidate item earns its place in the discovery set by the genetic information it is expected to add beyond what the items already held recover, so the bank grows toward the dispositions whose genetics is still imprecise and stops growing where more items add nothing.

**5 · The return path: what the crossing gives back to every other axis**

*The bank.* Every item gains a tag: heritable share within its frame, its genetic neighbours, and its role. No bank computes any of that about itself. The tag tells the bank which items are about the person and which about the world, and the admission bar of 4.5 tells it what to ask next.

*The derived trait scales.* Authored scales cut the bank into traits by an author's theory. The genetic structure of 4.2 is an external test of the cut: a scale whose items share genetic architecture is genetically coherent, and a scale whose items split across genetic factors is measuring two dispositions under one name. The scales are then read with that verdict attached, and revised where the recovered structure and the genetic structure agree against the author.

*The keyed ability axis.* Paper 1 gave the bank a residual discrimination per item, how much its split moves with ability. This paper gives the same item its genetic decomposition, and the two together say whether an item's ability-loading is itself genetic, which is a question about the item and about ability at once.

*The profile facts.* The country reading of 4.3 gives the anchors a map of where culture decides, and it gives every cross-country comparison of a self-reported trait a label: comparing a trait whose heritable share differs by country compares two partly different things.

*Interests and the body.* Declared interests are items, and the map of 4.1 covers them: which of what people are into carries a heritable share, item by item, before any inventory is designed. And the body's occasion reports raise the within-person question this paper cannot answer alone: whether the variability of an answer across days is heritable, which needs occasions and belongs to the body's paper.

*The design layer.* The item-role rule of section 3 and the admission bar of 4.5 are constraints on the serving policy of paper 0a, and they change what the policy is for: not only placing people and identifying couplings, but growing the discovery set toward the dispositions whose genetics is least known, blind to the genome for those items, on a budget the policy prices.

**6 · What would have to hold**

Consumer genome files are genotyped at common markers and imputed to millions of common variants; imputation quality falls with allele frequency and with distance from the reference panel; chip, version and vendor are batch structure aligned with country and ancestry; the files carry no quality metrics and no proof of whose they are. Paper 1's section 6 states these conditions and they apply here unchanged: every claim is a common-variant claim, ancestry is modelled, batch enters every model, and identity is established from the data before a file is a phenotype's partner.

Items mean the same thing across the groups compared. An item's heritable share compared across countries is meaningless if the item was read differently in each, so invariance is tested per item and per language, and the map is drawn over the items that pass.

Discovery items are served blind to the genome. The serving policy may use the genome as a prior for placement, never for whether or when a person is asked an item whose genetics is being read, and an item's role travels with it.

Selection is anchored. Who answers a voluntary bank, and who among them imports a genome, is not a random sample of anyone, and a self-selected population cannot validate its own selection model without an outside anchor. Every number carries the population it is about.

Output privacy is budgeted against an unbounded bank. Per-variant summary statistics released per item are the surface through which a participant can be inferred from a publication, and the bank has no end while any privacy budget does. So the system does not publish per-variant statistics per item. It publishes low-dimensional summaries, an item's heritable share and its genetic correlations with a stated set of others, under a stated budget over the whole publication history, and where a variant-level result is worth publishing for one item, it spends budget deliberately and the release schedule forbids differencing across a changing cohort. Paper 0's section 3.3 names the mechanism; this paper names the tension that makes it binding.

Consent is familial-aware and scoped to the use. A genome describes relatives who did not consent, and its consent is stated at family scope and for the reading of traits the system itself measures.

**7 · The requirements the design generates**

Per-item precision needs people, and the bank divides people among items, so the discovery set is served on a matrix design with the anchor set guaranteeing every discovery item a floor of genotyped answerers; the map's precision varies by item and the honesty grammar carries it. The country reading needs the genotyped population spread across countries at shared ancestry, and the ancestry reading needs it spread across ancestries within countries. The disposition-level precision of 4.5 needs items per disposition and people per item in the balance the price list states. None is a reason to want less. Each is what a fully populated bank beside a fully populated genome looks like from the inside.

**8 · The potential, at full population**

Fully populated, this crossing is a genetics of everyday life: for everything a population was ever asked, whether the answer is something the person is or something the world made, and how the two shares move across the world. It reads the structure of what people are like from how they answer rather than from how an author divided them, tests that structure against the one source of variation that precedes culture, and grows toward the dispositions it understands least. It makes the bank the deepest phenotype battery that exists beside genotypes, at a depth set by a population's attention rather than by a committee's budget, and it does so on genomes the population already held.

Inside the system it tags every item, tests every scale, prices every disposition's genetics in attention, and gives the design layer a new job. Paper 1's keyed score says what reasoning is; paper 2's observers say what a self-report is worth; this paper says which of the things a person says about themselves are about them at all.

**9 · Open questions the data would have to answer**

Whether the heritable-share map saturates before it reaches the long tail of the bank, or whether the tail is where the surprises are. How the placement and discovery roles interact when an item is both the best placement item for a person and a discovery item for the population, and how much attention the blind-serving rule costs against the cycle. How a finite privacy budget is best spent across an unbounded set of items, which is a design problem with no precedent at this shape. Whether the genetic and phenotypic factor structures of a deep bank disagree a little or a lot, which decides how much of what people say about themselves is the world talking. And whether a disposition's genetics estimated from many weak items on many people converges to what a purpose-built instrument would find, or reads something the instrument never could.
