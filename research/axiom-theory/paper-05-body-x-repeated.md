# Which changes are the body

*Paper 5 of the axiom series: occasion reports of the body crossed with every measurement a person repeats.*

**Status: theory research.** Nothing here describes the app or proposes a change to it; `research/README.md` says what a paper is and how one reaches the product. Paper 0 carries the general argument and the level separation this paper lives inside; paper 0a the repetition schedules it needs.

**Perfect-form test.** If the app did not exist, this would still be worth saying because everything measured about a person is measured on a day the measurement ignores, a person's answers move from day to day, and without a record of what the body was doing on each day that movement is noise; any population that reports its sleep, energy and illness daily beside measurements it repeats would be the first in which the movement can be read.

**Abstract.** A measurement of a person is taken on an occasion, and the occasion is almost never recorded. So when a person's answers move between two days, nothing says whether the person changed, the day changed, or the measurement did. What is known about the body's effect on the mind is known between people, where it is small and confounded, and the question that matters, what a night's sleep or a week's illness does to this person's answers, is a within-person question that has not been measured at scale because no population has reported its body daily beside things it repeatedly answered. This paper describes such a population and argues that occasion reports of the body, sleep, energy and illness, later joined by measured bands from a worn device, are the covariate that makes within-person change interpretable everywhere else in the series. It states what becomes knowable: for every repeated item and trait, the share of a person's day-to-day change the body accounts for, which sorts the bank into states and traits by measurement rather than by an author's belief; the timescales on which the body's effects arrive and fade; which way the coupling runs, readable only where a measured channel stands beside the perceived one; what follows a reported illness, under the identification a self-reported and state-dependent event allows; and who is body-sensitive, a trait of the person no one-shot instrument can hold, with a genetics of its own. It names the return path to every axis and the conditions, chief among them that the days a person feels worst are the days they are least likely to answer, so the body covariate is missing exactly where it matters unless the design makes reporting it cheaper than skipping.

**1 · The problem, stated structurally**

A questionnaire item is answered on a Tuesday. The Tuesday is not recorded, beyond a timestamp, and the answer is treated as a fact about the person. When the same item is answered differently a month later, there are three explanations and the record cannot distinguish them: the person changed, the day was different, or the measurement is noisy. Every claim about how people change rests on telling those apart, and almost none of the data anyone holds can.

The body is the largest part of what a day is. Sleep, illness, energy, pain, hunger and the hormonal week move mood, attention, patience and appetite for risk, and they move them within a person from one day to the next. What is known about that is mostly known between people: whether people who sleep less are, on average, different from people who sleep more. Between-person couplings of body to mind are small and confounded by everything that differs between two people. The within-person coupling, what a bad night does to this person compared with their own usual, is a different quantity that need not agree with the between-person one in size or even in sign, and it has not been measured at scale because measuring it needs the same people reporting the body and repeating the mind, daily, for a long time.

A population that reports its body every day beside items it repeats is that measurement.

**2 · The two axes alone, and where each stops**

The occasion reports are the body axis: a daily self-report of sleep, energy and illness on a short scale, and, where a person consents to it, a small set of bands folded on their own device from a worn sensor, sleep duration, resting heart rate, movement, one band a day. Alone, the reports are a diary of the body. They can describe a person's sleep and say how it varies, and they can say nothing about what it does. Their ceiling is consequence.

The repeated measures are every other axis on the days it repeats: state-worded items in the bank served on a schedule, trait-scale items re-served, a keyed test taken again, a day of sealed predictions about a friend. Alone, they show change without a cause. A person's answers move, and the record cannot say whether the movement is the person, the day or the noise. Their ceiling is explanation.

Each axis's ceiling is the other axis. The body gives change a cause to be tested against; the repeated measures give the body something to be a cause of.

**3 · The crossing, and the two channels in it**

Paper 0's section 5.2 sets the level: nothing here is a claim about how people differ, and everything is a claim about how a person moves around their own centre. Two parts of the mechanism are specific to this pair.

The body is a covariate for the occasion, and an occasion covariate is what makes within-person change interpretable. Given a person's own history of answers and their own history of body reports, the part of today's answer movement that tracks today's body is separable from the part that does not, and the residual is what remains to be explained by the person's development, by the world, or by noise. Without the covariate, all of it is noise together. This is paper 0's claim that a fixed axis cannot see change and an occasion axis cannot tell change from noise, instantiated.

The body is reported through two channels and they are not the same instrument. A self-report of sleep is a perception: it tracks how the person felt about the night and it is coloured by the mood of the morning it is asked in. A band from a worn device is a measurement: it tracks what the body did and it is blind to how that felt. The two predict different things. Perceived sleep should track the affective side of the mind's movement, and measured sleep should track the cognitive and physiological side. So the design does not rank the two channels and discard one; it routes them, reading each against the outcomes it should govern, and treats their disagreement as data. Where only the perceived channel exists, every reading is a reading of perception, stated as such.

**4 · What becomes knowable**

*4.1 The state share of every item and trait.* For each repeated item and each trait a scale recovers, the share of a person's day-to-day movement that the body accounts for, estimated within persons and then described across them as a distribution. Items whose movement tracks the body are state items: they measure the day as much as the person. Items whose movement does not are trait items whose day-to-day noise is noise. The bank is thereby sorted into states and traits by measurement rather than by what an author believed when writing the item, and the sorting is per item across an unbounded bank. A scale written to measure a stable trait whose items carry a large state share is measuring the week, and the crossing says so.

*4.2 Timescales.* The body's effects arrive and fade on timescales, and the repetition schedules of paper 0a are what resolve them. Last night against last week; a single poor night against a run of them; an illness's onset against its tail. For each state dimension the lag structure of the coupling becomes knowable, and with it the sampling rate at which a within-person series on that dimension is worth collecting at all. A dimension that moves within hours is aliased by a daily report and is not an estimand of this design, and the theory says so rather than reading noise as dynamics.

*4.3 Direction.* Whether a bad night lowers the answers or a low morning lowers the report of the night is not readable from the perceived channel alone, because the report and the answers share a morning. It becomes readable where the measured channel exists: a band that recorded the night before the person woke is not coloured by the morning, so a coupling between the band and the day's answers has a direction the perceived coupling does not. The order of measurement is fixed as part of the design, the body report before the day's items, and the perceived and measured channels are read against each other to say how much of the perceived coupling was the morning talking.

*4.4 What follows an illness.* An illness is an event of paper 0's second class: the person did not choose it, but neither did the system, and its onset is confounded with sleep, stress and season, while its reporting is itself state-dependent. Under the identification that class allows, the onset as the unit of analysis, an explicit assumption of no time-varying confounder affected by prior outcomes, and the reporting modelled beside the event, it becomes knowable what a reported illness does to a person's answers over the following days and weeks, on which dimensions, and how far the return to their own centre is complete. Across many illnesses in many people the shape of that curve is a fact about the body's reach into the mind that no clinic has, because no clinic holds a person's baseline of a hundred prior days.

*4.5 Who is body-sensitive.* The state share of 4.1 is estimated within each person, so it varies across them. Some people's answers move with their body and some people's barely do. That sensitivity is a trait of the person that no one-shot instrument can hold, because it is a property of a series, and it becomes a phenotype for every other crossing: whether body-sensitivity travels with the trait scales, whether it differs in the declared groups of paper 4, whether it is heritable under paper 3's conditions, and whether the people who know a person can see it, under paper 2's readings.

*4.6 The occasion covariate everywhere else.* Every within-person reading in the series inherits this one. Paper 1's ability trajectory gains a covariate that separates a bad test day from a decline. Paper 2's post-reveal dynamics gain one that separates learning how one is seen from a bad week. Paper 4's variability signature gains the covariate it is partly about. The body is the axis the within-person half of the whole series stands on, and it is the only axis whose occasions are as frequent as the days.

**5 · The return path: what the crossing gives back to every other axis**

*The bank.* Every repeated item gains a state share, so the bank knows which of its items measure the day and which the person. A state item is not a worse item. It is the right item for a within-person reading and the wrong one for a trait, and knowing which is which is a fact about the bank's own vocabulary.

*The trait scales.* Each scale gains a within-person reliability: how much of a person's movement on it is the body and the day, against how much is stable. A scale with a large state share is re-read as a state measure, and the trait it names is read from the person's centre across occasions rather than from any one administration.

*The keyed ability axis.* Paper 1's separation of cognitive state from cognitive trait is made concrete: how much of a person's test-day score the body accounts for, and therefore how much of a level survives the body.

*Ties.* Whether a bad night changes how a person reads the people they know, on the distinctive part of paper 2's decomposition, is a reading about the body's reach into social perception that no design has held, because none has held sleep beside graded predictions about friends across days.

*The genome.* Body-sensitivity is a phenotype, and so is the within-person variance of each state dimension. Their genetics, under paper 3's conditions, asks whether how much a person's mind moves with their body is itself heritable, which is a question about the genome axis's object that a one-shot phenotype cannot pose.

*The profile facts.* Body couplings read by age band and by country are a map of how the body's reach changes across a life and across places, and the anchors gain a within-person reading they have never had.

*The design layer.* The state share tells paper 0a's policy which items to repeat and at what lag, and the timescales of 4.2 set the schedules. The body axis is also the one whose reports must be cheapest of all, for the reason section 6 gives first.

**6 · What would have to hold**

The days a person feels worst are the days they are least likely to answer. This is the condition that matters most and it is a design condition before it is a statistical one. If the body covariate is missing exactly on the days the body was worst, every coupling is estimated on the good days and understates or misreads the body's reach. So the body report is the cheapest act the system asks, cheaper than skipping, one tap on a day the person does nothing else; the leaving hazard is modelled jointly with the body reports, so a missing day is read against the person's own hazard rather than dropped; and the estimates carry the fraction of bad days that were reported at all.

The order of measurement is fixed. The body report comes before the day's items, or the coupling's direction is not readable and the report is coloured by the answers it should explain.

The channels are named on every number. A reading from the perceived channel is stated as perception; a reading from a measured band is stated as measurement; and a coupling read across the two is stated as such.

The repetition schedules exist and are designed. A within-person series needs occasions, occasions on the mind's side need repeated items on a schedule dense enough for the timescale and sparse enough not to teach the item, and the schedule is paper 0a's to set from 4.2's results.

Sampling bounds the estimands. A daily report resolves dynamics slower than two days and nothing faster, and the theory claims nothing about dimensions that move within a day.

Illness reporting is modelled as state-dependent, and the event readings of 4.4 carry the assumptions of paper 0's second class explicitly.

Measured bands live under consent and custody of their own. A band from a worn device is an involuntary measurement rather than a chosen answer, so it is consented to separately and folded on the device to one band a day, and the coupling readings that use it run under paper 0's custody rule, at the efficiency cost that rule states.

**7 · The requirements the design generates**

Occasions per person in the dozens before a within-person coupling is a number rather than a coincidence, and in the hundreds before the lag structure of 4.2 is readable. The same items repeated on the same people, on schedules. Body reports daily, including on the days nothing else is answered. Years, for seasons and for the illness curves of 4.4 to accumulate. A consented population carrying measured bands, for 4.3's direction reading. None is a reason to want less. Each is what a fully populated body axis looks like from the inside.

**8 · The potential, at full population**

Fully populated, this crossing is the first within-person map of the body's reach into the mind at population scale: for every item and trait, how much of a person's movement is the body; on what timescales; in which direction, where a measured channel makes direction readable; what an illness does over the days that follow; and who is sensitive to their body and who is not, as a trait with a genetics. It sorts the vocabulary of what people say about themselves into states and traits by measurement rather than by belief.

Inside the system it is the covariate under everything that changes. Paper 1 reads what reasoning is, paper 2 what a self-report is worth, paper 3 which of the things a person says are about them at all, paper 4 the shape of a declared difference; this paper reads which of the ways a person changes are the body, and without it none of the others can read change at all.

**9 · Open questions the data would have to answer**

How large the bad-day reporting gap is even when reporting is one tap, and whether the hazard model recovers what it costs. Whether the perceived and measured channels disagree a little or a lot, and on which outcomes each is the better instrument. Whether body-sensitivity is one trait of a person or several, one per state dimension. Whether the state share of the bank's items is stable across cultures and ages or itself a moving quantity. And whether the lag structures of 4.2 are shared across people or are themselves a way people differ.
