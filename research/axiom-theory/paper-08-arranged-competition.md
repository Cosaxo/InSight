# What a person does when it counts

*Paper 8 of the axiom series: competitions the system arranges, the cancellation that decides what such a design can measure at all, and the anchors that buy back what the comparison model destroys.*

**Status: theory research.** Nothing here describes the app or proposes a change to it; `research/README.md` says what a paper is and how one reaches the product. Paper 0a supplies the allocation argument and the survival term; paper 1 the measured ability and the rule against difference scores; paper 2 the accuracy structure this gives a criterion to; papers 5 and 6 the non-ignorability band it owes; papers 6 and 7 the worn instrument and the verification staked play needs.

**Perfect-form test.** If the app did not exist, this would still be worth saying because a paired-comparison design over a population destroys every component of an effect that is common to both sides of the comparison, which means an arranged competition cannot measure the thing it most obviously promises unless it is built with anchors that the obvious design omits — and that structural result holds for every ranked system anyone runs, whether or not anybody is studying its players.

**Abstract.** Every other axis in this series is supplied by the person and observed as it arrives. In a competition the system sets the task, the opponent, the payoff and the timing per encounter, and that combination is distinctive even though the series already randomizes reveal timing, challenge timing and offers elsewhere. The paper's centre is a cancellation. If a person's latent performance under stake s is an intercept plus a slope times s, and the stake attaches to the contest rather than to one side, then the comparison likelihood sees only the difference between two players, the common part of the slope cancels exactly, and how much a population's performance rises with what is at risk is invisible in principle rather than merely imprecise. What survives is the between-person contrast, so the estimand is a variance and a set of contrasts, and the level is recoverable only by breaking the symmetry: stakes assigned asymmetrically, a graded performance score rather than a win, or fixed-difficulty anchor contests that stand outside the comparison network. The same cancellation removes population-wide learning, so a learning rate is identified against anchors and not against a moving population mean, and conditioning on exposure to fix it conditions on a collider. From there the paper states what an arranged competition makes knowable and at what price in encounters, since a binary result carries at most one bit and its information about a latent difference is at most a quarter, which puts a usable per-person slope at the order of a hundred encounters per domain and the decisive study at tens of thousands of encounters rather than at the small trial an earlier draft claimed. Two arms are required and they cannot be one: assigned stakes and assigned opponents identify the slope and the comparison effect, while chosen stakes and chosen opponents are the only place the after-loss behaviours exist at all. Selection into entry is repaired to an intention-to-treat and a complier effect rather than to an assumption, dropout caused by the measurement's own outcome is published as a band over a stated non-ignorability parameter as papers 5 and 6 require, and the integrity model owes the three numbers paper 6 demands of any detector.

**1 · The problem, stated structurally**

Almost everything known about persons at scale is a report: a questionnaire is a report, an observer rating is a report about someone else, a passive sensor is a trace of a body rather than of a choice. Even a scored test measures a real performance in a situation with nothing at stake and nobody opposite.

What that leaves out is not a topic but an estimand. A report about how a person behaves when something is at risk is generated by a different process from the behaviour: the person must model the situation, predict themselves in it, and describe the prediction. Every step is available to self-presentation, to what the person believes about themselves, and to the fact that they have never been in that situation while being measured. So the report and the behaviour are two measurements of two things, and no depth of questioning converts one into the other — asking more about persistence measures belief about persistence more precisely.

Closing it requires observing the person in consequential situations. Observing them as life supplies them gives situations the person selected, at stakes the person chose, which is exactly the confounding to be removed. Building them is the alternative, and an arranged competition is a built situation: a task the system sets, an opponent it picks, a payoff it fixes, at a time it chooses, repeated on the same people across domains and years.

That is distinctive but it is not unprecedented in this series, and an earlier draft claimed it was. Paper 2 randomizes the timing of a reveal and unbundles the treatments it carries; paper 6 defines its central phenotype on a fixed challenge whose timing is randomized within the quarter; paper 7 randomizes an offer; paper 0a is an entire paper arguing the serving decision is the experiment. What is distinctive here is narrower and still large: difficulty, opponent and payoff are all set per encounter, and the payoff is the only manipulable incentive anywhere in the series.

**2 · The axis alone, and where each side stops**

*A competition alone is a ranking, and a ranking is thin.* A result says one person beat another under conditions that were not recorded, and carries no information about how hard the task was, how much either cared, or what either would have done at a different stake. Its ceiling is a leaderboard, and section 3 shows the ceiling is lower than it looks.

*The self-supplied axes alone have no behavioural criterion.* The bank can ask about persistence, competitiveness and risk with unlimited depth; the answers will factor cleanly and say nothing about what the person does. The logic test measures ability where nothing is at stake, so it cannot separate a person who is capable from one who is capable and shows it under pressure. Observers can be asked to predict behaviour, but their predictions are scored against other reports, which is closed. The ceiling of every self-supplied axis is a criterion.

Each is the other's ceiling, which is the shape this series keeps finding.

**3 · Three structural facts, and the one that decides the paper**

*3.1 The comparison model cancels what is common, and this is the paper's centre.* An outcome is a property of a pair, so a latent-comparison model sees only the difference between the two sides. Let a person's latent performance under stake s be an intercept plus a slope times s. If the stake attaches to the contest, both sides carry the same s, and the difference is the difference of intercepts plus the difference of slopes times s. Every component of the slope shared across the population cancels exactly.

Two consequences, and neither is a matter of precision.

The *level* of the stakes effect — how much people in general do better when it matters — is not weakly identified in this design, it is not identified at all. A finding that "stakes move everybody the same way" would be an invariance of the design being reported as a result, and an earlier draft's decisive study proposed exactly that inference.

What survives is the *contrast*: the between-person variance of the slope, and each person's slope relative to the population's. That is a real and valuable estimand, and it is the one 4.1 states.

The level is recoverable, by breaking the symmetry, in exactly three ways. Assign the stake asymmetrically, so the two sides face different risk in the same contest, and the difference of slopes is multiplied by a difference of stakes rather than by a common one. Measure a graded performance score per person rather than only who won, which takes the reading out of the comparison likelihood altogether. Or run fixed-difficulty anchor contests — a frozen opponent, or a keyed task of the kind paper 1 supplies — which sit outside the comparison network and give an absolute scale for the network to be pinned to. The third is the cheapest and it does double duty in 4.7.

*3.2 The same cancellation removes population-wide learning.* Add any common function of time to every player's latent and the comparison likelihood is unchanged. So if everyone improves, nobody's rating moves, and a learning rate estimated inside the network is a rate relative to a contemporaneous population mean that is itself moving with entry, with dropout and with everyone else's improvement. The anchors of 3.1 are what make a learning rate an absolute quantity, and section 4.7 is written against them rather than against the network.

*3.3 The matcher is a bipartite allocation, not an adaptive test.* The analogy is tempting and an earlier draft asserted it as an equivalence. It fails on three points. Adaptive testing calibrates item parameters on separate data and treats them as known, which is what makes selection on the examinee's own history ignorable; a matcher selects on an estimate of the opponent's parameter drawn from the same encounter network, so the quantity it maximizes is a plug-in whose error is correlated with the target's own history. The objective is a different shape: adaptive testing maximizes information about one person, while a matcher must serve two people at once, plus the connectivity constraint of 3.1's network, plus the experience of playing — which is a graph-level allocation and not a greedy per-encounter maximum. And the information-maximizing opponent is not the closest one: pairing two uncertain players maximizes information about their difference and about neither of them, so the informative opponent is the one whose own parameter is best known near the player's estimate. A fourth difference has no analogue at all — an item does not adapt, and an opponent plays differently against a stronger player, which breaks the conditional independence a comparison model assumes and is stated here as a condition rather than passed over.

The equivalence survives only as a limiting case: known, non-strategic opponent parameters and a one-sided objective. What generalizes is weaker and still worth stating, which is that in any ranked system the matcher is part of the measurement model and must be recorded with every result.

**4 · What becomes knowable**

*4.1 The stakes slope on performance, as a contrast, at a stated price.* An earlier draft called this effort elasticity. Nothing here measures effort: what is measured is a performance change under a stake change, which can arise from effort, arousal, attention, a shift in risk posture or strategy, from manipulation, or from the opponent's decline — three of which this paper's own later sections supply. So the parameter is named for what it is, the stakes slope, and the mechanisms behind it are separated only by further design: time-on-task, strategy records, asymmetric assignment.

Its price is computable and an earlier draft did not compute it. A binary result carries at most one bit, and its Fisher information about the latent difference is at most a quarter in logit units. So a per-person slope from a two-level stake contrast has a standard error of roughly four over the square root of the encounter count — about a hundred encounters per person per domain for a standard error near four tenths of a logit, before opponent-parameter uncertainty and before the opponent's own slope enters the same difference. A graded per-person score cuts this by roughly an order of magnitude, which is why 4.3's domain set is partitioned by whether a domain supplies one.

*4.2 Reported against enacted, as a discrepancy factor rather than a difference score.* The bank asks about persistence, competitiveness and risk; the competition enacts them. An earlier draft made the raw difference the reading, which papers 1 and 2 both refuse: a difference between two positively correlated measures is less reliable than either, and here the two are on unlinked scales — a bounded ordinal response against a latent in comparison logits — so the *sign* of the discrepancy, which the draft made the finding, is an artefact of centring and of response style. The correct object is the enacted measure residualised on the reported one inside a bivariate latent model carrying a discrepancy factor, published with that factor's own reliability. So bounded, the discrepancy is a person parameter and the bank gains what it has never had: a criterion outside itself, item by item, against which its corpus can be curated.

*4.3 Domain structure, under a sampling principle.* Contests in many domains on the same people give the covariance structure — a general factor if one exists, domain factors if they do. Two conditions that an earlier draft omitted. A factor structure is meaningful only against a stated sampling of the domain space, so the sampling principle is written down and the finding is reported as relative to it. And the between-person cross-domain covariance a factor model needs is legal only under a within-domain standardisation, since the commensurability rule of section 6 forbids comparing raw results across domains; what enters the model is each person's standardised deviation from their own expected performance under a model that contains the matcher's policy. Finally, the relation to 4.1 is stated rather than double-counted: if pressure is stakes, then a general performance-under-pressure factor is the common factor of 4.1's slopes and is the same finding, and a second factor exists only if a second source of pressure — an audience, a time limit, a ranked consequence — is manipulated separately.

*4.4 What happens after a loss, in the arm where it can happen.* Re-entry, quitting, raising the stake after losing, avoiding the opponent who won, switching domains. Each is behaviour rather than report, and each requires the player to be choosing — which is the opposite of what 4.1 and 4.5 require. A pre-committed stake schedule forbids raising the stake after a loss; a randomization floor on matching forbids avoiding an opponent. So these readings live in a chosen-stake, chosen-opponent arm, and every reading in this paper is labelled with the arm it came from.

*4.5 Assigned opponent strength, read as an intention to treat.* Randomizing the assigned strength within a band randomizes what a person learns about themselves, and the effect of that assignment on their subsequent self-assessment, entry decision and bank answers is identified by design. What is identified is the effect of the *assignment*, which bundles standing information, the experience of winning or losing, effort spent and enjoyment — and paper 2, facing the same bundling on a reveal, names three treatments and adds arms to separate them. The same is owed here. What is not available is a contrast of winners against losers: within a randomized band, who wins is decided by ability and by the day, so that contrast conditions on a post-treatment variable and is observational however the band was drawn. Realised outcome enters only under principal stratification, or with assignment as an instrument whose exclusion restriction is stated.

*4.6 The body crossing, named to a channel.* A scheduled contest is a timed event with a known onset and an experimentally assigned intensity, which makes it a clean event class for the worn stream — with two corrections. The analyte that would carry an acute-stress reading is not in paper 6's panel: that panel is metabolic, carries no proteins, and puts a steroid on a separate affinity channel with its own drift and its own validation, so the reading names its channel or it is not a reading. And the exertion carve-out an earlier draft relied on does not save it, because psychological arousal drives secretion directly and secretion is paper 6's ruler; the analyte most responsive here is the one that paper says is made in the gland and divided by flow. So the crossing is stated as the effect of assigned opponent strength on a named channel, carrying paper 6's full ruler list.

*4.7 Learning, measured against anchors.* Against fixed-difficulty anchors the intercept is ability on an absolute scale and the slope is a learning rate, and both are quantities rather than positions in a moving field. Two cautions. Exposure count is caused by prior outcomes, so entering it as a control conditions on a collider and biases the slope rather than cleaning it; it is modelled or instrumented instead. And the slope is estimated on people still present, whose presence is caused by winning, which is caused by improving — so the estimate is truncated on its own parameter and is published with the band section 6 requires.

**5 · The return path, the two arms, and the ranking**

*The bank* gains a behavioural criterion per item. *The logic test* gains the separation between capability and capability under pressure, which its own format cannot make. *The instruments* gain enacted counterparts. *Observers* gain a behavioural criterion — for paper 2's distinctive accuracy rather than for its meta-accuracy, which an earlier draft mislabelled, and computed as a profile correlation across many contests with the normative and stereotype components removed, since a single contest's outcome is mostly the matcher's band and the published rating. *The genome* gains behavioural phenotypes of the never-collected class, at the reliabilities 4.1 prices. *The body* gains an event class. *Verification* is a precondition for the staked half.

*The two arms are the design, not a detail.* One arm assigns stakes and opponents and carries 4.1, 4.5 and 4.7; the other lets the player choose both and carries 4.4. They cannot be the same arm, and an earlier draft asked for both properties at once without noticing.

*And a collinearity to break.* Where staked play runs only among verified players and unstaked play runs everywhere, stakes and verification are perfectly confounded and neither effect is readable. The design therefore runs stakes crossed with tier wherever it is permitted, and carries paper 7's offer instrument into every slope estimate as the selection correction — which is also what keeps the central parameter from being measured only on a self-selected subpopulation.

*Ranked by what they make knowable.* First, the stakes-slope contrast and its cross-domain structure, because the variance of the slope is the quantity nothing else in the series can reach. Second, the reported-against-enacted discrepancy, which turns a large self-report corpus into a validated one and needs no stake manipulation at all. Third, the assignment effects of 4.5, which the arm already runs. Fourth, the body crossing, conditional on paper 6's own measurements.

**6 · What would have to hold**

*Entry is chosen on expected outcome, and randomizing the offer repairs less than it appears to.* A randomized offer makes the invitation exogenous; acceptance is still chosen. What is identified is the effect of being offered, and — under an exclusion restriction and monotonicity, both stated — a complier effect with its complier population described. An earlier draft claimed the design attacked selection directly and then conceded in its own open questions that the repair rested on an untested assumption. There is also an estimand difference the design cannot argue away: performance under a nudged entry differs from performance under a sought one precisely through motivation, which is what 4.1 exists to measure, so readings from offered entries are labelled as such.

*The matcher randomizes, and the floor is a two-sided budget.* Every eligible opponent has a bounded-below chance, a designed share is drawn from a wider band, and the realised probability is recorded — but a wide-band match is not a dull minute for one person, it is a near-certain loss for one and a near-certain win for the other, neither of whom is the exploration's target. So the floor is stated as a per-person budget for being the mismatched side, the survival term is evaluated for both sides of every encounter, and 4.5's manipulation is reported under the attrition it induces, since its treated group is the group most likely to leave.

*Dropout is caused by the measurement's own outcome, and no model identifies that.* Papers 5 and 6 both state that a mechanism of this kind is not identified by any model and publish a band over an explicit non-ignorability parameter. This case is worse than either, so the same discipline applies with no exception: every longitudinal reading is published as a band over how much worse those who left would have performed, the joint hazard is a component of that and not a substitute for it, and 4.7's truncation on its own parameter is stated where it is read.

*Domains are not commensurable, and only standardised within-person deviations travel* — with the further condition that a between-person factor model over those deviations is legal only under the standardisation of 4.3.

*Connectivity is not the quantity; the variance across the cut is.* For paired comparisons the precision of a cross-cohort contrast is governed by the effective resistance across the cut in the encounter graph, so one bridging player with two games connects the graph and fixes nothing. Bridges must also be exchangeable across the cut — no cohort-specific advantage, no differential drift — and since 4.7 asserts everyone is improving, a bridge from three years ago does not connect today's scales, which makes a re-bridging cadence a monitored requirement.

*Integrity is a model with numbers, and its two obvious defences cancel.* A player who throws contests is estimated as weaker, which makes their thrown losses probable, so a fit that is not bounded-influence absorbs the contamination it exists to detect; the estimator is bounded-influence with a clean hold-out. A motif detector for coordinated pairs is defeated by the randomization floor offered as the strongest defence, since colluders who cannot choose each other leave no motif. Sandbagging — underperforming to draw weaker opponents, or to inflate a measured slope — is the natural attack in an assigned-opponent staked system and is in the threat model. And the detector owes what paper 6 demands of any detector: an alert rate, the positive predictive value at a stated manipulation base rate, the named person who adjudicates, and what the estimator does with a flagged encounter — since dropping flagged encounters is selection on surprising outcomes, which are the informative ones.

*Repetition is training*, so exposure enters every longitudinal model as something modelled rather than conditioned on, per 4.7.

*The stakes bound is on cumulative exposure, not on the single contest.* A ceiling per contest does not bound a loss trajectory, and a trajectory is what chasing is, so the bound is on cumulative exposure per person per window. The chasing detector is specified rather than promised: its statistic, its threshold, its latency and the action it triggers. Consent to compete at randomized stakes is specific and separate from consent to be measured. And a stake must be visible to move performance, so the manipulation cannot be blinded — an imposed stake known to be random is a different psychological object from one chosen or earned, and what is identified is elasticity to imposed stakes, which is an external-validity limit on the central parameter and is stated with it.

*Verification is a precondition for staked play*, under the factorial of section 5 rather than as a gate that confounds the two.

*The arousal conditions of paper 6 apply to 4.6 in full*, not only its exertion clause.

**7 · The requirements the design generates**

*Encounters per person of the order of a hundred per domain where the outcome is binary, and roughly a tenth of that where a graded score exists*, which is what 4.1's arithmetic costs and the reason this is a years-long instrument.

*A domain set partitioned by outcome type*, with the graded domains carrying the readings that need per-person performance and the binary ones carrying what can be read from a comparison.

*Fixed-difficulty anchor contests on a schedule*, which supply the absolute scale that 3.1's cancellation removes and without which neither the slope's level nor any learning rate exists.

*Bridging players with enough encounters on both sides of every cut to hold the cross-cohort variance below a stated bound*, re-bridged on a cadence, and exchangeable across it.

*A pre-committed randomized stake schedule with bounds on cumulative exposure*, in the assigned arm, and a chosen-stake arm beside it.

*A randomization floor stated as a per-person budget for the mismatched side.*

*Offered entries, randomized, with every reading labelled by its estimand.*

*Dropout tracked as an outcome, with a non-ignorability band published on every longitudinal reading.*

*An integrity model with its alert rate, its predictive value and its adjudication path.*

*Verified entrants for staked play, and a stakes-by-tier factorial wherever it is permitted.*

None is a reason to want less. Each is what a fully populated competitive axis looks like from the inside.

**8 · What would decide it**

*Does the stakes slope vary between people?* This is the estimand section 3.1 leaves standing, and the study is not small. Detecting a variance component needs roughly eight over the square of the slope's reliability in people; with the per-person standard error of 4.1 and a true slope standard deviation of two tenths of a logit, that reliability is about two tenths and the study needs on the order of two hundred people, a hundred encounters each, in two domains — some tens of thousands of encounters. A graded-outcome domain cuts it by an order of magnitude and is the version to run first. The decision rule is an equivalence test against a pre-specified minimum meaningful slope standard deviation with its power stated, not a failure to reject: the parameter sits at a boundary where a null distribution is a mixture, and an earlier draft's inference from a non-significant variance was invalid twice over, since section 3.1 also makes the conclusion it drew untestable in that design.

*Is the reported-against-enacted discrepancy a trait or a method artefact?* The second study runs the bank's items on the same cohort and estimates 4.2's discrepancy factor. Stability alone does not settle it, because a stable method artefact — response style, scale anchoring, domain familiarity — is equally stable, so a discriminant condition is pre-specified: a method factor entered, or a second reported instrument in a different format, with the discrepancy required to survive both.

*And the anchor question, which precedes both.* Whether a fixed-difficulty anchor can be built that people will take seriously without a stake decides whether the level of anything is measurable, and it is answerable before any of the above.

**9 · The potential, priced with each score's own condition**

A ten is a dataset after which a field's standard design changes; a five answers questions the field already had. Each score names its condition.

*Behavioural measurement of persons.* Seven if the slope's between-person variance survives its equivalence test and anchors give the level; four if the variance survives and no anchor does, since contrasts without a scale still order people; three if it does not survive. A population measured repeatedly in built situations with assigned stakes across domains does not exist at any size.

*The validity of self-report at scale.* Six, conditional on 4.2's discrepancy factor showing discriminant validity against a method factor, and unconditional on the stakes work, since it needs no stake manipulation.

*Behaviour genetics.* Conditional on the product of overlap and phenotype reliability that papers 6 and 7 state — and the reliability factor here is the binding one, since 4.1 puts a per-person slope's reliability near two tenths at a hundred encounters, which is a heavy discount. Four at that reliability, six only where a graded domain and anchors raise it.

*Assignment effects on self-assessment.* Six, conditional on the unbundling arms of 4.5 existing, and two without them, since a bundled treatment names no mechanism.

*The cancellation result itself.* Not a dataset and not priced on this scale: it is a structural statement about every ranked system, it is free, and it is the most portable thing in the paper.

The paper's value is concentrated in one variance component that a stated study settles, and the study is large rather than small — which is the correction that matters most to anyone planning it.

**10 · Open questions the data would have to answer**

Whether the stakes slope varies between people at all, which the first study settles under an equivalence bound and on which most of the paper depends. Whether an anchor contest can be built that carries effort without a stake, which decides whether any level or any learning rate is measurable. Whether a graded performance score exists in enough domains to escape the binary outcome's information limit. Whether the reported-against-enacted discrepancy survives a method factor. Whether an entry that was offered is answered by the same process as one that was sought, since every selection repair assumes it is. How wide the non-ignorability band over those who left turns out to be, given the departure is caused by the outcome itself. Whether elasticity to an imposed stake predicts anything about behaviour under a chosen one, which is the external-validity question the randomization creates and cannot answer. And whether a matcher can serve two-sided information, connectivity and the experience of playing at once, or whether those three objectives are simply in conflict and one of them has to be named as the loser.
