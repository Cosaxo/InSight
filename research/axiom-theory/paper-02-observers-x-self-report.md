# What the people who know you see

*Paper 2 of the axiom series: sealed predictions and observer ratings crossed with every self-report axis.*

**Status: theory research.** Nothing here describes the app or proposes a change to it; `research/README.md` says what a paper is and how one reaches the product. Paper 0 carries the general argument; paper 1 supplies the keyed-ability crossing this paper leans on in one place.

**Perfect-form test.** If the app did not exist, this would still be worth saying because every self-report carries a bias that cannot be seen from inside it, the correction that scales is to ask people who know the person, and any population in which many observers make sealed predictions and ratings about people they know, graded against those people's own sealed answers, across many items and many occasions, would measure that bias at a depth and breadth no informant study has assembled.

**Abstract.** A self-report is a measurement of a person taken by the one instrument that cannot see its own error. Keys, records and behaviour correct it where they exist, and for most of what people report about themselves they do not; the correction that scales is to ask someone who knows the person, and that check has been run at size but never with many items, many ties of several kinds, repeated occasions and a reveal whose timing the system controls, all at once. This paper describes a population in which each person's answers on many items are paired with sealed predictions and ratings from many people who know them, graded when both are revealed. It argues that the observer is a second instrument for the same trait whose biases differ from the self's in every mechanism but one, and names that one: self-presentation reaches the observer through the target's own conduct, so what an observer corrects is the residue of presentation their vantage does not carry, and the full term needs a key. It decomposes a prediction into three parts, the population's answer, the answer of people who share the target's profile facts, and what only acquaintance supplies, and it treats the observer's own answer as the assumed-similarity covariate the system holds for free. On that footing it argues seven things become knowable that no self-report axis reaches alone: legibility and perceptiveness as separate properties of separate people, with the reciprocity the sealed exchange supplies; two observability numbers per item, consensus among observers and agreement with the self; the self-observer discrepancy per trait and per culture, and on one keyed trait its decomposition into flattery and frame; the plane of self-other gaps rather than four cells; the effect of an early reveal against a late one, with the treatments it bundles pulled apart; whether the person knows how they are seen, measured before they are shown; and how all of it varies by kind of tie. The return path gives every self-report axis a second channel with weights that are an estimand, gives the bank two observability coefficients per item, and gives the genome crossing a discrepancy factor as a phenotype. One design rule governs the rating channel and is fixed here: a person sees only the aggregate of their observers' ratings, under a release schedule that forbids recovering any one of them, and the rule is a display rule, because every reading in the paper needs the individual rating inside the model.

**1 · The problem, stated structurally**

Most of what is measured about a person is what the person says about themselves. A self-report is cheap, scales without limit, and can ask about anything the person can introspect on. It also has one property no amount of items fixes: it is taken by an instrument that cannot see its own error. Self-presentation, the wish to be seen well; recall, the tendency to remember oneself selectively; and the absence of any vantage point from which to see how one comes across, each enter every answer, and no second answer from the same person removes them, because the second answer is taken by the same instrument.

The correction that scales is to ask someone else. Keys, records and behaviour correct a self-report where they exist, and for most of what people report about themselves they do not. The informant check does exist and has been run at size, but it is bounded in the dimensions this paper needs: few items per person, one occasion, one or two ties per target and usually one kind of tie, with any comparison across cultures made between studies rather than within one instrument. What has never been assembled is depth and breadth at once: many items, times many ties of several kinds, times repeated occasions, times a reveal whose timing is the system's to set. Every reading below needs all four.

**2 · The two axes alone, and where each stops**

The dyadic sealed-prediction record is the relational axis. For a pair of people who know each other it holds, per item and per exchange, each one's sealed answer about themselves and each one's sealed prediction of the other's answer, graded when both are revealed. Each partner is target and observer in the same exchange, so the record holds both directions of every dyad by construction. Alone it is a game with a score: it knows who predicts well and who is predictable. It cannot say what a right prediction means. A correct guess may be knowledge of this person, knowledge of people like this person, knowledge of people in general, or a projection of the guesser's own answer, and the record alone cannot tell them apart, nor say whether the answer it graded against was true. Its ceiling is meaning.

The self-report axes are the bank of unkeyed items, the trait scales derived from them, the minor scales beside those, and declared interests. Alone they measure what people say about themselves, at any depth, and they cannot see the gap between that and anything else. Their ceiling is truth: not the truth of any one fact, but the truth of the self-picture as a whole, which no item answered by the same person can check.

Each axis's ceiling is the other axis. The record gives the self-report an outside vantage point. The self-report gives the record something to be about.

**3 · The crossing, and the decomposition it needs**

Paper 0 states the general argument. Three of its parts are specific to this pair.

An observer is a second instrument for the same trait with a different bias structure, with one exception, and it is the one that matters. The self lies by self-presentation and by recall. The observer lies by closeness, seeing only some situations; by projection, assuming the other is like oneself; and by regard, wishing a friend well. These mechanisms are independent of the self's except one: an observer's picture of a target is built partly from what the target has shown and told them, so self-presentation reaches the observer's rating through the target's own conduct and disclosure. A target who presents as patient to everyone produces observers who rate patience high. What is identified against an observer's rating is therefore the residue of self-presentation the observer's own vantage does not carry: larger for tie types whose vantage is conduct rather than talk, larger for ties formed before the presentation was current, and never the whole term. The whole term is identified only against a keyed axis, and every reading in section 4.3 states which of the two it is on.

A prediction decomposes into three parts, and a fourth rides with it. For any item there is a normative prediction, the population's answer profile, which anyone can supply. There is a stereotype prediction, the answer profile of people who share the target's profile facts, which anyone who can see those facts can supply, and which is a separate accuracy, computable only because the profile-fact axis exists. What remains after both is the distinctive part: predicting this person's departure from what their category predicts, which only acquaintance can supply. Every reading below is stated on the distinctive part with the stereotype component removed, and the stereotype component is reported as its own reading, because how far a person is predicted by their category is a fact about the person and about the category. The fourth part is assumed similarity: the observer's own answer to the same item, which the system holds because every observer is a self-reporter on the same items. It is entered as a covariate rather than escaped by subsetting, because restricting to items where the pair differs conditions on both answers and keeps only the items where one of them is unusual. Distinctive accuracy net of assumed similarity is the number reported, and assumed similarity is itself a reading: how far a person predicts others by projecting.

The decomposition is a profile operation. A residual on one item has no reliability, so predictions and criteria are centred on the item means across the many items a pair shares, and the components are read as profile correlations. That is why section 7 asks for many shared items per pair. Even so, distinctive accuracy is not yet knowledge and not yet the tie's. It still divides into what this perceiver achieves with anyone, what this target grants anyone, and the residual, which is the only part that belongs to the pair. Section 4.1 makes that split and every reading names which term it is on.

Two questions, not one, have to be asked of the observer. The prediction, what will they answer, measures knowledge of the target's self-view. The rating, what is true of them, measures the observer's own view. They come apart exactly where the self-report is biased: a friend who knows you will say you are patient, and does not think you are, gives a correct prediction and a disagreeing rating. Asking only the first conflates knowing how you see yourself with agreeing, and the whole of the bias reading needs the second.

**4 · What becomes knowable**

*4.1 Legibility and perceptiveness, as separate properties of separate people.* A person who is predicted by several observers is a target measured across perceivers, and a person who predicts several targets is a perceiver measured across targets. Over a population where each person is both, the variance in distinctive accuracy splits into three parts: how predictable the target is to anyone who knows them, which is legibility, a property of the target; how good the perceiver is at reading anyone they know, which is perceptiveness, a property of the perceiver; and the residual, the relationship term, which is what this pair knows about each other that neither general legibility nor general perceptiveness explains. The split needs more than each person appearing in both roles. Perceiver and target effects need several observers per target and several targets per observer. The relationship term needs replication inside the pair, many items shared by the pair or the same item on separate occasions, because with one observation per pair the relationship term and dyadic error are the same quantity. And the reciprocity readings, whether a pair reads each other uniquely well together, need both directions of the pair, which the sealed format supplies free, since each partner is target and observer in the same exchange. All of it needs items whose splits are not degenerate, because an item everyone answers the same way carries no distinctive part to predict, and the same condition applies to people: a target's distinctive variance sets the ceiling on how well anyone can be right about them, so legibility is the target effect normalized by that variance and reported with it, and a target whose distinctive variance is near zero has no legibility estimate rather than a low one. Legibility is a trait no self-report can validate: a person can state how they believe they come across, and 4.7 measures how far that belief is right, but nothing inside their own answers could check it.

*4.2 Which items are public and which are private, as two numbers.* For every item in the bank, two quantities. Inter-observer consensus is how far informed observers converge with each other on a target, after the normative and stereotype components are removed. Self-observer agreement is how far that converged view matches the self-report. Both are disattenuated by each channel's own reliability, which the bank's repetition supplies; both are computed within tie type and pooled with the aggregate's per-target reliability entered, because a target with many observers contributes a steadier aggregate than one with few; and neither is defined on an item whose distinctive variance is near zero, which is invariance, not privacy. Some traits are enacted where others can see them, and on those items observers converge with each other and with the self. Some are lived from inside, and on those observers scatter, or converge with each other and not with the self, which is the signature of a trait others see one way and the person another. Across an unbounded bank this gives every item a pair of numbers, within a kind of tie, that says how far it can be checked from outside, and 4.6 says why no single number over a mixed tie population is a property of the item alone.

*4.3 The self-observer discrepancy per trait, per culture, and what it takes to call it flattery.* Across people, the average signed gap between self-report and aggregate rating, per trait, is the self-observer discrepancy on that trait: the difference between the self's presentation and the observers' regard and vantage, and it is named self-flattery only where the observer-side term is bounded. Read through the profile facts it becomes a map of how the discrepancy runs by country, age band and gender, and the map carries two honesty conditions. Observers share the target's culture, so a frame the whole culture shares is invisible to the crossing, because observer and self carry it together. And a positive discrepancy may be a difference in how the two roles anchor the scale rather than in the trait, which is why section 6 requires the scale to mean the same thing in both roles before a gap is read. Where a keyed axis exists the frame can be separated from the flattery on that trait: paper 1's reasoning score is one trait on which self-report, observer rating and a key are all available, over the items shown invariant across the cultures compared, and the three-way comparison decomposes the discrepancy on reasoning into self-flattery and shared frame. That result does not transfer to other traits. It establishes that a shared frame exists and gives its size in one domain, and a correction on any other trait needs its own key or its own external criterion. The number of keyed traits spanning the trait space is therefore a requirement this crossing generates, and until it is met the map is a map of self-observer discrepancy per culture, not of self-flattery.

*4.4 The plane of self-other gaps.* Because the observer answers two questions, every self-other gap is a point in a plane: the signed prediction discrepancy, prediction minus self on the item's norm-centred scale, against the signed rating discrepancy, rating minus self. The four kinds are quadrants, and the reading is the joint distribution over the plane rather than a classification. A transparent self-view is the origin. Known self-flattery or known modesty is the axis where the prediction discrepancy is near zero and the rating discrepancy is signed against the self: the observer knows how the target sees themselves and disagrees. An expected flattery that did not come is a prediction above the self with a rating that matches the self. An unshared self-view is both discrepancies signed the same way against the self, where neither the target's self-picture nor the observer's picture matches. Distance from the origin carries its own error, no person-trait point is reported without it, and the step count of the scale is a design variable that sets how fine the plane is.

*4.5 What learning how one is seen does, read against a delayed reveal.* When the aggregate of observers' ratings is shown to a person, they learn something about the gap. The reveal is an event the system controls, so its timing can be randomized, and what is identified by design is the effect of an early reveal against a delayed one, at a stated horizon, on that person's later answers: an estimand that shrinks as the delayed arm is revealed in turn, and is reported with its horizon. The reveal bundles three things, the content of the gap, the fact of having been rated, and the salience of the trait, and two further arms separate them: a reveal that says ratings exist without showing the aggregate, and a reveal on a trait whose gap is near zero. The size and sign of the gap are not randomized, so their interaction with timing is a dose reading and is stated as observational. What the person is shown is an aggregate, not a gap, so the gap is learned only against their memory of their own answer, and the sealed meta-prediction of 4.7 is what makes the dose a measured surprise rather than an assumed one. Assignment is at the person and trait, but interference runs on the tie graph and across traits: an observer who has recalibrated on one revealed target rates their other targets differently, so a target's controls are contaminated through shared observers, and a reveal on one trait moves the traits correlated with it. The design that bounds both assigns over an observer's whole tie neighbourhood at once, with neighbourhoods staggered, and keeps a reserved block of traits unrevealed in every person as a within-person control. Dropout after an unflattering aggregate is attrition on the outcome, and it enters every reading through the continuation hazard rather than as a cleaned sample. It becomes knowable whether an early reveal moves later self-reports toward the observers' view, away from it, or not at all, and whether it changes what observers later see, with the cost the theory names: a person who has seen an aggregate is no longer a naive self-reporter on that trait, and every later reading on that person states which of their answers came before and after.

*4.6 Tie type.* A partner, a friend, a parent and a colleague see different situations and hold different regard, so observability is a property of an item and a tie type together. It becomes knowable which traits a partner sees that a friend does not, which a colleague sees that a family member does not, and whether legibility is one property of a person or several. This needs the tie type recorded as data, independently of accuracy, because inferring closeness from accuracy and then reading accuracy by closeness is circular.

*4.7 Meta-accuracy: whether the person knows how they are seen.* Before any aggregate is shown, the target predicts it, sealed. The gap between that prediction and the aggregate is meta-accuracy, distinct from legibility, which is a property of the target as seen, and from self-insight, which is the self-observer discrepancy itself. It is the reading this design carries most cheaply, one more sealed answer per exchange, and it supplies the surprise that 4.5's reveal actually delivers, so that a reveal to an accurate meta-predictor is the design's own zero-dose arm. It also adds a third dimension to 4.4's plane: how far the person's belief about how they are seen sits from where they are seen.

**5 · The return path: what the crossing gives back to every other axis**

*The self-report trait scales.* Every scale gains an externally checked version: the self-report, the aggregate observer rating, and a weighted combination of the two. Combining raises precision wherever the two channels' noise is independent, and lowers bias only in the component the two channels do not share; the shared component, self-presentation reaching the observer through the target, survives any weighting. The weights are an estimand, not a half each: they are fixed against an external criterion, which is what section 9's question about later keyed outcomes decides, and until they are fixed the composite is reported as a pair of channels with their disagreement, not as one number. These are the first scales administered at population depth with a second instrument on the same items and the same people.

*The bank.* Every item gains its two observability numbers, so the bank knows which of its items observers agree on, which of those the self agrees with, and which cannot be checked from outside at all. The items that cannot are not worse; they are the ones that measure something only available from inside, and knowing which those are is a fact about the bank's own reach.

*The genome.* A weighted combination of self and observers is a cleaner phenotype than self-report alone where the weights are fixed, so every genetic reading on a self-report trait sharpens. And a new phenotype appears: the self-observer discrepancy, modelled as a residual rather than a subtraction, the self-report regressed on the observer aggregate or a bivariate latent model with a discrepancy factor, because a raw difference between two correlated, bounded measures is less reliable than either and is confounded with the level of both. The discrepancy factor's own reliability is reported with every reading that uses it, and whether self-insight, legibility and perceptiveness are heritable, and whether they share a genetic signal with the traits they are about, become questions the genome crossing can ask.

*The keyed ability axis.* Paper 1 names the reading: a good guesser may be perceptive or merely clever. The three-part decomposition of section 3 is what makes it precise. Ability should buy accuracy on the normative and stereotype parts and not on the distinctive part, and a perceiver whose distinctive accuracy, net of assumed similarity, exceeds what their ability predicts is perceptive in the only sense that means knowledge of people rather than of the population.

*The profile facts.* The anchors gain two readings. The stereotype component of section 3 is a measurement of how far people are predicted by their category, per item and per category, which is a fact about the categories the anchors define. And the map of 4.3 gives a reading of culture-level self-observer discrepancy, and a way to read whether a difference between two countries on a self-reported trait is a difference in the trait or in how the trait is presented, on the traits where a key allows the split.

*Interests.* Observers can predict and rate what a person is into. Declared interests are a self-report like any other, and the observer's view of them gives the interests axis its first external measurement, before any behavioural record exists: whether a person's declared interests are the ones people who know them would name.

*The body.* An observer can report on another's state on the same day, sealed, without either having seen the other's report: whether they seemed tired, low or unwell. That is the only external measurement of state a system of self-reports can obtain, and it gives the body's occasion reports a second channel against which their own bias can be read.

**6 · What would have to hold**

The observer knows the target, and tie type is recorded as data. Closeness inferred from accuracy and then used to read accuracy is circular; the tie type comes from the people, and accuracy is read against it.

The rating channel is honest, and honesty is bought by design. A person sees only the aggregate of their observers' ratings, never any one rating. The rule is a display rule. Each rating is held at dyad resolution with its tie type, because the perceiver, target and relationship terms of 4.1 and every reading that conditions on tie type are computable only from ratings that have not been collapsed; what the design forbids is attribution to a named observer at any surface a person can read, and a rating is readable as a single datum only by the observer who made it. The asymmetry is deliberate and is the one place this design withholds. A rating is not a claim about oneself; it is a claim about someone else, and its custody follows the person it is about, because a rating any third party could read invites the retaliation that makes any honest report about another person unsafe. What is given up is the one thing a reader would most want to see, which friend said what, and what it buys is that what they said is true. The aggregate itself is released under a schedule that forbids recovering one rating from two aggregates: a minimum increment in observer count between releases, coarsened or noised release under a stated budget over the whole history of that person's aggregates, and no release at all on an observer set the target could partition from what they already know, because the target knows who their observers are. Every published estimate carries the same output-privacy budget as any other in the system, and none is released at a resolution from which one rating is recoverable.

Prediction and rating are answered as two questions and observers distinguish them. Presentation comes first: the two questions are separated in the serving order, their order is randomized, and where the schedule permits they are asked on different days, so that conflation is reduced by design and only what survives is diagnosed. The diagnosis is an excess, not a rate: an observer's coincidence between rating and prediction is compared against what this target's own measured gap predicts, with the expected rate under the item's step count stated, and the residual is the conflation reading.

The scale means the same thing in both roles. Whether an item's response function is the same when answered about oneself and about another is an estimand tested per item, not an assumption, and the signed gap of 4.3 is defined only over items that pass. A cross-country comparison of the gap needs the item invariant in both roles in both countries, and runs over the items that clear both.

Items are shared between the self-report axes and the dyadic record, and which items a pair was asked is a policy variable, logged with its probability, so that every accuracy is reported conditional on it. An observer can only predict or rate an item the target has answered, so the items the exchange asks are drawn from the items the trait scales use, and the pairing ledger of paper 0 shows the overlap.

Pairs vary in similarity, and assumed similarity is a covariate rather than a subset. Ties form assortatively, so a prediction that equals the observer's own answer and a prediction that equals the target's coincide for alike pairs; the observer's own answer is entered, the relationship term is estimated over the range of similarity the pairs span, and that range travels with every number.

Selection enters three times and is modelled three times. Who joins the system. Which ties are recorded, which is assortative on the traits being read. And which ties keep playing, which is selection on the outcome itself, because a pair who read each other badly stop, and their exit raises measured accuracy with no learning behind it. The third is the one no statement of population repairs, and the device against it is allocation: a floor share of partners assigned by the system rather than chosen, and a floor share of exchanges scheduled rather than volunteered, so that the self-selected record is read against a slice that was not.

Reactivity is modelled. A person who has seen how they are seen is a different self-reporter afterwards, and every reading states whether it uses answers given before or after that reveal.

**7 · The requirements the design generates**

Several observers per target, for two reasons that set different numbers: the release schedule of section 6 is a display minimum, and the reliability of a target's legibility estimate is what sets the count the readings actually need, which is larger. Several targets per observer, because perceptiveness is a property measured across targets. Many shared items per pair, and repeated occasions on the same items, because the relationship term has no estimate without replication inside the pair. A range of tie types, because 4.6 has no reading with one. Pairs that differ in similarity, because alike pairs identify little even with the covariate entered. A share of assigned partners and scheduled exchanges, because the volunteered record selects on its own outcome. And keyed traits spanning the trait space, because 4.3's flattery reading exists only on the traits that have one. None of these is a reason to want less. Each is what a fully populated relational axis looks like from the inside.

**8 · The potential, at full population**

Fully populated, this crossing is the first population-scale map of self-knowledge: for every trait, how far people see themselves as others see them; for every culture, how the self-observer discrepancy runs and, where a key exists, how much of it is flattery; for every person, where their self-picture is shared and where it is theirs alone, whether they know it, and how that changes when they learn of it. It makes the self-report axes, which are most of what any system measures about a person, into externally checked measurements for the first time at this depth, and it adds three traits no self-report reaches: legibility and perceptiveness from 4.1, and self-insight from the discrepancy, each with a genetics the genome crossing can ask about.

Inside the system it is the bias corrector for everything reported. Paper 1's keyed score anchors what reasoning is; this crossing anchors what a self-report is worth, trait by trait, and the two together give the reported axes the two external checks a measurement can have: a truth for the keyed items and a witness for the rest.

**9 · Open questions the data would have to answer**

Whether the aggregate-only channel is honest enough, or whether observers soften even an aggregated rating of a friend; the conflation reading of section 6 measures this rather than assuming either way. What release schedule hides any one rating given that the target knows who their observers are: how large an increment, at what coarsening, on what scale. Whether observer ratings predict a person's later keyed outcomes and behaviour better than self-reports do, or worse, or differently by trait, which is the question that fixes the composite's weights. How large the reactivity of 4.5 is, how far it spreads through a tie neighbourhood, and whether it fades. How much of self-presentation the observer's vantage carries, by tie type, which decides how much a witness can correct and how much only a key can. And whether legibility is one property of a person or as many as there are kinds of tie.
