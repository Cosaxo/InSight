# Where the join runs

*Paper 12 of the axiom series: the genome crossings as computations under a column that must not move — the per-person join that runs on the device because the device holds both sides, the cross-person structure that runs nowhere cheap because it needs everyone's immovable genomes, the inversion of cost that follows, and the releases the owner must ration.*

**Status: theory research.** Nothing here describes the app or proposes a change to it; `research/README.md` says what a paper is and how one reaches the product. Papers 1, 3 and 6 supply the genome crossings this paper computes; paper 11 the learned representation whose genome join this paper resolves; G2 the custody architecture — computation by the column's custodian, secure aggregation with its trust conditions, the budget on a named unit of protection, the publication ledger; paper 7 the attestation that reaches integrity but not authenticity, and the pairwise primitive relatedness needs; paper 0a the policy that must ration a finite budget; G2's publication ledger, where a release has its row, and G4's value ledger, which prices a source and not a release.

**Perfect-form test.** If the app did not exist, this would still be worth saying because a custody constraint that keeps one column from moving does not forbid the joins that column enters — it re-sorts them by cost, and the sort is counter-intuitive: the per-person computation that seems most invasive is the one that runs on the device for free, while the population association that seems the plain baseline is the one that needs every immovable genome at once and is therefore the expensive, budget-bound, hard-to-identify computation. Naming that inversion, and what it makes affordable and unaffordable, is a statement about computation under an immovable column that holds wherever one column may not move.

**Abstract.** The genome is the column that must not move: a file its owner holds on their device, which no design in this series uploads. An earlier draft concluded that the learned representation therefore cannot join it, because the server cannot assemble a per-person row across the genome and the other axes. That is the wrong conclusion, and this paper is the correction. The device holds both sides of the per-person join — the genome and the person's own copy of their own public answers — so that join is single-custodian and runs where both columns already sit: the device computes, and the teacher of paper 11 ingests the genome as a budgeted gradient contributed from the device, not as a fixed summary chosen in advance. What genuinely does not run cheaply is the other direction: a per-variant association needs population structure, and population structure — the ancestry components that adjust every genotype, the linkage between variants, the cryptic relatedness that inflates every uncorrected statistic — is a computation over everyone's immovable genomes at once, which the summation channel does not reach and the pairwise primitive reaches only at cost. So the cost ordering inverts. On-device per-person modelling, including the richest thing the representation can consume, is the affordable channel; the population genome-wide association that looks like the plain baseline is the expensive one, budget-bound and confounded by the very structure it cannot cheaply compute. Three facts organize the rest. Attestation binds a computation to code and an enrolled holder but proves nothing about whether the file is a real genome or this person's, so integrity is not authenticity and authenticity routes to paper 7's supervised sample; the genome's protection rests on secure aggregation's trust conditions, which the paper states rather than assuming. The budget is not an endless allocation but a finite lifetime cap on total releases about a cohort, its unit of protection is family-scope because a genome discloses relatives, and dividing it across ancestry strata starves the small ones as a certainty rather than a risk. And the join itself is a re-identification act, because a public individual phenotype linked to a genome-derived term names the person. The paper ends where its siblings do and an earlier draft did not: with the decisions this most privacy-loaded axis puts to the owner, stated as forks and not settled as rules.

**1 · The problem, stated structurally**

A crossing needs two data about a person in one computation. The genome cannot be brought to the other data, because the only posture that admits it keeps the file on its owner's device, never uploaded. An earlier draft read that as: the server cannot assemble a row across the genome and the other axes, so the cross-axis join cannot be computed. The second clause does not follow from the first, and correcting it is this paper.

The device holds more than the genome. It holds the person's own answers — which are public, but the device has its own copy — and it holds, or can fold, the person's body channels and everything else the person supplies. So the per-person join across the genome and the other axes is a computation over columns one custodian already holds, and G2's rule is explicit that where one custodian holds every private column a join touches, the join runs there. It runs on the device. What leaves is whatever the computation is designed to emit — a gradient, a score, a summary — under the budget, and the genome file stays.

What does not sit on any one device is the population. A per-variant association is not a per-person quantity; it is a statement about how a variant tracks a phenotype across everyone, and computing it honestly needs quantities defined over everyone's genomes at once: which ancestry a person's genome places them in, which variants travel together, which participants are secretly related. Those are the joins the constraint makes expensive, because each is a computation over immovable columns that no single device holds. The paper's real subject is that sort — what the constraint makes cheap, what it makes dear, and that the ordering is the reverse of the intuition.

**2 · The two axes alone, and the custody asymmetry, stated as an assumption**

*The genome is a column pinned by its custody class.* Most identifying, unchangeable, and describing relatives who never consented, it is device-only: parsed, scored and modelled where it lives, never uploaded. Its ceiling alone is that it predicts nothing without a phenotype on the same people.

*The phenotype is public — under a design assumption this paper names rather than assumes.* This series' model makes the bank's answers readable and their population statistics exact. That is a design choice, not a law, and the whole asymmetry below depends on it: if the phenotype were protected, both columns of a genome crossing would be private and every crossing would be the hard two-custodian case. So the paper states public-phenotype as an assumption and marks where its conclusions are contingent on it, rather than presenting it as given.

*The asymmetry, and its sting.* One side public, one immovable. For the per-person join this is pure help: the device holds the immovable side and a copy of the public side, so it computes alone. But the public side has a sting the earlier draft missed — a public, individually attributable phenotype makes the genome-derived term easier to re-identify, because the term is a function of a named person's public answers and their private genome, and linking the two is the identifying operation. The public side helps the computation and is itself a re-identification surface, and section 3.4 budgets it as one.

**3 · Four facts about the computation**

*3.1 The per-person join is single-custodian and runs on the device.* Everything defined on one person at a time — a polygenic score, a per-person model contribution, the gradient paper 11's teacher needs — is a computation over columns the device holds, so it runs on the device and emits a budgeted output. Paper 11's teacher therefore does join the genome, and an earlier draft's marquee impossibility is withdrawn: the teacher ingests the genome as a clipped, budgeted gradient contributed per round from the device, which is the federated route G2 names and paper 11 relies on. A fixed pre-chosen summary — a score, a small embedding — is a cheaper, lossier alternative to the gradient, not the only option, and the choice between them is a cost-versus-fidelity decision the design makes rather than a limit it suffers.

*3.2 The cross-person structure is the hard case, and its pieces fall on two different channels.* A per-variant association needs population structure, and structure is not a per-person quantity. Ancestry components — the axes that place each genome in the population so an association is not confounded by who is related to whom — are the eigenvectors of a cross-person genotype covariance, a computation over everyone's immovable genomes and then a loading applied to each; the covariance is a sum over people, enormous and itself disclosive to release, and the eigensolve and the loadings are further computation and further releases. Linkage between variants is likewise a sum over people, and likewise large. Cryptic relatedness — whether two participants secretly share ancestry that would inflate every uncorrected statistic — is a quantity defined on pairs of people, which a sum does not reach at all, and which runs only on paper 7's pairwise primitive at its cost. So the honest decomposition is three-way, not the earlier draft's two: quantities summed over people (covariance, linkage), which the summation channel reaches but at a size and a disclosure the draft ignored; quantities defined pairwise over people (relatedness), which summation cannot reach; and quantities defined per person (scores, gradients), which run on the device. The per-variant association the draft called "one sum, the easy case" needs all three, so it is the hard case, and its adjustment pipeline is its real cost.

*3.3 Attestation is integrity, not authenticity, and the protection rests on aggregation's conditions.* A device computing over its own genome can run the wrong code, and attestation binds the computation to stated code and an enrolled holder, so the emitted term is what that code produced. It proves nothing about the file: a person can attest a correct computation over a fabricated genome, or a relative's, or one they do not hold, because attestation reaches the integrity of the computation and not the authenticity of its input. Paper 7 makes exactly that its hard attribution problem, soluble only by a supervised biological sample, and this paper routes input authenticity there rather than claiming attestation covers it. And the genome's protection when a term leaves is not attestation at all but secure aggregation, whose trust conditions the paper states: a threshold of non-colluding participants, no server-participant collusion, and a participant set the server does not choose — with the hard part named, that attesting a term while hiding it is a bound proved to the aggregate without revealing the contribution, which is a computation, not an assertion.

*3.4 The budget is a finite lifetime cap, its unit is the family, and small strata starve.* Every released coupling leaks about its contributors, and G2 budgets the leakage; the earlier draft called the budget the binding resource and then treated it as an endless allocation a policy schedules. Under any honest composition a finite total budget over a genuinely unbounded release stream means only finitely many couplings are ever released about a cohort — a hard lifetime cap, not a schedule — so paper 3's unbounded map is releasable only to a finite prefix, and which couplings make the prefix is the allocation. The unit of protection is not the person: a genome discloses about half of a first-degree relative's, so a genomic release leaks about non-contributing, non-consenting relatives, and the unit is family-scope, as G2 provides for a quantity on a relation. And dividing a finite budget across ancestry strata starves the small ones with certainty — rare-ancestry people get no genetics unless budget is reserved for them — which is an equity choice, not an open question. The public-phenotype re-identification surface of section 2 spends against this same budget, because linking a named person's public answers to their genome term is itself a disclosure.

**4 · What becomes knowable**

*4.1 The inversion, which is the finding.* Under the unconstrained architecture that biobanks use, the per-variant association is the cheap baseline and rich per-person modelling is the luxury. Under the immovable-column constraint the order reverses: per-person modelling runs on the device for the cost of a budgeted release, while the per-variant association needs the whole population's structure and is the expensive, budget-bound, confounded computation. This is a statement neither axis makes alone and neither paper 1 nor paper 3 could make, because it is a property of the crossing under custody rather than of the genetics: the constraint does not merely tax the genome's crossings uniformly, it re-sorts them, making affordable the per-person joins those papers treated as advanced and making dear the population associations they treated as basic. A programme that budgeted its genome work on the biobank's cost ordering has it backwards.

*4.2 The learned representation's join, corrected.* Paper 11's teacher joins the genome on the device, as a budgeted gradient per round, and the release surface is the sequence of those rounds, budgeted as a sequence per G2 and per paper 11's differencing condition. What the teacher consumes is not the file and not a summary chosen in advance but a contribution the training computes, and the adequacy question — how much a teacher loses by never seeing the file — is mostly dissolved by the gradient route, since a gradient carries what the objective needs rather than what a pre-chosen summary guessed. Where a fixed summary is used instead, for cost, its adequacy against the gradient is the measurement, and it is bounded by the budget the gradient sequence would have spent.

*4.3 The per-variant couplings, priced with their real pipeline.* The genetics of measured ability, of the bank's items, of the body's functionals — each as a per-variant association whose cost is the adjustment pipeline of 3.2: an ancestry computation over immovable genomes with its own release, a linkage computation likewise, a relatedness control on the pairwise primitive, and then the association itself, all spending against the finite cap of 3.4. This is most of what papers 1, 3 and 6 publish, and it is the expensive channel, not the cheap one.

*4.4 Within-family genetics, on the pairwise primitive, priced as the dear channel it shares with relatedness control.* The kinship papers 1 and 3 want and the cryptic-relatedness control every association needs are the same pairwise quantity, so within-family designs do not add a channel — they use, at scale, the one the associations already needed, and the paper prices relatedness once as a population-scale cost that both consume.

*4.5 What the constraint gives back — input privacy and take-back, not output richness.* An earlier draft claimed the device-side computation is richer than an upload's. Against an upload-and-compute architecture the release is a budgeted summary either way, so the releasable output is the same, and the on-device benefit is not richer output but input privacy — the raw file never sits on a server — and erasability, the file can be taken back, which a released coupling cannot. The paper claims those two and drops the richness claim, and notes that a released coupling is as permanent under the device channel as under an upload, so take-back applies to the raw file and never to what was released from it.

**5 · The return path, and the decisions this axis puts to the owner**

*The genome axis* gains the correct cost ordering of its crossings, the on-device per-person channel, and the three-way sort of what runs where.

*The learned representation* gains its genome join as a budgeted gradient, which is the position this paper and paper 11 hold jointly rather than a limit either leaves open.

*The bank and the body* gain the honest price of their per-variant genetics, which is the adjustment pipeline and not a single sum.

*Verification* is load-bearing twice: paper 7's attestation gives the computation integrity, and paper 7's supervised sample is the only thing that gives its input authenticity, so a genome crossing without paper 7 computes correctly over files it cannot vouch for.

*The design layer* rations the finite budget through paper 0a's policy and gives each release its row in G2's publication ledger.

*The decisions, as forks the owner rules — because this is the most privacy-loaded axis and an earlier draft wrote them as settled rules.* Which couplings win the finite lifetime prefix of section 3.4, since the bank proposes without end and only finitely many are ever released: the owner sets the allocation, with what each release exposes and to whom. Whether a per-person genome embedding may be released at all, and to whom, which is the most identifying release in the series and which paper 11 already forks for its own representation. Whether within-family genetics is a population product or a designed pilot, since the pairwise primitive's cost may make only the second affordable. Whether an enclave may hold raw genomes at all to measure a summary's adequacy, which is a second custodian and therefore a custody exception G2 says each deployment decides in the open — so section 8's experiment is conditional on this fork, not a given. Whether small ancestry strata get reserved budget or are left to starve, which section 3.4 makes a certainty and therefore a choice. And how a minor's file is handled — parental consent built, per this series' rule that a consent requirement is met by building the consent, not an absolute refusal the earlier draft wrote.

*Ranked by what they make knowable.* First, the inversion of 4.1, because it is the finding the cost analysis produces and the rest is its consequence. Second, the corrected learned-representation join, because it repairs a crossing the previous paper could not complete. Third, the honest price of the per-variant channel. The within-family designs are ranked by their cost, which is the population-scale pairwise cost.

**6 · What would have to hold**

*The genome does not leave a device it is not already on* — the per-person join runs on the device that holds the file, and the only exception, an enclave holding raw genomes to measure summary adequacy, is a forked custody decision named in section 5, not an assertable given, so section 6 does not say "at any stage" as if there were no exception.

*Public-phenotype is stated as the assumption it is*, and every conclusion that rests on the asymmetry is marked contingent on it.

*The three-way sort is respected* — per-person on the device, summed-over-people on the aggregation channel at its true size and disclosure, pairwise-over-people on paper 7's primitive — and no per-variant association is priced as a single sum.

*Attestation is used for integrity and paper 7's supervised sample for authenticity*, and a crossing states which of its guarantees is which, because attestation over an unauthenticated file vouches for the arithmetic and not the genome.

*Secure aggregation's trust conditions are stated with every summed release* — the non-collusion threshold, the server not choosing the set, and the attest-under-hiding bound — because "a summary leaves" is a claim about those conditions and not about attestation.

*The budget is a finite lifetime cap on total releases, at family-scope unit*, composed under a named rule, with the prefix it affords stated and the differencing sequence of re-releases counted, and small-stratum starvation surfaced as a choice.

*The re-identification surface of the join is budgeted*, because linking a public phenotype to a genome term is a disclosure.

*Ancestry adjustment is itself a computation with a release cost*, not a free covariate, and its confounding of an unadjusted association is stated where the association is.

*Consent is per crossing and at family scope*, a minor's handling is a built parental consent or an owner fork, and no conclusion rests on an absolute refusal.

**7 · The requirements the design generates**

*An on-device computation capable of a clipped budgeted gradient and a per-person score*, since the per-person channel is the affordable one and the gradient is what the representation consumes.

*A secure-aggregation protocol with its non-collusion threshold, its server-blind participant set, and its attest-under-hiding bound stated*, for the summed-over-people quantities.

*A pairwise primitive at population scale*, for relatedness control and within-family genetics, priced once as the cost both share.

*An ancestry-structure pipeline* — the population covariance, its eigensolve, its loadings — with the release each stage spends, since every association depends on it.

*An output-privacy budget as a finite lifetime cap at family-scope unit*, composed under a named rule, with a release policy allocating its prefix and a reserve decision for small strata.

*Paper 7's supervised-sample attribution*, for input authenticity, and paper 7's attestation for computation integrity, named apart.

*A forked enclave custody decision* before any raw-genome adequacy measurement, and the measurement designed to estimate the field gap rather than assume the enclave's transfers.

*Family-scope consent, and a built parental consent for minors.*

None is a reason to want less; each is what the genome crossings look like when the column does not move and the cost is counted honestly.

**8 · What would decide it**

*What does one per-variant association actually cost, adjustments included, against a stated budget?* Compute one association with its full pipeline — ancestry components over immovable genomes, linkage, relatedness control on the pairwise primitive, the association, each release budgeted — and report the total spent against a named lifetime cap at a stated family-scope unit and composition rule. The decision this settles is quantitative and the earlier draft never put a number on it: if one adjusted association spends a large fraction of the cap, the per-variant channel affords only a handful of couplings for the cohort's life and the inversion of 4.1 is decisive; if it spends little, the map of 4.3 is an allocation problem.

*How much does the on-device gradient give the representation, against the file?* Conditional on the enclave fork of section 5, and designed to estimate the field gap rather than assume the enclave's — a stratified or reweighted enclave, since consent selects on ancestry and disposition — the teacher's code length consuming the device gradient against consuming the file, per target. Small, and paper 11's genome axis is nearly the file; large, and even the gradient loses the file's structure, which bounds paper 11 here.

*And the pairwise question that precedes the population associations, not only the within-family designs.* Whether relatedness control runs on the pairwise primitive at population scale, since every unbiased association needs it — if it does not, per-variant genetics is confounded for the whole cohort, not merely expensive, and the affordable genetics is the per-person channel alone.

**9 · The potential, priced with each release's own condition**

A ten is a dataset after which a field's standard design changes; a five answers questions the field already had. Every score is conditional on the budget the first experiment measures and, where marked, on the forks of section 5.

*The inversion of the cost ordering.* Seven as a finding about computation under an immovable column, conditional on the first experiment showing the per-variant channel is genuinely budget-bound, because a re-sort of which genetic analyses are cheap is a statement any custody-constrained genomics inherits and none assumes.

*The on-device per-person genetics, including the representation's gradient.* Seven, conditional on the second experiment's gap being small, because a device-only, budgeted, take-back-able per-person genetic channel is an architecture biobanks do not have and it is the affordable half of papers 1, 3, 6 and 11; three where the gradient loses the file's structure.

*The per-variant population associations.* Four, conditional on relatedness control running at scale and on the budget affording more than a prefix; two if it is confounded or the cap is tight, since then the population channel barely publishes.

*Within-family genetics.* Four, conditional on the pairwise primitive at scale and the owner's population-versus-pilot fork.

The value concentrates in the inversion and the on-device channel, both decided by the two experiments and the forks section 5 hands the owner.

**10 · Open questions the data would have to answer**

What one adjusted per-variant association actually costs against a named budget, which decides whether the per-variant channel is affordable at all and is the number under everything. How much the on-device gradient gives the representation against the file, and whether the enclave gap transfers to the field. Whether relatedness control runs at population scale on the pairwise primitive, without which every association is confounded and not merely dear. How large the ancestry pipeline's own releases are, since they spend before any association does. Whether the family-scope unit of protection can be made precise enough to compose, given a genome discloses relatives by degrees. Whether the finite lifetime cap leaves a prefix worth calling a database, or whether an unbounded bank's genetics is a few dozen releases and then silence. And whether, on the balance of all of it, a genome computed where it sits and never released whole is worth more than one that uploaded once and could never be taken back — which is the bet the whole posture makes, and which the owner's forks in section 5, not this paper, decide.
