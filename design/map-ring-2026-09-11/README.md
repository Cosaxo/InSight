# The Map ring, every kind of dot — the accepted canvas (D464)

**Request 13 of `docs/VISUAL-REQUESTS.md`**, drafted on a Claude Design
canvas 2026-09-10 and **accepted by the owner 2026-09-11** (*"its fine how
it is now"*). Built the same day; D464 records what shipped.

The canvas itself is
<https://claude.ai/code/artifact/e4f27851-16c6-4250-a6cf-ef82bfdb8523> —
ephemeral, like every upload, which is why these eight artboards are here.
They are the durable record of what was accepted, not a live surface: the
tree is the live one.

| Artboard | What it decides |
| --- | --- |
| `Main.dc.html` | the ring at rest, bead groups parted by a hairline tick, You off |
| `YouOn.dc.html` | the You arc INSIDE the rim, labelled, its beads solid where the value is carried |
| `ChoiceTapped.dc.html` | a three-option question: the chip row, the tapped bead's own ties |
| `PickTapped.dc.html` | a catalogue card: chips wearing the catalogue's pictures, the `+7 more`, the Pikachu sentence |
| `ScaleTapped.dc.html` | a scale: one dot, and a sentence that says high/low rather than an option |
| `YouTapped.dc.html` | a profile value tapped — counted inside one question's sample, by the chips it carries |
| `OverBudget.dc.html` | 300 of 885 drawn, a trimmed group folded into `+n` at its place |
| `Legend.dc.html` | the guide ⓘ, with every basis sentence the picture owes |

`canvas.json` is the layout (eight 390 × 1040 frames, four to a row).

**What the draft chose, where the plan left the choice to the canvas** —
each is one edit here if it is ever revisited:

- the You arc sits **inside** the rim, so the rim stays a ring of
  questions and the arc reads as who gives them;
- a bead carries **no picture on the rim** (a bead is 3 px); the pictures
  are on the tapped card's chips, from the catalogue's own tiles (D421);
- a group the budget trims keeps its **strongest** bead and folds the rest
  into a small **+n** at its place;
- groups are parted by a **hairline tick**, not a gap — a gap is what
  parts topics, and two silences of different sizes read as one hierarchy.
